# El banco: three stories (draft 3, after two rounds of reviews)

Place 2, El parque. Scene 4 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (eleven stories), and El camino, El campo and El lago (stories 12 to 20).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (El camino): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres.
Words he has met (El campo): bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene.
Words he has met (El lago): pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, entra, come, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for El banco (from the 40-scene list): hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.

The picture: a paved corner of the park. Three wooden benches stand in a row, facing us, with grass and trees behind. On the left, a small cart with an umbrella: the piragua man (el piragüero) sells shaved ice in paper cones (piraguas) and open bottles of water. A lamp post stands behind the benches; a wire runs across the top of the picture, and five pigeons (pájaros) sit on it. At the right edge, a hedge with a gate in it: the way home.

People, each drawn so that nobody is mistaken for another:
- the man (el hombre; "el señor" when someone speaks to him or of him): black hair, a short beard, a blue shirt.
- the woman (la mujer; "la señora" in story 22): long dark hair, a yellow blouse.
- an old gentleman (un señor): white hair, a white moustache, a brown jacket, a hat, a cane.
- a young man (un joven): dark hair, a red cap, a big orange rucksack.
- a lady with a pushchair (la señora, story 23): a ponytail, a green dress.
- the piragua man: an apron, behind his cart.
- two dogs of the same size that belong to the piragua man and lie by the cart (story 22): an old one (grey, a white muzzle, white eyebrows, ears and tail hanging, slow) and a young one (white with black patches, ears up, quick).

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Carmen dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!". In story 23, where a baby sleeps, it is stars only, from the first step to the last: no chime, no hop, no line.
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a line that says what was right ("No, Coquí." and then a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said); then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays without its reward.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es un banco." "Es un pájaro." "Es Carmen." "Es el piragüero.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
A line that says what somebody is doing is spoken while they are doing it, never after.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Answer bubbles over the frog, as before:
- **Doing bubbles**: pictures of what the frog can do; he touches one. Places drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game: a star for a round done right at the first try, a red cross for a round that went wrong. Both stay.

The three games do not start the same way: story 21 starts with something he says, story 22 with a call, story 23 with something he does.

## Story 21: La piragua

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a cart of shaved ice, people on the benches, and a row of pigeons on the wire with their eyes on the food. Get each thing to the one it is for, and keep it from the pigeons.
- **At stake:** peace on the benches. Whatever goes wrong, a pigeon gets the food or the drink, and that pigeon does not go back to the wire: it settles on the back of a bench and stays. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** hombre, mujer, come, bebe.
- **Back again:** pájaro, agua, para, niño, niña, quiere, mira, gracias, no más.
- **Heard along the way:** piragua.

**Start:** the cart on the left; the piragua man behind it, shaving ice. Carmen stands at the cart. The man sits on the first bench, the woman on the second, Luis on the third; all empty-handed. Five pigeons on the wire. The frog stands in front of the cart with a little tray on his head.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un hombre." (He twinkles.) NARR: "Y una mujer." (She twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. Carmen puts a piragua on the frog's tray. NARR: "La piragua es para el hombre." The big sign is on the man. He touches him. The frog carries it over; the man takes it and eats. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "El hombre come."
3. Carmen puts an open bottle of water on the tray. NARR: "El agua es para la mujer." The sign is on the woman. He touches her. The frog carries it over; she takes it and drinks. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "La mujer bebe agua."
4. **He warns them, taught.** The man lays his piragua down beside him on the bench to open his newspaper; the woman stands her bottle beside her to look in her bag. A pigeon flies down from the wire to the man's bench and pecks at the piragua. It goes on pecking. Two speaking bubbles: "¡El pájaro come!" and "¡El pájaro bebe!" The big sign is on "¡El pájaro come!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El pájaro come!" The man looks up and snatches his piragua away; the pigeon flies back up to the wire. Right signal. NARR: "El hombre dice: ¡Gracias!" The man eats it up; Carmen takes the paper away.
5. The pigeon comes down again, to the woman's bench, and puts its beak in the open bottle. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡El pájaro bebe!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El pájaro bebe!" The woman snatches her bottle away; the pigeon flies back up. Right signal. NARR: "La mujer dice: ¡Gracias!" She drinks it dry; Carmen takes the bottle away, and the frog's tray with it. Everybody is empty-handed again.
6. **Eat it, drink it, taught.** Carmen gives the frog a piragua and an open bottle of water of his own; he holds one in each hand. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Come, Coquí!" Two doing bubbles: eating (the frog with the cone at his mouth), drinking (the frog with the bottle tipped up). The big sign is on eating. He touches it. The frog eats the piragua, all of it. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Coquí come piragua."
7. Carmen gives him a new piragua for his empty hand. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Bebe, Coquí!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on drinking. He touches it. The frog tips the bottle up and drinks it dry. Right signal. While he drinks: NARR: "Coquí bebe agua."

**The turn**

8. The frog has done as he was told. He turns to Carmen and holds up the empty bottle. Behind his back a pigeon flies down, lands on his other hand and eats the piragua he was keeping, all of it. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El pájaro se come la piragua de Coquí." The frog looks at the empty paper. The pigeon does not go back to the wire: it settles on the back of the first bench and stays. Carmen takes the paper and the bottle. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Say (he warns them).** Carmen takes the man a new piragua and the woman a new bottle. He lays his down beside him and opens his newspaper; she stands hers beside her and looks in her bag. A pigeon flies down from the wire. It lands on the man's bench and pecks at the piragua, and goes on pecking. (Or: it lands on the woman's bench and puts its beak in the bottle, and keeps it there. Drawn fresh.) Two speaking bubbles: "¡El pájaro come!" and "¡El pájaro bebe!"
   - Right: the one whose thing it is looks up and snatches it away. The pigeon flies back up to the wire. Right signal. A star. NARR: "El hombre dice: ¡Gracias!" (Or: "La mujer dice: ¡Gracias!")
   - Wrong: the other one looks up, picks up their own thing and looks about: nothing there. The pigeon goes on eating (or drinking). While it is still at it: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pájaro come." (Or: "El pájaro bebe.") The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; the owner snatches away what is left: half of it. The pigeon, full, settles on the back of a bench. A cross.
   - After either: the man and the woman finish what they have; Carmen takes paper and bottle away, gives the frog his tray again, and stays by the cart.
10. **Decision B, Touch (who it is for).** Four people, all empty-handed: the man on the first bench, the woman on the second, Luis on the third, Carmen standing by the cart. The piragua man puts a piragua on the frog's tray. NARR: "La piragua es para la mujer." (Or: "para el hombre", "para el niño", "para la niña". Drawn fresh; the man and the woman twice as often as the children.) He touches one of the four.
   - Right: the frog carries it over; that one takes it and eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to the one he touched, who shakes their head and does not take it. The frog stands there holding the tray up; a pigeon comes down, eats the piragua off the tray, and settles on the back of a bench. A cross. The piragua man puts a new one on the tray. NARR: "No, Coquí. La piragua es para la mujer." (The true line.) The sign; he touches the right one; that one eats. Carmen takes the tray.
11. **Decision C, Do, last (eat it or drink it).** Carmen gives the frog a piragua and an open bottle of water, one in each hand (which hand is drawn fresh). A pigeon flies down and lands behind him, in the middle, where he cannot see it; Carmen can, and she can see which hand it has its eye on. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Come, Coquí!" (Or: "¡Bebe, Coquí!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: eating, drinking.
   - Right: the frog eats the piragua up (or drinks the bottle dry). The pigeon hops out on that side and finds the thing at his mouth. Carmen claps her hands; it flies back up to the wire. Right signal. A star. Then he finishes the other in peace.
   - Wrong: the frog does the other. The pigeon hops out on the side of the thing he was told to finish, and has it: it eats the piragua out of his hand (or it knocks the bottle out of his hand, and drinks the water from the ground). It settles on the back of a bench. A cross. Carmen gives him another of each. NARR: "No, Coquí. Carmen dice: ¡Come!" (Or "¡Bebe!") The sign; he does it. Then he finishes the other.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Carmen brings the frog one more piragua, and a bottle of water for herself. They sit down together in front of the benches. By the pigeons sitting on the benches:
   - Only the one from the turn (three stars): the piragua is a big one in three colours. He eats in peace; the one pigeon watches from its bench. Stars and a heart.
   - Two or three pigeons: they sit along the backs of the benches and watch every bite.
   - Four pigeons: they come down and stand all round him. He eats all the same.
13. While they do it: NARR: "Coquí come piragua. Carmen bebe agua." NARR: "¿Y el pájaro? ¡El pájaro quiere más!" (The pigeon from the turn leans toward the piragua.) COQUÍ: "¡No más!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** hombre 4 (step 1; step 2 twice; step 4), mujer 4 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 5), come 7 (step 2; step 4: the bubble, the frog; step 5, the bubble; step 6 twice; the turn), bebe 6 (step 3; step 4, the bubble; step 5: the bubble, the frog; step 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A and C: come or bebe (the one that was true or was said). B: hombre or mujer when one of them was called or wrongly touched; nothing when it lay between the children.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in four. C one in two. In B, "el" and "la" halve it for a learner who hears them; the noun must do the rest.

## Story 22: ¡Señor! ¡Señora!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** evening. People get up from the benches to go home through the gate, and things are left behind. Get each thing to its owner before the owner has gone.
- **At stake:** the thing in play. If it goes wrong, the owner goes home without it, and the thing goes into Luis's box of lost things under the lamp post and stays there. (The dog's bone stays with the wrong dog.) The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** señor, señora, joven, viejo.
- **Back again:** hombre, perro, camina, despacio, corre, ¿dónde está?, gracias, de nada, es, de, mira, adiós, no.
- **Heard along the way:** del, de la, mi sombrero.

**Start:** the three benches in evening light. On the first, the old gentleman, his hat on the bench beside him; on the second, the woman, her yellow sun umbrella leaning beside her; on the third, the young man, his rucksack beside him, and at the far end of it the man from story 21, reading his newspaper. The two dogs lie in front of the cart. Carmen and the frog stand in the middle. Luis is by the lamp post, with an empty box.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un señor." (The old gentleman twinkles.) NARR: "Una señora." (The woman twinkles.) NARR: "Y un joven." (The young man twinkles.) NARR: "Un perro viejo." (The old dog twinkles.) NARR: "Y un perro joven." (The young dog twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. **He calls, taught.** All three get up at once and walk to the cart; the hat, the umbrella and the rucksack stay on the benches. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Señor!", "¡Señora!", "¡Joven!" The big sign is on "¡Señor!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Señor!" The old gentleman turns round, sees his hat, comes back and puts it on. Right signal. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Mi sombrero! ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ: "De nada, señor."
3. The three bubbles; the sign is on "¡Señora!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Señora!" The woman turns round, comes back and takes her umbrella. Right signal. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ: "De nada, señora."
4. The three bubbles; the sign is on "¡Joven!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Joven!" The young man turns round, comes back and takes his rucksack. Right signal. NARR: "El joven dice: ¡Gracias!" All three sit down again with something from the cart.
5. **Whose it is, taught.** Carmen comes from behind the benches holding up a small paper bag from the cart. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es de la señora!" The big sign is on the woman. He touches her. The frog carries the bag to her. Right signal. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!"
6. **The dogs, taught.** Carmen finds a bone under a bench and gives it to the frog; she turns to the dogs. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del perro joven!" The big sign is on the young dog. He touches him. The frog gives him the bone. Right signal. The young dog runs round the cart with it. While he runs: NARR: "El perro joven corre." The old dog lies where he is. NARR: "El perro viejo no corre."
7. Carmen finds another bone. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del perro viejo!" The sign is on the old dog. He touches him. The frog gives him the bone. Right signal. The old dog gets up and walks slowly under the cart with it. While he walks: NARR: "El perro viejo camina despacio."

**The turn**

8. The man from story 21 folds his newspaper, lays it on the bench, gets up and walks out through the gate. Nobody sees. The newspaper lies there. NARR: "¡Ay, no!" Luis sees it, snatches it up and runs to the gate. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Señor! ¡Señor!" The man has gone. NARR: "¿Dónde está el hombre?" Luis comes back, puts the newspaper in his box and stands the box under the lamp post. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Say (he calls).** One of the three, drawn fresh, gets up to go home. As they get up, a small brown wallet drops from their pocket onto the paving; it is the same wallet whoever drops it. They walk to the gate and stop there, back to us, a hand on the gate. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Señor!", "¡Señora!", "¡Joven!"
   - Right: the frog calls. That one turns round, comes back and picks the wallet up. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias!" (Or "La señora dice:", "El joven dice:".) Right signal. A star. That one waves and goes home through the gate.
   - Wrong: the frog calls. One of those still sitting looks up, puzzled. The one at the gate opens it and steps through, and stops to shut it. While they stand there: NARR: "No, Coquí. Es un señor." (Or "Es una señora.", "Es un joven.") The sign shows the right bubble; he says it. The one beyond the gate hears, turns, waves as to a goodbye, and goes: too far to see what lies on the paving. A cross. Luis puts the wallet in his box.
10. **Decision B, Touch (which dog).** The two dogs sit side by side in front of the cart (which is on the left is drawn fresh); both look at the frog. Carmen finds a bone under a bench, gives it to the frog and turns to the dogs. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del perro viejo!" (Or: "¡Es del perro joven!" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: the frog gives him the bone. He chews it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog gives the bone to the one he touched, who takes it gladly and lies down on it. The other dog watches, ears down. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Es del perro viejo." (He twinkles.) The sign; he touches him; the frog strokes him. There is no other bone.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (whose it is).** The two people who are left get up and stand at the cart for something to take home, side by side, facing the cart (who stands on the left is drawn fresh). Carmen comes from behind the benches holding up a small paper bag. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del señor!" (Or "de la señora", "del joven": one of the two who are there. Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: the frog hops over with the bag; that one takes it. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" (Or the true one.) Right signal. A star. Both go home through the gate.
   - Wrong: the frog hops to the one he touched, who looks at the bag and shakes their head. Meanwhile the owner has been served, walks to the gate, steps through and stops to shut it. While the owner stands there: NARR: "No, Coquí. Es del señor." The sign is on the owner; he touches; the frog holds the bag up high. The owner waves as to a goodbye, and goes: too far to see what the frog is holding. A cross. Luis puts the bag in his box. The other one goes home too.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. The lamp comes on. By what did not get home:
   - Only the newspaper from the turn (three stars): the man from story 21 comes back through the gate, looking about; Luis holds up the newspaper; the man takes it and waves. The box is empty. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise: what was lost (the wallet, the paper bag, or both) waits in Luis's box under the lamp, with the newspaper, for tomorrow.
13. The piragua man shuts his umbrella and pushes the cart away. The two dogs go with him: the old one slowly, the young one running ahead. If the bone went to the wrong dog, the one that got none walks with his head down. While they go: NARR: "El perro viejo camina despacio. El perro joven corre." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señor!" The piragua man waves.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** señor 7 (step 1; step 2: the bubble, the frog, "El señor dice", "De nada, señor"; steps 3 and 4, the bubble; and twice more at the turn), señora 9 (step 1; steps 2 and 4, the bubble; step 3: the bubble, the frog, "La señora dice", "De nada, señora"; step 5 twice), joven 9 (step 1 twice; steps 2 and 3, the bubble; step 4: the bubble, the frog, "El joven dice"; step 6 twice), viejo 4 (step 1; step 6; step 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A: the call that was right. B: viejo or joven. C: the one it belonged to.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in three. B one in two. C one in two.

## Story 23: El bebé duerme

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a baby asleep in a pushchair by the benches, at the end of a long day. Look after everybody without waking the baby.
- **At stake:** the baby's sleep. Each wrong move ends in a noise; the baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out of the pushchair. The lady rocks him to sleep again, but the toy stays on the ground, where nobody dares pick it up. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** cansado, contento, triste, duerme.
- **Back again:** bebé, perro, está, ¿quién?, ¿cómo estás?, señor, señora, mira, ahora, no.
- **Heard along the way:** contenta (Carmen está contenta), ¿cómo está?

**Start:** evening light. The lady sits on the middle bench with the pushchair beside her; its hood is up; the baby is asleep in it; we see his face. Luis sits slumped on the first bench, arms hanging, head drooping, puffing. Canelo stands wagging his tail. The man from story 21 sits on the third bench looking down at a piragua that has fallen on the ground; a tear on his cheek. Carmen, with her rucksack on, and the frog stand in front.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un bebé." (The pushchair twinkles.) NARR: "El bebé duerme." NARR: "Carmen dice: Shh. El bebé duerme."
2. Luis droops. NARR: "Luis está cansado." Canelo wags his tail. NARR: "Canelo está contento." The man looks at his piragua on the ground. NARR: "El señor está triste." Carmen smiles. NARR: "Y Carmen está contenta."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong; stars only)

3. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está triste?" The big sign is on the man. He touches him. The frog takes him a new piragua from the cart. He smiles. Stars. NARR: "Ahora el señor está contento."
4. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está cansado?" The sign is on Luis. He touches him. The frog brings Carmen's rucksack for a cushion; Luis leans back on it. Stars. NARR: "Luis está cansado."
5. **He says how somebody is, taught.** Abuela comes in, sits down on the first bench beside Luis and shuts her eyes. Canelo stands at her feet, wagging his tail. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cómo está Canelo?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Está contento!", "¡Está cansado!", "¡Está triste!" The big sign is on "¡Está contento!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Está contento!" Stars. Abuela, eyes shut, takes a ball from her lap and rolls it along the ground; Canelo fetches it without a sound, and again, and then drops at her feet, flat, tongue out, eyes half shut. NARR: "Ahora Canelo está cansado." Abuela lets her shawl down over him.
6. It is getting late. Abuela feels for Canelo's collar and clips his lead to the bench. He sits up, ears down, a tear on his cheek. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y ahora? ¿Cómo está Canelo?" The three bubbles; the sign is on "¡Está triste!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Está triste!" Stars. Abuela strokes his head and unclips the lead; he wags. NARR: "Ahora Canelo está contento."
7. **The baby sleeps, or does not, taught.** The lady pulls the hood of the pushchair down over the baby; we cannot see him now. She looks in under it, then turns to the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: Shh. El bebé duerme." Two doing bubbles: singing (the frog with his mouth open and notes), keeping still (the frog standing still, the raised hand of "wait" beside him). The big sign is on keeping still. He touches it. The frog stands quite still. The pushchair is still. Stars.
8. A small hand waves out from under the hood. The lady looks in. NARR: "La señora dice: Ahora el bebé está contento." The two doing bubbles; the sign on singing. He touches it. The frog sings his two notes, softly: "co-quí, co-quí". A laugh under the hood; the hand goes in. The lady lifts the hood: the baby is asleep. Stars. NARR: "Ahora el bebé duerme."

**The turn**

9. Luis gets up, rested, stretches, and goes to the cart for a piragua; the rucksack stays on the bench. A pigeon lands on the handle of the pushchair. The frog shouts at it, as loud as he can. COQUÍ: "¡Coquí!" The pigeon flies off. The baby wakes with a cry and throws his rattle out; it lands on the paving. Everybody freezes. The lady rocks the pushchair; the baby sleeps again. The rattle stays where it fell. NARR: "Carmen dice: Shh, Coquí. El bebé duerme." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign; stars only)

10. **Decision A, Do (sing or keep still).** The lady pulls the hood down over the baby; we cannot see him. The pushchair is still. She looks in under the hood, then turns to the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: El bebé duerme." (Or: "La señora dice: El bebé está contento." Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: singing, keeping still.
   - Right, "duerme": he stands still. The pushchair is still. Stars. A star on the card. The lady lifts the hood: he sleeps.
   - Right, "está contento": he sings softly. A laugh under the hood. Stars. A star on the card. The lady lifts the hood: he has sung him to sleep.
   - Wrong (he sings; the baby was asleep): a cry under the hood, and a toy flies out. A cross. The lady lifts the hood and rocks the pushchair until he sleeps. Then: NARR: "No, Coquí. Shh. Ahora el bebé duerme." She pulls the hood down again; the sign shows keeping still; he does it; she lifts the hood.
   - Wrong (he keeps still; the baby was awake): nobody plays with him. A whimper under the hood, then a cry, and a toy flies out. A cross. While he cries: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El bebé no duerme." The sign shows singing; he sings; a laugh; the lady lifts the hood: he sleeps.
11. **Decision B, Say (how is Canelo?).** Abuela sits on the first bench with her eyes shut; Canelo is at her feet, off the lead. Something happens to him first, drawn fresh: abuela holds the ball up and he stands wagging (contento); or he runs twice round the benches after a pigeon and drops at her feet, flat, tongue out, eyes half shut (cansado); or abuela feels for his collar and clips his lead to the bench, and he sits, ears down, a tear (triste). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cómo está Canelo?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Está contento!", "¡Está cansado!", "¡Está triste!"
   - Right: abuela, eyes shut, does what fits. Contento: she rolls the ball; he fetches it without a sound. Cansado: she lets her shawl down over him; he shuts his eyes. Triste: she strokes his head and unclips the lead; he wags. Stars. A star on the card.
   - Wrong: abuela does what fits the frog's answer, and it ends in a noise.
     - He is tired; the frog said "contento": she rolls the ball; it bumps his nose; he yelps.
     - He is tired; the frog said "triste": she strokes his head and goes on stroking; he grumbles out loud.
     - He is happy; the frog said "cansado": she lets the shawl down over him; he takes it for a game, grabs it and shakes it, growling.
     - He is happy; the frog said "triste": she strokes his head; he jumps up at her, barking for joy.
     - He is sad; the frog said "contento": she rolls the ball; he goes after it, stops at the end of the lead, and whines out loud.
     - He is sad; the frog said "cansado": she lets the shawl down over him; he whines out loud from under it.
     The baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out. A cross. The lady rocks him to sleep. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Canelo está cansado." (How he is; he is still like that.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; abuela does what fits. If the lead was clipped, it is unclipped now.
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (who feels that way).** Three things happen, one after the other. Canelo runs round the benches once more and drops in front of them, flat, tongue out, eyes half shut. Luis, standing by the cart, tips his piragua; the ice slides out of its paper onto the ground; his head goes down; a tear on his cheek. The man on the third bench opens his newspaper and laughs at something in it, without a sound, his feet swinging. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está cansado?" (Or: "¿Quién está triste?", "¿Quién está contento?" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the three.
   - Right: the frog goes to that one, quietly, with what fits: the rucksack from the bench, for Canelo's head; a new piragua for Luis; for the man nothing: the man turns the page to him, and the frog laughs without a sound. Stars. A star on the card.
   - Wrong: the frog goes to the one he touched with what fits the word, and it ends in a noise.
     - She asked who is tired; he touched Luis: Luis pushes the rucksack away and sobs out loud.
     - She asked who is tired; he touched the man: the man laughs out loud at the rucksack.
     - She asked who is sad; he touched Canelo: Canelo licks the ice, and yelps at the cold.
     - She asked who is sad; he touched the man: NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Otra! ¡Gracias!", far too loud.
     - She asked who is happy; he touched Canelo: the frog bounces up to him to laugh; Canelo grumbles out loud.
     - She asked who is happy; he touched Luis: the frog bounces up to him to laugh; Luis sobs out loud.
     The baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out. A cross. The lady rocks him to sleep. NARR: "No, Coquí. Canelo está cansado." (Or: "Luis está triste.", "El señor está contento." That one twinkles.) The sign; he touches the right one; the frog goes to him with what fits.
   - After either: Carmen sees to whoever is left: the rucksack under Canelo's head, a new piragua for Luis.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Abuela opens her eyes and gets up. Everybody goes, on tiptoe. The baby sleeps. By the toys on the ground:
   - Only the rattle from the turn (three stars): the frog picks it up without a sound and lays it in the pushchair. Stars and a heart, without a sound.
   - Two or three toys: Carmen and Luis gather them on tiptoe as they go.
   - Four toys: they lie all round the pushchair; the lady gathers them herself, one by one, a finger to her lips.
14. Canelo lies flat, his head on the rucksack. NARR: "Canelo está cansado." The frog droops, arms hanging. NARR: "Y Coquí está muy cansado." NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a casa." COQUÍ: "Hasta mañana."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** duerme 5 (step 1 twice; step 7; step 8; the turn), contento 6 (step 2; step 3; step 5: the bubble, the frog; step 6: the bubble, the line after; step 8 makes 7), cansado 6 (step 2; step 4 twice; step 5: the bubble, the line after; step 6, the bubble), triste 5 (step 2; step 3; step 5, the bubble; step 6: the bubble, the frog).

**A miss is filed under:** A: duerme or contento (the one she said). B: the feeling that was true. C: the feeling that was asked.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in three. C one in three.

## For the owner, one line each

- A piragua is Puerto Rican shaved ice in a paper cone. The word is not on your list; it is heard along the way.
- "Hombre" and "mujer" are only ever said by the narrator. To a person, and about a person who can hear, the stories say "señor", "señora" and "joven".
- "¡Joven!" is how a young man or a young woman is called in Puerto Rico. "Un joven" is a young man.
- "Viejo" is taught on a dog. Said of a person it is blunt; the polite word is "mayor" ("un señor mayor"), which comes later.
- "Bebe" (drinks) and "bebé" (baby) differ only in which half is said louder. Story 21 has no baby in it; story 23 never says "bebe". Of a person the stories say "bebe agua", never a bare "bebe", which is heard as drinking alcohol. Everyday Puerto Rican speech says "toma agua" more than "bebe agua"; "bebe" is your word and is correct.
- A crying baby is not "triste" in Puerto Rico (he "está llorando"), so the baby is only ever "contento" or asleep.
- "Cansado" is drawn as worn out (slumped, drooping), not as yawning: yawning is "tiene sueño".
- The feeling words are taught with "está": "Luis está cansado." The feminine is heard once ("Carmen está contenta").

## What the first round of reviews changed (draft 1 to draft 2)

- Story 21: the frog's warning names what the pigeon is truly doing (it pecks, or has its beak in the bottle), so "¡El pájaro come!" is never said of a pigeon that only stands there. Commands are "¡Come, Coquí!", with the name after, so they are not heard as "Coquí eats". "Bebe" of a person always has "agua" after it. The pigeons are a flock of five, and the one that steals is the one that settles. The cup became an open bottle (a cup and a cone looked alike). The piragua man no longer speaks ("señor" was not yet taught, and he would not say "la mujer").
- Story 22: nobody is called "viejo". "Viejo" and "joven" are taught on two dogs; people are "señor", "señora", "joven". Nobody who leaves is replaced by "another just like" them. The young lady is gone (she would be "joven" too).
- Story 23: the baby's waking could not stay in the picture, so each miss leaves a toy on the ground. "No duerme" let old "no" decide. Tired is no longer lying down (it read as asleep), and is not cured with water (it read as thirsty). Sad and tired Canelo no longer both lie down. "Luis bebe" is gone (a baby is in the picture).

## What the second round of reviews changed (draft 2 to draft 3)

- Story 21: at the turn he had drunk as told and lost his piragua for it; now the pigeon strikes after he has finished and turned away. In the last decision the pigeon comes out on the side of the thing Carmen named, and she claps it off. A wrong warning no longer needs a second theft: he puts it right while the pigeon is still at it, and half is saved. The man and the woman sit on different benches, each with their own thing beside them. "Para el niño" and "para la niña" can be called too, so that the children are not always wrong.
- Story 22: with no clock, someone "walking away" cannot be caught or missed; now there is a gate, and each one stops at it. The three calls are taught in one scene (all three go to the cart at once). In the game the thing is a wallet that drops from a pocket, so nobody forgets twice, and the old gentleman never leaves his cane. Luis calls "¡Señor!" after the man in the blue shirt, so "señor" is not only an old man. "El perro viejo no corre" gives "viejo" a fourth hearing, and the dogs are the same size, so that "viejo" is not taken for "big". Nobody is drawn from behind.
- Story 23: a crying baby "está triste" was not Puerto Rican; the lady now says "El bebé duerme." or "El bebé está contento." Touching Luis for "cansado" was wrong although he had been taught as the tired one; now he has rested and gone to the cart before the game. "¿Quién está contento?" can be asked too, so the happy man is not always wrong. Canelo's lead is unclipped whenever abuela strokes him. A tired dog is seen and named under the sign. Abuela no longer looks in a bag (story 19); she sits with her eyes shut. The turn is no longer Canelo's bark (story 20): the frog himself shouts at a pigeon. The whole story is stars only.
- Pairs: the child is the other picture in two pairs, not three. The pigeon drinks from a bottle, not a puddle.
- Left as it is: story 21 has the shape of story 18 (a touch on the one named, then saying what a bird does). The hood of the pushchair hides the baby, as the bush did in story 14; both states are taught under the sign first.

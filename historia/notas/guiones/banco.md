# El banco: three stories (draft 1, before reviews)

Place 2, El parque. Scene 4 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (eleven stories), and El camino, El campo and El lago (stories 12 to 20).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (El camino): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres.
Words he has met (El campo): bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene.
Words he has met (El lago): pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, entra, come, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for El banco (from the 40-scene list): hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.

The picture: a paved corner of the park. Three wooden benches stand in a row, facing us, with grass and trees behind. On the left, a small cart with an umbrella: the man who owns it sells shaved ice in paper cones (piraguas) and bottles of water. A lamp post. Pigeons (pájaros) walk about on the paving. Luis (the boy, green shirt) and Carmen (the girl, braids, pink dress) are here; abuela comes at the end of the day.

New people, each drawn so that nobody is mistaken for another:
- the man (el hombre): black hair, a short beard, a blue shirt.
- the woman (la mujer): long dark hair, a yellow blouse.
- an old gentleman (un señor viejo): white hair, a white moustache, a cane, a brown jacket.
- a young gentleman (un señor joven): dark hair, a red cap, a rucksack.
- an old lady (una señora vieja): grey hair in a bun, a purple shawl.
- a young lady (una señora joven): a ponytail, a green dress.
- a baby in a pushchair (el bebé).

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Carmen dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a line that says what was right ("No, Coquí." and then a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said); then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays. Putting it right brings no reward.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es un banco." "Es un pájaro." "Es Carmen.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
A line that says what somebody is doing is spoken while they are doing it, never after.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Answer bubbles over the frog, as before:
- **Doing bubbles**: pictures of what the frog can do; he touches one. Places drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game: a star for a round done right at the first try, a red cross for a round that went wrong. Both stay.

## Story 21: La piragua

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a cart of shaved ice, people on the benches, and pigeons walking about with their eyes on the food. Carry each thing to the one it is for. Keep it from the pigeons.
- **At stake:** peace on the benches. Whatever goes wrong, a pigeon gets the food or the drink, and one more pigeon settles on the back of a bench and stays. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** hombre, mujer, come, bebe.
- **Back again:** pájaro, agua, para, niño, niña, quiere, mira, tiene, toma, gracias, hay.
- **Heard along the way:** piragua, banco.

**Start:** the cart on the left; the cart man behind it, shaving ice. Carmen stands at the cart with a tray. On the first bench, the man; on the second, the woman; on the third, Luis. Two pigeons walk about on the paving. The frog stands in front of the cart.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un hombre." (He twinkles.) NARR: "Y una mujer." (She twinkles.) Carmen puts a piragua on the frog's head-tray. NARR: "Carmen tiene una piragua."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡La piragua es para el hombre!" The big sign is on the man. He touches him. The frog carries it over; the man takes it and eats. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "El hombre come."
3. Carmen gives the frog a bottle of water. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡El agua es para la mujer!" The sign is on the woman. He touches her. The frog carries it over; she takes it and drinks. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "La mujer bebe."
4. **The pigeon, taught.** The man puts his piragua down on the bench beside his own bottle of water, to open his newspaper. A pigeon lands on the back of the bench, just between the two. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mira! ¡El pájaro come!" The big sign is on the piragua. He touches it. The frog snatches the piragua up; the pigeon pecks at the bare bench and flies down. Right signal. The frog gives the piragua back. NARR: "El hombre dice: ¡Gracias!"
5. The pigeon lands again, between the two. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡El pájaro bebe!" The sign is on the bottle. He touches it. The frog snatches the bottle up; the pigeon dips its beak at nothing and flies down. Right signal. He gives the bottle back.
6. **Eat it, drink it, taught.** Carmen gives the frog a piragua of his own and a little cup of water; he holds one in each hand. A pigeon walks up behind him. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¡come!" Two doing bubbles: eating (the frog with the cone at his mouth), drinking (the frog with the cup tipped up). The big sign is on eating. He touches it. The frog eats the piragua, all of it. The pigeon looks at the empty cone. Right signal. NARR: "Coquí come."
7. A new piragua in one hand; the cup in the other. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¡bebe!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on drinking. He touches it. The frog drinks the cup dry. The pigeon looks into the empty cup. Right signal. NARR: "Coquí bebe."

**The turn**

8. Luis, on the third bench, puts his piragua down beside him to tie his shoe. A pigeon lands, eats half of it, and stays sitting on the back of the bench. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El pájaro come la piragua de Luis." Luis looks at the pigeon; the pigeon looks at Luis. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Touch (who it is for).** Carmen sits down on the third bench beside Luis; four people sit on the benches now. The cart man hands the frog a piragua (or a bottle of water; which one is drawn fresh). NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Para la mujer!" (Or "el hombre", "el niño", "la niña". Drawn fresh, each as often as the others.) He touches one of the four.
   - Right: the frog carries it over; that one takes it and eats (or drinks). Right signal. A star on the card.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to the one he touched, who shakes their head and does not take it. The frog stands there holding it out; a pigeon comes down and takes it from his hands. Another pigeon settles on the back of a bench. A cross on the card. The cart man gives him a new one. NARR: "No, Coquí. El señor dice: ¡Para la mujer!" The sign; he touches the right one; that one eats.
10. **Decision B, Touch (what the pigeon is after).** The woman puts her piragua and her bottle down side by side on the bench (which is on the left is drawn fresh) to look in her bag. A pigeon lands on the back of the bench, just between the two. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mira! ¡El pájaro come!" (Or: "¡El pájaro bebe!" Drawn fresh.) He touches the piragua or the bottle.
   - Right: the frog snatches that one up; the pigeon gets nothing and flies down. He gives it back. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog snatches up the other one. The pigeon eats the piragua (or tips the bottle over and drinks). Another pigeon settles on the back of a bench. A cross. While the pigeon is still at it: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pájaro come." (Or "El pájaro bebe.") The cart man brings the woman a new one; the pigeon lands between the two again. The sign; he touches the right one.
11. **Decision C, Do, last (eat it or drink it).** Carmen gives the frog a piragua and a cup of water, one in each hand (which hand is drawn fresh). A pigeon walks up behind him, where he cannot see it; Carmen can. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¡come!" (Or: "Coquí, ¡bebe!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: eating, drinking.
   - Right: the frog eats the piragua (or drinks the cup dry) before the pigeon gets there. The pigeon finds it gone. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog does the other. The pigeon hops up and takes what he was told to finish: it eats the piragua out of his hand (or drinks from his cup). Another pigeon settles on a bench. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Carmen dice: ¡Come!" (Or "¡Bebe!") Carmen gives him another of each. The sign; he does it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. By the pigeons sitting on the benches:
   - Only the one from the turn (three stars): the man, the woman, Luis and Carmen eat and drink in peace; the one pigeon dozes. Stars and a heart.
   - Two or three pigeons: they sit along the backs of the benches and watch every bite.
   - Four pigeons: one sits on the man's head. The man eats all the same.
13. While they do it: NARR: "El hombre come. La mujer bebe." NARR: "¿Y el pájaro? ¡El pájaro quiere más!" COQUÍ: "¡No más!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** hombre 4 (step 1; step 2 twice; step 4), mujer 3 (step 1; step 3 twice), come 5 (steps 2, 4, 6 twice, 8), bebe 4 (steps 3, 5, 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A: hombre or mujer (nothing, when it was "el niño" or "la niña"). B and C: come or bebe.

## Story 22: ¡Señor! ¡Señora!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** people get up from the benches and walk off, and things are left behind: a hat, a cane, a red cap, a purple shawl. Give each thing back before its owner has gone.
- **At stake:** the thing in play. If it goes wrong, the owner walks out of the picture without it, and the thing stays lying on the bench to the end. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** señor, señora, joven, viejo.
- **Back again:** hombre, mujer, camina, despacio, rápido, espera, gracias, de nada, ¿cuál?, es, de, mira.
- **Heard along the way:** vieja (una señora vieja), del, de la.

**Start:** the three benches. On the first, the old gentleman with his cane and the young gentleman in his red cap; on the second, the old lady in her shawl and the young lady. Carmen and the frog stand by the lamp post. Luis is at the cart.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un señor." (The old gentleman twinkles.) NARR: "Es un señor viejo." NARR: "Y un señor joven." (He twinkles.) NARR: "Una señora." (The old lady twinkles.) NARR: "Es una señora vieja." NARR: "Y una señora joven." (She twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. The old gentleman gets up and walks off slowly; his cane stays leaning on the bench. Carmen picks it up and gives it to the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del señor viejo!" The big sign is on the old gentleman. He touches him. The frog hops after him with the cane. Right signal. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ: "De nada, señor." He sits down again.
3. The young lady gets up and walks off; her book stays on the bench. Carmen gives it to the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es de la señora joven!" The sign is on the young lady. He touches her. The frog hops after her with it. Right signal. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ: "De nada, señora." She sits down again.
4. **He calls, taught.** The young gentleman gets up and walks off; his red cap stays on the bench. This time the frog is alone by the bench. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Señor!" and "¡Señora!" The big sign is on "¡Señor!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Señor!" The young gentleman turns round, sees the cap, comes back and takes it. Right signal. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Mi gorra! Gracias."
5. The old lady gets up and walks off; her shawl stays on the bench. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Señora!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Señora!" She turns round, comes back and takes it. Right signal. NARR: "La señora dice: Gracias, Coquí." She sits down again.
6. **Which one, taught.** The two gentlemen get up together and walk off side by side, their backs to us; a hat lies on their bench. Carmen did not see who left it; the frog did (it slid off the old gentleman's knee as he got up). NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuál señor?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡El señor viejo!" and "¡El señor joven!" The big sign is on "¡El señor viejo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El señor viejo!" Carmen runs after the old gentleman with the hat. Right signal. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias!" They both sit down again.

**The turn**

7. The young lady gets up and walks off quickly, and her book is on the bench again. Nobody sees. She walks out of the picture. The book lies there. NARR: "¡Ay, no! La señora joven…" Luis runs after her, comes back and shakes his head: she has gone. He brings another lady from the cart to the bench: a young lady in a green dress, the same as the first to our eyes. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

8. **Decision A, Touch (whose it is).** All four get up at once and walk slowly away in a line across the picture, their backs to us (the order drawn fresh). A thing lies on a bench: a plain paper bag, the same whoever left it. Carmen picks it up and gives it to the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es de la señora vieja!" (Or "del señor viejo", "del señor joven", "de la señora joven". Drawn fresh, each as often as the others.) He touches one of the four.
   - Right: the frog hops after that one with the bag. NARR: "¡Gracias!" Right signal. A star. All four come back and sit down.
   - Wrong: the frog hops after the one he touched, who looks at the bag and shakes their head. Meanwhile the owner walks out of the picture. A cross. The bag stays on the bench to the end. NARR: "No, Coquí. Es de la señora vieja." (The one who has gone; the place where she went out twinkles.) The other three come back and sit down; another person just like the one who left comes from the cart and sits down too, and leaves a bag as they get up again. The sign; he touches that one; the bag goes back.
9. **Decision B, Say (he calls).** One of the four, drawn fresh, gets up and walks off, back to us; something of theirs stays on the bench (the cane; the cap; the shawl; the book). The frog is alone by the bench. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Señor!" and "¡Señora!"
   - Right: the frog calls. That one turns round, comes back and takes it. NARR: "¡Gracias!" Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog calls the other. The one walking does not turn; one of those still sitting looks up, puzzled. The owner walks out of the picture. A cross. The thing stays on the bench. NARR: "No, Coquí. Es un señor." (Or "Es una señora.", as another person just like the one who left sits down, gets up and walks off, leaving the same kind of thing.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; that one comes back for it.
10. **Decision C, Say, last (which one).** Two of the same get up together and walk off side by side, backs to us: the two gentlemen, or the two ladies, drawn fresh. As they get up, a hat slides off the knee of one of them onto the bench (which one is drawn fresh; we see it). Carmen is looking the other way. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuál señor?" (Or: "¿Cuál señora?") Two speaking bubbles: "¡El señor viejo!" and "¡El señor joven!" (Or: "¡La señora vieja!" and "¡La señora joven!")
   - Right: Carmen runs after that one with the hat. NARR: "¡Gracias!" Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Carmen runs after the other one, who shakes their head. The owner walks out of the picture. A cross. The hat stays on the bench. NARR: "No, Coquí. Es del señor viejo." (Or the true one.) The two come back (another just like the owner); it happens again the same way. The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Carmen takes the hat to its owner.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

11. By the things left on the benches:
   - Only the book from the turn (three stars): the four sit on their benches in the evening light; the old gentleman lifts his hat to the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two or three things: they lie on the benches; Luis gathers them into a box by the cart.
   - Four things: the benches are covered in bags, hats and canes; Luis's box is full.
12. NARR: "El señor viejo camina despacio." (He does, away along the path.) NARR: "El señor joven camina rápido." (He passes him.) COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señor! ¡Adiós, señora!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** señor 9 (step 1 three times; step 2 three times; step 4 three times), señora 8 (step 1 three times; step 3 three times; step 5 twice), viejo 4 (step 1; step 2; step 6 twice: the bubble, the frog), joven 5 (step 1 twice; step 3; step 6, the bubble; step 7).

**A miss is filed under:** A: the word that told them apart from the one he touched (señor or señora; viejo or joven). B: señor or señora. C: viejo or joven.

## Story 23: El bebé duerme

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a baby asleep in a pushchair by the benches, at the end of a long day. Look after everybody without waking the baby.
- **At stake:** the baby's sleep. Each wrong move ends in a noise (a bark, a shout, a song), and the baby wakes a little more: he stirs; he sits up; he cries. It stays. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** cansado, contento, triste, duerme.
- **Back again:** bebé, perro, está, ¿quién?, ¿cómo estás?, bien, no, agua, bola, mira, señora.
- **Heard along the way:** contenta (Carmen está contenta), ¿cómo está?, canta.

**Start:** evening light. A lady sits on the middle bench with a pushchair beside her; the baby is asleep in it, under its hood. Luis lies along the first bench with one arm hanging down. Canelo sits wagging his tail, a ball at his feet. The young gentleman from story 22 sits on the third bench looking at a piragua that has fallen on the ground. Carmen and the frog stand by the lamp post.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un bebé." (The pushchair twinkles.) NARR: "El bebé duerme." NARR: "Carmen dice: Shh. El bebé duerme."
2. Luis yawns. NARR: "Luis está cansado." Canelo wags his tail. NARR: "Canelo está contento." The young gentleman looks at his piragua on the ground. NARR: "El señor está triste." Carmen smiles. NARR: "Y Carmen está contenta."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está triste?" The big sign is on the young gentleman. He touches him. The frog takes him a new piragua from the cart. He smiles. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el señor está contento."
4. Luis yawns again, lying on his bench. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está cansado?" The sign is on Luis. He touches him. The frog brings Luis a cup of water. Right signal. NARR: "Luis está cansado. Luis bebe."
5. **He says how somebody is, taught.** Abuela comes in at the far side of the picture, a long way off, and waves. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo está Canelo?" Canelo is wagging his tail. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Está contento!", "¡Está cansado!", "¡Está triste!" The big sign is on "¡Está contento!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Está contento!" Right signal. Abuela throws Canelo's ball; he fetches it without a sound.
6. Canelo lies down flat, tongue out. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y ahora? ¿Cómo está Canelo?" The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Está cansado!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Está cansado!" Right signal. Abuela puts down a bowl of water; he drinks.
7. **The baby sleeps, or does not, taught.** The lady with the pushchair turns to the frog and whispers. NARR: "La señora dice: El bebé duerme." Two doing bubbles: singing (the frog with his mouth open and notes), hushing (the frog standing still, the raised hand of "wait" beside him). The big sign is on hushing. He touches it. The frog stands quite still. The baby sleeps on. Right signal (no hop, no chime: stars only).
8. The baby wakes by himself and whimpers under the hood. NARR: "La señora dice: Ahora el bebé no duerme. Está triste." The two doing bubbles; the sign on singing. He touches it. The frog sings his two notes, softly: "co-quí, co-quí". The baby laughs, and goes back to sleep. Right signal. NARR: "El bebé está contento. El bebé duerme."

**The turn**

9. A pigeon lands on the hood of the pushchair. Canelo sees it and barks, once. The baby stirs: a small hand comes out from under the hood. Everybody freezes. The pigeon flies off. The hand goes back in. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Shh, Canelo! El bebé duerme." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Touch (who feels that way).** Three of them, each in a different state, drawn fresh among Luis, Canelo and the young gentleman: one tired (lying down, yawning), one happy (smiling, wagging), one sad (head down, a tear). NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está cansado?" (Or "contento", or "triste". Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the three.
   - Right: the frog brings that one what he needs, quietly (water for the tired one; the ball or a clap on the back for the happy one; a piragua for the sad one). Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings it to the one he touched, who does not want it and says so out loud (Luis: "¡No!"; the gentleman: "¡No, gracias!"; Canelo barks). The baby stirs more: he sits up under the hood. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Luis está cansado." (The one who is; he twinkles.) The lady rocks the pushchair. The sign; he touches the right one.
11. **Decision B, Say (how is he?).** Abuela, a long way off across the picture, waves. Canelo is in one of three states, drawn fresh: wagging, with his ball; flat out, tongue out; or head on his paws, whining without a sound, his ball gone under the bench. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cómo está Canelo?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Está contento!", "¡Está cansado!", "¡Está triste!"
   - Right: abuela does the right thing from where she is: she throws the ball (contento); she nods and lets him lie (cansado); she comes and gets the ball out for him (triste). Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuela does what fits the frog's answer: she throws the ball at a dog that is flat out, and it bounces off the bench with a bang; or she lets a sad dog lie, and he howls; or she comes to look for a ball that is not lost, and Canelo jumps up barking. The baby stirs more. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Canelo está cansado." (How he is.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; abuela does the right thing.
12. **Decision C, Do, last (sing or hush).** The hood of the pushchair is down over the baby; we cannot see him. The lady turns to the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: El bebé duerme." (Or: "La señora dice: El bebé no duerme. Está triste." Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: singing, hushing.
   - Right, "duerme": he stands still. The baby sleeps on. Right signal (stars only). A star.
   - Right, "no duerme": he sings softly. The baby laughs under the hood and settles. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong (he sings; the baby was asleep): the baby wakes with a start. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. La señora dice: El bebé duerme." The lady rocks the pushchair until he sleeps; the sign shows hushing; he does it.
   - Wrong (he stands still; the baby was awake and sad): the whimper grows to a wail. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. La señora dice: El bebé no duerme." The sign shows singing; he does it; the baby settles.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Abuela arrives. By how awake the baby is:
   - Asleep, as at the start (three stars): everybody tiptoes out past the pushchair. Stars and a heart, without a sound.
   - Stirring, or sitting up: the lady walks the pushchair up and down as they go.
   - Crying: the lady picks the baby up; abuela pulls a face at him, and he stops, for a moment.
14. NARR: "Luis está cansado. Canelo está cansado. Y Coquí está muy cansado." The frog yawns. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Vamos a casa!" COQUÍ: "Hasta mañana."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cansado 6 (step 2; step 4 three times; step 5, the bubble; step 6 three times), contento 5 (step 2; step 3; step 5 three times; step 8), triste 5 (step 2; step 3; steps 5 and 6, the bubble; step 8), duerme 7 (step 1 three times; step 7; step 8 twice; step 9), no duerme 1 under the sign (step 8).

**A miss is filed under:** A and B: the feeling that was asked or was true. C: duerme.

## For the owner, one line each

- A piragua is Puerto Rican shaved ice in a paper cone. The word is not on your list; it is heard along the way.
- "Señor" and "señora" are taught as what you call out to a stranger, and as "a gentleman", "a lady".
- "Viejo" is only ever said of a gentleman politely ("un señor viejo") or of a dog; calling a person "viejo" to his face can be rude.
- The feeling words are taught with "está": "Luis está cansado." The feminine is heard once ("Carmen está contenta").

# El banco: three stories (draft 2, after the first two reviews)

Place 2, El parque. Scene 4 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (eleven stories), and El camino, El campo and El lago (stories 12 to 20).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (El camino): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres.
Words he has met (El campo): bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene.
Words he has met (El lago): pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, entra, come, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for El banco (from the 40-scene list): hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.

The picture: a paved corner of the park. Three wooden benches stand in a row, facing us, with grass and trees behind. A bin stands by the benches. On the left, a small cart with an umbrella: the piragua man (el piragüero) sells shaved ice in paper cones (piraguas) and open bottles of water. A lamp post on the right; a flock of five pigeons (pájaros) sits along its arm. Luis (the boy, green shirt) and Carmen (the girl, braids, pink dress) are here.

People, each drawn so that nobody is mistaken for another, from the front or from behind:
- the man (el hombre; also "el señor" in story 23): black hair, a short beard, a blue shirt.
- the woman (la mujer; "la señora" in story 22): long dark hair, a yellow blouse.
- an old gentleman (un señor): white hair, a white moustache, a brown jacket, a cane, a hat.
- a young man (un joven): dark hair, a red cap, a big rucksack.
- a lady with a pushchair (la señora, story 23): a ponytail, a green dress.
- the piragua man: an apron, behind his cart.
- two dogs that belong to the piragua man and lie by the cart (story 22): an old one (big, grey, a long white beard and white eyebrows, slow) and a young one (small, white with black patches, quick).

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Carmen dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a line that says what was right ("No, Coquí." and then a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said); then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays without its reward.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es un banco." "Es un pájaro." "Es Carmen." "Es el piragüero.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
A line that says what somebody is doing is spoken while they are doing it, never after.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Answer bubbles over the frog, as before:
- **Doing bubbles**: pictures of what the frog can do; he touches one. Places drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game: a star for a round done right at the first try, a red cross for a round that went wrong. Both stay.

The three games do not start the same way: story 21 starts with something he says, story 22 with a call, story 23 with something he does.

## Story 21: La piragua

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a cart of shaved ice, people on the benches, and a row of pigeons on the lamp post with their eyes on the food. Get each thing to the one it is for, and keep it from the pigeons.
- **At stake:** peace on the benches. Whatever goes wrong, a pigeon gets the food or the drink, and that pigeon does not go back to the lamp post: it settles on the back of a bench and stays. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** hombre, mujer, come, bebe.
- **Back again:** pájaro, agua, para, quiere, mira, gracias, no más.
- **Heard along the way:** piragua.

**Start:** the cart on the left; the piragua man behind it, shaving ice. Carmen stands at the cart. The man and the woman sit side by side on the middle bench, empty-handed. Luis sits on the third bench. Five pigeons on the lamp post. The frog stands in front of the cart with a little tray on his head.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un hombre." (He twinkles.) NARR: "Y una mujer." (She twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. Carmen puts a piragua on the frog's tray. NARR: "La piragua es para el hombre." The big sign is on the man. He touches him. The frog carries it over; the man takes it and eats. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "El hombre come."
3. Carmen puts an open bottle of water on the tray. NARR: "El agua es para la mujer." The sign is on the woman. He touches her. The frog carries it over; she takes it and drinks. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "La mujer bebe agua."
4. **He warns them, taught.** The man puts his piragua down on the bench between them to open his newspaper; the woman stands her bottle beside it to look in her bag. A pigeon flies down from the lamp post and pecks at the piragua. It goes on pecking. Two speaking bubbles: "¡El pájaro come!" and "¡El pájaro bebe!" The big sign is on "¡El pájaro come!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El pájaro come!" The man looks up and snatches his piragua away; the pigeon flies back up to the lamp post. Right signal. NARR: "El hombre dice: ¡Gracias!" The man eats it up and drops the paper in the bin.
5. The pigeon comes down again and puts its beak in the open bottle. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡El pájaro bebe!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El pájaro bebe!" The woman snatches her bottle away; the pigeon flies back up. Right signal. NARR: "La mujer dice: ¡Gracias!" She drinks it dry and drops the bottle in the bin. Both are empty-handed again.
6. **Eat it, drink it, taught.** Carmen gives the frog a piragua and an open bottle of water of his own; he holds one in each hand. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Come, Coquí!" Two doing bubbles: eating (the frog with the cone at his mouth), drinking (the frog with the bottle tipped up). The big sign is on eating. He touches it. The frog eats the piragua, all of it. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Coquí come la piragua."
7. Carmen gives him a new piragua for his empty hand. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Bebe, Coquí!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on drinking. He touches it. The frog tips the bottle up and drinks, his eyes on the sky. Right signal. While he drinks: NARR: "Coquí bebe agua."

**The turn**

8. While the frog is still drinking, a pigeon flies down, lands on his other hand and eats his piragua, all of it. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El pájaro se come la piragua de Coquí." The frog looks at the empty paper. The pigeon does not go back to the lamp post: it settles on the back of the first bench and stays. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Say (he warns them).** Carmen takes the man a new piragua and the woman a new bottle. They put them down side by side between them (which is on the left is drawn fresh), he to open his newspaper, she to look in her bag. A pigeon flies down from the lamp post. It pecks at the piragua, and goes on pecking. (Or: it puts its beak in the bottle, and keeps it there. Drawn fresh.) Two speaking bubbles: "¡El pájaro come!" and "¡El pájaro bebe!"
   - Right: the one whose thing it is looks up and snatches it away. The pigeon flies back up to the lamp post. Right signal. A star. NARR: "El hombre dice: ¡Gracias!" (Or: "La mujer dice: ¡Gracias!")
   - Wrong: the other one looks up and snatches the wrong thing away. The pigeon goes on: it eats the piragua up (or the bottle tips over and it drinks the water from the bench). While it is still at it: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pájaro come." (Or: "El pájaro bebe.") The pigeon settles on the back of a bench. A cross. The piragua man brings a new one. The settled pigeon hops down to it again. The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; the owner snatches it away; the pigeon goes back to its bench.
   - After either: the man and the woman finish what they have; paper and bottle go in the bin. Carmen sits down beside Luis.
10. **Decision B, Touch (who it is for).** Four people sit on the benches, all empty-handed: the man, the woman, Luis, Carmen. The piragua man puts a piragua on the frog's tray. NARR: "La piragua es para la mujer." (Or: "para el hombre". Drawn fresh, each as often as the other.) He touches one of the four.
   - Right: the frog carries it over; that one takes it and eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to the one he touched, who shakes their head and does not take it. The frog stands there holding the tray up; a pigeon comes down, eats the piragua off the tray, and settles on the back of a bench. A cross. The piragua man puts a new one on the tray. NARR: "No, Coquí. La piragua es para la mujer." The sign; he touches the right one; that one eats.
11. **Decision C, Do, last (eat it or drink it).** Carmen gives the frog a piragua and an open bottle of water, one in each hand (which hand is drawn fresh). A pigeon flies down and lands behind him, in the middle, where he cannot see it; Carmen can. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Come, Coquí!" (Or: "¡Bebe, Coquí!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: eating, drinking.
   - Right: the frog eats the piragua up (or drinks the bottle dry). The pigeon dives at that hand and finds nothing there. It flies back up to the lamp post. Right signal. A star. Then he finishes the other in peace.
   - Wrong: the frog does the other. The pigeon dives at the thing he was told to finish: it eats the piragua out of his hand (or it knocks the bottle out of his hand, and drinks the water from the ground). It settles on the back of a bench. A cross. Carmen gives him another of each. NARR: "No, Coquí. Carmen dice: ¡Come!" (Or "¡Bebe!") The sign; he does it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Carmen brings the frog one more piragua, and a bottle of water for herself. They sit down together on the third bench. By the pigeons sitting on the benches:
   - Only the one from the turn (three stars): the piragua is a big one in three colours. He eats in peace; the one pigeon watches from its bench. Stars and a heart.
   - Two or three pigeons: they sit along the back of his bench and watch every bite.
   - Four pigeons: they sit all round him, one on the arm of the bench at his elbow. He eats all the same.
13. While they do it: NARR: "Coquí come la piragua. Carmen bebe agua." NARR: "¿Y el pájaro? ¡El pájaro quiere más!" (The pigeon from the turn leans toward the piragua.) COQUÍ: "¡No más!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** hombre 4 (step 1; step 2 twice; step 4), mujer 4 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 5), come 6 (step 2; step 4: the bubble, the frog; step 5, the bubble; step 6 twice; and the turn), bebe 6 (step 3; step 4, the bubble; step 5: the bubble, the frog; step 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A and C: come or bebe (the one that was true or was said). B: hombre or mujer.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in four (one in two for someone who rules out the children). C one in two.

## Story 22: ¡Señor! ¡Señora!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** evening. People get up from the benches to go home, and things are left behind. Get each thing to its owner before the owner has gone.
- **At stake:** the thing in play. If it goes wrong, the owner goes home without it, and the thing goes into Luis's box of lost things by the lamp post and stays there. (The dog's bone stays with the wrong dog.) The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** señor, señora, joven, viejo.
- **Back again:** hombre, perro, camina, despacio, corre, gracias, de nada, es, de, mira, adiós.
- **Heard along the way:** del, de la, mi sombrero.

**Start:** the three benches in evening light. On the first, the old gentleman, his hat on the bench beside him; on the second, the woman, her yellow sun umbrella leaning beside her; on the third, the young man with his rucksack, and at the far end of it the man from story 21, reading his newspaper. The two dogs lie by the cart. Carmen and the frog stand by the lamp post. Luis is at the cart, with an empty box.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un señor." (The old gentleman twinkles.) NARR: "Una señora." (The woman twinkles.) NARR: "Y un joven." (The young man twinkles.) NARR: "Un perro viejo." (The old dog twinkles.) NARR: "Y un perro joven." (The young dog twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. **He calls, taught.** The old gentleman gets up and walks off toward the cart; his hat stays on the bench. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Señor!", "¡Señora!", "¡Joven!" The big sign is on "¡Señor!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Señor!" The old gentleman turns round, sees the hat, comes back and takes it. Right signal. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Mi sombrero! ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ: "De nada, señor." He buys a piragua and sits down again.
3. The woman gets up and walks off toward the cart; her umbrella stays. The three bubbles; the sign is on "¡Señora!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Señora!" She turns round, comes back and takes it. Right signal. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ: "De nada, señora." She buys a bottle of water and sits down again.
4. The young man gets up and walks off toward the cart; his rucksack stays. The three bubbles; the sign is on "¡Joven!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Joven!" He turns round, comes back and takes it. Right signal. NARR: "El joven dice: ¡Gracias!" He buys a piragua and sits down again.
5. **Whose it is, taught.** Carmen comes from behind the benches holding up a small paper bag from the cart. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es de la señora!" The big sign is on the woman. He touches her. The frog carries the bag to her. Right signal. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!"
6. **The dogs, taught.** Carmen finds a bone under a bench and gives it to the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del perro joven!" The big sign is on the young dog. He touches him. The frog gives him the bone. Right signal. The young dog runs round the cart with it. While he runs: NARR: "El perro joven corre."
7. Carmen finds another bone. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del perro viejo!" The sign is on the old dog. He touches him. The frog gives him the bone. Right signal. The old dog walks slowly under the cart with it. While he walks: NARR: "El perro viejo camina despacio."

**The turn**

8. The man from story 21 folds his newspaper, lays it on the bench, gets up and walks out of the picture. Nobody sees. The newspaper lies there. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El hombre…" Luis runs after him, comes back and shakes his head: he has gone. Luis puts the newspaper in his box and stands the box under the lamp post. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Say (he calls).** One of the three, drawn fresh, gets up to go home and walks off, back to us; something of theirs stays on the bench (the old gentleman's cane; the woman's handbag; the young man's red cap). Three speaking bubbles: "¡Señor!", "¡Señora!", "¡Joven!"
   - Right: the frog calls. That one turns round, comes back and takes it. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias!" (Or "La señora dice:", "El joven dice:".) Right signal. A star. That one waves and goes home, out of the picture.
   - Wrong: the frog calls. One of those still sitting looks up, puzzled. The one walking does not turn. While they are still in the picture: NARR: "No, Coquí. Es un señor." (Or "Es una señora.", "Es un joven.") The sign shows the right bubble; he says it. Too late: at the very edge the walker lifts a hand without turning, as if to a goodbye, and is gone. A cross. Luis puts the thing in his box.
10. **Decision B, Touch (which dog).** The two dogs sit side by side in front of the cart (which is on the left is drawn fresh); both look at the frog. Carmen finds a bone under a bench and gives it to the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del perro viejo!" (Or: "¡Es del perro joven!" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: the frog gives him the bone. He chews it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog gives the bone to the one he touched, who takes it gladly and carries it off behind the cart. The other dog watches it go, ears down. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Es del perro viejo." (He twinkles.) The sign; he touches him; the frog strokes him. There is no other bone.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (whose it is).** The two people who are left get up and stand at the cart for something to take home, in a line, backs to us (who stands on the left is drawn fresh). What they hold in front of them we cannot see. Carmen comes from behind the benches holding up a small paper bag. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Es del señor!" (Or "de la señora", "del joven": one of the two who are there. Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: the frog hops over with the bag; that one takes it. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" (Or the true one.) Right signal. A star. Both go home, out of the picture.
   - Wrong: the frog hops to the one he touched, who looks at the bag and shakes their head. The owner has been served and walks away. While the owner is still in the picture: NARR: "No, Coquí. Es del señor." The sign is on the owner; he touches; the frog hops after, too late: the owner is gone. A cross. The frog brings the bag back; Luis puts it in his box. The other one goes home too.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. The lamp comes on. By what did not get home:
   - Only the newspaper from the turn (three stars): the man from story 21 comes back, looking about; Luis holds up the newspaper; the man takes it and waves. The box is empty. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two more things: they wait in Luis's box under the lamp, for tomorrow.
   - Three more: the box is full under the lamp; the dog that got no bone lies with his head on his paws.
13. The piragua man shuts his umbrella and pushes the cart away. The two dogs go with him. While they go: NARR: "El perro viejo camina despacio. El perro joven corre." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señor!" The piragua man waves.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** señor 6 (step 1; step 2: the bubble, the frog, "El señor dice", "De nada, señor"; steps 3 and 4, the bubble), señora 7 (step 1; steps 2 and 4, the bubble; step 3: the bubble, the frog, twice more; step 5 twice), joven 8 (step 1 twice; steps 2 and 3, the bubble; step 4: the bubble, the frog, "El joven dice"; step 6 twice), viejo 3 (step 1; step 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A: the call that was right. B: viejo or joven. C: the one it belonged to.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in three. B one in two. C one in two.

## Story 23: El bebé duerme

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a baby asleep in a pushchair by the benches, at the end of a long day. Look after everybody without waking the baby.
- **At stake:** the baby's sleep. Each wrong move ends in a noise; the baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out of the pushchair. The lady rocks him to sleep again, but the toy stays on the ground, where nobody dares pick it up. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** cansado, contento, triste, duerme.
- **Back again:** bebé, perro, está, ¿quién?, ¿cómo estás?, señor, señora, mira, ahora.
- **Heard along the way:** contenta (Carmen está contenta), ¿cómo está?

**Start:** evening light. The lady sits on the middle bench with the pushchair beside her; the baby is asleep in it; we see his face. Luis sits slumped on the first bench, yawning, his eyes open. Canelo stands wagging his tail. The man from story 21 sits on the third bench looking down at a piragua that has fallen on the ground; a tear on his cheek. Carmen and the frog stand by the lamp post.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un bebé." (The pushchair twinkles.) NARR: "El bebé duerme." NARR: "Carmen dice: Shh. El bebé duerme."
2. Luis yawns. NARR: "Luis está cansado." Canelo wags his tail. NARR: "Canelo está contento." The man looks at his piragua on the ground. NARR: "El señor está triste." Carmen smiles. NARR: "Y Carmen está contenta."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está triste?" The big sign is on the man. He touches him. The frog takes him a new piragua from the cart. He smiles. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el señor está contento."
4. Luis yawns again. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está cansado?" The sign is on Luis. He touches him. The frog brings Carmen's rucksack for a cushion; Luis leans back on it, eyes half shut. Right signal. NARR: "Luis está cansado."
5. **He says how somebody is, taught.** Abuela comes in and stands beside them, looking in her bag. Canelo is wagging his tail. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo está Canelo?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Está contento!", "¡Está cansado!", "¡Está triste!" The big sign is on "¡Está contento!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Está contento!" Right signal. Abuela takes a ball out of her bag and rolls it along the ground; Canelo fetches it without a sound.
6. Abuela clips Canelo's lead to the bench. He sits, ears down, a tear on his cheek. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y ahora? ¿Cómo está Canelo?" The three bubbles; the sign is on "¡Está triste!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Está triste!" Right signal. Abuela bends down and strokes his head; he wags. NARR: "Ahora Canelo está contento."
7. **The baby sleeps, or does not, taught.** The lady pulls the hood of the pushchair down over the baby; we cannot see him now. She looks in under it, then turns to the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: Shh. El bebé duerme." Two doing bubbles: singing (the frog with his mouth open and notes), keeping still (the frog standing still, the raised hand of "wait" beside him). The big sign is on keeping still. He touches it. The frog stands quite still. The pushchair is still. Right signal (no hop, no chime: stars only).
8. The pushchair shakes; tears fly out from under the hood. The lady looks in. NARR: "La señora dice: Ahora el bebé está triste." The two doing bubbles; the sign on singing. He touches it. The frog sings his two notes, softly: "co-quí, co-quí". A laugh under the hood; the pushchair is still. The lady lifts the hood: the baby is asleep. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el bebé duerme."

**The turn**

9. A pigeon lands on the hood of the pushchair. Canelo sees it and barks, once. The baby wakes with a cry and throws his rattle out; it lands on the paving. Everybody freezes. The pigeon flies off. The lady rocks the pushchair; the baby sleeps again. The rattle stays where it fell. NARR: "Carmen dice: Shh, Canelo. El bebé duerme." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Do (sing or keep still).** The hood is down over the baby; we cannot see him. The pushchair is still. The lady looks in under the hood, then turns to the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: El bebé duerme." (Or: "La señora dice: El bebé está triste." Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: singing, keeping still.
   - Right, "duerme": he stands still. The pushchair is still. Right signal (stars only). A star.
   - Right, "está triste": he sings softly. A laugh under the hood. The lady lifts the hood: the baby is asleep. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong (he sings; the baby was asleep): the baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out. A cross. The lady rocks the pushchair until he sleeps. Then: NARR: "No, Coquí. Shh. Ahora el bebé duerme." The sign shows keeping still; he does it.
   - Wrong (he keeps still; the baby was sad): the pushchair shakes, tears fly out from under the hood, the cry grows, and a toy flies out. A cross. While he cries: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El bebé está triste." The sign shows singing; he sings; the baby laughs and sleeps.
11. **Decision B, Say (how is Canelo?).** Abuela stands beside them, looking in her bag. Something happens to Canelo first, drawn fresh: abuela holds up the ball and he stands wagging (contento); or he runs twice round the benches after a pigeon and drops, slumped, tongue out, yawning, eyes open (cansado); or abuela clips his lead to the bench and he sits, ears down, a tear (triste). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cómo está Canelo?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Está contento!", "¡Está cansado!", "¡Está triste!"
   - Right: abuela, still looking in her bag, does what fits. Contento: she rolls the ball; he fetches it without a sound. Cansado: she lays her shawl over him; he shuts his eyes. Triste: she strokes his head; he wags. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuela does what fits the frog's answer, and it ends in a noise.
     - He is tired; the frog said "contento": she rolls the ball; it bumps his nose; he yelps.
     - He is tired; the frog said "triste": she rubs his head hard with both hands; he grumbles out loud.
     - He is happy; the frog said "cansado": she lays the shawl over him; he takes it for a game, grabs it and shakes it, growling.
     - He is happy; the frog said "triste": she strokes his head; he jumps up at her, barking for joy.
     - He is sad; the frog said "contento": she rolls the ball; he runs for it, the lead pulls him up short, and he howls.
     - He is sad; the frog said "cansado": she lays the shawl over him; he howls from under it.
     The baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out. A cross. The lady rocks him to sleep. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Canelo está cansado." (How he is; he is still like that.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; abuela does what fits.
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (who feels that way).** Three things happen, one after the other. Canelo runs round the benches once more and drops, slumped, tongue out, yawning. Luis's piragua slides out of its paper onto the ground; his head goes down; a tear on his cheek. The man laughs at something in his newspaper, without a sound, and swings his feet. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién está cansado?" (Or: "¿Quién está triste?" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the three.
   - Right: the frog brings what that one needs, quietly: the rucksack for Canelo's head; a new piragua for Luis. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings it to the one he touched, and it ends in a noise.
     - She asked who is tired; he touched Luis: Luis pushes the rucksack away and sobs out loud.
     - She asked who is tired; he touched the man: the man laughs out loud.
     - She asked who is sad; he touched Canelo: Canelo licks the ice, and yelps at the cold.
     - She asked who is sad; he touched the man: "El señor dice: ¡Otra! ¡Gracias!", far too loud.
     The baby wakes with a cry and throws a toy out. A cross. The lady rocks him to sleep. NARR: "No, Coquí. Canelo está cansado." (Or: "Luis está triste." That one twinkles.) The sign; he touches the right one; the frog brings it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Abuela shuts her bag. Everybody gets up to go, on tiptoe. The baby sleeps. By the toys on the ground:
   - Only the rattle from the turn (three stars): the frog picks it up without a sound and lays it in the pushchair. Stars and a heart, without a sound.
   - Two or three toys: Carmen and Luis gather them on tiptoe as they go.
   - Four toys: they lie all round the pushchair; the lady gathers them herself, one by one, a finger to her lips.
14. Luis yawns; Canelo yawns. NARR: "Luis está cansado. Canelo está cansado. Y Coquí está muy cansado." The frog yawns. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a casa." COQUÍ: "Hasta mañana."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** duerme 5 (step 1 three times; step 7; step 8; and the turn), triste 6 (step 2; step 3; step 5, the bubble; step 6: the bubble, the frog; step 8), cansado 5 (step 2; step 4 twice; steps 5 and 6, the bubble), contento 7 (step 2; step 3; step 5: the bubble, the frog; step 6: the bubble, the line after).

**A miss is filed under:** A: duerme or triste (the one she said). B: the feeling that was true. C: the feeling that was asked.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in three. C one in three.

## For the owner, one line each

- A piragua is Puerto Rican shaved ice in a paper cone. The word is not on your list; it is heard along the way.
- "Hombre" and "mujer" are only ever said by the narrator. To a person, and about a person who can hear, the stories say "señor", "señora" and "joven".
- "¡Joven!" is how a young man or a young woman is called in Puerto Rico. "Un joven" is a young man.
- "Viejo" is taught on a dog. Said of a person it is blunt; the polite word is "mayor" ("un señor mayor"), which comes later.
- "Bebe" (drinks) and "bebé" (baby) differ only in which half is said louder. Story 21 has no baby in it; story 23 never says "bebe".
- The feeling words are taught with "está": "Luis está cansado." The feminine is heard once ("Carmen está contenta").

# El cruce: three stories (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `cruce`). Stories 57 to 59. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 56.

Scene words (page 02): izquierda, derecha, derecho, al lado de, delante, detrás, entre, sigue, dobla, hasta, perdón.

The picture (as the set `cruce-set.html` draws it): a crossing of two streets in town, drawn square to the picture: one street runs straight up and down, the other straight across. The frog comes up from the bottom with his back to us, so his left is the picture's left. Two views of one picture: `wide` (stories 57 and 58) and `plaza` (story 59; the window moved to the right). Three ways leave the crossing: a street to the left, a street to the right, a street straight on. Where each leads is out of the picture: nothing in it says what is down a street (no signs, no fronts; the same pavement and low hedge on all three). At the far end of the street straight on stands one big tree. At the near right corner is a small square: low bushes, a seat with a back that faces us, and a short piece of low wall at its near edge (story 59: the family's photo). A row of cones can close the street to the right (story 58).

New person: the piragüero with his cart (he is the piragua man of El parque), story 58. The others: the family, the señora and the señor, don Pepe on his bicycle, the obrero of story 50.

How a walk is shown: whoever takes a street walks or rides out of the picture along it. NARR: "Y luego..." Then they come up from the bottom again: the same crossing stands for the next one. Somebody who is asked the way in a game round stands in the middle of the crossing, facing us: he does not point, and does not look along a street.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 57: ¿Izquierda o derecha?

- **Goal he can see:** a full bottle of water with three marks, standing on abuela's bag; a high, hot sun. Abuela fans herself.
- **At stake:** the water (3 parts). A walk down the wrong street and back, in the sun: the frog comes back tired, trips on abuela's bag, and the bottle tips: a part runs out over the pavement.
- **New words:** izquierda, derecha, dobla, perdón.
- **Back again:** calle, correo, farmacia, tienda, colmado, ¿adónde vas?, voy, vas, yo, tú, señor, señora, agua, cansado, luego, toma, gracias, casa, ahora, aquí.

**Start:** midday. Abuela and the frog come up to the crossing. Abuela has a letter in her hand and looks left, right and straight on.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela y Coquí están en el cruce." **(cruce: heard)** NARR: "Abuela dice: Tengo una carta." Don Pepe rides up on his bicycle and stops. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Hola! Coquí, ¿adónde vas?" COQUÍ [TURN: correo (correo)]: "¡Al correo!"
2. COQUÍ: "Perdón, don Pepe. ¿Dónde está el correo?" Don Pepe points; the street to the left twinkles. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Dobla a la izquierda, Coquí. A la izquierda." Ask (signed, the left street): "¿Para dónde dobla Coquí?" He touches it: abuela and the frog walk out along it. NARR: "Y luego..." They come up from the bottom again; abuela shows her empty hands. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡El correo está a la izquierda!"
3. The señora crosses in front of them. COQUÍ: "Perdón, señora. ¿Dónde está la farmacia?" The señora points; the street to the right twinkles. NARR: "La señora dice: Dobla a la derecha. Sí, a la derecha." Ask (signed, the right street): "¿Para dónde dobla Coquí?" He touches it: they walk out along it, and come up again; abuela holds up a small paper bag. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡La farmacia está a la derecha!"

**The turn**

4. Abuela sits down on the seat in the square, in the tree's shade, and stands the bottle on her bag beside her. She holds her purse out to the frog. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué calor! Estoy cansada. Coquí, ¿vas tú?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Sí, yo voy!" The card shows two places: a kite, a loaf. The signs go.

**The game.** The frog goes for two things: a kite from the tienda and a loaf from the colmado, in a drawn order. At the crossing he stops somebody and asks; the answer is left or right, drawn, each round on its own. The one who is asked does not point.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the left street, the right street; tests the word said).** The señor walks by. COQUÍ: "Perdón, señor. ¿Dónde está la tienda?" NARR: "El señor dice: Dobla a la derecha." (Or, drawn: "El señor dice: Dobla a la izquierda.")
   - Right: the frog hops out along that street. NARR: "Y luego..." He comes up from the bottom with the thing. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hops out along the other street. He comes back along it with nothing, slowly, tongue out; he stumbles against abuela's bag and the bottle tips: water runs out over the pavement, down to the next mark, before abuela catches it. A cross. NARR: "El señor dice: No, Coquí. Dobla a la derecha." The sign is on the right street; he touches it: out, and back with the thing.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; Carmen is asked).** Carmen runs up with her skipping rope. COQUÍ: "Carmen, ¿dónde está el colmado?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Dobla a la izquierda." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A; on a right move Carmen skips out along the same street ahead of him.

**The ending**

7. The frog puts the two things on the seat. Abuela holds the bottle out to him. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! Toma agua."  COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, abuela!"
   - Three parts left: the frog drinks, abuela drinks, and abuela pours the last part over the frog's head; he shuts his eyes and smiles. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the frog drinks, abuela drinks.
   - One left: abuela gives it all to the frog and fans herself.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y ahora, a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** izquierda 3 (step 2), derecha 3 (step 3). Not deciding: dobla 4 before the game (the two answers and the two asks); perdón 2 before the game and 3 on a clean run, all in the frog's own voice.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (izquierda or derecha).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the streets look alike and nobody points.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 4, 7): correo; voy; gracias.

## Story 58: Sigue derecho

- **Goal he can see:** a big block of ice in the basket of Luis's bicycle, dripping. A thought over Luis and the frog: the piragüero at his cart, with a piragua. The card shows a short road of two steps with the cart at its end and the bicycle at its start.
- **At stake:** the ice (3 sizes). A ride down the wrong street and back melts it a size.
- **New words:** derecho, sigue, hasta.
- **Back again:** dobla, izquierda, perdón, calle, bicicleta, esquina, árbol, cerrado, rápido, frío, señor, señora, toma, gracias, van, luego, mira, casa.

**Start:** afternoon, hot. Luis rides up to the crossing with the block of ice in his basket and the frog on the rack behind him. The street to the right is shut off by cones; the obrero stands there.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van en bicicleta. Tienen hielo para el piragüero." **(hielo, piragüero: heard; the thought and the picture carry them)** A drop falls from the basket. The señor walks across the crossing, away from them; Luis points at him. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está el piragüero? Ese señor sabe. ¡Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: perdón (perdón, perdon)]: "¡Perdón, señor!" The señor turns round.
2. NARR: "El señor dice: ¿El piragüero? Sigue derecho, hasta el árbol." He points straight on; that street twinkles, and the big tree at its end. Ask (signed, the street straight on): "¿Por dónde sigue Luis?" He touches it: Luis rides straight on, stops under the tree for a moment, and rides out. NARR: "Luis sigue derecho." On the card the bicycle has not moved yet: this was the way to the first crossing. NARR: "Y luego..." They come up from the bottom again.
3. Don Pepe stands at the corner with his broom. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿El piragüero? Dobla a la izquierda. Luego sigue derecho, hasta la esquina." Ask (signed, the left street): "¿Para dónde dobla Luis?" He touches it: Luis rides out to the left; they come up from the bottom again.

**The turn**

4. Luis looks into the basket: the block drips fast; a small puddle under the bicycle. The obrero waves at the cones. NARR: "El obrero dice: La calle está cerrada." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡El hielo! ¡Mira!" He looks at the frog. COQUÍ [TURN: rápido (rápido, rapido)]: "¡Rápido, Luis!" The card shows two places on its road. The signs go.

**The game.** The right street is closed; touching it does nothing but a shake of the obrero's head. Somebody at the crossing says: straight on, or turn left. Drawn, each round on its own. After each right ride the bicycle on the card moves a step nearer the cart.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the street straight on, the left street; tests the word said).** The señora is asked. NARR: "Luis dice: Perdón, señora. ¿Dónde está el piragüero?" NARR: "La señora dice: Sigue derecho, hasta la esquina." (Or, drawn: "La señora dice: Dobla a la izquierda y sigue hasta la esquina.")
   - Right: Luis rides out along it; they come up from the bottom again. A star; the bicycle on the card moves a step.
   - Wrong: Luis rides out along the other street, and comes back along it, puffing; a trail of drops behind the bicycle. The block is a size smaller. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No. Sigue derecho, hasta la esquina." The sign is on the right street; he touches it: out, and up from the bottom again; the card's bicycle moves.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; Carmen is asked).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Dobla a la izquierda y sigue hasta la esquina." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A. This time, when they come up from the bottom, the piragüero and his cart stand in the square: the card's bicycle is at the cart.

**The ending**

7. A bell. NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¡Mi hielo! ¡Gracias, Luis! ¡Gracias, Coquí!" He lifts the block onto his cart and scrapes it.
   - A big block: three piraguas: Luis, the frog, and one the piragüero eats himself. Stars and a heart.
   - A smaller block: two piraguas: Luis and the frog.
   - A small block: one piragua with two spoons.
8. NARR: "El piragüero dice: Toma, Coquí. ¿Está fría?" COQUÍ [TURN: frío (fría, fria, frío, frio)]: "¡Sí! ¡Muy fría!" NARR: "Y luego Luis y Coquí van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** derecho 3 (steps 2, twice; step 3), sigue 4 (steps 2 and 3). Not deciding: hasta 2 before the game, with the tree as its picture, and 4 on a clean run.
**A miss is filed under:** derecho when "Sigue derecho" was said; dobla when "Dobla a la izquierda" was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. A learner who knows izquierda and dobla from story 57 and not derecho is right too: he takes the left street when he hears them and the other when he does not. "Derecho" is never set against "derecha": the right street is closed in this story, and the voice pauses before "hasta" so that derecho is not heard as derecha.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 4, 8): perdón; rápido; frío.

## Story 59: Delante y detrás

- **Goal he can see:** papá's camera, the kind whose picture slides out of the front. Three white sheets show at its side (and on the card).
- **At stake:** the good photos (3 sheets). The frog in the wrong place when the flash goes: the picture comes out spoiled (his back over a face, or only his eyes at the edge).
- **New words:** al lado de, delante, detrás, entre.
- **Back again:** familia, abuela, abuelo, mamá, papá, bebé, aquí, uno, dos, tres, yo, soy, mira, también, contento, hay, ahora.

**Start:** late afternoon, a soft light. The small square at the crossing: the seat, the short wall. The family walks in; papá has the camera round his neck. The frog is behind papá, peeping round his legs.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La familia está en el cruce. Papá tiene una cámara." **(cámara: heard)** NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Una foto de la familia!" Abuelo, mamá and Carmen stand in a row well behind the seat, in that order, so that each is seen above whoever sits in front.
2. NARR: "Papá dice: Luis, delante de Carmen." Luis sits down on the seat in front of Carmen. NARR: "Papá dice: Abuela, delante de mamá." Abuela sits down in front of mamá; a gap is left on the seat between her and Luis. Ask (signed, Luis): "¿Quién está delante de Carmen?" He touches him: Luis waves.
3. Carmen puts her hands on Luis's shoulders; mamá bends to abuela's ear. NARR: "Carmen está detrás de Luis. Mamá está detrás de abuela."
4. NARR: "Carmen está al lado de mamá. Y abuela está al lado de Luis." Ask (signed, abuela): "¿Quién está al lado de Luis?" He touches her: abuela pats Luis's knee. Canelo trots in. NARR: "Papá dice: Canelo, ¡aquí!" Canelo lies down on the ground in front of the seat, at the right.
5. Mamá lifts the bebé and holds him up between herself and abuelo; abuelo takes his hand. NARR: "El bebé está entre abuelo y mamá. Y mamá está entre abuelo y Carmen."

**The turn**

6. Papá looks through the camera and lowers it. NARR: "Papá dice: ¿Y Coquí? ¿Dónde está Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Aquí!" The frog hops out from behind papá. NARR: "Coquí está detrás de papá." Papá taps the three sheets. NARR: "Papá dice: Tres fotos. Y Coquí va en las tres." The card shows two places and the three sheets. The signs go.

**The game.** Papá says where the frog goes; then he counts and presses the button. Round A is at Canelo, who lies low enough for the frog to be seen behind him: in front of him (the ground before his paws) or behind him (the ground between him and the seat). Round B is at abuela: beside her (on the seat, in the gap) or in front of her (the ground at her feet). Which is said is drawn, each round on its own.

7. **Decision A, touch a place (two places at Canelo; tests the word said).** NARR: "Papá dice: Coquí, ¡detrás de Canelo!" (Or, drawn: "Papá dice: Coquí, ¡delante de Canelo!")
   - Right: the frog hops there. NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres!" The flash. A sheet slides out and is shown big for a moment: everybody, and the frog where he should be. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hops to the other place. NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres!" The flash. The sheet is shown big: the frog's back over a face (he was nearer the camera than papá wanted), or only his eyes at an edge (he was farther). Papá laughs and shakes his head. A cross: a sheet spoiled. NARR: "Papá dice: No, Coquí. Detrás de Canelo." (Or: "Papá dice: No, Coquí. Delante de Canelo.") The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog hops there, and papá gives him a thumb up (no picture is taken).
8. **Decision B, touch a place (two places at abuela; tests the word said).** NARR: "Papá dice: Coquí, ¡al lado de abuela!" (Or, drawn: "Papá dice: Coquí, ¡delante de abuela!") Right and wrong as in A, with "Papá dice: No, Coquí. Al lado de abuela." or "Papá dice: No, Coquí. Delante de abuela."

**The ending**

9. Papá stands the camera on the short wall, presses something and runs to the seat. NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Y ahora yo también! Coquí, aquí hay un lugar: ¡entre abuela y Luis!" Papá kneels at the left end of the seat, beside abuela; the frog hops into the gap on the seat (if he sits there already, he bounces). The flash: the last sheet, always a good one. The photos are laid out on the seat.
   - Three good photos: the family looks at all three; abuela holds one to her heart. Stars and a heart.
   - Two good, one spoiled: they laugh at the spoiled one; Luis holds it up.
   - One good, two spoiled: the bebé chews a spoiled one; mamá takes it away, laughing.
10. Abuela holds out the last photo and points at the frog in it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira la foto. ¿Quién es?" COQUÍ [TURN: yo (yo)]: "¡Soy yo!" NARR: "Coquí está en la foto, entre abuela y Luis. La familia está contenta."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** delante 3 (step 2), detrás 3 (steps 3 and 6), al lado de 3 (step 4). Not deciding: entre 2 before the game (step 5) and 4 on a clean run (steps 9, 10).
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (delante or detrás in round A; al lado de or delante in round B).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the dog or abuela, two places. "Al lado de abuela" is true of the gap on the seat and not of the ground at her feet.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 6, 10): aquí; yo.

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 57: a stranger answering a grandmother says "Doble" (usted), a second form of the new word: the frog asks, so everybody answers a child with "Dobla". Abuela asked the same question twice. A miss let the frog drink, which read as a treat: now the bottle tips and the water runs away. The step with Luis and the school moved nothing and had a line with no picture: cut; the third hearings come from don Pepe's and the señora's own repeats and from abuela on the way back. "Perdón" to his own grandmother and sister is not said. "Banco" is also a bench, and abuela sat on one: the frog goes to the tienda and the colmado, and the seat is a seat. The one who is asked no longer points. The last question, which he could not answer, is cut.
- 58: "El señor sabe" is heard as "the Lord knows": "Ese señor sabe". "Dobla a la izquierda, hasta la farmacia" is not Spanish: "Dobla a la izquierda y sigue hasta la esquina". Right rides changed nothing: the card has a road, and the bicycle on it moves a step nearer the cart; the piragüero is in the square when they come up the last time, whichever way they went. "Hasta" had no picture: the tree at the end of the street, where Luis stops. Don Pepe's route no longer says the same instruction three times. "¿Vamos rápido o despacio?" handed him the word: Luis shows the puddle and looks at him. "Esta calle está cerrada" put esta, está and hasta together.
- 59: "al lado de abuela" was true of both places, so a learner who knew the word could be marked wrong: round B is at Canelo, beside him or in front of him. "¿Quién está al lado de Luis?" had two right answers: the question is about Canelo. The bebé "between abuelo and mamá" was in mamá's arms: he stands on the seat's back between them. The closing line put the frog between abuela and abuelo when he was on abuelo's head: the last photo has him in the gap between abuela and Luis, and says so. The turn "¡Tres!" sat inside a costed decision and only on some runs: papá counts himself. The first examples of delante and detrás no longer turn on abuela against abuelo.

Left, and why:
- "delante de": people say "al frente de"; delante is the plan's word (page 02).
- "Y luego...": "Después..." is what is said; luego is story 50's word.
- 58 never sets derecho against derecha. They differ by one unstressed vowel, and before "hasta" not even that; a miss between them would be a hearing test, not a meaning. The cost: a learner can pass 58 on izquierda alone. Said in the script.
- entre decides nothing. Set against "al lado de" it leaks through the names (two names against one). It is heard four times and is where the frog ends up.
- "¡Sí, yo voy!" and "¡Sí! ¡Muy fría!" turn the question's word round; that is how a child answers.
- Length: the beginner reader guesses 95 to 110 s for each. A step and several lines are cut from each; the real number comes from machine play.

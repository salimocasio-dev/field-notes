# El lago: three stories (draft 2, after the first round of fresh reviews)

Place 2, El parque. Scene 3 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (eleven stories), El camino (stories 12 to 14) and El campo (stories 15 to 17).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (El camino): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres.
Words he has met (El campo): bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene.
Heard along the way so far: pájaro, bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, entra, come, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, a jugar, muy, va, por arriba, por abajo, no más, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for El lago (from the 40-scene list): pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?

The picture: the pond of the park. Pale water fills the lower half of the picture. On the left bank, a short wooden dock. A wooden post stands in the water. Out in the middle, a clump of tan reeds. The far bank runs along the top of the water; it is a strip of grass a child can stand on. Sky and hills above. Luis (the boy, green shirt) and Carmen (the girl, braids, pink dress) are here. Canelo is asleep on the bank.

The animals:
- the duck (el pato): white, orange bill. It paddles on the water, and it can fly.
- the fish (el pez): silver in story 18. It swims just under the clear water, where its colour shows, and it jumps.
- the bird (el pájaro): small and blue in story 18. It sits on the post, and it flies.

The three colours of story 19 are drawn so that they differ in how light they are, not only in hue: a light orange-red, a middle blue, a dark blue-green. Everything coloured has a dark outline.

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Carmen dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a line that says what was right ("No, Coquí." and then a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said); then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es un pato." "Es el agua." "Es Carmen."). The line "Es un pato." is only ever said of a duck.
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Speaking bubbles over the frog, as before: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game. A round done right fills its place with a star. A round done wrong fills it with the mark of what went wrong. Both stay.

## Story 18: Pan para el pato

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** Carmen has a paper bag of bread. A duck, a fish and a bird all watch the bag. Give each one its piece.
- **At stake:** the bread. From the turn on, six pieces lie in a row on the dock. A round done right uses one. A round done wrong uses two: the wrong mouth eats one, and it takes another to put it right. What is left at the end is shared out. The card shows a bitten crust for a round done wrong, a star for a round done right.
- **New words:** pato, pez, pájaro, nada, vuela.
- **Back again:** agua, hay, mira, para, tiene, ¿quién?, más, ahora, amigo.
- **Heard along the way:** lago, pan, come, se come.

**Start:** the frog stands at the end of the dock. Carmen stands beside him with the bag. Luis sits on the dock with his feet over the water. The duck paddles near the dock. The fish swims in front of it. The bird sits on the post.

1. NARR: "Este es el lago." NARR: "Mira. Hay un pato." (The duck twinkles.) NARR: "El pato nada en el agua." NARR: "Hay un pez." (The fish jumps and twinkles.) NARR: "El pez nada en el agua." NARR: "Y hay un pájaro." (It twinkles.) The bird flies a loop over the pond and lands on the post again. NARR: "El pájaro vuela."
2. Carmen holds up the bag. The duck quacks. NARR: "Carmen tiene pan."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Carmen gives the frog a piece. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pato!" The big sign is on the duck. He touches it. The frog throws the bread; the duck eats it. Right signal. NARR: "El pato come pan."
4. **Who flies, taught.** The bird takes off and flies round over the pond; the duck paddles below it; the fish has gone deep, out of sight. A new piece. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién vuela?" The big sign is on the bird. He touches it. The frog throws the bread up; the bird takes it in the air and goes back to its post. Right signal. NARR: "¡Sí! El pájaro vuela."
5. **Who swims, taught.** The duck beats its wings, takes off and flies round; the fish swims in front of the dock; the bird has gone. A new piece. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién nada?" The sign is on the fish. He touches it. The frog drops the bread on the water; the fish comes up and eats it. Right signal. NARR: "¡Sí! El pez nada." The duck comes down on the water. The bird comes back to its post.
6. **He says what the duck does, taught.** Luis is busy with his shoe, head down. The duck takes off and flies round over the pond. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y el pato?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡El pato vuela!" and "¡El pato nada!" The big sign is on "¡El pato vuela!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El pato vuela!" Luis looks up. Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Sí! ¡El pato vuela!"
7. The duck comes down on the water and paddles. Luis is at his shoe again. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y ahora?" The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡El pato nada!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡El pato nada!" Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: Sí. El pato nada en el agua."

**The turn**

8. Luis takes a piece and throws it for the duck, without looking. It lands in front of the fish. The fish eats it. The duck turns its back. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El pez se come el pan del pato." Carmen tips the bag out onto the dock: six pieces in a row. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira. Hay más pan." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The duck paddles near the dock; the bird is on its post; the fish swims in front of the dock. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Touch (who the bread is for).** The frog takes a piece from the row. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pez!" (Or "el pato", or "el pájaro". Drawn fresh, each as often as the others.) He touches the duck, the fish or the bird.
   - Right: the frog throws it to that one; it eats. Right signal. A star on the card.
   - Wrong: the frog throws it to the one he touched; that one eats it. The one it was for goes off a little way (the duck turns its back; the fish dives; the bird hops round on its post). A crust on the card. The frog takes a new piece from the row. NARR: "No, Coquí. Este pan es para el pez." (The one that was named; it comes back.) The sign; he touches the right one; it eats.
10. **Decision B, Touch (the one that flies, or the one that swims).** Two animals, one in the air and one on the water, drawn fresh: the duck flying round and the fish swimming by the dock; or the bird flying round and the duck paddling. (The third is out of sight: the bird gone; or the fish deep.) The frog takes a piece. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Quién vuela?" (Or: "¿Quién nada?" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: the frog throws the bread to it (up, or onto the water); it eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog throws the bread to the one he touched; it eats. A crust. The frog takes a new piece. NARR: "No, Coquí. El pato vuela." (Or the true line for the one that was asked for: "El pájaro vuela." / "El pez nada en el agua." / "El pato nada en el agua.") The sign; he touches it; it eats.
   - After either: the duck is on the water again, the bird on its post, the fish in front of the dock.
11. **Decision C, Say, last (he sees the duck; Luis does not).** Luis takes a piece from the row and bends over his other shoe, head down, the bread in his hand. The duck does one of two things, drawn fresh: it takes off and flies round over the pond; or it paddles round the post. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y el pato?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡El pato vuela!" and "¡El pato nada!"
   - Right: the frog says what it is doing. Luis, still looking down, throws the bread that way (high, or onto the water); the duck gets it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other. Luis throws the bread the wrong way; the fish eats it (or the bird takes it). A crust. NARR: "No, Coquí. El pato vuela." (Or "El pato nada en el agua.", while the duck is still doing it.) Luis takes a new piece. The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis throws; the duck gets it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. By the pieces left in the row on the dock:
   - Three left (no round went wrong): Carmen, Luis and the frog take one each and throw: the duck, the fish and the bird all eat again. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two left: those are thrown; one or two of the animals eat again.
   - None left: Carmen turns the bag upside down. A few crumbs fall. The duck looks at them.
13. Then, in every ending: the fish dives, the bird flies off over the hills, and the duck paddles away and takes off after it. NARR: "El pez nada. El pájaro vuela. Y el pato nada y vuela." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, amigos!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** pato 9 (step 1 twice; step 3 twice; step 6 four times; step 7), pez 5 (step 1 twice; step 5; step 8 twice), pájaro 3 (step 1 twice; step 4), vuela 7 (step 1; step 4 twice; step 6 four times: the bubble twice, the frog, Luis), nada 7 (step 1 twice; step 5 twice; step 6, the bubble; step 7 three times).

**A miss is filed under:** A: the animal that was named (pato, pez or pájaro). B and C: vuela or nada, the one that was asked or was true.

## Story 19: Los botes

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** three little toy boats on the dock, and Carmen on the far bank with her arms out. Send the boats across to her. They gather in a row at her feet.
- **At stake:** the boat in play. A boat that is not the one wanted is set down at the edge of the dock; it slips into the water, drifts out to the reeds in the middle of the pond and sticks there, out of anybody's reach. It stays there. The card shows a boat in reeds for that round. A right round shows a star.
- **New words:** rojo, azul, verde, ¿de qué color?
- **Back again:** pez, nada, dame, mi, hay, tres, dos, uno, mira, allá, va, es, este.
- **Heard along the way:** bote, pinta, de rojo.

**Start:** the frog and Luis on the dock. Three toy boats stand in a row on the dock: each a small hull with one sail, the whole boat one colour: red, blue, green. Luis has a cloth bag with more boats in it. Beside him stand three bare boats, plain wood with white sails, and three pots of paint, well apart: red, blue, green, with a brush. Carmen stands on the far bank.

1. NARR: "Mira. Hay tres botes." (They twinkle one by one.) NARR: "Un bote rojo. Un bote azul. Y un bote verde."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¡dame el bote rojo!" The big sign is on the red boat. He touches it. The frog pushes it to Luis; Luis sets it on the water and blows; it sails across to Carmen, who lifts it out and stands it at her feet. Right signal. NARR: "¡Allá va el bote rojo!"
3. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dame el bote azul!" The sign is on the blue boat. He touches it. It sails across. Right signal. NARR: "¡Allá va el bote azul!"
4. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dame el bote verde!" The sign is on the green boat. He touches it. It sails across. Right signal. NARR: "¡Allá va el bote verde!" Three boats stand at Carmen's feet. Luis takes three more out of his bag, red, blue and green, and stands them in the row on the dock (the order drawn fresh).
5. **He says a colour, taught.** A red fish swims up to the dock and circles there, just under the water. Luis has picked up a bare boat and the brush; he looks at his boat, not at the water. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Un pez! ¿De qué color es?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Es rojo!", "¡Es azul!", "¡Es verde!" The big sign is on "¡Es rojo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Es rojo!" Right signal. Luis paints the bare boat red and sets it on the water; he blows; the red fish swims along beside the red boat all the way across to Carmen. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Un pez rojo y un bote rojo!"
6. A blue fish swims up and circles. Luis picks up the next bare boat. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y este pez? ¿De qué color es?" The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Es azul!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Es azul!" Right signal. Luis paints the boat blue; it sails across with the blue fish beside it.
7. **He paints, taught.** Luis takes two more bare boats from his bag; three stand by the pots again. He holds one out to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¡pinta mi bote de verde!" The big sign is on the pot of green paint. He touches it. The frog takes the brush and paints: the boat is green. Right signal. Luis sails it across. NARR: "¡Allá va el bote verde!"
8. **Two at once, taught.** Luis holds out two bare boats. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ahora dos botes! Uno rojo y uno azul." The big sign is on the red pot. He touches it; the frog paints one boat red. The sign moves to the blue pot. He touches it; the frog paints the other blue. Right signal. Luis sails both across. Luis takes more bare boats out of his bag.

**The turn**

9. Luis sets a red boat on the water and turns to wave to Carmen. Nobody blows. A gust of wind comes; leaves fly; the boat slides sideways, drifts out to the reeds in the middle of the pond and sticks there. NARR: "¡Ay, no! ¡El bote rojo!" Luis reaches; it is too far. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Touch (which boat).** The three boats stand in their row on the dock (the order drawn fresh). NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¡dame el bote azul!" (Or "rojo", or "verde". Drawn fresh, each as often as the others.) He touches a boat.
   - Right: the frog pushes it to Luis; it sails across to Carmen. Right signal. A star. Luis puts another of that colour in the row.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the boat he touched to Luis. Luis looks at it and shakes his head. The right boat twinkles in the row. NARR: "No, Coquí. Este es el bote azul." (The colour that was called.) Luis sets the wrong boat down at the edge of the dock to take the right one; it slips into the water, drifts out to the reeds and sticks. A boat in reeds on the card. Luis puts another of the lost colour in the row. The sign; he touches the right boat; it sails across.
11. **Decision B, Say (he sees the fish; Luis does not).** A fish swims up to the dock and circles there: red, blue or green, drawn fresh. Luis has picked up a bare boat and the brush; he looks at his boat. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Un pez! ¿De qué color es?" Three speaking bubbles: "¡Es rojo!", "¡Es azul!", "¡Es verde!" The fish goes on circling until he has answered.
   - Right: Luis paints his boat that colour and sets it on the water; the fish swims beside it across to Carmen. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis paints his boat the colour the frog said. Then he looks at the fish, still circling, and shakes his head. NARR: "No, Coquí. Es un pez azul." (Its colour.) He sets the boat down at the edge of the dock to take a new one; it slips in, drifts out to the reeds and sticks. A boat in reeds. The fish swims off. Luis picks up a new bare boat. The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis paints; the boat sails across with the fish.
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (he paints two).** Luis holds out two bare boats. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ahora dos botes! Uno verde y uno rojo." (Two of the three colours, drawn fresh, in an order drawn fresh.) He touches a pot, and then another. Either of the two colours may come first.
   - Right (both): each touch paints one boat. Luis sails both across. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong (a touch on the pot that was not named, first or second): the frog paints a boat that colour. It stays that colour. Luis shakes his head; the pot that was wanted twinkles. NARR: "No, Coquí. Este es el rojo." (The colour still wanted.) Luis sets the wrongly painted boat down at the edge of the dock; it slips in, drifts out and sticks. A boat in reeds (one mark for the round, however it went wrong). A new bare boat. The sign shows the pot; he touches it; the frog paints. Then, if a colour is still to do, the sign shows that pot too. Luis sails the two right boats across.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. By the boats in the reeds:
   - Only the one the wind took (three stars): Carmen has a long row of boats at her feet. She sets them all on the water and blows; they sail back toward the dock in a line. Stars and a heart.
   - Two or three in the reeds: her row is shorter; she sends those back.
   - Four in the reeds (no star): Luis, Carmen and the frog stand and look at the boats stuck out in the middle. Carmen sends back the few she has.
14. NARR: "Un bote rojo. Un bote azul. Un bote verde." (Carmen has at least one of each, from the opening; they twinkle as they are named.) COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, botes!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** rojo 7 (step 1; step 2 twice; step 5 four times: the bubble, the frog, Luis twice; step 8), azul 6 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 5, the bubble; step 6 twice; step 8), verde 7 (step 1; step 4 twice; steps 5 and 6, the bubble; step 7 twice), ¿de qué color? 2 as a question (steps 5, 6); it is asked, never the answer.

**A miss is filed under:** A and B: the colour that was called or was true. C: the colour he should have touched.

## Story 20: ¿Cuál?

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** three ducks out on the water, a white one, a black one and a small yellow one, and bread on the dock. Bring the ducks to the dock.
- **At stake:** how near the ducks are. The water between the dock and the post has five places. A round done right at the first try brings all three one place nearer. A round done wrong sends all three one place further out, and putting it right brings nobody nearer. It stays. The card shows rings on the water for a round done wrong, a star for a round done right.
- **New words:** amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿cuál?
- **Back again:** pato, pájaro, pan, para, hay, tres, mira, flor, sí.
- **Heard along the way:** amarilla (una flor amarilla), ¿cuál pato?

**Start:** the frog and Carmen on the dock, the bag of bread between them. Luis sits on the bank beside Canelo, who is asleep. A yellow flower grows on the bank by the dock. Three ducks paddle in the middle place of the five: one white, one black, one small and yellow.

1. NARR: "Mira. Una flor amarilla." (It twinkles.) NARR: "Y mira. Hay tres patos." NARR: "Un pato blanco." (It twinkles.) NARR: "Un pato negro." (It twinkles.) NARR: "Y un pato amarillo." (It twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuál es el pato blanco?" The big sign is on the white duck. He touches it. The frog throws it bread; it eats. Right signal. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! El pato blanco."
3. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuál es el pato negro?" The sign is on the black duck. He touches it. Bread; it eats. Right signal. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! El pato negro."
4. **Two words at once, taught.** Two birds land on the rail of the dock, a white one and a black one. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pájaro negro!" The sign is on the black bird. He touches it. Bread; it eats. Right signal. NARR: "Un pájaro negro."
5. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pájaro blanco!" The sign is on the white bird. He touches it. Bread; it eats. Right signal. NARR: "Un pájaro blanco."
6. **He asks which one, taught.** A new piece of bread. One speaking bubble shows over the frog; it will stay there for the rest of the story. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pato!" The three ducks all look up, as they do every time she speaks. The big sign is on the speaking bubble. He touches it. COQUÍ: "¿Cuál?" NARR: "Carmen dice: El pato amarillo." The sign is on the yellow duck. He touches it. Bread; it eats. Right signal. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! El pato amarillo."

**The turn**

7. Canelo wakes up, sees the ducks and barks. The ducks flap, quack, and paddle one place further out. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Canelo!" Canelo lies down again. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The birds are on the rail. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

8. **Decision A, Touch (which duck).** The three ducks paddle about and stop in a new order. The frog takes a piece of bread. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuál es el pato negro?" (Or "blanco", or "amarillo". Drawn fresh, each as often as the others.) He touches a duck. (The speaking bubble is there; if he touches it, the frog says "¿Cuál?" and Carmen says her line again. It costs nothing.)
   - Right: the frog throws it bread; it eats; all three come one place nearer. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog throws bread to the duck he touched; the other two flap, and all three paddle one place further out. Rings on the card. The right duck twinkles. NARR: "No, Coquí. Este es el pato negro." A new piece. The sign; he touches it; it eats. Nobody comes nearer.
9. **Decision B, Touch (a duck or a bird, white or black).** The frog takes a piece. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pájaro blanco!" (Or "el pájaro negro", "el pato blanco", "el pato negro". Drawn fresh, each as often as the others.) He touches a duck or a bird. (The speaking bubble as before: free.)
   - Right: bread; it eats; the ducks come one place nearer. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: bread to the one he touched; the ducks flap and paddle one place further out. Rings. The right one twinkles. NARR: "No, Coquí. Este es el pájaro blanco." (The one that was named.) A new piece. The sign; he touches it; it eats.
10. **Decision C, last (does he know which duck?).** The frog takes a piece. One of two lines, drawn fresh. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pato!" (Which duck is not said.) Or: "Carmen dice: ¡Pan para el pato blanco!" (Or "negro", or "amarillo".) He touches a duck, or the speaking bubble.
   - She said which. Right: he touches that duck; bread; it eats; one place nearer. Right signal. A star. If he touches the bubble first: COQUÍ: "¿Cuál?" NARR: "Carmen dice: El pato blanco." It costs nothing; he then touches a duck.
   - She said which. Wrong (another duck): as in Decision A. Rings. NARR: "No, Coquí. Este es el pato blanco."
   - She did not say which. Right: he touches the bubble. COQUÍ: "¿Cuál?" NARR: "Carmen dice: El pato negro." (One of the three, drawn fresh.) Then he touches a duck: the right one eats, one place nearer, right signal, a star; another one costs as in Decision A.
   - She did not say which. Wrong (he touches a duck without asking): the frog throws the bread at that duck; all three go for it at once, flap and quack, and paddle one place further out. Rings. NARR: "No, Coquí. Hay tres patos. ¿Cuál pato?" A new piece. The sign shows the bubble; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¿Cuál?" NARR: "Carmen dice: El pato negro." The sign shows that duck; he touches it; it eats.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

11. Where the ducks are now:
   - At the dock (three stars): the three ducks climb onto the dock and sit in a row beside the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Anywhere else: they paddle where they are, nearer or further, as the game left them. Carmen throws them one last piece.
12. NARR: "Un pato blanco. Un pato negro. Y un pato amarillo." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuál es tu amigo, Coquí?" COQUÍ: "¡El pato amarillo!" The small yellow duck quacks.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** blanco 5 (step 1; step 2 twice; step 5 twice), negro 5 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 4 twice), amarillo 3 for a duck (step 1; step 6 twice) and "amarilla" once, ¿cuál? 4 (steps 2 and 3; step 6: the bubble's line is heard when he touches it, and the frog says it).

**A miss is filed under:** A and B: the colour that was called (blanco, negro or amarillo). C: ¿cuál? when he did not ask; the colour when he asked and then touched the wrong duck.

"¿De qué color?" and "¿cuál?" have no picture pair; they are questions, and come back in the listening stories and in the call.

## What the first round of reviews changed

- Story 18 no longer hides the duck behind the reeds and no longer asks "where"; that was story 16 again. Now two animals are in view, one flying and one swimming, and Carmen asks "¿Quién vuela?" or "¿Quién nada?": the name cannot help, only the verb.
- Story 18: the bread is in view on the dock and a wasted piece is gone; the ending shares out what is left. The closing line is true in every ending.
- Story 19: the cart is gone (a step could not happen). Boats gather at Carmen's feet; the reeds are out in the middle, where nobody can reach a stuck boat; a wrong boat is lost because it is set down at the edge and slips in. Every correction is true to the eye ("Este es el bote azul.", as that boat twinkles). The fish circles while he chooses. The last decision is new: two colours in one line.
- Story 20: "¿Cuál?" is now a bubble he can always touch, free. It costs only to guess a duck when Carmen did not say which. The middle decision needs two words (pato or pájaro; blanco or negro). The ducks end where the game left them. The turn is Canelo, not Luis.
- Spanish: "¿Y el pato?" for "¿Dónde está el pato?"; "nada en el agua" at the first hearings and in corrections, so "nada" is not heard as "nothing"; "¡Allá va el bote rojo!"; "¡pinta mi bote de verde!"; "Es un pez azul."; no bare "pato" called out, no "tu pato".
- Colours are drawn to differ in lightness as well as hue, for players who cannot tell red from green.

## For the owner, one line each

- "Lago" is said for the pond, as on your list.
- Colours come after the thing, as in Spanish: "el bote rojo". One feminine form is heard once ("una flor amarilla").
- "Pato" is also an insult in Puerto Rico when called at a person. In these stories it is always "el pato" or "un pato", said of a duck, never called out.
- "¿Cuál?" is taught as the thing to say when you were not told which one.
- Your list has "vuela" and "nada". For something happening now, people say "está volando", "está nadando". The stories keep your forms.

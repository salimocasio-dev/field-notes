# La comida: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `comida`). Stories 90 to 92. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 89.

Scene words (page 02): salado, dulce, picante, delicioso, mejor, menos, bastante, prueba, falta, está bien, servilleta, hielo.
How they are shared out: 90 prueba, salado, dulce, picante; 91 falta, bastante, hielo, servilleta; 92 menos, está bien, mejor, delicioso.
(Bastante here is the island's: plenty, a lot. Not "enough".)

The picture: the fonda's terrace, at night. One view. A wooden floor. At the back a rail, with a string of small lights along it (low, behind everyone), and the dark sea and the sky beyond. In the middle, a long table; the family sits in a row along its far side, facing us; the frog stands on the table. At the right, the mesera's side table: a salt shaker, a sugar bowl, an ice bucket with tongs, a pile of napkins. At the left, a serving table with the cocinero's big pot and a pile of plates; under it, the fonda's dog and its bowl. No roof.

People: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; the mesera; the cocinero; guests (the señor, the señora, the joven, the mujer).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 90: Prueba

- **Goal he can see:** in the middle of the table, a big jar of lemonade with slices of green lemon in it, cold, with drops on the glass, and three empty glasses beside it: one for Luis, one for Carmen, a small one for the frog. Abuela taps the three glasses: for afterwards.
- **At stake:** the lemonade (3 glasses). Whoever gets the wrong mouthful fills a glass and gulps it down: that glass is set aside, empty.
- **New words:** prueba, salado, dulce, picante.
- **Back again:** tostones, limonada, piña, sal, agua, quiero, quieres, me gusta, cierra (cierro), abre (abro), ojo, boca, tiene, otro, eso, esto, ahora, después, no, sí, gracias, por favor.

**Start:** night on the terrace; the lights along the rail. The family along the table. In the middle, the jar of lemonade and the three empty glasses; by abuela's hand, a small jug of water. In front of the frog, three small plates: tostones, slices of pineapple, small red peppers.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela holds out a tostón. NARR: "Abuela dice: Prueba, Coquí." The frog eats it; grains of salt shine on it. NARR: "Abuela dice: El tostón es salado. Tiene sal." **(tostón: a form of tostones)** Ask (signed, the plate of tostones): "¿Cuál es el plato salado?" He touches it: it shines.
2. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora, prueba la piña." The frog eats a slice; small hearts. NARR: "Luis dice: La piña es dulce. ¡Qué dulce!" Ask (signed, the pineapple): "¿Cuál es el plato dulce?" He touches it.
3. Abuelo holds out a small red pepper, with a grin. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y ahora... prueba esto." The frog bites it: he turns red; fire comes out of his mouth. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Picante!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, no! Eso es picante, Coquí. ¿Qué quieres?" COQUÍ [TURN: agua (agua, agua por favor)]: "¡Agua, por favor!" Abuela pours him water from the small jug; steam rises off his head.
4. Abuela puts new pieces on the three plates. Carmen shuts her eyes and opens her mouth. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora yo. Cierro los ojos y abro la boca. Quiero algo salado." **(cierro, abro: forms of cierra and abre; algo: from ¿algo más?)** Ask (signed, the plate of tostones): "Carmen quiere algo salado. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the frog feeds her a tostón. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! Salado." Luis shuts his eyes. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Y yo quiero algo dulce!" The frog feeds him pineapple. NARR: "Luis dice: Mmm. Dulce." Abuelo shuts his eyes. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo quiero algo picante." The frog feeds him a pepper; he chews it, calm. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Picante. Me gusta."

**The turn**

5. Abuela puts new pieces on the plates again, and taps the jar and the three glasses. NARR: "Abuela dice: La limonada es para después." Everyone wants to play. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The three small plates stand before the frog: tostones, pineapple, red peppers (their order is drawn each round). One of the family (drawn) shuts his eyes, opens his mouth and says what he wants (drawn). The frog feeds him from one plate.

6. **Decision A, touch a plate (three; tests salado, dulce and picante).** NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero algo dulce." (Drawn.)
   - Right: he chews, and smiles. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dulce! Gracias." A star.
   - Wrong: he chews, and makes a face (after a pepper: he turns red). NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No! Eso es picante. ¡Quiero algo dulce!" He fills a glass from the jar and gulps it down; the empty glass is set aside. A cross. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a plate (the same game; the plates in a new order, another of the family).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Quiero algo salado." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela pours what is left in the jar.
   - Three glasses: Luis, Carmen and the frog drink, with a straw each. Stars and a heart.
   - Two glasses: the children's; they give the frog the slices of lemon.
   - One glass: three straws in one glass.
9. Abuelo holds out another red pepper, with a grin. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Otro, Coquí? Prueba." COQUÍ [TURN: no (no, no gracias)]: "¡No, gracias!" Abuela laughs, and gives him a piece of pineapple. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí quiere algo dulce."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** salado 5 (steps 1, 4), dulce 4 (steps 2, 4), picante 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: prueba 3 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the taste that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): agua; no.

## Story 91: Falta algo

- **Goal he can see:** the mesera sets down three bowls of soup: for abuela, abuelo and Luis (Carmen has her salad). They steam. The three hold their spoons, and wait for what is missing.
- **At stake:** the hot soup (3 bowls). A wrong thing fetched means another trip across the terrace: by then one bowl has stopped steaming, and its spoon is put down.
- **New words:** falta, bastante, hielo, servilleta.
- **Back again:** sopa, tostones, refresco, ensalada, sal, azúcar, café, hay, tiene, caliente, frío, mesera, mesero, aquí tiene, ¿algo más?, nada, ya, mira, ven, ahora, mi, cuidado, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** night on the terrace. The family along the table, with a plate of tostones, drinks in tall metal cups, abuela's coffee, Carmen's salad. The mesera lifts the frog onto her side table (the salt shaker, the sugar bowl, the ice bucket, the pile of napkins) and ties a small apron on him.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The mesera walks off towards another table. NARR: "La mesera dice: Coquí, ahora tú eres el mesero." Abuela bites a tostón, and stops. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mmm, tostones. ¡Ay! Falta sal." Ask (signed, the salt shaker): "¿Dónde está la sal para abuela?" He touches it: the frog carries the shaker over; abuela shakes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Ya no falta sal."
2. Luis looks into his metal cup. NARR: "Luis dice: Mi refresco está caliente. Falta hielo." Ask (signed, the ice bucket): "¿Dónde hay hielo para Luis?" He touches it: the frog carries cubes in the tongs, one, two, three, till the cup is full to the brim. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hielo! Gracias."
3. Abuelo has sauce on his chin; he looks in his lap, and under the table. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Ay! Falta una servilleta." Ask (signed, the napkins): "¿Dónde están las servilletas?" He touches them: the frog carries a napkin over. COQUÍ [TURN: aquí tiene (aquí tiene)]: "¡Aquí tiene!" NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias por la servilleta, mesero." Carmen lifts her own napkin from her lap, and puts it back. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mi servilleta está aquí."
4. The frog, eager, carries the tongs to Luis again. Luis covers his cup. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, no! Hay bastante hielo. Mira." Abuela holds up her coffee cup, and looks into it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay bastante hielo, Coquí. Falta azúcar." Ask (signed, the sugar bowl): "Hay bastante hielo. ¿Qué falta?" He touches it: the frog carries the sugar bowl over; she stirs. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Ahora tiene bastante azúcar."

**The turn**

5. The mesera brings three bowls of soup, steaming, and sets them down. NARR: "La mesera dice: La sopa. ¡Cuidado! Está caliente." She goes off to another table. Abuela, abuelo and Luis take up their spoons, and look at the frog. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One of the family (drawn) says, in one breath, what there is plenty of and what is missing (the two things are drawn from sal, azúcar, hielo and servilletas; which is said first is drawn too). Nothing on the table shows it: the cups are metal, the napkins are in laps. The frog fetches one of the four things from the side table. (Napkins: "Hay bastantes servilletas", "Falta una servilleta".)

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (four on the side table; tests falta against bastante, and the thing's name).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Hay bastante sal. Falta hielo." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog carries it over. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias, mesero." A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries the wrong thing over; a shake of the head. NARR: "Abuelo dice: No, Coquí. Falta hielo." The frog goes back for it; by then one bowl has stopped steaming, and its spoon is put down. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another of the family, two other things).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Falta una servilleta. Hay bastante azúcar." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The family eats the soup.
   - Three bowls steam: they blow on their spoons. Stars and a heart. NARR: "La sopa está caliente."
   - Two steam; abuelo eats his as it is, with a shrug. NARR: "La sopa de abuelo está fría."
   - One steams; abuela shares hers with Luis. NARR: "Dos sopas están frías."
9. The frog bows, with a napkin over his arm. COQUÍ [TURN: ¿algo más? (algo más)]: "¿Algo más?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. ¡Falta Coquí! Ven acá, mesero." The frog hops onto the table, among them. NARR: "Ahora no falta nada."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** falta 6 (steps 1, 2, 3, 4), bastante 4 (step 4), hielo 6 (steps 2, 4), servilleta 4 with servilletas (step 3).
**Forms:** servilletas with servilleta; bastantes with bastante.
**A miss is filed under:** bastante, if he fetched the thing there was plenty of; else the name of the thing that was missing (hielo or servilleta; for sal or azúcar, falta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four; one in two for a learner who catches the two names and not which is which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): aquí tiene; ¿algo más?

## Story 92: Más y menos

- **Goal he can see:** beside the cocinero's big pot of arroz con pollo, the frog's own small bowl. The cocinero puts three spoonfuls in it, one, two, three, and taps it: the helper's share, for afterwards.
- **At stake:** the helper's share (3 spoonfuls). A plate served wrong goes into the dog's bowl; the cocinero fills a new one, scrapes the pot, and tops the plate up with a spoonful out of the frog's bowl.
- **New words:** menos, está bien, mejor, delicioso.
- **Back again:** más, prueba, falta, sal, arroz, pollo, para mí, cocinero, pone (pones), rico, un poco, después, ahora, esto, por favor, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** night on the terrace. The cocinero at the serving table with the big pot, a pile of plates and a bottle of red sauce; the frog on the table's edge with a big spoon, his small bowl, empty, beside him. Under the table, the fonda's dog beside its bowl. The family in a line with their plates.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Arroz con pollo, Coquí. Prueba." The frog tastes: he tilts his hand, so-so. The cocinero tastes. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Mmm... falta sal." He shakes salt in, and tastes. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Mejor." He adds a drop from the red bottle, and tastes; his eyes go wide. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Mejor! Ahora está delicioso. Prueba." The frog tastes: hearts. The cocinero puts three spoonfuls in the frog's small bowl. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Esto es para ti. Después."
2. Abuela holds out her plate; the cocinero puts a heap on it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Para mí, menos. Menos arroz, por favor." Ask (signed, the dog's bowl): "Abuela quiere menos arroz. ¿Dónde pones el arroz?" He touches it: the frog takes a spoonful off her plate and drops it into the dog's bowl; the dog eats. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Así está bien. Mmm, delicioso."
3. Luis holds out his plate; a heap. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Para mí, más! Más arroz, por favor." Ask (signed, the pot): "Luis quiere más arroz. ¿Dónde hay más arroz?" He touches it: the frog adds a spoonful from the pot. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora está mejor. Así está bien."
4. Carmen holds out her plate; a heap. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para mí, así está bien." Ask (signed, Carmen): "El plato de Carmen está bien. ¿Dónde está Carmen?" He touches her: the frog pushes the plate into her hands. She tastes. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Qué rico! Está delicioso."
5. Abuelo holds out his plate; a heap. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para mí, menos." The frog takes a spoonful off, into the dog's bowl. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Así está bien. Gracias." NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Más arroz o menos arroz?" COQUÍ [TURN: más (más, más por favor)]: "¡Más, por favor!" The cocinero laughs, and taps the frog's small bowl: afterwards.

**The turn**

6. Guests from the other tables come with their plates, one behind another. The cocinero looks into the pot: not much left. He taps the frog's bowl once more, and takes up his ladle. The dog looks up. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn), looking at his plate, holds it out; the cocinero puts a heap on it (the same heap every time), and the guest says one of three things (drawn). The frog adds a spoonful from the pot, takes a spoonful off into the dog's bowl, or pushes the plate into the guest's hands as it is.

7. **Decision A, touch a place (the pot, the dog's bowl, the guest; tests menos and está bien against más).** NARR: "La señora dice: Para mí, menos." (Or, drawn: "Para mí, más." Or: "Para mí, así está bien.")
   - Right: the frog does it; the guest goes off with the plate. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog does the wrong thing; the guest looks at the plate and shakes her head. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. ¡Menos!" (or "¡Más!", or "¡Así está bien!") The plate goes into the dog's bowl. The cocinero fills a new plate, scrapes the pot, and tops the plate up with a spoonful out of the frog's small bowl. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "El joven dice: Para mí, así está bien." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

9. The guests are gone. The cocinero hands the frog his small bowl.
   - Three spoonfuls: a full bowl. Stars and a heart.
   - Two spoonfuls.
   - One spoonful; the dog sleeps on its back, round.
10. The cocinero holds the red bottle over the frog's bowl. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¿Pique, Coquí?" **(pique: heard; the island's word for the bottle of hot sauce)** COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco, sí un poco, un poquito, sí un poquito)]: "¡Un poco!" One drop. The frog eats. NARR: "El arroz con pollo está delicioso. Y con un poco de pique, mejor."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** menos 4 (steps 2, 5), está bien 5 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5). Not deciding: mejor 3 (steps 1, 3), delicioso 3 (steps 1, 2, 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (menos, está bien or más).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own; every one of the three lines begins "Para mí" and has no "gracias" or "por favor" to tell them apart.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): más; un poco.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- All three: asks that were a bare word and then a question ("Salado. ¿Cuál es?", "Menos. ¿Dónde pone Coquí el arroz?") straight after the same word had been said: each ask is now one whole sentence of its own, and the voice says "pones" to him, not "pone Coquí". The hearing counts were recounted without the echoes.
- 90: the jar of lemonade was the goal, was not in the first picture, and was drunk from before the game: it stands on the table from the start with its three glasses, the frog's burning mouth gets water from another jug (his turn is "¡Agua, por favor!"), and a wrong mouthful empties one glass, which is set aside. Cheese, a banana and a spoon of red sauce stood in for the three tastes in the game and could not be read: the game uses only the three foods the opening taught. The plates were eaten empty and used again: abuela puts new pieces on. "Cierro los ojos, abro la boca" is said once, with its "y". The last line used "le gusta", which he has never met.
- 91: "bastante" was taught with no touch, and the game's sentence ("Hay bastante X. Falta Y.") was first heard when it cost: step 4 has it, with a sign. Of her own coffee abuela says "tiene bastante azúcar"; napkins take the plural ("Hay bastantes servilletas"), and a missing one keeps "una". Nobody had said the soup was hot: the mesera does, and each ending names it. One cube became "full to the brim": he carries three. "Ahora no falta sal" is "Ya no falta sal". "Una servilleta." tacked on as a label is "Gracias por la servilleta". A miss on the thing there was plenty of is filed under bastante. Carmen's salad is in the first picture.
- 92: "Más, por favor", "Menos, por favor" and "Está bien, gracias" could be told apart by their last word, and "más" is an old word: all three lines are "Para mí, ..." with nothing after. "No, Coquí. ¡Está bien!" sounded like forgiveness: "así está bien" throughout. The stake was rice above a line in the pot, which a right "más" also took: it is three spoonfuls in the frog's own bowl. Each of the three moves was shown once: abuelo's plate shows two of them again. The bottle of hot sauce on a fonda's table is "el pique". "¡Qué rico!" is what is said most at the table: Carmen says it, with "delicioso".

Left, and why:
- Three and four things to choose from: his rule asks for two real options, and every round has at least that. In 91 the sentence names two of the four.
- Delicioso stays as the scene's word: it is said on the island, and "rico" is already his (A comer); both are heard.
- "Falta sal" without "le" (les falta sal): one plain pattern for A1.
- Prueba, mejor and delicioso decide nothing; each is heard three times.
- "Un poco" as the turn's word: the island says "un poquito" nearly always; both are taken as right.
- Three practice rounds in the openings of 90 and 92: kept, with one ask each instead of three; the length is measured when the story files exist.

# La comida: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `comida`). Stories 90 to 92. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 89.

Scene words (page 02): salado, dulce, picante, delicioso, mejor, menos, bastante, prueba, falta, está bien, servilleta, hielo.
How they are shared out: 90 prueba, salado, dulce, picante; 91 falta, bastante, hielo, servilleta; 92 menos, está bien, mejor, delicioso.

The picture: the fonda's terrace, at night. One view. A wooden floor. At the back a rail, with a string of small lights along it (low, behind everyone), and the dark sea and the sky beyond. In the middle, a long table; the family sits in a row along its far side, facing us; the frog stands on the table. At the right, the mesera's side table: a salt shaker, a sugar bowl, an ice bucket with tongs, a pile of napkins. At the left, a serving table with the cocinero's big pot and a pile of plates; under it, the fonda's dog and its bowl. No roof.

People: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; the mesera; the cocinero; guests (the señor, the señora, the joven, the mujer).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 90: Prueba

- **Goal he can see:** in the middle of the table, a big jar of lemonade with slices of lemon in it, cold, with drops on the glass: three glasses' worth. Abuela sets three empty glasses beside it: one for Luis, one for Carmen, a small one for the frog. For afterwards.
- **At stake:** the lemonade (3 glasses). Whoever gets the wrong mouthful gulps down a glass of it.
- **New words:** prueba, salado, dulce, picante.
- **Back again:** tostones, limonada, piña, guineo, queso, sal, quiero, me gusta, cierra, abre, ojo, boca, ¿algo más? (algo is heard in it), eso, ahora, no, sí, gracias, cuidado, ¿qué quieres?

**Start:** night on the terrace; the lights along the rail. The family along the table. In front of the frog, three small plates: a tostón, a slice of pineapple, a small red pepper.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela holds out the tostón. NARR: "Abuela dice: Prueba, Coquí." The frog eats it; grains of salt shine on it. NARR: "Abuela dice: El tostón es salado. Salado, con sal." **(tostón: a form of tostones)** Ask (signed, the plate of tostones): "¿Cuál es salado?" He touches it: it shines.
2. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora, prueba la piña." The frog eats the slice; small hearts. NARR: "Luis dice: La piña es dulce. ¡Qué dulce!" Ask (signed, the pineapple): "¿Cuál es dulce?" He touches it.
3. Abuelo holds out the small red pepper, with a grin. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y ahora... prueba esto." The frog bites it: he turns red; fire comes out of his mouth. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Picante!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, no! Eso es picante, Coquí. ¡Picante, picante!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Qué quieres, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: limonada (limonada, una limonada)]: "¡Limonada!" Abuela pours him a little; steam rises off his head.
4. Carmen shuts her eyes and opens her mouth. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora yo. Cierro los ojos, abro la boca." **(cierro, abro: forms of cierra and abre)** NARR: "Carmen dice: Quiero algo salado." Ask (signed, the plate of tostones): "Salado. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the frog feeds her a tostón. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! Salado." Then abuelo shuts his eyes. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo quiero algo picante." Ask (signed, the pepper): "Picante. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: abuelo chews it, calm. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Picante. Me gusta." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Y yo quiero algo dulce!" The frog feeds him pineapple. NARR: "Luis dice: Mmm. Dulce."

**The turn**

5. Abuela sets the big jar of lemonade in the middle, and the three glasses. Three new small plates come. Everyone wants to play. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Three small plates stand before the frog: something salty (tostones or cheese), something sweet (pineapple or a banana), something hot (the red pepper, or a spoon of red sauce); what they are and their order are drawn each round. One of the family (drawn) shuts his eyes, opens his mouth and says what he wants (drawn). The frog feeds him from one plate.

6. **Decision A, touch a plate (three; tests salado, dulce and picante).** NARR: "Luis dice: Cierro los ojos. Quiero algo dulce." (Drawn.)
   - Right: he chews, and smiles. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dulce! Gracias." A star.
   - Wrong: he chews, and makes a face (after the pepper: he turns red). NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No! Eso es picante. ¡Quiero algo dulce!" He pours himself a glass of lemonade and gulps it down: the jar goes down. A cross. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a plate (the same game; new plates, another of the family).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Cierro los ojos. Quiero algo salado." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela pours what is left in the jar.
   - Three glasses: Luis, Carmen and the frog drink, with a straw each. Stars and a heart.
   - Two glasses: the children's; they give the frog the slices of lemon.
   - One glass: three straws in one glass.
9. Abuelo holds out another red pepper, with a grin. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Otro, Coquí? Prueba." COQUÍ [TURN: no (no, no gracias)]: "¡No, gracias!" NARR: "A abuelo le gusta el picante. A Coquí le gusta la piña: es dulce."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** salado 6 (steps 1, 4), dulce 5 (steps 2, 4), picante 7 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: prueba 3 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the taste that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): limonada; no.

## Story 91: Falta algo

- **Goal he can see:** the mesera sets down three bowls of soup: for abuela, abuelo and Luis (Carmen has her salad). They steam. Abuela holds her hands over hers: hot.
- **At stake:** the hot soup (3 bowls). A wrong thing fetched means another trip across the terrace: by then one bowl has stopped steaming.
- **New words:** falta, bastante, hielo, servilleta.
- **Back again:** sopa, tostones, refresco, sal, azúcar, café, hay, no hay, caliente, frío, mesera, mesero, aquí tiene, ¿algo más?, nada, mira, ven, ahora, mi, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** night on the terrace. The family along the table, with a plate of tostones, drinks in tall metal cups, a coffee. The mesera lifts the frog onto her side table (the salt shaker, the sugar bowl, the ice bucket, the pile of napkins), ties a small apron on him, and goes.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La mesera dice: Coquí, ahora tú eres el mesero." Abuela bites a tostón, and stops. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mmm, tostones. ¡Ay! Falta sal." Ask (signed, the salt shaker): "Falta sal. ¿Dónde está la sal?" He touches it: the frog carries the shaker over; abuela shakes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Ahora no falta sal."
2. Luis looks into his metal cup. NARR: "Luis dice: Mi refresco está caliente. Falta hielo." Ask (signed, the ice bucket): "Falta hielo. ¿Dónde está el hielo?" He touches it: the frog brings a cube in the tongs; plop. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hielo! Gracias."
3. Abuelo has sauce on his chin; he looks in his lap, and under the table. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Ay! Falta una servilleta." Ask (signed, the napkins): "¿Dónde están las servilletas?" He touches them: the frog carries a napkin over. COQUÍ [TURN: aquí tiene (aquí tiene)]: "¡Aquí tiene!" NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias, mesero. Una servilleta."
4. The frog, eager, carries the sugar bowl to abuela's coffee. She covers the cup with her hand. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, gracias. Hay bastante azúcar." He carries the tongs to Luis; the cup is full of cubes to the brim. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, no! Hay bastante hielo. Mira." NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay bastante sal, hay bastante hielo... Está bien, Coquí." **(está bien: heard)**

**The turn**

5. The mesera brings three bowls of soup, steaming, sets them down, and goes off to another table. The frog stands ready on the side table. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One of the family (drawn) says, in one breath, what there is plenty of and what is missing (the two things are drawn from sal, azúcar, hielo and servilletas; which is said first is drawn too). Nothing on the table shows it: the cups are metal, the napkins are in laps. The frog fetches one of the four things from the side table.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (four on the side table; tests falta against bastante, and the thing's name).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Hay bastante sal. Falta hielo." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog carries it over. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias, mesero." A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries the wrong thing over; a shake of the head. NARR: "Abuelo dice: No, Coquí. Falta hielo." The frog goes back for it; by then one bowl has stopped steaming. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another of the family, two other things).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Falta una servilleta. Hay bastante azúcar." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The family eats the soup.
   - Three bowls steam: they blow on their spoons. Stars and a heart.
   - Two steam; abuelo eats his as it is.
   - One steams; abuela shares hers with Luis.
9. The frog bows, with a napkin over his arm. COQUÍ [TURN: ¿algo más? (algo más)]: "¿Algo más?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. ¡Falta Coquí! Ven, mesero." The frog hops onto the table, among them. NARR: "Ahora no falta nada."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** falta 7 (steps 1, 2, 3), bastante 4 (step 4), hielo 6 (steps 2, 4), servilleta 4 with servilletas (step 3).
**A miss is filed under:** falta, if he fetched the thing there was plenty of; else the name of the thing that was missing (hielo or servilleta; for sal or azúcar, falta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four; one in two for a learner who catches the two names and not which is which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): aquí tiene; ¿algo más?

## Story 92: Más y menos

- **Goal he can see:** the cocinero's big pot of arroz con pollo. Inside it, above a line, three spoonfuls more than the guests need: he taps the line with his spoon and points at the frog: the helper's share.
- **At stake:** the helper's share (3 spoonfuls). A plate served wrong is spoilt (it goes into the dog's bowl), and the cocinero makes it again out of the helper's share.
- **New words:** menos, está bien, mejor, delicioso.
- **Back again:** más, prueba, falta, sal, picante, arroz, pollo, plato, cuchara, olla, perro, cocinero, pone, un poco, por favor, gracias, ahora, no, sí.

**Start:** night on the terrace. The cocinero at the serving table with the big pot and a pile of plates; the frog on the table's edge with a big spoon. Under the table, the fonda's dog beside its bowl. The family in a line with their plates.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Arroz con pollo, Coquí. Prueba." The frog tastes: he tilts his hand, so-so. The cocinero tastes. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Mmm... falta sal." He shakes salt in, and tastes. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Mejor." He adds a drop of red sauce, and tastes; his eyes go wide. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Mejor! Ahora está delicioso. Prueba." The frog tastes: hearts.
2. Abuela holds out her plate; the cocinero puts a heap on it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Menos, por favor. Menos arroz." Ask (signed, the dog's bowl): "Menos. ¿Dónde pone Coquí el arroz?" He touches it: the frog takes a spoonful off her plate and drops it into the dog's bowl; the dog eats. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Está bien."
3. Luis holds out his plate; a heap. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Más! Más arroz, por favor." Ask (signed, the pot): "Más arroz. ¿Dónde está el arroz?" He touches it: the frog adds a spoonful from the pot. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora está mejor. Está bien, gracias."
4. Carmen holds out her plate; a heap. NARR: "Carmen dice: Está bien. Gracias." Ask (signed, Carmen): "Está bien. ¿Dónde está Carmen?" He touches her: the frog pushes the plate into her hands. She tastes. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Delicioso!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. Está delicioso."
5. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Más arroz o menos arroz?" COQUÍ [TURN: más (más, más por favor)]: "¡Más, por favor!" The cocinero laughs, and taps the line inside the pot. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Después, Coquí. Esto es para ti."

**The turn**

6. Guests from the other tables come with their plates: a line. The cocinero taps the line in the pot once more, and takes up his ladle. The dog looks up. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn) holds out a plate; the cocinero puts a heap on it (the same heap every time), and the guest says one of three things (drawn). The frog adds a spoonful from the pot, takes a spoonful off into the dog's bowl, or hands the plate over as it is.

7. **Decision A, touch a place (the pot, the dog's bowl, the guest; tests menos and está bien against más).** NARR: "La señora dice: Menos, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Más, por favor." Or: "Está bien, gracias.")
   - Right: the frog does it; the guest goes off with the plate. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog does the wrong thing; the guest looks at the plate and shakes her head. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. ¡Menos!" The plate goes into the dog's bowl; the cocinero fills a new plate out of the helper's share: the rice in the pot sinks towards the line. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "El joven dice: Está bien, gracias." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

9. The line is gone. The cocinero scrapes out what is above the line onto the frog's plate.
   - Three spoonfuls: a mountain. Stars and a heart.
   - Two spoonfuls: a hill.
   - One spoonful; the dog sleeps on its back, round.
10. The cocinero holds the bottle of red sauce over the frog's plate. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¿Picante, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco, sí un poco)]: "¡Un poco!" One drop. The frog eats. NARR: "El arroz con pollo está delicioso. Y con un poco de picante, mejor."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** menos 3 (steps 2, 5), está bien 5 (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: mejor 3 (steps 1, 3), delicioso 3 (steps 1, 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (menos, está bien or más).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): más; un poco.

# A comer: three stories (draft 3, after two rounds of reviews)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 4 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa (stories 1 to 11), El parque (12 to 23) and three scenes of La cocina: La mesa (24 to 26), La estufa (27 to 29) and La nevera (30 to 32).

Words he has met (place 3, La estufa): arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace, pollo, huevo, sal, huele, caliente, frío, cuidado, ya (as "ya está"). Heard there: listas, huele a, no hay más, ponle.

Scene words for A comer (from the 40-scene list): café, azúcar, me gusta, no me gusta, rico, un poco, mucho, también, quieres, ¿qué quieres?, todo, buen provecho.

The picture: abuela's kitchen table (the drawing of La mesa, historia/escenas/mesa.js): the table in the middle, seen a little from above, with four chairs behind it and a place at each (a plate in front, a cup behind it); the counter at the left, the stove at the right, the door to the yard at the left edge. Whoever stands in front of the table is lower in the picture than its top, so nothing on the table is hidden.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in the other scenes of La cocina. A poke never names food, a cup, the sugar or a pot.

Every story has two scored rounds (the game model allows two or three), and the card shows two places. The three games differ: 33 is touch a thing, then touch a thing again (each time one line of four); 34 is say, then touch a person of three; 35 is touch a person (sugar), then say (his own cup). The three losses differ: in 33 a roll filled wrong is bitten and put down on its eater's plate, and the second basket has a roll fewer for abuela; in 34 a helping that somebody did not ask for stays on his plate, and the next mound comes off the lunch kept for papá; in 35 a cup of coffee is spoiled for its owner, or the frog's own cup comes out the wrong colour. The three turns differ: a second basket of hot rolls; abuela goes to the gate to look for papá; abuela pours out the last of the coffee and the last of the milk and sits down with her own cup.

What is true of a table (and shaped these games): too little can be topped up and solid food can be passed on, so only what mixes or melts cannot be undone: butter or chocolate in a hot roll, sugar in coffee, coffee in milk, a mound tipped into the rice on a plate. A roll that somebody has bitten is not passed on, and a helping that somebody did not ask for stays on his plate: nobody eats it.

## Story 33: ¿Qué quieres?

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a basket of five hot rolls under a cloth on the table, steam rising (the cloth lies back, so the five can be counted); beside it the butter dish and a pot of melted chocolate with a spoon; abuela's own empty plate by the basket. The frog stands on the table with a knife. Fill each roll with what its eater likes.
- **At stake:** the rolls. Butter or chocolate melts into a hot roll and cannot come out, and the filling is inside: nobody can see from outside which it is. Whoever comes back for his roll bites it without looking; if the filling is wrong he makes a face and puts it down on his plate with a bite out of it. A bitten roll is not passed on: it stays on his plate to the end, and nobody else eats it; the frog fills a second roll from the basket. After the turn the basket has four rolls; what is left at the end is abuela's, who eats last and likes her bread plain.
- **New words:** quieres, ¿qué quieres?, me gusta, no me gusta.
- **Back again:** pan, mantequilla, chocolate, caliente, tengo, quiere, come, aquí, más.

**Start:** the table. On it: the basket with five rolls, the butter dish, the pot of chocolate, abuela's empty plate by the basket. Abuela at the stove with a second tray of rolls in the oven. The frog on the table. Abuelo, mamá, papá, Carmen and Luis are in the yard, past the door at the left. In the opening abuelo, mamá and Luis come in, one at a time; papá and Carmen come in for the first time at the turn.

1. NARR: "Aquí hay pan." (the basket twinkles) NARR: "El pan está caliente." NARR: "Aquí hay mantequilla." NARR: "Aquí hay chocolate." Abuela moves her own empty plate close up to the basket; during it: NARR: "Abuela dice: Me gusta el pan." Abuela goes out to the yard.

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **Two lines, taught.** Abuelo comes in. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, abuelo?" NARR: "Abuelo dice: Me gusta el chocolate." NARR: "Abuelo dice: No me gusta la mantequilla." He goes out to wash his hands. Sign on the chocolate. He touches it; the frog cuts a roll open and spoons chocolate in: it melts, and the roll is shut again. Abuelo comes back, sits and bites it; during it: NARR: "Abuelo come pan con chocolate."
3. Mamá comes in. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, mamá?" NARR: "Mamá dice: No me gusta el chocolate." NARR: "Mamá dice: Me gusta la mantequilla." She goes out to wash her hands. Sign on the butter. He touches it; the frog fills a roll with butter. She comes back and bites it; during it: NARR: "Mamá come pan con mantequilla."
4. **One line, taught.** Abuelo comes in for a second roll. He takes a roll from the basket and holds it up, cut open and empty. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, abuelo?" NARR: "Abuelo dice: No tengo chocolate." He goes out to wash his hands. Sign on the chocolate. He touches it; the frog fills the roll. Abuelo comes back and bites it; during it: NARR: "Abuelo come pan con chocolate."
5. Mamá comes in for a second roll. She takes a roll from the basket and holds it up, cut open and empty. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, mamá?" NARR: "Mamá dice: No me gusta el chocolate." She goes out to wash her hands. Sign on the butter. He touches it; the frog fills the roll. She comes back and bites it; during it: NARR: "Mamá come pan con mantequilla."
6. Luis comes in, takes a roll from the basket and holds it up, cut open and empty. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, Luis?" NARR: "Luis dice: No tengo mantequilla." He goes out to wash his hands. Sign on the butter. He touches it; the frog fills Luis's roll. Luis comes back and bites it; during it: NARR: "Luis come pan con mantequilla." The basket is empty (five rolls).

**The turn**

7. Abuela comes in with the second tray from the oven and tips four hot rolls into the basket. NARR: "Abuela dice: Aquí hay más pan." She points at her own empty plate by the basket; then she goes out to the yard with the tray. Papá and Carmen come in from the yard for the first time, look at the full basket and go out again. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Whoever asks takes a roll, holds it up cut open and empty, says it and goes out to wash his hands; nobody else is in the kitchen while the frog fills the roll.

8. **Decision A, Touch a thing (one line of four).** Papá comes in, takes a roll from the basket and holds it up, cut open and empty. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, papá?" One line of four (drawn): NARR: "Papá dice: No me gusta el chocolate." / "Papá dice: No me gusta la mantequilla." / "Papá dice: No tengo chocolate." / "Papá dice: No tengo mantequilla." He goes out. He touches the butter or the chocolate; the frog fills the roll.
   - Right: he comes back, bites it and eats it. A star.
   - Wrong: he comes back, bites, makes a face and puts the roll down on his plate with a bite out of it. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá quiere pan con chocolate." (Or: "…con mantequilla.") The sign is on the right thing; he touches it; the frog fills a second roll from the basket; papá comes back and eats that one. The bitten roll stays on his plate.
9. **Decision B, Touch a thing (one line of four).** Carmen comes in, takes a roll from the basket and holds it up, cut open and empty. COQUÍ: "¿Qué quieres, Carmen?" One line of four (drawn): NARR: "Carmen dice: No me gusta el chocolate." / "Carmen dice: No me gusta la mantequilla." / "Carmen dice: No tengo chocolate." / "Carmen dice: No tengo mantequilla." She goes out.
   - Right and wrong as in A: "Carmen quiere pan con chocolate." (Or: "…con mantequilla.")

**Count:** the first basket: five rolls, one for each of steps 2 to 6 (abuelo, mamá, abuelo, mamá, Luis); the frog has none, and the basket is empty after step 6. The second basket: four. Each round takes one (right) or two (wrong): both right, two are left; one miss, one is left; two misses, none. A redo always has its roll. Butter and chocolate do not run out. A bitten roll stays on the plate of whoever bit it; nobody else eats it.

**The ending.** Abuela comes in and sits down at her plate; the family is at the table. If a roll is left she eats it plain, and the other, if any, she lays under the cloth. NARR: "La familia come pan." COQUÍ: "¡Me gusta el pan de abuela!" (only if abuela has bread; otherwise he says nothing and she looks at the bitten rolls on the plates)
- Nothing wrong (two stars): two rolls left: abuela eats one, the other lies under the cloth. Stars and a heart.
- One miss: one roll left: abuela eats it; the basket is empty; the bitten roll stays on a plate.
- Two misses: the basket is empty and abuela has no roll; she looks at the two bitten rolls on the plates.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost** (a bubble's voice is not counted; the frog's own line counts once): no me gusta 3 (steps 2, 3, 5), no tengo 2 (steps 4, 6). Not deciding: me gusta 3 (steps 1, 2, 3); ¿qué quieres? 5 before the game (steps 2 to 6) and twice in it; quieres 5 before the game (the five questions).
**A miss is filed under:** no me gusta.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two each. Every game line opens on the old word "no", so "no" alone decides nothing: "No me gusta…" asks for the other thing, "No tengo…" for the thing named. A learner who knows the old word "tengo" (La mesa: tengo hambre, tengo sed) can tell the two kinds apart and is right every time: the owner is told.

## Story 34: Buen provecho

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** lunch on the plates: yellow rice. Abuelo, mamá with the baby and Carmen sit; Luis stands at the end of the table; the frog and his little plate are on the table, his stool beside it. Papá's chair is empty, and so is his plate. Keep papá's lunch for him.
- **At stake:** papá's lunch. After the turn the pot is scraped out onto a platter: five mounds. Two are second helpings that will be asked for; three are papá's, pushed to his end of the platter with his fork on them. A mound tipped onto the plate of somebody who did not ask for it mixes into his rice and cannot be lifted off for papá; he leaves it, and nobody eats it. The next mound comes off papá's three.
- **New words:** buen provecho, también, rico, todo.
- **Back again:** arroz, más, quiero, quieres, quiere, por favor, come, aquí, hola, familia.

**Start:** as above. Abuela at the stove with the pot.

1. NARR: "Papá no está aquí." (his chair twinkles) NARR: "Este plato es para papá."

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **He says it, taught.** Everybody holds a fork up and waits. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Buen provecho!" / "¡Yo también!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡Buen provecho!" NARR: "La familia dice: ¡Buen provecho!" They eat. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡El arroz está rico!"
3. **He says it, taught again.** Luis takes his last forkfuls; during them: NARR: "Luis se come todo el arroz." His plate is clean. NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero más arroz, por favor." Abuela gives him the big spoon; he takes a mound for himself, eats it, and holds a second mound out on the spoon. Over the frog, a thought bubble: a mound with a heart. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Quieres más?" Bubbles: "¡Yo también!" / "¡Buen provecho!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡Yo también!" The mound goes on his plate and he eats it; during it: NARR: "Coquí se come todo el arroz."
4. Luis holds out another mound; the frog has a round belly and a clean plate. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Quieres más?" Sign on the second bubble. COQUÍ: "¡Buen provecho!" He hops off the table to his stool: he is done. Luis eats the mound himself.
5. **Touch a person of three, taught.** The frog hops back on the table. NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero más arroz, por favor." He lifts one mound onto the big spoon and hands the frog the spoon with it. The three at the table are seated, in the same pose, and still eating. NARR: "Mamá dice: Yo también." NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡El arroz está rico!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Buen provecho!" Nobody gets up. Sign on mamá. He touches her; the frog tips the mound onto her plate; during it: NARR: "Mamá se come todo el arroz." Then Carmen gets up with her plate, which is clean, and carries it to the counter.
6. Carmen sits again. NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero más arroz, por favor." He lifts one mound onto the big spoon and hands the frog the spoon with it. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo también." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡El arroz está rico!" NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Buen provecho!" Nobody gets up. Sign on abuelo. He touches him; the frog tips the mound onto his plate; during it: NARR: "Abuelo se come todo el arroz." Then mamá gets up with the baby.

**The turn**

7. Mamá sits again. Abuela gives each of the three at the table (abuelo, mamá, Carmen) a small spoon of rice from the pot, so that all three still have rice on their plates. Then she scrapes the pot out onto a platter in the middle of the table: five mounds; she tips the pot towards us: empty. She pushes three of the mounds to papá's end of the platter, with a gap, and lays his fork on them. Then she takes her shawl and goes out to the gate to look for papá. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela is out at the gate. Luis has the big spoon. The frog is on the table (if he is on his stool, he hops back up).

8. **Decision A, Say (his own wish).** Luis takes a mound from the two helpings and holds it out. Over the frog a thought bubble (drawn): a mound with a heart, or his round belly beside his clean plate. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Quieres más?" Bubbles: "¡Yo también!" / "¡Buen provecho!" Whenever he says "¡Buen provecho!" he is done: he hops off the table to his stool.
   - Right "¡Yo también!": the mound is the frog's, and he eats it. Right "¡Buen provecho!": he hops down and Luis eats it. One mound either way. A star.
   - Wrong, he wanted it and said "¡Buen provecho!": he hops down; Luis eats the mound. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Tú quieres más arroz." The sign is on "¡Yo también!"; he hops back on the table and says it; a second mound for the frog, from papá's three.
   - Wrong, he was full and said "¡Yo también!": he eats the whole mound and lies flat on his back on the table; nothing stays on his plate. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Tú estás lleno." The sign is on "¡Buen provecho!"; he says it and hops down; Luis takes a mound for himself, from papá's three.
9. **Decision B, Touch a person of three.** The frog hops back on the table if he is on his stool. NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero más arroz, por favor." He lifts one mound from the two helpings onto the big spoon and hands the frog the spoon with it. Abuelo, mamá and Carmen, each seated, with rice on his plate, in the same pose: one says "Yo también.", one "¡El arroz está rico!", one "¡Buen provecho!" (who says which, and in which order, drawn). Nobody gets up before the touch; the one who said "¡Buen provecho!" gets up only after it. He touches one; the frog tips the mound onto that one's plate, where it mixes into the rice.
   - Right: the one who said "Yo también." eats it. A star.
   - Wrong: the one touched did not ask: he leaves the mound, a heap on his plate that stays, and nobody eats it (if he is the one who said "¡Buen provecho!", he gets up and leaves his plate with the heap on the table); the one who asked looks at the platter. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá quiere más arroz." (the one who said "Yo también.") The sign is on that one; he touches her; a second mound, from papá's three, for her.

**Count:** the platter: five mounds, two helpings and papá's three. A takes one (right) or two (wrong, the second from papá's three); B takes one (right) or two (wrong, the second from papá's three). So papá's three become three (nothing wrong), two (one miss) or one (two misses). A redo always has its mound. A mound left on a plate by a miss in B stays there.

**The ending.** Abuela comes in with papá. NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Hola, familia!" He sits; abuela scrapes what is left of the platter onto his plate. COQUÍ: "¡Buen provecho, papá!" During his last forkfuls: NARR: "Papá se come todo el arroz."
- Nothing wrong (two stars): three mounds on papá's plate. Stars and a heart.
- One miss: two mounds. Two misses: one mound, and papá looks at the empty platter. A heap left on a plate stays on it.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost** (a bubble's voice is not counted; the frog's own line counts once): también 3 (the frog in step 3; steps 5 and 6), buen provecho 5 (step 2 twice, the frog in step 4, steps 5 and 6). Not deciding: rico 3 before the game (steps 2, 5, 6) and once in B; todo 4 before the game (steps 3 twice, 5, 6) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** A: the word of the right bubble (también when he wanted more, buen provecho when he was full). B: también.
**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two; B one in three. In A either word alone is enough (with two, it always is). In B somebody who knows only "también" is right (and the old word "yo" also marks that line); somebody who knows only one of the other two lines has one in two.

## Story 35: Café con azúcar

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** the table is cleared. Abuelo and papá have cups of black coffee; Luis and Carmen have cups of hot milk; the frog stands on the table beside the sugar bowl with its big spoon, his own small empty cup by him. On the stove: the big coffee pot, a little pouring pot, the milk pan. Give everybody the cup he asks for.
- **At stake:** each one's cup. Sugar stirred in does not come out; coffee poured into milk does not come out. A spoiled cup is pushed to the middle of the table (the frog's own cup stands by him after one taste) and has no steam. There is no second cup.
- **New words:** café, azúcar, un poco, mucho.
- **Back again:** leche, taza, toma, más, por favor, huele a, le pone, no hay, rico, sí, quieres.
- Cups show their colour from above: black, brown (much coffee in milk), pale (a little).

**Start:** as above. Abuela at the stove. Mamá is out with the baby.

1. NARR: "Abuela hace café." NARR: "Aquí hay café." (the pot twinkles) NARR: "Aquí hay azúcar." (the bowl twinkles) NARR: "Huele a café."

**The opening** (big sign at steps 2, 3 and 6, four signs in all)

2. **Touch a person (sugar), taught.** The frog lifts a heaped spoon; abuela comes with the big pot. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Más azúcar, por favor." NARR: "Papá dice: Más café, por favor." Sign on abuelo. He touches him; during it: NARR: "Coquí le pone azúcar al café." Abuela fills papá's cup.
3. The same with the lines the other way round: NARR: "Abuelo dice: Más café, por favor." NARR: "Papá dice: Más azúcar, por favor." Sign on papá. NARR: "Coquí le pone azúcar al café."
4. **A scene, no sign.** Abuela gives the frog the little pot. Luis and Carmen stand their cups of milk in front of the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: Mucho café, por favor." NARR: "Carmen dice: Un poco de café, por favor." They go out to the yard. The frog pours as told: a long pour into Luis's cup, and the milk turns brown; then a splash into Carmen's: pale. They come back. NARR: "Luis toma mucho café." NARR: "Carmen toma un poco de café."
5. **A scene, no sign.** The same again with fresh cups of milk and the lines the other way round: NARR: "Carmen dice: Mucho café, por favor." NARR: "Luis dice: Un poco de café, por favor." They go out; the frog pours as told; they come back and drink.
6. **He says it, taught, twice.** Over the frog a thought bubble: a pale cup. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres café, Coquí?" Bubbles: "Sí, un poco, por favor." / "Sí, mucho, por favor." Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "Sí, un poco, por favor." Abuela pours a splash from the little pot into his cup and fills it with milk; he drinks. Then a thought bubble with a brown cup; the same question; sign on the second. COQUÍ: "Sí, mucho, por favor." A long pour, a little milk. COQUÍ: "¡El café está rico!"

**The turn**

7. The cups are empty. Abuela pours the last of the big pot: a half cup each for abuelo, papá and herself, and tips the pot: one last pour is left in it, a finger. Abuelo and papá each stir one spoon of sugar into his half cup and taste it and nod. She pours the last milk: Luis's and Carmen's cups three quarters full; one small cupful stays in the pan. She sets the little pot, full, on the shelf above the stove. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Mucho café para mí!" She sits down at the stove with her own cup. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game**

8. **Decision A, Touch a person (sugar).** Abuela gets up with the big pot and its last pour, a finger. Abuelo and papá hold their half cups out, both looking at her. One says "Más azúcar, por favor.", one "Más café, por favor." (who, and in which order, drawn). The frog has the heaped spoon; in one move he tips it into the cup of the one touched and stirs, and in the same move abuela pours.
   - Right: the heap goes into the cup of the one who asked for sugar and the finger of coffee into the other's; the big pot is empty. A star.
   - Wrong: the heap goes into the cup of the one who asked for coffee, and abuela, already pouring, lands the finger of coffee in the same cup; the big pot is empty. He looks into his cup (two heaps of sugar) and pushes it to the middle; he need not taste. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá quiere más café." (the one who was touched) The sign is on the other; he touches him; a heap for him.
   After A, in both branches, abuela puts the empty big pot on the stove, takes the little pot down from the shelf and hands it to the frog, full; he stands it by his small cup.
9. **Decision B, Say (his own cup).** Over the frog a thought bubble (drawn): a brown cup or a pale cup. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres café, Coquí?" Bubbles: "Sí, un poco, por favor." / "Sí, mucho, por favor." Abuela takes the little pot from beside his cup, pours from it what he says, tips what is left in the little pot into her own cup, and fills the frog's cup to the brim with the last milk; she tips the pan towards us: empty.
   - Right: he drinks. A star.
   - Wrong: the cup is the wrong colour and full to the brim. He tastes it once and puts it down. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Tú quieres mucho café." (Or: "Tú quieres un poco de café.") The sign is on the right bubble; he says it. Abuela drinks her own cup off and shows him her cup, the little pot and the pan: all empty. NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay más café." (after he wanted more) or "Abuela dice: No hay más leche." (after he wanted less). The cup stays as it is.

**Count:** the big pot at the turn: three half cups (abuelo, papá, abuela) and one last pour, a finger (used in A in both branches: in the cup of the one who asked for coffee). Milk: two cups for the children and one small cupful in the pan (used in B in both branches). The little pot holds the opening pours (steps 4 to 6) and, full when abuela hands it over, one pour for B (a long pour or a splash) and a mouthful over; what is left goes into abuela's cup. Abuela's cup is a big one that holds a whole cup: a half cup from the turn and at most a half cup more from the pot, so it never runs over; after a miss in B she drinks it all off, and it is empty at the end. Nothing wrong: no cup is spoiled. A miss in A: one man's cup stands in the middle. A miss in B: the frog's cup stands by him and abuela's cup is empty. Both: both. Sugar does not run out. Cups: abuelo, papá, Luis, Carmen, the frog, abuela: six, no spare. No redo needs a new cup.

**The ending.** Abuela sits at the stove with her cup. NARR: "Abuela toma café." (said only if her cup holds coffee: not after a miss in B, when she has drunk it off) COQUÍ: "¡Huele a café!"
- Nothing wrong (two stars): everybody drinks. Stars and a heart.
- Otherwise: each spoiled cup stands in the middle of the table and its owner looks at it; after a miss in B the frog's cup stands by him, tasted once; the others drink.

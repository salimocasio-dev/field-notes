# El patio: three stories (draft 3, final: after two rounds of fresh reviews)

Place 1, La casa. Scene 4 of 4, the last of this place. The learner is the little frog (the coquí), a guest at abuela's house.
He has played eight stories: three at the front of the house, two on the porch, three in the living room.

Words he has met so far: casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo.
Heard along the way so far: mira, ven, puedes, dónde está, también, ahora, quiere, tiene, entra, pájaro, bizcocho, palma, lluvia, y tú, o, mesa, balcón, sala, hace calor, dos, tres, un, una, y, de, del, con, para, pone, come, foto, quién, es, este, esta, Carmen, Luis.

Scene words for El patio (from the 40-scene list): niño, niña, amigo, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allí, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
One change to that list: **allí is taught as allá.** Across a yard, "allá" is what is said in Puerto Rico, and aquí / allá are easier to tell apart by ear than aquí / allí. (The Spanish reviewer's judgement.)

The picture: abuela's back yard. Grass. On the left, the back wall of the house with its door. A wooden fence along the back with a garden gate (el portón) in it. A tree on the right with the bird's nest in it. Two flower beds of bare earth, one on the left and one on the right, the same distance from us. A crate of flowers in little pots stands on the grass half-way between the beds; that is where the frog stands between moves. A big sun in the sky. The two children from the living room are here: Luis (the boy, short hair, green shirt) and Carmen (the girl, two braids, pink dress). The bird from the earlier stories lives in the tree. It only chirps.

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Luis dice:", "Carmen dice:", "Abuela dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!". It plays after every right answer, also where it is not written out.
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a short "No." line that repeats the word; then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es una flor." "Es el sol." "Es el portón." "Este es Luis.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision.

## Story 9: Las flores

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** the two beds are bare earth, five empty holes in each. A crate full of flowers in pots stands between them. Plant abuela's flowers.
- **At stake:** the daylight. A small card in the corner shows the sun on its way down to the fence, in three steps. After a wrong move the sun in the sky sinks one step and the sky turns more orange. Three steps and it is sunset. The sun never moves by itself.
- **New words:** niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá.
- **Back again:** hola, no, sí, tiene, es, está, para, ahora, tú, yo, también.
- **Heard along the way:** patio, bola, con, una.

**Start:** Luis bounces a ball. Carmen stands by the crate. The frog hops out of the back door.

1. NARR: "Este es el patio de abuela."
2. NARR: "Mira. Luis y Carmen." Then: "Luis es un niño." (He twinkles.) "Carmen es una niña." (She twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "¿Puedes tocar al niño?"
   - He touches Luis. Right signal. Luis waves. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola, Coquí!" He kicks the ball up and catches it. NARR: "El niño juega con la bola."
4. NARR: "¿Y la niña? ¿Dónde está la niña?"
   - He touches Carmen. Right signal. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Hola!" She lifts a pot from the crate. NARR: "La niña tiene una flor."
5. NARR: "Mira el sol." (The sun twinkles.) Then: "¿Dónde está el sol?"
   - He touches the sun. Right signal. It beams. NARR: "Es el sol."
6. **Planting, taught.** The frog hops to the crate. Carmen walks to the left bed and stands behind it. NARR: "Carmen dice: Una flor aquí." The big sign is on the left bed, where she stands. He touches it. The frog takes a pot, hops there and plants it. The flower opens and stays. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! La flor está aquí." The frog hops back to the crate.
7. She does not move. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y una flor allá." The big sign is on the right bed, away from her. He touches it. The frog plants one there. It opens. NARR: "Carmen dice: La flor está allá." The frog hops back to the crate.
8. Luis puts his ball down, walks to the right bed and stands behind it. Carmen steps back to the fence. NARR: "Luis dice: Una flor aquí." The big sign is on the right bed, where he stands. He touches it; the frog plants; it opens. NARR: "Luis dice: La flor está aquí."
9. Luis does not move. NARR: "Luis dice: Y una flor allá." The big sign is on the left bed. He touches it; the frog plants; it opens. NARR: "Luis dice: La flor está allá."

**The turn**

10. Carmen pushes the crate toward the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora tú, Coquí." The sun card appears in the corner and shows once what it is for: its little sun ticks down the three steps to the fence, then jumps back to the top. The sun in the sky does not move. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). The frog starts every round at the crate, between the beds.

11. **Round 1, Do (aquí or allá).** One child (drawn fresh) walks to one bed (drawn fresh) and stands behind it. The other child waits by the fence. NARR: "Carmen dice: Una flor aquí." (Or "Una flor allá." Drawn fresh. Or Luis says it.)
   - Right: the frog plants it in the right bed. It opens and stays. A butterfly comes.
   - Wrong: he plants it in the other bed. It stays there, shut, a green bud that never opens. NARR: "No. Allá." (Or "No. Aquí.": the word that was said.) The sun sinks a step. The sign shows the right bed; he plants one there; it opens.
12. **Round 2, Do (niño or niña).** Luis and Carmen come to stand left and right of the crate (sides drawn fresh), each with empty hands held out. Abuela leans out of the back door. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una flor para la niña." (Or "para el niño". Drawn fresh.) He can touch Luis or Carmen.
   - Right: the frog gives that child a pot. The child plants it in the nearer bed. It opens. The child claps.
   - Wrong: the other child takes it and plants it; it stays a shut bud. NARR: "No. Para la niña." The sun sinks a step. The sign shows the right child; the frog gives one; it opens.
13. **Round 3, Do (both words in one line; "no" decides).** One child (drawn fresh) stands behind one bed (drawn fresh). The line names both places; which one gets the "no", and which comes first, are both drawn fresh. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Aquí no! ¡Allá!" Or "¡Allá! ¡Aquí no!" Or "¡Allá no! ¡Aquí!" Or "¡Aquí! ¡Allá no!"
   - Right and wrong as in round 1. The "No." line: "No. Allá." (Or "No. Aquí.")

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

14. By the sun:
   - In every ending Luis and Carmen first plant what is left in the crate, so all ten holes are filled; theirs open. His shut buds stay shut.
   - Still high (no wrong moves): every flower is open, butterflies everywhere. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two steps down: an orange sky, long shadows; the open flowers and the shut buds as he left them.
   - Sunset (three wrong): the sun sits on the fence, the sky is red and purple, the first firefly comes out. It is pretty. It is not a fail screen.
15. Luis kicks the ball to Carmen; she kicks it back. NARR: "El niño juega. La niña juega." The ball comes to the frog. COQUÍ: "¡Yo también!" The frog bounces the ball on his head.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** aquí 4, allá 4 (steps 6 to 9, at both beds and from both children). Niño 3, niña 4 before round 2.
**Whole clean run:** flor 9 or more, sol 3, juega 3.

**A miss is filed under:** round 1, the word said (aquí or allá); round 2, niño or niña; round 3, the word without the "no" (the one to act on).

## Story 10: ¿Qué es esto?

- **Goal he can see:** Luis has a cloth sack with lumps in it. The frog has an empty basket. Fill the basket with what comes out of the sack.
- **At stake:** three things, one a round. Get it right and it goes in his basket. Get it wrong and the bird snatches it and carries it up to its nest in the tree. Two scores show in the top corners from the turn on: his basket, the bird's nest.
- **New words:** ¿qué es esto?, amigo, mira, ven.
- **Back again:** pájaro, flor, vaso, silla, bizcocho, bola, es, un, una, sí, no, tú, eres, ¿cómo estás?, ¿quién eres tú?
- **Heard along the way:** esto, mi.

Two kinds of answer bubble over the frog:
- **Speaking bubbles**, as in ¡Qué calor! and ¿Quién eres tú?: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Which side each is on, and so which speaks first, is drawn fresh.
- **Doing bubbles**, new here and taught under the sign: two pictures of what the frog can do. One shows the frog in the middle of a hop, off the ground, his shadow under him (he goes to the one who spoke). There is no arrow. One shows the frog's two big eyes wide open (he stays and looks). Which side each is on is drawn fresh.

**Start:** Luis sits on the grass on the right with his sack. Carmen stands on the left. The bird is in its nest. The frog is at the back door with his basket.

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

1. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ven, Coquí!" The two doing bubbles show. The big sign is on the hopping one. He touches it. The frog hops over to Luis. Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola, amigo!" COQUÍ: "¡Hola, Luis! Tú eres mi amigo."
2. The bird flies a loop over the tree. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mira, Coquí! ¡El pájaro!" The doing bubbles show. The big sign is on the eyes. He touches it. The frog's eyes go wide and follow the bird. Right signal. The bird lands on the frog's head for a moment. NARR: "Luis dice: El pájaro es mi amigo." COQUÍ: "¡Hola, amigo!"
3. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ven, Coquí! ¡Ven!" The doing bubbles; the sign on the hopping one. He touches it. The frog hops across to Carmen. She pats his head. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí es mi amigo."
4. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira, Coquí! ¡Mira!" The doing bubbles; the sign on the eyes. He touches it. The frog looks. Luis pulls a flower out of the sack and holds it up.
5. **The sack, taught.** NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Qué es esto?" Two speaking bubbles: "Es una flor." and "Es un vaso." The big sign is on "Es una flor." He touches it. COQUÍ: "Es una flor." Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Sí! Es una flor." He gives the flower to Carmen, who puts it in her hair.
6. **Asking, taught.** Luis holds the sack open to the frog: his turn. The frog pulls out a glass, turns it over, and a "?" shows over his head. Two speaking bubbles: "¿Qué es esto?" and "¿Cómo estás?" The big sign is on "¿Qué es esto?" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¿Qué es esto?" Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: Es un vaso." The frog hands the glass to Carmen.

**The turn**

7. The bird drops down, pecks at the sack and pulls at its string. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, amigo! ¡No!" It flaps up to a branch just above them and waits, eyes on the sack. The two scores appear, both empty. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

8. **Decision A, Do (ven or mira).** The frog hops back to the door. Carmen stands across the yard holding the glass from the sack in plain sight. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ven, Coquí!" (Or "Carmen dice: ¡Mira, Coquí!" Drawn fresh.) The doing bubbles show.
   - "¡Ven!", and he picks hopping: the frog hops to her. Right signal. She puts the glass in his basket.
   - "¡Mira!", and he picks the eyes: the frog watches. Right signal. She spins once on her toes with the glass on her head, bows, and walks it over to his basket.
   - Wrong, she said "¡Ven!" and he only looks: the frog stares; she waits, arm out; the bird swoops and takes the glass to its nest. NARR: "No. ¡Ven, Coquí!" One on the bird's score. The sign shows the hopping bubble; he touches it; the frog hops to her; she shows her empty hands and laughs.
   - Wrong, she said "¡Mira!" and he hops over: he bumps her as she starts her spin; the glass flies up; the bird catches it. NARR: "No. ¡Mira, Coquí!" One on the bird's score. The frog stays where he landed, beside her; the sign shows the eyes; he touches it; he looks; she spins with nothing on her head and bows.
9. **Decision B, Say (answer the question).** The frog is beside Luis. Luis pulls a thing out of the sack: a small chair or a little round cake (drawn fresh). NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Qué es esto?" Two speaking bubbles: the right name and one other, drawn fresh from "Es una flor.", "Es una silla.", "Es un bizcocho."
   - Right: COQUÍ: "Es una silla." Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Sí! Es una silla." It goes into his basket.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other name. NARR: "Luis dice: No. Es una silla." The bird snatches the thing and flies it to the nest. One on the bird's score. The sign shows the right bubble; it speaks again; he touches it. COQUÍ: "Es una silla."
10. **Decision C, Say (ask the question).** Luis holds the sack open to the frog: his turn. The frog pulls out something round and red with a white stripe, turns it over, and a "?" shows over his head. Two speaking bubbles: "¿Qué es esto?" and one other, drawn fresh from "¿Cómo estás?" and "¿Quién eres tú?"
   - Right: COQUÍ: "¿Qué es esto?" Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: Es una bola. ¡Es mi bola!" He laughs. The ball goes into the frog's basket.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other question. Luis answers it ("Luis dice: Bien." or "Luis dice: Yo soy Luis.") and shrugs. The bird snatches the ball and flies it to the nest. One on the bird's score. NARR: "No. ¿Qué es esto?" The sign shows the right bubble; it speaks again; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¿Qué es esto?" NARR: "Luis dice: Es una bola. ¡Es mi bola!"

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

11. By the basket:
   - Three things in his basket: stars and a heart; the bird hops down to look in.
   - One or two: the basket and the nest each show what they got.
   - None: the nest is full, the basket is empty.
   - In every ending the bird then brings one thing down and drops it in his basket, as a friend. (If its nest is empty it brings a flower from the tree.)
12. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí es mi amigo." COQUÍ: "¡Y tú eres mi amigo!" The bird chirps. COQUÍ: "¡Y tú también, amigo!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ven 3 (steps 1 and 3), mira 3 (steps 2 and 4), ¿qué es esto? 4 before decision C (step 5; step 6, where a bubble says it and then the frog; decision B). Amigo is heard 9 times, and names Luis, the bird and the frog.

**A miss is filed under:** A, the word said (ven or mira); B, the thing that was shown; C, ¿qué es esto?

## Story 11: Hasta mañana

- **Goal he can see:** the sun is on the fence and the sky is red. Time to go home: the bird sits on the garden gate. Three fireflies glow in the yard, each with someone: one on Carmen's finger, one on the napkin in abuelo's hands, one on the gate.
- **At stake:** the three fireflies, one a round. A right move and that person sends theirs over to fly beside him, for good. A wrong one and it slips off and drifts away over the fence while they look puzzled. A card in the corner shows three lights, lit or gone.
- **New words:** vamos, hasta mañana.
- **Back again:** adiós, hola, ven, gracias, de nada, abuela, abuelo, mamá, papá, sol, mira, ahora, para.
- **Heard along the way:** portón, al, ti.
- The speaking bubbles are the ones he knows; their sides and order are drawn fresh.

**Start:** dusk in the yard. The family stands at the back door of the house, on the left: abuela, abuelo, mamá with the baby, papá. Luis and Carmen are on the grass. The gate is shut, on the right, with the bird on it. The frog is in the middle.

1. NARR: "Mira el sol." The sun sinks behind the fence. The first star comes out.

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. Luis stands beside the frog, in the middle of the yard. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos, Coquí! ¡Vamos al portón!" The big sign is on the gate. He touches it. Luis and the frog go to the gate together, side by side. COQUÍ: "¡Vamos!"
3. Abuela waves from the door, far from them. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí! ¡Ven!" The big sign is on abuela. He touches her. The frog hops back across the yard to her, alone. Then Luis walks back and stands by the family.
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Adiós, Coquí. ¡Hasta mañana!" She waves. COQUÍ: "¡Hasta mañana, abuela!"
5. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!" NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Hasta mañana!" The baby claps. COQUÍ: "¡Hasta mañana!" The frog hops to the middle of the yard.

**The turn**

6. It gets darker. The three fireflies light up and blink, each where it sits. The card with three lights appears. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Decisions A and B come in an order drawn fresh; C is last.

7. **Decision A, Say (the goodbye).** Carmen walks up to the frog with her firefly on her raised finger. NARR: "Carmen dice: Adiós, Coquí." Two speaking bubbles: "¡Hasta mañana!" and one other, drawn fresh from "¡Hola!" and "De nada."
   - Right: COQUÍ: "¡Hasta mañana!" Right signal. She blows her firefly over to him; it stays beside him. She waves.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other line. Carmen tilts her head, a "?" over her; her firefly slips off her finger and drifts away over the fence. NARR: "No. ¡Hasta mañana!" The sign shows the right bubble; it speaks again; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Hasta mañana!" She waves.
8. **Decision B, Say (the gift).** Abuelo comes down from the door with a little round cake on a napkin and holds it out with both hands; his firefly sits on the napkin. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para ti, Coquí." Two speaking bubbles: "¡Gracias, abuelo!" and one other, drawn fresh from "De nada." and "¡Hola, abuelo!"
   - Right: COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuelo!" Right signal. He takes the cake, and the firefly comes with it and stays beside him. NARR: "Abuelo dice: De nada."
   - Wrong: the frog says the other line. Abuelo looks puzzled, a "?" over him; the firefly slips off the napkin and drifts away. NARR: "No. ¡Gracias!" The sign shows the right bubble; it speaks; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuelo!" He takes the cake. NARR: "Abuelo dice: De nada."
9. **Decision C, Do, last (ven or vamos).** Luis stands by the family at the door, on the left. The gate is on the right. The frog is in the middle. The last firefly sits on the gate. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ven, Coquí!" (Or "Luis dice: ¡Vamos al portón!" Drawn fresh.) He can touch Luis or the gate.
   - "¡Ven, Coquí!", and he touches Luis: the frog hops to him. Right signal. Luis hugs him. Then NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos al portón!" and the two of them go to the gate together; the firefly on it comes to the frog and stays.
   - "¡Vamos al portón!", and he touches the gate: Luis and the frog go to the gate together. Right signal. The firefly on it comes to the frog and stays.
   - Wrong, he said "¡Ven, Coquí!" and the frog goes to the gate: he lands there alone with a bump, a "?" over him; the firefly is shaken off the gate and drifts away. NARR: "No. ¡Ven, Coquí!" The sign shows Luis; the frog hops to him; the hug; then "Luis dice: ¡Vamos al portón!" and they go to the gate together.
   - Wrong, he said "¡Vamos al portón!" and the frog goes to Luis: Luis has already run to the gate and bumps it; the frog stands by the family alone, a "?" over him; the firefly is shaken off the gate and drifts away. NARR: "No. ¡Vamos al portón!" The sign shows the gate; the frog hops there, to Luis.
   - Either way, Luis then opens the gate.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

10. The gate is open. Everyone waves. NARR: "La familia dice: ¡Adiós, Coquí! ¡Hasta mañana!" COQUÍ: "¡Adiós! ¡Hasta mañana!"
11. He hops out along the path, the bird flying beside him. By the fireflies:
   - Three: they fly around him in a ring and the path is bright. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two: a small glow goes with him.
   - None: it is dark.
12. In every ending he then sings his own call (the coquí sound, no words), and the fireflies he has blink back. With none, one comes out of the dark to him. His call again, from far off.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** hasta mañana 5 (steps 4 and 5), vamos 3 (step 2), ven 1 here and 3 or more in story 10.

**A miss is filed under:** A, hasta mañana; B, gracias; C, the word said (ven or vamos).

## For the owner, one line each

- The plan's word "allí" is taught as "allá": the Puerto Rican reviewer says that is what is said across a yard, and it is easier to tell from "aquí" by ear. This is the reviewer's judgement, not a count.
- The garden gate is "el portón", so "puerta" stays the door of the house.
- Aquí and allá are taught as they are used: "aquí" is where the person speaking stands, "allá" is the other bed. Both children say both, from both beds.
- In story 10 the frog also asks "¿Qué es esto?" himself: he picks the question, not the answer. It is taught once under the sign first.
- The Spanish reviewer notes that to a small child one more often says "pajarito" than "pájaro". The course has used "pájaro" since story 3; I left it.

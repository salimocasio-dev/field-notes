# A comer: the orchestrator's decisions for draft 5 (Nov 16)

From the last gate on draft 4 (a fresh beginner reviewer and a fresh Puerto Rican Spanish reviewer). Apply all of these to historia/notas/guiones/comer.md. Where these words and the draft differ, these win. Change nothing else. Add a section "What the last gate changed (draft 4 to draft 5)" at the end, one bullet per story plus one for the pairs.

## Story 33

1. "No tengo chocolate." / "No tengo mantequilla." are heard as "I have none", not as asking for a filling. Every such line becomes "Mi pan no tiene chocolate." / "Mi pan no tiene mantequilla." (the opening and the four game lines). The game's four lines: "No me gusta el chocolate." / "No me gusta la mantequilla." / "Mi pan no tiene chocolate." / "Mi pan no tiene mantequilla."
2. The opening loses its repeat (step 5 was step 3 again). New order, same five rolls:
   - 2 abuelo: "Me gusta el chocolate." / "No me gusta la mantequilla."; sign on the chocolate.
   - 3 mamá: "No me gusta el chocolate." / "Me gusta la mantequilla."; sign on the butter.
   - 4 abuelo's second roll: "Mi pan no tiene chocolate."; sign on the chocolate.
   - 5 Luis's roll: "No me gusta la mantequilla."; sign on the chocolate.
   - 6 mamá's second roll: "Mi pan no tiene mantequilla."; sign on the butter.
3. At the turn, abuela's "No tengo pan." goes. After "Aquí hay más pan." and her pointing at her empty plate: NARR: "El plato de abuela no tiene pan." Hearings: no me gusta 3 (steps 2, 3, 5), no tiene 3 (steps 4, 6, the turn).
4. B's line is drawn from the three lines that are not A's.
5. A miss is filed under the drawn line's word: "no me gusta" for a "No me gusta…" line, "tiene" for a "Mi pan no tiene…" line.
6. "For the owner": a learner who knows the old word "tiene" can tell the two kinds of line apart ("tiene" asks for the thing named); "no" alone decides nothing.

## Story 34

1. Round A and opening steps 3 and 4 get a lead-in, so "¡Yo también!" answers something, and "¡Buen provecho!" is a wish to Luis, not a way of saying no:
   - NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero más arroz, por favor." Luis serves himself one mound onto his plate, lifts a second onto the big spoon and holds it out to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Bubbles: "¡Yo también!" / "¡Buen provecho!"
   - Right "¡Yo también!": the spoon's mound goes on the frog's plate and he eats it. Right "¡Buen provecho!": Luis eats the spoon's mound too. The frog no longer hops off the table anywhere in the story.
   - Wrong, he wanted it and said "¡Buen provecho!": Luis eats the spoon's mound; cross; "No, Coquí. Mira. Tú quieres más arroz."; he says "¡Yo también!"; Luis glances at papá's fork, then takes one mound from papá's three for the frog.
   - Wrong, he was full and said "¡Yo también!": he eats the whole mound and lies flat on his back; cross; "No, Coquí. Mira. Tú estás lleno."; he says "¡Buen provecho!"; Luis, who asked for more, glances at papá's fork and takes one mound from papá's three for himself.
   - The order of the two bubbles is drawn in A. Steps 3 and 4 show both orders (one each).
2. Round B and opening steps 5 and 6: "yo" was in one line only, so the old word alone could decide, and the man who praised the rice and then left a mound refused for no reason. The three lines become: "Yo también." / "Yo estoy lleno." (mamá and Carmen: "Yo estoy llena.") / "Yo me voy. ¡Buen provecho!". Each is said seated. Whoever is full puts a hand on his belly as he says it and leaves a new mound for that reason; whoever said "Yo me voy. ¡Buen provecho!" gets up only after the touch, as before. Teach steps 5 and 6 with these lines (each person says a different one in 5 and 6).
3. "Rico" is no longer in B. Keep it heard three times in the story: abuelo in step 2 ("¡El arroz está rico!"), Luis in step 3 after his mound, papá in the ending over his first forkful.
4. Count: the platter at the turn holds SEVEN mounds: four helpings in the middle, papá's three at his end with his fork on them. A takes two helpings in every branch (Luis's own and the spoon's); B two (Luis's own and the spoon's); every miss takes one of papá's. The middle is empty after B; papá ends with 3, 2 or 1. Recount the opening from the pot too. Rewrite the Count, the hearings line (también, buen provecho, lleno), the chance line and "For the owner" so every branch is true.

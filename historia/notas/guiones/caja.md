# La caja: three stories (draft 1)

Place 4, El colmado. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `caja`). Stories 45 to 47. He has finished stories 1 to 44.

Scene words (page 02): the numbers 11 to 20 (once, doce, trece, catorce, quince, dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve, veinte), dólar, chavos, ¿cuánto es?, caro, barato, paga, cambio.

The picture: the till end of don Pepe's counter. One view, `wide`: a big old cash register with a little window on top that shows the amount in numerals, a bell, a drawer of dollar bills and coins; the counter running left from it; don Pepe behind it; customers in front; the shop window at the left edge with the street beyond. The frog sits on the register's side shelf, where two big keys stand out: his two answers. A key shows a numeral.

Numerals are on screen in this scene: on price tags, on the keys, in the register's window, on a twenty-dollar bill. They are not words, and he has to match the word he hears to them. In the game a price tag always faces don Pepe, who reads it out; the frog cannot see it until after his answer.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as before. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places.

## Story 45: ¿Cuánto es?

- **Goal he can see:** three dollar bills under a clip on the side of the register, with a small drawing of the frog on the clip. Don Pepe pays his helper at the end of the day.
- **At stake:** the frog's three dollars. When the register rings the wrong price, don Pepe gives the customer one of them, for the trouble.
- **New words:** ¿cuánto es?, dólar, chavos, paga, once, doce.
- **Back again:** diez, nueve, tres, colmado, compra, necesito, buenas tardes, gracias, aquí tiene, lata, botella, caja.

**Start:** afternoon. Don Pepe at the register; the frog hops onto its side shelf. Luis in front of the counter.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The drawer slides open with a ring. NARR: "Esta es la caja de don Pepe." The bills twinkle, then the coins. NARR: "Aquí hay dólares. Y aquí hay chavos." Don Pepe counts three bills under the clip. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Uno, dos, tres. Tres dólares para Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, don Pepe!"
2. Luis sets a box of cans on the counter; its tag (10) faces us. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son diez dólares." Ask (signed, the key 10; the other key is 9): "¿Dónde está el diez?" He touches it: the bell rings, the window shows 10. Luis counts bills into don Pepe's hand. NARR: "Luis paga diez dólares."
3. The bell at the door: the señor comes in. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenas tardes (buenas tardes)]: "¡Buenas tardes!" He sets down a pot; tag 11. NARR: "El señor dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son once dólares." Ask (signed, the key 11; the other is 10): "Toca el once." He touches it: ring, 11. NARR: "El señor paga once dólares."
4. The señora sets down a bag of rice; tag 12. NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son doce dólares." Ask (signed, the key 12; the other is 11): "¿Y el doce?" He touches it: ring, 12. NARR: "La señora paga doce dólares."
5. A child reaches up with a sweet from the jar; a tag with a coin on it and a 5. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Cinco chavos." The child lays five coins down. NARR: "El niño paga cinco chavos."

**The turn**

6. People come in one after the other: a line forms at the counter. Don Pepe starts to pack each customer's things into a bag as he goes, and holds each tag up to his own glasses to read it: its back is to us. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Ay! ¡Once, doce! ¡Doce, once! Coquí, ¡la caja!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Each round a customer asks, don Pepe reads a tag we cannot see, and the frog rings it. The price is once or doce (drawn; round B is the other). The two keys are the price and one number beside it (10, 11 or 12, drawn).

7. **Decision A, touch a key (two keys; tests once or doce).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son once dólares." (Or: "Son doce dólares.")
   - Right: ring; the window shows the price; don Pepe turns the tag round: the same. She pays. NARR: "La señora paga once dólares." A star.
   - Wrong: ring; the window shows the wrong number; don Pepe turns the tag round and she looks from one to the other and shakes her head. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Son once dólares." Don Pepe takes one bill from under the frog's clip and gives it to her. The sign is on the right key; he touches it: ring. She pays.
8. **Decision B, the other price: the señor.** As A.

**The ending**

9. The shop is empty. Don Pepe takes what is left from under the clip.
    - Three: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Toma, Coquí. Tres dólares." COQUÍ: "¡Tres dólares!" The frog drops them into his jar. Stars and a heart.
    - Two: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Toma, Coquí. Dos dólares." Into the jar.
    - One: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Toma, Coquí. Un dólar." Into the jar.
10. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!" COQUÍ: "¡Hasta mañana!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** once 3 before the game (step 3 three times) and twice more in the turn; doce 3 (step 4) and twice more. Not deciding: ¿cuánto es? 3 before the game; dólar 6 or more; paga 4; chavos 3 (steps 1, 5, 5).
**A miss is filed under:** once or doce, whichever was the price.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two: the tag's back is to us until he has answered.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 3): gracias; buenas tardes.

## Story 46: Caro y barato

- **Goal he can see:** on the counter, the cup the frog is buying for abuela, with a flower on it; on the paper holder beside the register, four bright sheets of gift paper. Don Pepe wraps what people buy.
- **At stake:** the sheets of paper (4). Don Pepe wraps at once whatever is pushed along the counter to him: paper, string, a bow. A thing wrapped that was not the one asked for is unwrapped again, and its sheet is torn. Two customers need a sheet each, abuela's cup needs one, and one is over.
- **New words:** caro, barato, trece, catorce, quince.
- **Back again:** taza, olla, plato, bolsa, dólar, ¿cuánto es?, paga, quiero, compra, once, doce, grande, pequeño, abuela, para.

**Start:** the frog on the counter with his jar of dollars (from story 45). Two cups stand in front of him: a large one with a gold rim, tag 15; a small one with a flower, tag 13.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Coquí compra una taza para abuela." Don Pepe lays a hand on the large cup. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Esta taza es cara. Quince dólares." On the small one. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y esta taza es barata. Trece dólares." The frog looks into his jar. COQUÍ: "Quiero la taza barata, por favor." Ask (signed, the small cup): "¿Dónde está la taza barata?" He touches it. The frog pays. NARR: "Coquí paga trece dólares."
2. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Para quién es la taza?" COQUÍ [TURN: abuela (abuela)]: "Es para abuela." Don Pepe points at the gift paper and stands the cup beside it, for later.
3. The señora comes in. Two pots on the counter: a large one, tag 15; a small one, tag 14. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: La olla grande es cara: quince dólares. La olla pequeña es barata: catorce dólares." NARR: "La señora dice: Quiero la olla cara." Ask (signed, the large pot): "Toca la olla cara." He touches it: the frog pushes it along to don Pepe, who wraps it in a blink: paper, string, a bow. (This sheet comes off a fifth one: four are left.) NARR: "La señora paga quince dólares."
4. Luis comes in. Two bags hang on the hook: tag 13 and tag 14. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Trece dólares y catorce dólares." NARR: "Luis dice: Quiero la bolsa de trece dólares." Ask (signed, the bag with tag 13): "¿Dónde está la bolsa de trece dólares?" He touches it. NARR: "Luis paga trece dólares." Luis takes it as it is. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: de nada (de nada)]: "¡De nada!"

**The turn**

5. The bell at the door: a line of people, each with a birthday hat or a balloon. Don Pepe pulls the paper holder close and cracks his knuckles. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Rápido, Coquí!" From now on his hands wrap whatever reaches them, without a look. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Each round two things of one kind stand in front of the frog, each with its tag towards us: one dear, one cheap (the prices are two of 13, 14, 15, drawn; the dear one is also the larger or the finer one). The customer says which she wants. Round A names it by caro or barato (drawn); round B by its price (drawn).

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two plates; tests caro or barato).** NARR: "El señor dice: Quiero el plato barato." or NARR: "El señor dice: Quiero el plato caro."
   - Right: the frog pushes it along; don Pepe wraps it. He pays. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the other one; it is wrapped before anybody can speak; the señor shakes his head. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Este plato es caro." (Or: "…es barato.") Don Pepe unwraps it: the sheet tears. One sheet gone. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it; it is wrapped in a new sheet.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (two bags; tests the number).** NARR: "La señora dice: Quiero la bolsa de catorce dólares." (Or trece, or quince: the price of one of the two.)
   - Right and wrong as in A. NARR (wrong): "No, Coquí. Mira. Esta bolsa es de quince dólares." (the number on the one he pushed)

**The ending**

8. Don Pepe looks at the paper holder and at abuela's cup.
    - Two sheets left: he wraps the cup, with a bow, and folds the last sheet into a paper hat for the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y esto es para abuela." Stars and a heart.
    - One left: he wraps the cup. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y esto es para abuela."
    - None left: he puts the cup into a plain paper bag. NARR: "No hay más papel." **(papel: heard)**
9. The frog carries it out. COQUÍ: "¡Abuela! ¡Mira!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** caro 3 (steps 1, 3, 3), barato 4 (steps 1, 1, 1, 3); trece 4 (steps 1, 1, 4 three times), catorce 3 (steps 3, 4, 4), quince 3 (steps 1, 3, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** caro or barato (A); the number asked for (B).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two. In A the picture shows which is dear; the line says which is wanted. In B both tags are in view; the line picks one.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 4): abuela; de nada.

## Story 47: El cambio

- **Goal he can see:** a big glass jar on the counter with a drawing of a ball taped to it, a few coins and bills in it and two lines marked on the glass above them. In the shop window stands the ball itself. Three children look at it from the street.
- **At stake:** the change for the jar. A customer whose change is right drops it into the jar, and it rises to the next line. A customer who had to wait while it was counted twice puts it in her purse.
- **New words:** cambio, dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve, veinte.
- **Back again:** dólar, chavos, ¿cuánto es?, paga, bola, niño, quiere, uno to cuatro, compra, para, aquí.

**Start:** don Pepe at the register; the frog on its side shelf; Luis in front with a pot. The children at the window.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The ball twinkles in the window. NARR: "Los niños quieren la bola." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Qué quieren los niños?" COQUÍ [TURN: bola (bola)]: "¡Una bola!" The jar twinkles. NARR: "Aquí hay chavos para la bola."
2. Luis's pot; tag 16. He holds out one bill with 20 on it. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son dieciséis dólares." Ask (signed, the key 16; the other is 15): "¿Dónde está el dieciséis?" He touches it: ring, 16. NARR: "Luis paga con veinte dólares." Don Pepe counts the change up into Luis's hand, a bill at a time. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: El cambio: diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve, veinte." Luis drops the four bills into the jar. NARR: "Luis dice: El cambio es para los niños."
3. Carmen; a bag, tag 18; a twenty. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son dieciocho dólares." Ask (signed, the key 18; the other is 17): "Toca el dieciocho." He touches it: ring, 18. NARR: "Carmen paga con veinte dólares." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: El cambio: diecinueve, veinte." Carmen drops it into the jar. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Para quién es el cambio?" COQUÍ [TURN: niño (niños, ninos)]: "¡Para los niños!"

**The turn**

4. The children press their noses to the window; one holds up two fingers and points at the two lines on the jar. Don Pepe starts packing and reading tags with their backs to us, as in story 45. He holds up the two things the next customers will buy, their tags towards us for a moment: 17 and 19. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Diecisiete dólares. Y diecinueve dólares." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, ¡la caja!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Each round a customer pays with a twenty. The price is diecisiete or diecinueve (drawn; round B is the other). The two keys are the price and a number beside it (16 to 19, drawn).

5. **Decision A, touch a key (two keys; tests diecisiete or diecinueve).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Son diecisiete dólares." (Or: "Son diecinueve dólares.") She holds out her twenty.
   - Right: ring; the window and the tag agree. NARR: "La señora paga con veinte dólares." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: El cambio: dieciocho, diecinueve, veinte." (Or: "El cambio: veinte.") She drops it into the jar: it rises to the next line. A star.
   - Wrong: ring; the window and the tag do not agree; don Pepe has begun to count the change and stops; she shakes her head. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Son diecisiete dólares." The sign is on the right key; he touches it: ring. Don Pepe counts the change again; she puts it in her purse and goes.
6. **Decision B, the other price: the señor.** As A.

**The ending**

7. Don Pepe lifts the jar and looks at its lines. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Niños!" The children come in.
    - Both lines reached: he tips the jar out on the counter and takes the ball from the window. NARR: "Los niños compran la bola." They run out, bouncing it. COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, niños!" Stars and a heart.
    - One line reached: he shows them the jar and the line still above the money. NARR: "No hay chavos para la bola. Mañana." **(mañana: heard in "hasta mañana")** The children look at the ball.
    - No line reached: the same picture, the money lower.
8. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¡Hasta mañana!" COQUÍ: "¡Hasta mañana!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** diecisiete 2 (steps 2, 4), diecinueve 3 (steps 2, 3, 4), each said while its numeral or its bill is in the picture. Dieciséis 2, dieciocho 4, veinte 5, cambio 5.
**A miss is filed under:** diecisiete or diecinueve, whichever was the price.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 3): bola; niño.

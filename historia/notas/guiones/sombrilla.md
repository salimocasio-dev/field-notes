# La sombrilla: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `sombrilla`). Stories 75 to 77. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 74.

Scene words (page 02): cabeza, cara, ojo, boca, nariz, oreja, pelo, mano, brazo, pierna, pie, me duele.
How they are shared out: 75 cara, ojo, nariz, boca; 76 cabeza, pelo, oreja, brazo, mano; 77 pierna, pie, me duele.

The picture: abuela's beach umbrella, close. One view. A big striped umbrella at the left with a blanket in its shade; abuela on the blanket. In the middle and at the right, a wide flat stretch of damp sand: the children's drawing ground. On it, drawn with a stick, a face as big as Luis (story 75), or a whole lying figure with a head, arms and legs (stories 76, 77). The sea is a strip at the top. A bucket of shells, a heap of seaweed and abuela's bag beside the drawing ground.

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. Nobody new.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 75: La cara

- **Goal he can see:** abuela asleep on her blanket under the umbrella, three Z's rising over her. On the damp sand, a big round face drawn with a stick, still empty; Carmen points at abuela and at the face, and puts a finger to her lips: a surprise.
- **At stake:** abuela's sleep (the three Z's). A shell dropped in the wrong place lands on another shell with a loud clack, the children gasp "¡No!", and a Z goes; abuela stirs.
- **New words:** cara, ojo, nariz, boca.
- **Back again:** abuela, duerme, grande, pequeño, dos, uno, una, otro, va, aquí, mira, tiene, cuidado, sí, no, contento, ¿dónde está?, ¿quién?, es, soy, yo, más, concha (heard), arena.

**Start:** midday. Abuela asleep in the shade. Luis, Carmen and the frog at the drawing ground; Carmen has just drawn a big circle in the sand with a stick. A bucket of white shells beside the frog.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela está durmiendo." **(durmiendo: heard; "duerme" is a word of El banco. This story is whispered until the ending.)** Carmen whispers, a finger to her lips. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí: una cara. La cara de abuela." Ask (signed, the big circle): "¿Dónde está la cara?" He touches it: the circle twinkles; abuela breathes on.
2. Carmen points at her own eye, then at the circle. NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuela tiene dos ojos. Un ojo va aquí." She presses a shell into the sand. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Y el otro ojo?" Ask (signed, the place for the second eye; the ask is Carmen's own question): he touches it: the frog presses a shell in. The face has two eyes.
3. Luis touches his nose. NARR: "Luis dice: Y una nariz. Abuela tiene una nariz pequeña." Ask (signed, the middle of the face): "¿Dónde va la nariz?" He touches it: the frog presses a small shell in.
4. Luis draws a wide line under the nose with the stick. NARR: "Luis dice: Y la boca. Una boca grande." Carmen giggles; Luis hushes her. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No! La boca de abuela es pequeña." She rubs it out with her foot and draws a small one. Ask (signed, the small mouth): "¿Dónde va la boca?" He touches it: the frog presses a shell into the middle of the mouth.

**The turn**

5. The face has an eye-shell in each eye, one in the nose and one in the mouth. Carmen tips the bucket: many more shells, for a face of shells. Abuela turns over in her sleep; the three Z's rise. NARR: "Carmen dice: Más conchas. Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Luis or Carmen says where the next shell goes: an eye, the nose or the mouth, drawn, each round on its own (round B can be the same part again: every part takes more shells). The frog presses it in.

6. **Decision A, touch a place on the face (the two eyes, the nose, the mouth; tests the word).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta concha va en la boca." (Drawn: "... en la nariz.", "... en un ojo.")
   - Right: the frog presses the shell in beside the others. Carmen nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog drops it somewhere else: it lands on a shell with a loud clack. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Va en la boca." They clap their hands over their own mouths, too late: abuela stirs, and a Z is gone. A cross. Carmen takes the shell out. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the shell goes in.
7. **Decision B, touch a place on the face (the same game).** NARR: "Luis dice: Y esta concha va en la nariz." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis and Carmen put in the last shells themselves and draw a sun hat over the face.
   - Three Z's left: abuela sleeps on. The children lie down beside the face and the frog curls up on its nose; they are all asleep when she wakes, looks, and laughs. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: abuela opens one eye, sees the face, smiles and shuts it again; then she sits up.
   - One left: abuela is sitting up already, and puts her hands to her own cheeks.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Una cara! ¿Quién es?" COQUÍ [TURN: abuela (abuela)]: "¡Abuela!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Soy yo? ¡Qué cara más linda! Estoy contenta." **(linda: heard)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ojo 3 with ojos (step 2), nariz 3 (step 3), boca 4 (step 4). Not deciding: cara 3 (step 1).
**Forms:** ojos counts with ojo.
**A miss is filed under:** the part that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three (the two eyes count as one answer), each round on its own. Every part already has a shell and takes more, so nothing is ruled out.
**Speaking turns:** one in the ending (abuela) and none before: the story is whispered. (Two are asked for: see "Left".)

## Story 76: La cabeza y los brazos

- **Goal he can see:** a sand figure of abuela lying across the drawing ground: a head, and a body in stick lines with two arms and two legs. Beside it three big spiral shells in a row; Carmen holds one against the figure's middle, a button, and puts it back. Canelo sniffs at the row.
- **At stake:** the three spiral shells. A piece put in the wrong place has to come off again; while Luis carries it back, Canelo takes a spiral shell and buries it.
- **New words:** cabeza, pelo, oreja, brazo, mano.
- **Back again:** cara, ojo, nariz, boca, dos, grande, otro, va, aquí, tiene, tengo, mira, perro, no, sí, dame, toma, más, verde, también, me gusta, soy, yo, es, para, esto.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuela awake on her blanket, watching. The sand figure lies across the drawing ground: a round head with a face of shells (no hat, no hair), a body in stick lines. Luis, Carmen and the frog beside a heap of seaweed and a bucket of flat stones. Canelo beside the three spiral shells.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen pats the top of the sand head. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira: la cabeza de abuela. Una cabeza grande." Ask (signed, the head): "¿Dónde está la cabeza?" He touches it: it twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Mi cabeza? ¿Y mi pelo?" She pats her own hair. Luis lifts a handful of seaweed. NARR: "Luis dice: Aquí tengo pelo. ¡Pelo verde!" He lays it along the top of the head. Abuela laughs. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Tengo el pelo verde!"
2. Carmen holds two flat shells to her own ears. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y dos orejas. Una oreja aquí, y otra oreja aquí." She sets one at each side of the head. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué orejas más grandes tengo!"
3. Luis runs the stick along the two lines at the figure's sides. NARR: "Luis dice: Dos brazos. Un brazo... y otro brazo." Ask (signed, an arm): "¿Dónde hay un brazo?" He touches it: it twinkles. At the end of each arm Luis draws five short lines. NARR: "Luis dice: Y dos manos. Una mano aquí y otra mano aquí." Abuela holds up her own hand and waves it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, dame la mano." COQUÍ [TURN: toma (toma)]: "¡Toma!" The frog hops over and puts his hand in hers.
4. Carmen brings a starfish. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí: una estrella." **(estrella: heard; a word of El cielo)** Ask (signed, a hand of the figure): "¿Dónde hay una mano?" He touches it: Carmen lays the starfish in the sand hand.

**The turn**

5. Luis tips out the bucket: flat stones, all alike. Canelo edges nearer the three spiral shells; Carmen wags a finger at him. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Canelo, no!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Luis or Carmen holds out a flat stone and says where it goes: the head, an arm or a hand, drawn, each round on its own (round B can be the same part again).

6. **Decision A, touch a place on the figure (the head, the two arms, the two hands; tests the word).** NARR: "Luis dice: Toma, Coquí. Esto es para el brazo de abuela." (Drawn: "... para la mano de abuela.", "... para la cabeza de abuela.")
   - Right: the frog sets the stone there; it becomes a bracelet, a ring or a hat pin. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog sets it somewhere else. NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. Es para el brazo." He picks the stone up and carries it back to the bucket; behind him Canelo takes a spiral shell in his mouth, trots off, and digs. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the stone goes there.
7. **Decision B, touch a place on the figure (the same game).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Y esto es para la cabeza de abuela." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Carmen sets the spiral shells that are left down the figure's middle: the buttons.
   - Three: three buttons, and abuela gets up to lie down beside herself, arms out the same way. Stars and a heart. NARR: "La abuela de arena tiene tres botones." **(botones: heard)**
   - Two: two buttons; Canelo comes back with sand on his nose. NARR: "La abuela de arena tiene dos botones. Y Canelo tiene uno."
   - One: one button; Canelo brings a stick instead, and lays it where a button should be. NARR: "La abuela de arena tiene un botón. Y Canelo tiene dos."
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Tengo cabeza, pelo, orejas, brazos y manos. ¡Soy yo! ¿Te gusta, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cabeza 4 (step 1), brazo 4 with brazos (step 3), mano 5 with manos (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: pelo 4 (step 1); oreja 4 with orejas (step 2).
**Forms:** brazos, manos, orejas count with brazo, mano, oreja.
**A miss is filed under:** the part that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own. The stones are alike, and every part takes more than one.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): toma; me gusta.

## Story 77: Me duele

- **Goal he can see:** Luis bounces a ball on the wet sand and counts heads: abuela, Carmen, himself. Three players for the game, and the frog to keep the score.
- **At stake:** the players (3). Somebody who is brought the wrong thing for what hurts gives up: she goes to lie down on the blanket in the shade, and is out of the game.
- **New words:** pierna, pie, me duele.
- **Back again:** cabeza, ojo, mano, brazo, sol, arena, juega, bola, vamos, toma, gracias, ya, también, sí, no, necesito, abuela, otro, hay, concha (heard), sombrero, gafas, chancletas (heard: words of La ropa; the pictures carry them).

**Start:** the hottest hour. Abuela, Luis and Carmen stand on the sand in the sun; Luis with the ball. The frog beside abuela's open bag: in it sun hats, dark glasses and sandals, more than one of each. The sand figure lies near, with its arms and legs.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis points along the sand figure with his foot. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí. Dos piernas. Una pierna... y otra pierna." At the end of each leg Carmen presses a shell. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y dos pies. Un pie aquí. Y otro pie aquí." Ask (signed, the figure's feet): "¿Dónde están los pies?" He touches them: the shells twinkle.
2. Carmen steps back, onto a sharp shell, and hops on one foot. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ay! ¡Una concha! ¡Me duele un pie!" She hops onto another. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Me duelen los pies!" The sandals in the bag twinkle. Ask (signed, the sandals): "¿Qué necesita Carmen?" He touches them: the frog drags a pair over; Carmen puts them on and sighs. NARR: "Carmen dice: Gracias, Coquí. Ya no me duelen."
3. Abuela puts a hand on top of her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué sol! Me duele la cabeza." Ask (signed, the sun hats): "¿Qué necesita abuela?" He touches them: the frog brings a hat; she puts it on. Luis screws up his eyes. NARR: "Luis dice: Y a mí me duelen los ojos." Ask (signed, the dark glasses): "¿Y Luis? ¿Qué necesita?" He touches them: the frog brings a pair; Luis puts them on.
4. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Jugamos, Coquí?" **(jugamos: a form of juega)** COQUÍ [TURN: vamos (vamos)]: "¡Vamos!" The ball goes round: abuela to Carmen to Luis.

**The turn**

5. A gust of wind takes the hat, the glasses fall in the sand, the sandals are kicked off in a jump: the three things are back beside the bag, and everybody stops. Abuela and Carmen stand still, each with a hand out to the frog; they show nothing of what hurts. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ay, ay, ay!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela in one round and Carmen in the other (which comes first is drawn) says what hurts: the head, the eyes or the feet, drawn, each round on its own. She stands still: nothing in the picture shows it. The frog brings a hat, a pair of glasses or a pair of sandals from the bag (there is always one of each left in it). Anybody can have anything hurt.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the hats, the glasses, the sandals; tests the part in "me duele").** NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, me duelen los pies." (Drawn: "Me duele la cabeza.", "Me duelen los ojos.")
   - Right: the frog brings the thing; she puts it on and takes her place again. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings another thing. She holds it, and shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Me duelen los pies." She goes to the blanket and lies down in the shade: out of the game. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the frog takes it to her on the blanket, and she puts it on there.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the other one).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, me duele la cabeza." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis throws the ball up.
   - Three players: the ball goes round all three, high, and the frog jumps to head it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two players: Luis and one more, to and fro; the one on the blanket claps.
   - One player: Luis bounces the ball alone, off the frog's head; two clap from the blanket.
9. The ball comes down on the frog's head and bounces off. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿te duele la cabeza?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "A Coquí no le duele la cabeza. ¡Coquí juega!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** me duele 5 with "me duelen" (steps 2, 3); pie 5 with pies (steps 1, 2); cabeza and ojo are words of stories 75 and 76, each heard here in "me duele" before the game. Not deciding: pierna 3 with piernas (step 1).
**Forms:** me duelen, le duele, te duele count with me duele; pies with pie; piernas with pierna.
**A miss is filed under:** the part that was said (pie, cabeza or ojo).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own. Which thing helps which part is shown in the opening, three times by touch; what the rounds test is hearing which part hurts.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): vamos; no.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- All three: round B was "another part", so the part of round A was ruled out (in 75 its mark was filled, in 77 its thing was gone from the bag): each round is drawn by itself, every part takes more than one shell or stone, and the bag has more than one of each thing.
- 75: "boca" could cost, and its only picture was a line that was rubbed out: Carmen draws the small mouth and he puts its first shell in. The cost came from the children's shout and not from his move: the shell lands on another with a clack first. The turn smoothed away everything the opening had built: the face stays, and the game adds shells. Asks that asked "where is" a nose not yet there, or spoke of the learner in the third person: "¿Dónde va la nariz?", and Carmen's own "¿Y el otro ojo?". "¡Qué cara!" is "what a sour face" or "how dear": "¡Qué cara más linda!". The last turn answers "¿Quién es?" with "¡Abuela!". A body part with "la" is first heard as the listener's own ("a shell in your mouth"): "Esta concha va en la boca", of the face in front of him.
- 76: the asks went to pelo and oreja, which decide nothing, and brazo and cabeza were never touched: now the head, an arm and a hand are each touched in the opening. "Un brazo... y un brazo" is "y otro brazo". "Abuela tiene pelo verde. Y Canelo tiene una concha." was false on a clean run and wrong twice over (the article; a spiral shell is "un caracol"): each ending has its own line. Nothing showed what the spiral shells were for: Carmen holds one to the figure's middle. "¡Qué orejas más grandes tengo!". The list at the end is a sentence.
- 77: the one who hurt showed it (hopping, a hand on her head), so the picture gave the answer: in the game she stands still. Hot sand burns ("me quemo"); "me duelen los pies" is an ache: Carmen steps on a shell. Luis could be drawn out of the game he then throws the ball in: the two who can hurt are abuela and Carmen. A hat helps sore eyes as well as glasses do: the opening shows by touch that Luis takes the glasses. "Ya no me duelen". "¿Jugamos?" for "¡Vamos!". A step number was missing.

Left, and why:
- 75 has one speaking turn, not two: the story is whispered, and a turn before the ending would have to be a whisper the machine may not hear. If the checker insists on two, the first is "¡Sí!" to Carmen's "¿Listo?" in step 5.
- Pelo, oreja and pierna decide nothing. Each is heard three times or more, with its picture.
- "Pequeña": the island's everyday word is "chiquita"; pequeño is the course's word since El frente.
- In 77 the new phrase "me duele" is the same in every line; what decides is the part. That is what the phrase is for.
- Length: the openings are long (four steps each). The real number comes from machine play.

# La sombrilla: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `sombrilla`). Stories 75 to 77. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 74.

Scene words (page 02): cabeza, cara, ojo, boca, nariz, oreja, pelo, mano, brazo, pierna, pie, me duele.
How they are shared out: 75 cara, ojo, nariz, boca; 76 cabeza, pelo, oreja, brazo, mano; 77 pierna, pie, me duele.

The picture: abuela's beach umbrella, close. One view. A big striped umbrella at the left with a blanket in its shade; abuela on the blanket. In the middle and at the right, a wide flat stretch of damp sand: the children's drawing ground. On it, drawn with a stick, a face as big as Luis (stories 75, 76), and later a whole lying figure with arms and legs (stories 76, 77). The sea is a strip at the top. A bag, a bucket of shells and a heap of seaweed beside the drawing ground.

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. Nobody new.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 75: La cara

- **Goal he can see:** abuela asleep on her blanket under the umbrella, three Z's rising over her. On the damp sand, a big round face drawn with a stick, still empty; Carmen points at abuela and at the face, and puts a finger to her lips: a surprise.
- **At stake:** abuela's sleep (the three Z's). A shell pressed into the wrong place: Luis and Carmen shout "¡No!", and a Z goes; abuela stirs.
- **New words:** cara, ojo, nariz, boca.
- **Back again:** abuela, duerme, grande, pequeño, dos, uno, una, pone, aquí, mira, tiene, despacio, cuidado, listo, sí, no, contento, ¿dónde está?, concha (heard), arena.

**Start:** midday. Abuela asleep in the shade. Luis, Carmen and the frog at the drawing ground; Carmen has just drawn a big circle in the sand with a stick. A bucket of white shells beside the frog.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela duerme." Carmen whispers, a finger to her lips. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí: una cara. La cara de abuela." **(the voices are low: this story is whispered until the ending)** Ask (signed, the big circle): "¿Dónde está la cara?" He touches it: the circle twinkles; abuela breathes out a Z.
2. Carmen points at her own eye, then at the circle. NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuela tiene dos ojos. Un ojo aquí..." She presses a shell into the sand. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y un ojo aquí." Ask (signed, the place for the second eye): "¿Dónde pone Coquí el ojo?" He touches it: the frog presses a shell in. The face has two eyes.
3. Luis touches his nose. NARR: "Luis dice: Y una nariz. Abuela tiene una nariz pequeña." Ask (signed, the middle of the face): "¿Dónde está la nariz?" He touches it: the frog presses a small shell in. Luis draws a line with the stick under it. NARR: "Luis dice: Y la boca. Una boca grande." Carmen giggles; Luis hushes her. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No! La boca de abuela es pequeña." She rubs it out with her foot.
4. The face has its eyes and its nose, and no mouth. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Listo, Coquí? Despacio." COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" (a whisper)

**The turn**

5. Carmen smooths the face over: the shells come out, and only the circle is left, with four faint marks: two for the eyes, one for the nose, one for the mouth. She gives the frog the bucket. Abuela turns over in her sleep; the three Z's rise. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora tú. ¡Cuidado con abuela!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Luis or Carmen says where the next shell goes: an eye, the nose or the mouth, drawn, each round on its own (round B's is not the same as round A's). The frog presses it in.

6. **Decision A, touch a place on the face (the two eyes, the nose, the mouth; tests the word).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Una concha en la boca." (Drawn: "... en la nariz.", "... en un ojo.")
   - Right: the frog presses the shell in. Carmen nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog presses it in somewhere else. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! En la boca." They clap their hands over their own mouths, too late: abuela stirs, and a Z is gone. A cross. Carmen takes the shell out. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the shell goes in.
7. **Decision B, touch a place on the face (another part).** NARR: "Luis dice: Y una concha en la nariz." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis and Carmen put in the last shells themselves and draw a sun hat over the face. NARR: "Luis dice: Dos ojos, una nariz y una boca. ¡La cara de abuela!"
   - Three Z's left: abuela sleeps on. The children lie down beside the face and the frog curls up on its nose; they are all asleep when she wakes, looks, and laughs. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: abuela opens one eye, sees the face, smiles and shuts it again.
   - One left: abuela sits up, sees the face, and puts her hands to her own cheeks.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién es? ¿Soy yo?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué cara! Estoy contenta."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ojo 4 with ojos (step 2), nariz 3 (step 3), boca 3 (step 3). Not deciding: cara 4 (steps 1, 5... counted in steps 1 and 3: three before the game).
**Forms:** ojos counts with ojo.
**A miss is filed under:** the part that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three (the two eyes count as one answer), each round on its own. The marks on the face are alike.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): sí; sí. (To settle in the reviews: the second could be "abuela".)

## Story 76: La cabeza y los brazos

- **Goal he can see:** the sand face of abuela with its hat, and under it, drawn with the stick, a body: two arms out to the sides, two legs. Beside it three big spiral shells in a row: the buttons for her dress. Canelo lies with his nose on his paws, watching them.
- **At stake:** the three spiral shells. A piece put in the wrong place has to come off again; while Luis carries it back, Canelo takes a spiral shell and buries it.
- **New words:** cabeza, pelo, oreja, brazo, mano.
- **Back again:** cara, ojo, nariz, boca, dos, grande, pequeño, largo? (not a word yet: not used), pone, aquí, tiene, mira, perro, no, sí, dame, toma, más, verde, también, me gusta.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuela awake on her blanket, watching. The sand figure lies across the drawing ground: the face with its shells, a body in stick lines. Luis, Carmen and the frog beside a heap of seaweed and a bucket of shells. Canelo beside the three spiral shells.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen pats the top of the sand face. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira: la cabeza de abuela. Una cabeza grande." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Mi cabeza? ¿Y mi pelo?" She pats her own hair. Luis lifts a handful of seaweed. NARR: "Luis dice: Aquí tengo pelo. ¡Pelo verde!" Ask (signed, the top of the head): "¿Dónde pone Coquí el pelo?" He touches it: the frog lays the seaweed along the top of the head. Abuela laughs.
2. Carmen holds two flat shells to her own ears. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y dos orejas. Una oreja aquí." She sets one at the side of the head. Ask (signed, the other side of the head): "¿Y la otra oreja?" He touches it: the frog sets the second shell. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué orejas grandes tengo!"
3. Luis runs the stick along the two lines at the figure's sides. NARR: "Luis dice: Dos brazos. Un brazo... y un brazo." At the end of each he draws five short lines. NARR: "Luis dice: Y dos manos. Una mano aquí y una mano aquí." Abuela holds up her own hand and waves it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, dame la mano." COQUÍ [TURN: toma (toma)]: "¡Toma!" The frog hops over and puts his hand in hers.
4. Carmen brings bracelets of seaweed and a starfish. NARR: "Carmen dice: Una estrella para la mano. Y más pelo para la cabeza." **(estrella: heard; a word of El cielo)** Ask (signed, a hand of the figure): "¿Dónde está la mano?" He touches it: the starfish lies in the sand hand.

**The turn**

5. Luis tips out the bucket: more seaweed, more shells, a crab's empty claw. Canelo edges nearer the three spiral shells; Carmen wags a finger at him. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Canelo, no!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Luis or Carmen holds out a piece and says where it goes: the head, an arm or a hand, drawn, each round on its own (round B's is not the same as round A's). The pieces are alike: flat stones.

6. **Decision A, touch a place on the figure (the head, the two arms, the two hands; tests the word).** NARR: "Luis dice: Toma, Coquí. Para el brazo." (Drawn: "Para la mano.", "Para la cabeza.")
   - Right: the frog sets the stone there; it becomes a bracelet, a ring or a hat pin. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog sets it somewhere else. NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. Para el brazo." He picks the stone up and carries it back to the bucket; behind him Canelo takes a spiral shell in his mouth, trots off, and digs. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the stone goes there.
7. **Decision B, touch a place on the figure (another part).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Y esto, para la cabeza." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Carmen sets the spiral shells that are left down the figure's middle: the buttons.
   - Three: three buttons, and abuela gets up to lie down beside herself, arms out the same way. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: two buttons; Canelo comes back with sand on his nose.
   - One: one button; Canelo brings a stick instead, and lays it where a button should be.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Cabeza, pelo, orejas, brazos y manos. ¡Soy yo! ¿Te gusta, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" NARR: "Abuela tiene pelo verde. Y Canelo tiene una concha."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cabeza 4 (steps 1, 4), brazo 3 with brazos (step 3), mano 5 with manos (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: pelo 4; oreja 4 with orejas.
**Forms:** brazos, manos, orejas count with brazo, mano, oreja.
**A miss is filed under:** the part that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): toma; me gusta.

## Story 77: Me duele

- **Goal he can see:** Luis bounces a ball on the wet sand and counts heads: abuela, Carmen, himself. Three players for the game, and the frog to keep the score. But the sun is fierce, and the sand is hot.
- **At stake:** the players (3). Somebody who is given the wrong thing for what hurts gives up: he goes to lie down on the blanket in the shade, and is out of the game.
- **New words:** pierna, pie, me duele.
- **Back again:** cabeza, ojo, mano, brazo, hace calor, sol, caliente, arena, juega, bola, vamos, toma, gracias, bien, ahora, también, sí, no, quiero, abuela, sombrero, gafas, chancletas (heard: words of La ropa; the pictures carry them).

**Start:** the hottest hour. Abuela, Luis and Carmen stand on the sand in the sun; Luis with the ball. The frog beside abuela's open bag: in it a sun hat, a pair of dark glasses and a pair of sandals. The sand figure lies near, with its arms and legs.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis points along the sand figure with his foot. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí. Dos piernas. Una pierna... y una pierna." At the end of each leg Carmen presses a shell. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y dos pies. Un pie aquí. Y un pie aquí." Ask (signed, a foot of the figure): "¿Dónde está el pie?" He touches it: the shell twinkles. Ask (signed, a leg): "¿Y la pierna?" He touches it.
2. Carmen hops from one foot to the other on the hot sand. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ay! La arena está caliente. ¡Me duele un pie! ¡Me duelen los pies!" The sandals in the bag twinkle. Ask (signed, the sandals): "A Carmen le duelen los pies. ¿Qué necesita?" He touches them: the frog drags the sandals over; Carmen puts them on and sighs. NARR: "Carmen dice: Gracias, Coquí. Ahora no me duelen."
3. Abuela puts a hand on top of her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué sol! Me duele la cabeza." Ask (signed, the sun hat): "A abuela le duele la cabeza. ¿Qué necesita?" He touches it: the frog brings the hat; she puts it on. Luis screws up his eyes. NARR: "Luis dice: Y a mí me duelen los ojos." He takes the dark glasses himself and puts them on. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Juegas, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: vamos (vamos)]: "¡Vamos!"

**The turn**

5. They play: the ball goes round, abuela to Carmen to Luis. Then a gust of wind takes the hat, the glasses fall in the sand, the sandals are kicked off in a jump: all three things are back beside the bag, and everybody stops. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ay, ay, ay!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One of the three (drawn; another one in round B) says what hurts: the head, the eyes or the feet, drawn, each round on its own. The frog brings the hat, the glasses or the sandals. Anybody can have anything hurt: abuela's feet, Carmen's eyes.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the hat, the glasses, the sandals; tests the part in "me duele").** NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, me duelen los pies." (Drawn: "Me duele la cabeza.", "Me duelen los ojos.")
   - Right: the frog brings the thing; she puts it on and takes her place again. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings another thing. She holds it, and shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Me duelen los pies." She limps to the blanket and lies down in the shade: out of the game. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the frog takes it to her on the blanket, and she puts it on there.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (another player).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, me duele la cabeza." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis throws the ball up.
   - Three players: the ball goes round all three, high, and the frog jumps to head it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two players: the ball goes to and fro; the one on the blanket claps.
   - One player: Luis bounces the ball alone against the frog's head; two clap from the blanket.
9. The ball comes down on the frog's head and bounces off. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿te duele la cabeza?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "A Coquí no le duele. ¡Coquí juega!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** me duele 7 with "me duelen", "le duele(n)" (steps 2, 3); pie 6 with pies (steps 1, 2); cabeza and ojos are words of stories 75 and 76. Not deciding: pierna 4 with piernas (step 1).
**Forms:** me duelen, le duele, le duelen, te duele count with me duele; pies with pie; piernas with pierna.
**A miss is filed under:** the part that was said (pie, cabeza or ojo).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own. Which thing helps which part is plain from the things (a hat for a head); what is new is hearing which part hurts.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): vamos; no.

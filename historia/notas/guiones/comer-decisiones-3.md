# A comer: the orchestrator's decisions for draft 3

Apply all of these to historia/notas/guiones/comer.md. Where these words and the draft differ, these win. Keep the script's shape and its sections; rewrite the three stories, the counts, the hearings, the chances, the pairs and "For the owner" to match; add a section "What the second round of reviews changed (draft 2 to draft 3)" at the end, one bullet per story plus one for the pairs, saying what changed and why.

Every story now has TWO scored rounds (the game model allows two or three). The card shows two places. The three stories' rounds: 33 touch a thing (one line), touch a thing (one line); 34 say, touch a person of three; 35 touch a person, say.

## Story 33: ¿Qué quieres?

- Cost: the filling is INSIDE the roll and cannot be seen from outside. Whoever comes back for his roll bites it without looking; if the filling is wrong he makes a face and puts it down on his plate with a bite out of it. A bitten roll is not passed on: it stays on his plate to the end. Nobody else eats it. No Luis eating wrong rolls.
- Abuela eats last, and she only likes her bread plain. In step 1 (unsigned), she says "Me gusta el pan." while she sets her own empty plate by the basket. That is a hearing of "me gusta".
- First frame: the basket with five hot rolls under a cloth (count them), the butter dish, the pot of melted chocolate, abuela's empty plate by the basket, the frog on the table with a knife.
- Opening, five signed steps, askers abuelo and mamá only (plus Luis once):
  2. abuelo: two lines, "Me gusta el chocolate." / "No me gusta la mantequilla." (two recordings), goes out; sign on chocolate.
  3. mamá: two lines, "No me gusta el chocolate." / "Me gusta la mantequilla.", goes out; sign on butter.
  4. abuelo comes back for a second roll, holds it up cut open and empty: "No tengo chocolate." (one line, agrees with step 2); sign on chocolate.
  5. mamá's second roll: "No me gusta el chocolate." (one line, agrees with step 3); sign on butter.
  6. Luis comes in, takes a roll, holds it up cut open: "No tengo mantequilla."; sign on butter.
  The basket is empty after step 6 (five rolls). Each asker goes out to wash his hands after saying his line, and nobody else is in the kitchen while the frog fills a roll.
- Turn: abuela brings a second tray from the oven and tips four hot rolls into the basket; she points at her own empty plate by the basket; then she goes out to the yard. Papá and Carmen come in from the yard for the first time.
- Game: the askers are papá (A) and Carmen (B), whose tastes have not been shown. Each takes a roll from the basket, holds it up cut open and empty, says ONE line drawn from four: "No me gusta el chocolate." / "No me gusta la mantequilla." / "No tengo chocolate." / "No tengo mantequilla.", and goes out. He touches the butter dish or the chocolate pot; the frog fills the roll. Right: he comes back and eats it. Wrong: he comes back, bites, makes a face, puts it down bitten on his plate; cross; "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá quiere pan con chocolate." (or the right filling); sign; the frog fills a second roll from the basket; he eats that one.
- Count: second basket 4: two rounds take one or two each; 2, 1 or 0 left. Ending: abuela comes in and sits; if a roll is left she eats it plain ("Abuela come pan." only in that branch, or keep the ending lines true in every branch); the other, if any, under the cloth. NARR: "La familia come pan." COQUÍ: "¡Me gusta el pan de abuela!" (only if abuela has bread; otherwise COQUÍ says nothing and abuela looks at the bitten rolls on the plates). Two misses: abuela has no roll.
- Hearings before cost: no me gusta 3 (steps 2, 3, 5), no tengo 2 signed (4, 6), me gusta 3 (steps 1, 2, 3; not deciding). ¿qué quieres? and quieres as before.
- Chance: one in two each. "No" alone no longer decides: every game line opens on "No". A learner who knows the old word "tengo" can tell the two kinds apart; tell the owner (as with "jugo" in a La nevera pair).
- Filed under: no me gusta.

## Story 34: Buen provecho

- Round A, the frog's own wish: the two bubbles are "¡Yo también!" / "¡Buen provecho!". Saying "¡Buen provecho!" means he is done: he hops off the table to his stool. Teach it that way in steps 3 and 4 (step 4: he is full and says "¡Buen provecho!" and hops down; Luis eats the mound). No "No, gracias" anywhere in the frog's bubbles. Flag for the Spanish check: "¡Buen provecho!" said to decline more by leaving the table.
- Wrong with a full belly: the frog eats the whole mound and lies flat on his back on the table; then Luis takes a mound for himself. Nothing stays on the frog's plate.
- Round B: Luis does NOT ask "¿Quién quiere más?". He says "Quiero más arroz, por favor.", serves himself a mound, then hands the frog the spoon with one mound. Then the three at the table speak, each still seated: "Yo también." / "¡El arroz está rico!" / "¡Buen provecho!" (who and order drawn). Nobody gets up before the touch; the one who said "¡Buen provecho!" gets up only after it. Teach the same shape in the opening (steps 5 and 6) with no "¿Quién…?".
- Plates: at the turn, before scraping the platter, abuela gives each of the three at the table a small spoon of rice, so all three still have rice on their plates during the game. State it.
- Cost in B: the frog tips the mound onto the plate of the one touched; it mixes into the rice there and cannot be lifted off for papá. Whoever did not ask leaves it (a heap on his plate that stays). No adult eats a mound he did not ask for.
- Turn: no cover by papá's plate. Abuela scrapes the pot onto the platter: five mounds; she pushes three of them to papá's end of the platter, with a gap, and lays his fork on them; then she goes out to the gate. Two mounds are for second helpings.
- Count: platter 5: A takes one or two; B one or two; papá's three lose one per miss: 3, 2 or 1 at the end. Ending lines as in draft 2 ("¡Hola, familia!", "¡Buen provecho, papá!", "Papá se come todo el arroz." during his last forkfuls).
- Words that decide: también (A and B), buen provecho (A and B). Rico is heard (4) and goes to its pair.

## Story 35: Café con azúcar

- Drop round B (the long pour into the children's cups). Steps 4 and 5 stay as narrated scenes WITHOUT a sign (the children say their lines and the frog pours as told): they are hearings of "mucho" and "un poco". In step 5 the order of the two lines is the other way round from step 4.
- Round A (sugar): the heap of sugar and the last pour of coffee land together in one move; the last pour is only a finger. The man looks into his cup and pushes it to the middle; he need not taste. After A abuela hands the frog the full little pot (that is the turn's gesture moved to after A, or keep it at the turn: choose whichever keeps the turn a turn; the pot must not stand by unused while a line about "más café" is said).
- Round C (his own cup): after a wrong answer, abuela drinks her own cup off and shows her cup, the little pot and the milk pan, all empty, before "Abuela dice: No hay más café." (or "No hay más leche."). The frog tastes the wrong cup once and puts it down.
- Count: the little pot holds the opening pours, one pour for C, and the rest goes into abuela's cup (say how much her cup holds, so every branch is true). Cups: six, no spare.
- Signed steps: 2, 3 (sugar), 6 twice (his own); four in all.

## Pairs

- también: "Luis come pan. Carmen también." fits Luis and Carmen each eating a roll; the other: Luis eating a roll, Carmen beside him with empty hands.
- todo: Canelo is eating in both pictures (licking a clean bowl / eating from a heaped bowl).
- quieres: the other picture is an offer too (abuela holding out a cup of coffee to Luis).
- ¿qué quieres?: both pictures are offers (abuela over two pots looking at Carmen / abuela holding out one plate to Carmen).
- me gusta: both faces glad (Luis biting a drumstick, a heart over it / Luis smiling, holding a drumstick out to Carmen).
- no me gusta: no green face (Carmen pushing away a plate with a manzana, frowning / Carmen at an empty plate, a manzana with a heart in her thought).

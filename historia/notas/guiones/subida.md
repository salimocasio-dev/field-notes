# El camino al fuerte: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `subida`). Stories 108 to 110. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 107.

Scene words (page 02): fuerte, muralla, bandera, fiesta, invita, trae, voy a (comer, tomar, dormir), vamos a (jugar, bailar), ¿quieres venir?, ¿cuándo?, ¿quién?, juntos.
How they are shared out: 108 fuerte, muralla, bandera, fiesta, ¿quieres venir?; 109 invita, trae, ¿quién?, ¿cuándo?; 110 voy a, vamos a, juntos.

The picture: the wide lawn in front of the old fort, late in the afternoon. One view. At the back, across the whole picture, the fort's wall (the muralla), with the fort's gate (a door) in its middle and a sentry box at its right corner; above the gate, on a pole, a flag. A path of pale stone runs from the picture's foot up to the gate. At the left of the path, a tree; at the right, a party table, a hammock between two posts, and open grass. The sea's line shows beyond the wall's ends. No roof.

People: the family (abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen), and the people of every place: don Pepe (the colmado), the maestra, the piragüero, the vendedor, the doctor, the people of El parque.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 108: La fiesta

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen's basket: three paper lanterns for tonight's party. She lifts one, and it glows a little.
- **At stake:** the three lanterns. A lantern carried to the wrong place has to be carried back across the lawn: the wind takes it out of the frog's hands, over the wall.
- **New words:** fuerte, muralla, bandera, fiesta, ¿quieres venir?
- **Back again:** puerta, hoy, hay, viento, grande, viejo, mira, quiero, vamos, aquí, allá, arriba, noche, sí, no, gracias, hola.

**Start:** late afternoon; a wind. The family comes up the path: Luis with a roll of paper under his arm, Carmen with her basket, the frog in front. Before them, the wall, the gate, the flag.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí: el fuerte. Es grande, y es viejo." Ask (signed, the gate): "¿Dónde está la puerta del fuerte?" He touches it: the frog hops up the path to the gate. NARR: "Luis dice: Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. ¿Quieres venir?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!"
2. Carmen points along the wall. NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta es la muralla del fuerte." Ask (signed, the wall, away from the gate): "¿Dónde está la muralla?" He touches it: the frog hops onto it and walks along its top. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Cuidado, Coquí! La muralla es alta."
3. The flag snaps in the wind. NARR: "Luis dice: Y allá arriba está la bandera." Ask (signed, the flag): "¿Dónde está la bandera?" He touches it: it waves. NARR: "Carmen dice: Hay viento. Mira la bandera."
4. The señor comes by with his dog. Luis unrolls his paper: a drawing of the fort with lanterns. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola! Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. ¿Quieres venir?" NARR: "El señor dice: ¿Una fiesta? ¡Sí! Gracias." Carmen lifts a lantern out of her basket; it glows a little. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para la fiesta: una en la puerta, una en la muralla y una en la bandera."

**The turn**

5. Luis goes off down the path with his paper, to ask more people; he stops a woman on the path. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola! Hay una fiesta. ¿Quieres venir?" She nods. Carmen sits down by the tree with her basket and takes out the first lantern; the wind pulls at it. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen, untangling the lanterns' strings with her eyes on her hands, says where the next lantern goes (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): on the wall, on the flag's pole, on the gate. The frog carries it there.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the wall, the flag, the gate; tests muralla against bandera, with puerta, which he has).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta va en la muralla." (Or, drawn: "Esta va en la bandera." Or: "Esta va en la puerta.")
   - Right: the frog hangs it there; it swings and glows. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to another place. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. En la muralla." He starts back across the lawn; the wind takes the lantern out of his hands, and it sails over the wall. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it (Carmen hangs another there herself, afterwards, if one is left).
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; another place).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta va en la bandera." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Evening comes; the lanterns that are left are lit.
   - Three lanterns: on the gate, on the wall, on the flag's pole. Stars and a heart.
   - Two lanterns.
   - One lantern, on the gate.
9. Luis comes back up the path, and people behind him. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí! Esta noche, la fiesta. ¿Vamos?" COQUÍ [TURN: vamos (vamos, sí vamos)]: "¡Vamos!" NARR: "Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. La bandera, la muralla y la puerta tienen luz."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** muralla 4 (steps 2, 4), bandera 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: fuerte 6 (steps 1, 2, 4); fiesta 4 (steps 1, 4); ¿quieres venir? 3 (steps 1, 4, 5).
**A miss is filed under:** the place that was said (muralla or bandera; for the gate, puerta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): quiero; vamos.

## Story 109: ¿Quién? ¿Cuándo?

- **Goal he can see:** on the road below the fort, three people walk by, one after another. Luis's paper hangs on the tree: the fort with lanterns, and under it two round pictures: abuela's face, and a moon over a clock at seven.
- **At stake:** the guests (3). Someone who gets the wrong answer to his question shrugs, and walks on.
- **New words:** invita, trae, ¿quién?, ¿cuándo?
- **Back again:** fiesta, fuerte, ¿quieres venir?, abuela, hoy, noche, a las siete, jugo, fruta, hielo, pan, voy, gracias, hola, sí, no.

**Start:** late afternoon. The tree by the path, with Luis's paper hung on it. Under the fort and its lanterns, two round pictures: at the left abuela's face, at the right a moon over a clock at seven. The frog on a stone beside the tree. Abuela with a list; Luis beside her.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. Yo invito." **(invito: a form of invita)** Ask (signed, abuela's picture on the paper): "Abuela invita. ¿Dónde está abuela?" He touches it: the picture shines. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela invita a la fiesta."
2. Don Pepe comes up the path. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola! Hay una fiesta. ¿Quieres venir?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Una fiesta? ¿Quién invita?" Ask (signed, abuela's picture): "¿Quién invita?" He touches it: it shines; abuela waves. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Abuela invita! ¿Y cuándo es la fiesta?" Ask (signed, the moon and the clock): "¿Cuándo es la fiesta?" He touches it: it shines. NARR: "Luis dice: Hoy, a las siete de la noche."
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Voy. Yo traigo jugo." **(traigo: a form of trae)** Abuela writes on her list; a small jug shows beside don Pepe. NARR: "Abuela dice: Don Pepe trae jugo. La maestra trae frutas. Y el piragüero trae hielo." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y quién trae a Coquí?" The frog hops onto Luis's shoulder. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Yo! Coquí, ¿vienes con abuela o con Luis?" COQUÍ [TURN: con (con luis, contigo, con Luis)]: "¡Con Luis!"

**The turn**

4. Abuela and Luis go up to the fort to lay the table. The frog stays on his stone by the paper. Down the path come three people, one after another, a little apart. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A passer-by (drawn) stops, looks at the paper, and asks one of two questions (drawn, each round on its own): who invites, or when the party is. The frog touches one of the paper's two round pictures: abuela's face, or the moon over the clock.

5. **Decision A, touch a picture (two; tests ¿quién? against ¿cuándo?).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Una fiesta? ¿Cuándo es?" (Or, drawn: "¿Una fiesta? ¿Quién invita?")
   - Right: the picture shines. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Ah! Gracias. Voy." She goes up the path to the fort. A star.
   - Wrong: the other picture shines; she frowns at it. NARR: "La señora dice: No... ¿Cuándo?" She shrugs, and walks on down the road. A cross. The sign is on the right picture; he touches it.
6. **Decision B, touch a picture (the same game; another passer-by).** NARR: "El joven dice: ¿Una fiesta? ¿Quién invita?" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The third passer-by reads the paper for himself, and goes up. At the fort's gate:
   - Three guests wait with abuela. Stars and a heart.
   - Two guests.
   - One guest.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Bienvenidos! Coquí, ¿quién trae hielo?" The piragüero comes up the path with his cart. COQUÍ [TURN: él (él, el piragüero)]: "¡Él!" NARR: "Abuela invita. La gente trae jugo, frutas y hielo. Hoy, a las siete, hay fiesta."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿quién? 3 (steps 2, 3), ¿cuándo? 3 (step 2). Not deciding: invita 5 with invito (steps 1, 2); trae 4 with traigo (step 3).
**Forms:** invito with invita; traigo with trae.
**A miss is filed under:** the question that was asked (¿quién? or ¿cuándo?).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): con; él.

## Story 110: Vamos a jugar

- **Goal he can see:** by the fort's gate, three paper lanterns, not lit. Abuela lights one with a long match for the first guest who is settled, and holds up the match: one for each.
- **At stake:** the lanterns lit (up to 3). A guest who is led to the wrong place stands there, lost; Luis has to fetch him, and no lantern is lit for him.
- **New words:** voy a, vamos a, juntos.
- **Back again:** come (comer), toma (tomar), duerme (dormir), juega (jugar), fiesta, mesa, jugo, bola, tengo hambre, tengo sed, tiene sueño, cansado, quiero, ¿quieres venir?, también, sí, no, gracias.

**Start:** evening; the lanterns on the wall are lit. On the lawn: the party table with food at one end and jugs at the other, a hammock between two posts, open grass with a ball. The frog at the gate, with abuela. Guests come up the path.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Don Pepe comes in at the gate, rubbing his stomach. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Tengo hambre! Voy a comer." Ask (signed, the table's end with the food): "Don Pepe va a comer. ¿Dónde?" **(va a: a form of voy a)** He touches it: the frog leads him there; he eats. Abuela lights a lantern by the gate.
2. The maestra, fanning herself. NARR: "La maestra dice: ¡Tengo sed! Voy a tomar jugo." Ask (signed, the table's end with the jugs): "La maestra va a tomar jugo. ¿Dónde?" He touches it: she pours a glass.
3. Abuelo yawns. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo estoy cansado. Voy a dormir." Ask (signed, the hammock): "Abuelo va a dormir. ¿Dónde?" He touches it: abuelo climbs into the hammock.
4. Luis and Carmen run in with the ball. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos a jugar!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! Vamos a jugar juntos. ¿Quieres venir, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy, ya voy)]: "¡Voy!" They run onto the grass; the ball goes from one to the other, and to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: Luis, Carmen y Coquí: ¡juntos!"

**The turn**

5. More guests come up the path. Abuela stands by the two lanterns that are not lit, with her long match. The frog goes back to the gate. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn), or two guests together, comes in at the gate and says what he is going to do (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): eat, drink, sleep, or play. One alone says "Voy a"; two say "Vamos a ... juntos". The frog leads them to the food, the jugs, the hammock or the grass. Nobody carries anything, and nobody rubs his stomach or yawns.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the food, the jugs, the hammock, the grass; tests the plan after voy a or vamos a).** NARR: "El piragüero dice: Voy a dormir." (Or, drawn: "Voy a comer." "Voy a tomar jugo." Or, two together: "Vamos a jugar juntos." "Vamos a comer juntos.")
   - Right: the frog leads him there; he settles. Abuela lights a lantern. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog leads him to another place; he stands there and looks about. NARR: "El piragüero dice: No, Coquí. Voy a dormir." Luis comes and takes him across. No lantern. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; other guests, another plan).** NARR: "El señor y la señora dicen: Vamos a comer juntos." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Night. By the gate:
   - Three lanterns lit. Stars and a heart.
   - Two lit.
   - One lit.
9. Abuela puts down her match. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes hambre?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre, sí tengo hambre, sí)]: "¡Sí, tengo hambre!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a comer juntos." They go to the table. NARR: "En la fiesta, la gente come, toma y juega. Juntos."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** voy a 3 (steps 1, 2, 3) and va a three times in the asks; comer, tomar and dormir once each with their pictures before the game (a known verb in a new form: the reviews must say if once is enough); jugar 2 (step 4). Not deciding: vamos a 2 (step 4) before the game, and once more in the ending (step 9); juntos 2 before the game (step 4), and twice in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the verb of the plan (come, toma, duerme or juega: the words he has).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): voy; tengo hambre.

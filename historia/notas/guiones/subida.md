# El camino al fuerte: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `subida`). Stories 108 to 110. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 107.

Scene words (page 02): fuerte, muralla, bandera, fiesta, invita, trae, voy a (comer, tomar, dormir), vamos a (jugar, bailar), ¿quieres venir?, ¿cuándo?, ¿quién?, juntos.
How they are shared out: 108 fuerte, muralla, bandera, fiesta, ¿quieres venir?; 109 invita, trae, ¿quién?, ¿cuándo?; 110 voy a, vamos a, juntos.

The picture: the wide lawn in front of the old fort, late in the afternoon. One view. At the back, across the whole picture, the fort's wall (the muralla), with battlements, the fort's gate (an arch) in its middle, an iron lamp at each side of the gate, and a small sentry box on its top at the right; over the gate, on a pole, a flag. Beyond the wall, over its top, the sea and the sky. A path of pale stone runs from the picture's foot up to the gate. At the left of the path, a tree with a flat stone beside it; at the right, a party table, a hammock between two posts, and open grass. No roof.

People: the family (abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen), and the people of every place: don Pepe (the colmado), the maestra, the piragüero, a boy with his dog, a girl, the señora, the joven.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign. While he waits, something always moves: the flag, the tree's leaves, the sea.

## Story 108: La fiesta

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen's basket: three paper lanterns for tonight's party. She lifts one, and it glows a little.
- **At stake:** the three lanterns. A lantern hung on its hook is safe. One held up at the wrong place is in the wind: a gust takes it out of the frog's hands, over the wall.
- **New words:** fuerte, muralla, bandera, fiesta, ¿quieres venir?
- **Back again:** puerta, luz, hoy, hay, viento, grande, viejo, mira, quiero, aquí, allá, arriba, noche, bonito, me gusta, sí, no, gracias, hola.

**Start:** late afternoon; a wind. The family comes up the path: Luis with a roll of paper under his arm, Carmen with her basket, the frog in front. Before them, the wall, the gate, the flag.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí: el fuerte. Es grande y es viejo." Ask (signed, the gate): "¿Dónde está la puerta del fuerte?" He touches it: the frog hops up the path to the gate. NARR: "Luis dice: Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. ¿Quieres venir?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!"
2. Carmen points along the wall. NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta es la muralla del fuerte." Ask (signed, the wall, away from the gate): "¿Dónde está la muralla?" He touches it: the frog hops onto it and walks along its top. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Cuidado, Coquí! La muralla es alta."
3. The flag snaps in the wind. NARR: "Luis dice: Y allá arriba está la bandera." Ask (signed, the flag): "¿Dónde está la bandera?" He touches it: it waves. NARR: "Carmen dice: Hay viento. Mira la bandera."
4. A boy comes by with his dog. Luis unrolls his paper: a drawing of the fort with lanterns. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola! Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. ¿Quieres venir?" The boy nods hard; the dog barks. Carmen lifts a lantern out of her basket; it glows a little. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para la fiesta: una luz en la puerta, una en la muralla y una en la bandera." As she names each place, its hook shines for a moment.

**The turn**

5. Luis goes off down the path with his paper; he stops a girl. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola! Hay una fiesta. ¿Quieres venir?" Carmen sits down by the tree with her basket; the frog hops down beside her. She takes out the first lantern; the wind pulls at it. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen, untangling the lanterns' strings with her eyes on her hands, says where the next lantern goes: one round the wall, one round the flag's pole (which comes first is drawn). The gate has a hook too, and can be touched in both rounds; it is never asked for. The frog carries the lantern there. The frog starts each round beside Carmen at the tree.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the wall, the flag, the gate; tests muralla against bandera).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta luz va en la muralla." (Or, drawn: "Esta luz va en la bandera.")
   - Right: the frog hangs it on its hook there; it swings and glows. He comes back to Carmen. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to another place and holds it up. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. En la muralla." A gust: the lantern goes out of his hands, and sails over the wall. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog hops there, with nothing to hang, and comes back to Carmen.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the other place).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta luz va en la bandera." (The one that round A did not ask for.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Evening comes. Carmen hangs the last lantern on the gate herself. The lanterns are lit, each where it hangs.
   - Three: on the wall, on the flag's pole, on the gate. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: one of the two places is dark.
   - One: on the gate.
9. Luis comes back up the path, and people behind him. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí. ¿Te gusta?" COQUÍ [TURN: bonito (bonito, qué bonito, sí bonito)]: "¡Qué bonito!" NARR: "Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. Hay luz en la muralla."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** muralla 4 (steps 2, 4), bandera 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: fuerte 6 (steps 1, 2, 4); fiesta 4 (steps 1, 4, 5); ¿quieres venir? 3 (steps 1, 4, 5).
**A miss is filed under:** the place that was said (muralla or bandera).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round: the gate can always be touched, and round A's place can be touched again.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): quiero; bonito.

## Story 109: ¿De quién? ¿Cuándo?

- **Goal he can see:** on the path, three people come up, one after another. Luis's paper hangs on the tree: the fort with lanterns, and under it two round pictures: abuela's face, and a moon over a clock at seven.
- **At stake:** the guests (3). Someone who gets the wrong answer to his question shrugs, and walks on.
- **New words:** invita, trae, ¿quién?, ¿cuándo?
- **Back again:** fiesta, fuerte, abuela, mi, tu, hoy, noche, a las siete, jugo, hielo, voy, yo, ¿cómo estás?, bien, gracias, hola, sí, no.

**Start:** late afternoon. The tree by the path. Luis hangs his paper on it: under the fort and its lanterns, two round pictures: at the left abuela's face, at the right a moon over a clock at seven. The frog on the flat stone beside the tree. Abuela, with a list, on the path.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hoy hay una fiesta en el fuerte. Yo invito a todos." **(invito: a form of invita; todos: heard)** She goes on up the path to the gate with her list. Luis taps the paper. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí. ¿De quién es la fiesta? De abuela." (signed, abuela's picture on the paper.) He touches it: it shines. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y cuándo es la fiesta? Hoy, a las siete." (signed, the moon over the clock.) He touches it: it shines.
2. Don Pepe comes up the path, and stops at the paper. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Hola, Luis! ¿Una fiesta? ¿De quién es?" (signed, abuela's picture.) He touches it: it shines; at the gate, abuela waves. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Tu abuela invita! ¿Y cuándo es?" (signed, the moon over the clock.) He touches it: it shines. NARR: "Luis dice: Hoy, a las siete de la noche. Abuela invita a todos."
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Voy. Yo traigo jugo." **(traigo: a form of trae)** A small jug shows on the paper's foot; Luis writes. He goes on up. The piragüero comes by with his cart. NARR: "Luis dice: Don Pepe trae jugo. ¿Y quién trae hielo?" The piragüero lifts his hand. NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¡Yo! ¿Cuándo?" NARR: "Luis dice: Hoy, a las siete." A small block of ice shows on the paper's foot. NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien, muy bien, bien gracias)]: "¡Bien, gracias!"

**The turn**

4. Luis runs up to the fort with his list. The frog stays on his stone by the paper. Up the path come three people, one after another, a little apart. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A passer-by (drawn) stops, looks at the paper, and asks one of two questions (drawn, each round on its own): whose party it is, or when it is. The two questions differ only in their first words. The frog touches one of the paper's two round pictures: abuela's face, or the moon over the clock.

5. **Decision A, touch a picture (two; tests ¿quién? against ¿cuándo?).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Una fiesta? ¿Cuándo es?" (Or, drawn: "¿Una fiesta? ¿De quién es?")
   - Right: the picture shines. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Ah! Gracias. Voy." She goes up the path to the fort. A star.
   - Wrong: the other picture shines; she frowns at it. NARR: "La señora dice: No... ¿Cuándo?" She shrugs, and walks on across the lawn. A cross. The sign is on the right picture; he touches it: it shines, too late.
6. **Decision B, touch a picture (the same game; another passer-by).** NARR: "El joven dice: ¿Una fiesta? ¿De quién es?" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The third passer-by reads the paper for himself, and goes up. At the fort's gate:
   - Three guests wait with abuela. Stars and a heart.
   - Two guests.
   - One guest.
8. The piragüero pushes his cart up to the gate. NARR: "El piragüero dice: Aquí está el hielo. Coquí, ¿quieres una piragua?" COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, sí gracias)]: "¡Sí, gracias!" He gets a small one. NARR: "Abuela invita. La gente trae jugo y hielo. Hoy, a las siete, hay fiesta."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿quién? 3 (steps 1, 2, 3), ¿cuándo? 4 (steps 1, 2, 3). Not deciding: invita 2 and invito once before the game (steps 1, 2), and once in the ending; trae 2 and traigo once (step 3), and once in the ending.
**Forms:** invito with invita; traigo with trae.
**A miss is filed under:** the question that was asked (¿quién? or ¿cuándo?).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): bien; gracias.

## Story 110: Vamos a jugar

- **Goal he can see:** by the fort's gate, three paper lanterns, not lit, and abuela with a long match. She holds up three fingers, and points down the path: three more guests are coming.
- **At stake:** the lanterns lit (up to 3): one for each guest who finds his place. A guest who is led to the wrong place stands there, lost; Luis has to fetch him, and no lantern is lit for him.
- **New words:** voy a, vamos a, juntos.
- **Back again:** come (comer), toma (tomar), duerme (dormir), juega (jugar), fiesta, mesa, jugo, bola, tengo hambre, tengo sed, tiene sueño, ¿algo más?, también, tres, yo, tú, sí, no, gracias.

**Start:** evening; the lamps at the gate are lit. On the lawn: the party table with food at one end and jugs at the other, a hammock between two posts, open grass with a ball. The frog at the gate, with abuela. Guests come in at the gate.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Don Pepe comes in, rubbing his stomach. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Tengo hambre! Voy a comer." (signed, the table's end with the food.) He touches it: the frog leads him there. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡A comer!" He eats.
2. The maestra, fanning herself. NARR: "La maestra dice: ¡Tengo sed! Voy a tomar algo." (signed, the table's end with the jugs.) He touches it: the frog leads her there. NARR: "La maestra dice: ¡A tomar jugo!" She pours a glass.
3. Carmen yawns. NARR: "Carmen dice: Tengo sueño. Voy a dormir." (signed, the hammock.) He touches it: she climbs into the hammock. NARR: "Carmen dice: A dormir..."
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Don Pepe va a comer, la maestra va a tomar algo y Carmen va a dormir." **(va a: a form of voy a)** As she names each, he lifts a hand from his place. Luis runs in with the ball. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Carmen! ¡Vamos a jugar!" Carmen jumps out of the hammock. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! Vamos a jugar juntos. Coquí, ¿tú también?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (yo también, también)]: "¡Yo también!" They run onto the grass; the ball goes from one to the other, and to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Los tres juntos!"

**The turn**

5. Don Pepe and the maestra carry their plate and their glass away to the tree. Luis and Carmen run off after the ball, out of the picture. The food, the jugs, the hammock and the grass are empty. Abuela stands by the three lanterns with her long match. The frog goes back to the gate. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn) comes in at the gate and says what he is going to do (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): eat, drink, or sleep. The frog leads him to the food, the jugs or the hammock; the grass can be touched too. Nobody carries anything, and nobody rubs his stomach, fans himself or yawns. What decides is the plan after "voy a": the plain form of a verb he has, heard here for the first time.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the food, the jugs, the hammock, the grass; tests the plan after voy a).** NARR: "El piragüero dice: Voy a dormir." (Or, drawn: "Voy a comer." "Voy a tomar algo.")
   - Right: the frog leads him there; he settles. Abuela lights a lantern. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog leads him to another place; he stands there and looks about. NARR: "El piragüero dice: No, Coquí. Voy a dormir." Luis runs in, and takes him across by the hand. No lantern. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; another guest, another plan).** NARR: "La señora dice: Voy a comer." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Night. The third guest finds his own place; abuela lights a lantern for him. By the gate:
   - Three lanterns lit. Stars and a heart.
   - Two lit.
   - One lit.
9. Abuela puts down her match. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Tienes hambre o tienes sed?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre, hambre)]: "¡Tengo hambre!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Vamos a comer juntos!" They go to the table. NARR: "En la fiesta, la gente come, toma jugo y juega. Juntos."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** voy a 3 (steps 1, 2, 3) and va a three times (step 4); comer 3, tomar 3, dormir 3 (each: its guest's line with the sign, his "¡A ...!" at the place, abuela's line with his lifted hand). Not deciding: vamos a 2 (step 4) before the game and once in the ending; juntos 2 (step 4) before the game and twice in the ending; jugar 2 (step 4).
**Forms:** va a with voy a; comer with come, tomar with toma, dormir with duerme, jugar with juega.
**A miss is filed under:** voy a (the plan after it is what he missed).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four in round A; one in three by the picture in round B (round B's plan is never round A's).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): también; tengo hambre.

## Reviews (draft 1 to draft 2)

A beginner's reviewer and a Puerto Rican Spanish reviewer read draft 1; each had only the script and the owner's rules.

- 108: the lanterns have a name he knows ("una luz", "Esta luz va en..."). The game asks the wall and the flag, in a drawn order; the gate is only a third thing to touch, and Carmen hangs the last lantern there herself, so the last picture shows the lanterns where they were hung. A wrong lantern is lost at the wrong place, held up in the wind, not on the way back. The frog starts each round beside Carmen. Luis asks two children, so "¿Quieres venir?" is right. The last turn is not an echo. "Hay luz en la muralla", not "tienen luz" (which is electricity).
- 109: the two questions differ only in their first words ("¿De quién es?", which is what people ask, against "¿Cuándo es?"), so "invita" cannot give the answer away. Abuela is at the gate, not beside her picture. "Yo invito a todos"; "tu abuela". Three hearings of each question without a line said twice. The turns are a greeting and a thank-you, not a quiz.
- 110: nothing is lit in the opening; the lanterns are for the three guests who come after. Carmen sleeps and then plays, so the hammock is free; every place is empty at the turn. "Voy a tomar algo", so "jugo" does not give the jugs away. Each plain form is heard three times with its place. The game has three plans and four places. "Tengo sueño. Voy a dormir." "¡Los tres juntos!" "Toma jugo" in the last line (bare "toma" is drinking). The turns are not echoes.
- Not taken: making voy a against vamos a decide by touch (it needs a contrived picture; the plan after the frame decides, and a miss is filed under voy a).

# La cuenta: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `cuenta`). Stories 93 to 95. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 92.

Scene words (page 02): cuenta, tarjeta, efectivo, propina, total, postre, flan, mantecado, nada más, estoy lleno, hasta luego, buenas noches (the plan had helado and satisfecho: see the reviews).
How they are shared out: 93 postre, flan, mantecado, nada más; 94 cuenta, total, tarjeta, efectivo; 95 propina, estoy lleno, hasta luego, buenas noches.

The picture: the front of the fonda, late at night. One view. Along the back wall, a counter: at its left a glass case with desserts; in its middle the register (the caja), a card machine, a spike with paper slips, a glass jar, a bowl of mints; at the right of the counter, the open door, with the night street beyond it: three street lamps in a row going away, and far off the lights of a guagua. In front of the counter, at the left, the family's table (they sit in a row along its far side); the rest of the floor is free. The dessert cart stands by the table.

People: the mesera, the mesero, the cocinero; abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; guests (the señor, the señora, the joven, the mujer).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game the guest who speaks faces the frog, stands as near to one choice as to the other, and looks at none of them.

## Story 93: El postre

- **Goal he can see:** on the cart's top, beside the frog, a small empty cup with a spoon. The mesera fills it for her helper: three scoops of ice cream. For afterwards.
- **At stake:** the three scoops. The night is warm: each mix-up takes time, and a scoop melts away.
- **New words:** postre, flan, mantecado, nada más.
- **Back again:** delicioso, rico, para mí, lleno, mesera, quiere, también, hay, toca, come, después, ¿algo más?, por favor, gracias, no.

**Start:** late evening. The family at its table; the plates are empty. The mesera rolls up the dessert cart: small caramel flans on plates, a tub of ice cream with a scoop, cups; a domed cover hangs at the cart's side; on the cart's top, a small empty cup with a spoon. The frog hops onto the cart, beside the small cup.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Postre? Hay flan y hay mantecado." Ask (signed, a flan): "¿Dónde está el flan?" He touches it: it wobbles. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Postre! ¡Mantecado, por favor!" Ask (signed, the tub): "Luis quiere mantecado. ¿Dónde está?" He touches it: the frog scoops a ball into a cup; it goes to Luis.
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: De postre, flan, por favor." The frog pushes a flan to her. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y para mí, flan también." Another flan goes across.
3. The mesera turns to abuelo. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Y para usted? ¿Flan o mantecado?" **(usted: heard)** Abuelo pats his belly. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para mí, nada más. Estoy lleno." **(estoy lleno: heard; a phrase of story 95)** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Nada más?" Ask (signed, the cover): "Abuelo no quiere nada más. ¿Qué toca Coquí?" He touches it: the frog swings the cover down over the desserts.
4. The cover goes up again. NARR: "La mesera dice: Mantecado para Luis y flan para Carmen. ¿Algo más?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Nada más, gracias." COQUÍ [TURN: para mí (y para mí, para mí)]: "¿Y para mí?" The mesera laughs. She fills the small cup: one scoop, two, three. NARR: "La mesera dice: Mantecado para ti, Coquí. Después."

**The turn**

5. The mesera pushes the cart, with the frog on it, to the next table. The three scoops shine; a first drop runs down the cup. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest at the next table (drawn) says one of three things (drawn): flan, ice cream, or nothing more. Every line begins "Para mí" and ends there. The frog pushes a flan across, scoops a cup of ice cream, or swings the cover down.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the flans, the tub, the cover; tests flan, mantecado and nada más).** NARR: "El señor dice: Para mí, mantecado." (Or, drawn: "Para mí, flan." Or: "Para mí, nada más.")
   - Right: the frog does it. A nod (after "nada más": he pats his belly). A star.
   - Wrong: the frog does another thing; the guest holds up a hand. NARR: "El señor dice: No, Coquí. Mantecado." (or "Flan.", or "Nada más.") While the mesera puts the cart right again, a scoop in the frog's cup melts away. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "La mujer dice: Para mí, nada más." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The mesera hands the frog his cup.
   - Three scoops. Stars and a heart.
   - Two scoops, in a little pool.
   - One scoop, in a big pool; the frog drinks the pool too.
9. The frog takes a spoonful. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Cómo está el mantecado, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: delicioso (delicioso, está delicioso)]: "¡Delicioso!" NARR: "Coquí come mantecado. ¡Qué rico!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** flan 6 (steps 1 to 4), mantecado 6 (steps 1, 3, 4), nada más 4 (steps 3, 4; three speakers). Not deciding: postre 3 (steps 1, 2).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (flan, mantecado or nada más).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): para mí; delicioso.

## Story 94: La cuenta, por favor

- **Goal he can see:** the glass jar beside the register. Abuelo, content, drops a coin in it. The mesera shakes the jar, clink, and points at the guests who wait to pay: each has a coin in his fingers.
- **At stake:** the guests' coins (the jar can end with 3). A guest who is served well drops his coin in; one who gets a muddle puts it back in his pocket.
- **New words:** cuenta, total, tarjeta, efectivo.
- **Back again:** nada más, ¿algo más?, ¿cuánto es?, peso, cambio, caja, paga, cierra, veinte, treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, mesera, aquí tiene, toma, toca, con, todo, mira, por favor, gracias, no.

**Start:** late evening. The mesera behind the register; the frog on the counter beside the jar, which is empty. On the counter: the card machine, the spike with slips, the bowl of mints. The family in front of the counter; abuelo has his wallet in his hand, and no glasses on.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuelo dice: La cuenta, por favor." The mesera pulls a slip off the spike. NARR: "La mesera dice: La cuenta. Aquí tiene." Ask (signed, the slip): "¿Dónde está la cuenta de abuelo?" He touches it: the frog carries the slip to abuelo.
2. Abuelo holds the slip far off, and near; he cannot read it. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Cuánto es?" NARR: "La mesera dice: El total son cuarenta pesos." The slip grows for a moment: at its foot, a big 40. NARR: "Abuela dice: Cuarenta pesos. Mira, Coquí: aquí está el total." Ask (signed, the number at the slip's foot): "¿Dónde está el total en la cuenta?" He touches it: the 40 shines.
3. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Tarjeta o efectivo?" Abuelo opens his wallet: a card, and bills. He takes out the card. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Tarjeta." Ask (signed, the card machine): "Abuelo paga con tarjeta. ¿Qué toca Coquí?" He touches it: the frog pushes the machine to abuelo; a beep. NARR: "La mesera dice: Con tarjeta. Gracias." Abuelo drops a coin in the jar: clink.
4. The señor steps up, with bills in his hand. NARR: "La mesera dice: El total son treinta pesos. ¿Tarjeta o efectivo?" NARR: "El señor dice: Cash." **(cash: heard; what most people say)** NARR: "La mesera dice: En efectivo. Gracias." Ask (signed, the register's drawer): "El señor paga en efectivo. ¿Qué toca Coquí?" He touches it: the drawer springs open; the frog puts the bills in. NARR: "La mesera dice: Aquí tiene el cambio."
5. The mesera holds out the bowl of mints to the frog. He takes one. NARR: "La mesera dice: Toma, Coquí. ¿Algo más?" COQUÍ [TURN: nada más (nada más, nada más gracias)]: "Nada más, gracias."

**The turn**

6. More guests come up to pay, one behind another, each with a coin in his fingers. The mesera shakes the jar: abuelo's coin clinks. She points at the guests' coins, and at the jar. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn) steps up and asks for the bill. The mesera reads the total from the register, which is turned away from us (a number drawn from veinte to noventa), and asks how he pays. The guest, his hands in his pockets, answers (drawn). The frog pushes the card machine to him, or springs the register's drawer.

7. **Decision A, touch a thing (the card machine, the drawer; tests tarjeta against efectivo).** NARR: "La señora dice: La cuenta, por favor." NARR: "La mesera dice: El total son sesenta pesos. ¿Tarjeta o efectivo?" NARR: "La señora dice: Efectivo, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Con tarjeta, por favor.")
   - Right: for "efectivo" the drawer springs open and she lays down her bills; for "tarjeta" she takes the machine: a beep. She drops her coin in the jar: clink. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the machine at a guest who holds out bills (or springs the drawer at a guest who holds out a card). NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Efectivo." (or "Tarjeta.") She pays, and puts her coin back in her pocket. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another guest, another total).** NARR: "El joven dice: La cuenta, por favor." NARR: "La mesera dice: El total son veinte pesos. ¿Tarjeta o efectivo?" NARR: "El joven dice: Con tarjeta, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

9. The last guest goes. The mesera tips the jar out on the counter, counts the coins, and slides them across to the frog.
   - Three coins. Stars and a heart.
   - Two coins.
   - One coin: abuelo's.
10. NARR: "La mesera dice: Esto es para ti, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, muchas gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" The mesera pushes the drawer shut. NARR: "Todo está en la caja. La mesera cierra la caja."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tarjeta 5 (steps 3, 4), efectivo 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: cuenta 4 (steps 1, 2), total 4 (steps 2, 4); both again in each round.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (tarjeta or efectivo).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): nada más; gracias.

## Story 95: Buenas noches

- **Goal he can see:** through the open door, the street: three lamps in a row, and far away, under the last one, the lights of the guagua: the last one tonight. Abuela points at it, and at the stop outside the door.
- **At stake:** the time before the guagua comes (three lamps). Each mix-up takes time: the guagua's lights come one lamp nearer.
- **New words:** propina, estoy lleno, hasta luego, buenas noches.
- **Back again:** cuenta, caja, peso, mesero, mesera, cocinero, flan, delicioso, para, otro, guagua, vamos, también, todo, bien, casa, adiós, esto, aquí, hay, más, ¿algo más?, pone (pones), por favor, gracias, no.

**Start:** late night. The frog on the counter, beside the register and the jar. The family at its table; the mesero beside it. The mesera stacks plates at another table; the cocinero stands in the kitchen's door. The door to the street is open: three lamps, and nothing under them yet.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El mesero dice: Buenas noches. ¿Algo más?" Abuelo looks at the frog, and nods. COQUÍ [TURN: la cuenta por favor (la cuenta por favor, la cuenta)]: "La cuenta, por favor." The mesero brings the slip. Abuelo lays bills on the counter. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Esto es para la cuenta." Ask (signed, the register's drawer): "¿Dónde pones los pesos de la cuenta?" He touches it: the drawer springs open; the frog puts the bills in.
2. Abuelo takes two more bills out of his pocket, folded. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y esto es para la propina." Ask (signed, the jar): "¿Dónde pones la propina?" He touches it: the frog drops them in the jar. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¡Gracias por la propina!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Y otra propina, para el cocinero. ¡El flan está delicioso!" Her bills go into the jar too; the cocinero bows in the kitchen's door.
3. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿Todo bien?" Abuelo pats his belly. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Todo bien. Estoy lleno." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Yo también estoy lleno!" Carmen looks at the dessert case. NARR: "Carmen dice: Yo no estoy llena. ¿Hay más flan?" They laugh.
4. The family goes to the door. NARR: "La mesera dice: Buenas noches. ¡Hasta luego!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Buenas noches. Hasta luego." NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Hasta luego, Luis! ¡Hasta luego, Carmen!" Abuela looks out at the street: far off, under the last lamp, the guagua's lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡La guagua! Vamos, Coquí."

**The turn**

5. The frog turns to go; but the last guests come up to the counter, with folded bills in their hands. The mesera has a chair in each hand: she is putting the chairs up on the tables. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Coquí, la caja, por favor!" The family waits in the doorway; abuela looks at the street. The guagua's lights stand under the far lamp. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn) holds out folded bills, the same every time, and says what they are for (drawn): the bill, or the tip. The frog puts them in the register's drawer, or in the jar.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the drawer, the jar; tests propina against cuenta).** NARR: "El señor dice: Esto es para la propina." (Or, drawn: "Esto es para la cuenta.")
   - Right: the bills go in. NARR: "El señor dice: Gracias. Hasta luego." He goes out past the family. A star.
   - Wrong: the bills go in the wrong place. NARR: "El señor dice: No, Coquí. Para la propina." (or "Para la cuenta.") The frog fishes them out again and puts them right. In the door, the guagua's lights have come one lamp nearer. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "La mujer dice: Esto es para la cuenta." (Drawn.) Right: NARR: "La mujer dice: Gracias. Buenas noches." Wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog hops down and across to the door. The mesera, the mesero and the cocinero wave. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Buenas noches, Coquí! ¡Hasta luego!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós!"
9. Outside, the family and the frog go to the stop.
   - The guagua is still far: they walk; the guagua comes; a seat for each of them. Stars and a heart.
   - It is at the middle lamp: they walk fast, and get on.
   - It is at the stop: they run; abuelo holds his hat; they jump on, and the door shuts behind the frog.
   NARR: "La familia va para casa. Buenas noches, fonda."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** propina 4 (step 2). Not deciding: estoy lleno 3 with llena (step 3), hasta luego 3 lines (step 4), buenas noches 3 (steps 1, 4).
**Forms:** llena counts with lleno.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (propina or cuenta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the bills look the same, and both lines begin "Esto es para la".
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): la cuenta por favor; adiós.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- Words. After eating, the island says "estoy lleno"; "satisfecho" is formal, and no child says it. Ice cream is "mantecado" in everyday speech ("helado" is on menus and understood). Both replace the plan's words, and page 02's list follows. With an amount it is "el total son cuarenta pesos". "Va para casa", not "a casa". A mesero is not surprised by a tip: "¡Gracias por la propina!". "Me gusta el flan" of a flan just eaten: "¡El flan está delicioso!".
- 93: "De postre, flan, por favor" against "Nada más, gracias" could be told apart by the last word: every line is "Para mí, ..." and stops. "Nada más" was heard from one mouth in one burst: three speakers now. The goal's cup is on the cart from the start. "El señor" meant abuelo in the opening and a guest in the game: the mesera says "¿Y para usted?". The asks are whole sentences.
- 94: the first round was won on number words, with the look-alike "setenta" as a trap, and the story's new words were not needed: both rounds are tarjeta against efectivo, and the total is heard in every round with a number that costs nothing. The jar was said to hold three coins and was empty, and guests took back coins they had never put in: the jar starts with abuelo's coin, and each guest shows his own coin, which goes in or back in his pocket. Card and cash were the same beat twice at full length: the second is shorter. All three endings have the same beats. The last line was a list: the mesera shuts the drawer.
- 95: no new word decided anything (the game turned on mesera, mesero and cocinero, and "la" picked out the only woman): the game is the tip against the bill, the jar against the drawer. The opening's first turn had nothing said before it: the mesero asks "¿Algo más?". Three "satisfecho" lines in a row with one belly pat: abuelo, Luis, and Carmen who is not full. "Buenas noches" depended on the draw to reach three hearings: it is in fixed lines. The chairs were already up and being put up: the mesera puts them up during the game, which is also why she asks the frog to take the money, and why the family has to wait.

Left, and why:
- Efectivo stays as the word he learns. The reviewer's "most used" is "cash" (he was about seven in ten sure): the learner has that word already, a game of "cash" against "tarjeta" would test nothing, and "efectivo" is what cashiers ask and signs say. He hears a guest answer "Cash" and the mesera say "En efectivo" in the same breath. This is the one place in La fonda where the plan's word was kept over the most used one: it goes to the owner in one line.
- Cuenta and total decide nothing in 94 (four hearings each); cuenta decides in 95.
- Three things to choose from in 93: as in La comida.
- "¿ATH o crédito?" is what a real register asks: too much for A1.

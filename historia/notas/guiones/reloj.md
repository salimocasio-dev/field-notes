# El reloj: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `reloj`). Stories 63 to 65. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 62.

Scene words (page 02): reloj, hora, ¿qué hora es?, es la una, son las, y media, en punto, minuto, the tens from 30 to 100.
How the tens are shared out: treinta in story 64 (with "y media"); cuarenta to cien in story 65.

The picture: the foot of the square's clock tower. One view. The tower's big round clock face fills the upper middle: twelve numerals, a short hand and a long hand (the hands are sprites). Under the face a small balcony with a rail, where somebody can stand and reach the hands, and a ladder up to it. Beside the face a little shuttered door for the tower's three wooden figures (story 64). At the tower's foot, at the right, the quiosco's counter with its fryer; at the left, tables (story 65: the bingo). Pigeons.

New person: don Tito, the clock man (old, a waistcoat, a pocket watch on a chain). The others: the vendedor of the quiosco (an apron, a paper hat), the señora who runs the bingo, Luis, Carmen, abuela, the señor.

Time cards (story 63): two cards over the frog, each a small clock face with its hands; touching one makes the frog set the tower's clock so.
Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 63: ¿Qué hora es?

- **Goal he can see:** on the quiosco's counter, a plate of three raw pastelillos beside the fryer. The vendedor looks up at the tower's clock, which has stopped, and scratches his head: he fries by the bell.
- **At stake:** the pastelillos (3). The vendedor drops one into the oil and waits for the bell. The clock set to a wrong hour: the bell rings the wrong number, he counts it on his fingers, shakes his head and goes on waiting; the pastelillo comes out black.
- **New words:** reloj, hora, ¿qué hora es?, es la una, son las.
- **Back again:** uno to doce, me llamo, mucho gusto, ¿cómo te llamas?, camina, grande, viejo, come, sí, gracias, mira, ahora, ya, otra vez, quieres.

**Start:** midday. The clock's hands hang straight down. Don Tito on the balcony with an oil can; Luis, Carmen and the frog at the tower's foot; the vendedor at his fryer, the fire out.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira el reloj de la plaza. Es un reloj grande y viejo. Y no camina." Ask (signed, the clock face): "¿Dónde está el reloj?" He touches it: the long hand swings and hangs again. Don Tito looks down. NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?" COQUÍ [TURN: me llamo (me llamo)]: "Me llamo Coquí." NARR: "Don Tito dice: Mucho gusto, Coquí. Yo soy Tito."
2. Don Tito pulls out his pocket watch. NARR: "Luis dice: Don Tito, ¿qué hora es?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Es la una." Two time cards appear: one o'clock, and four o'clock. Ask (signed, the one o'clock card): "¿En cuál reloj es la una?" He touches it: the frog hops up the ladder and pushes the short hand to the 1. The bell rings once. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Es la una!"
3. The frog comes down; time goes by (the light moves a little). NARR: "Carmen dice: Don Tito, ¿y ahora qué hora es?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las dos." Cards: two o'clock, and one o'clock. Ask (signed, the two o'clock card): "¿En cuál reloj son las dos?" He touches it: the hand goes to the 2; the bell rings twice.
4. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Qué hora es? ¿Son las tres?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Sí, ya son las tres." Cards: three, and nine. Ask (signed, the three o'clock card): "¿Y en cuál reloj son las tres?" He touches it: three rings. The vendedor counts three on his fingers, ties his apron and lights the fire under his fryer.

**The turn**

5. The vendedor shows the frog the plate of three raw pastelillos, and points at the clock. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Con el reloj, los pastelillos salen bien." **(pastelillos, salen: heard; the picture carries them)** Don Tito hands the frog the oil can and sits down on the balcony's step with his watch. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Tito reads the hour from his watch: one of four to twelve, drawn, each round on its own (round B's is later than round A's). The vendedor drops a pastelillo into the oil. Two time cards: that hour, and another, drawn (never the pairs dos and doce, seis and siete, once and doce; the other card is as often the earlier hour as the later one). Which card is at the left is drawn.

6. **Decision A, touch a card (two cards; tests the number in "son las").** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Don Tito, ¿qué hora es?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las cinco." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog sets the short hand; the bell rings that many times; the vendedor counts them on his fingers, nods, and lifts out a golden pastelillo. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog sets the other hour; the bell rings that many times. The vendedor counts, shakes his head and goes on waiting, looking up at don Tito. NARR: "Don Tito dice: No, Coquí. Son las cinco." Smoke rises from the fryer: a black pastelillo. A cross. The sign is on the right card; he touches it: the hand, the bell.
7. **Decision B, touch a card (the same game; a later hour).** NARR: "Don Tito dice: Ahora son las ocho." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Don Tito comes down the ladder and winds the clock with a big key; the long hand starts to move by itself. NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¡Ya! El reloj camina." The bell rings; the vendedor fries the last pastelillo, golden, and brings the plate.
   - Three golden: one for Luis, one for Carmen, one for the frog, which is as big as his head. Stars and a heart.
   - Two golden, one black: Luis and Carmen take one each and each break off a piece for the frog; the black one goes to a pigeon, which looks at it and walks away.
   - One golden, two black: the golden one is cut in three; two pigeons walk away.
9. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Quieres un pastelillo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si, sí gracias)]: "¡Sí, gracias!" NARR: "El reloj de la plaza camina otra vez."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** son las 5 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: reloj 6 before the game; ¿qué hora es? 3 (steps 2, 3, 4); hora counts with it; es la una 3 (step 2).
**A miss is filed under:** the number that was said. "Son las" is the same on both cards, so it cannot be what he missed.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. The numbers one to twelve are El colmado's words; what is new is hearing them as an hour and finding it on a clock.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): me llamo; sí.

## Story 64: Y media

- **Goal he can see:** the little shuttered door beside the clock face, with three painted figures on its shutters: a rooster, a man with a guitar, a small frog. Don Tito taps it: they come out when the clock is right.
- **At stake:** the three figures. The long hand pushed the wrong way: the works grind, a spring jumps out, and one figure's shutter sticks.
- **New words:** y media, en punto, minuto, treinta.
- **Back again:** reloj, hora, ¿qué hora es?, son las, dos to doce, ¿cómo estás?, bien, espera, arriba, abajo, cuidado, me gusta, mira, ahora, pequeño, vamos, medio.

**Start:** afternoon. The clock has stopped at half past one: the long hand points straight down. Don Tito on the balcony with his watch and a screwdriver; the frog at the tower's foot with Luis and Carmen.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien, bien gracias)]: "¡Bien, gracias!" The frog hops up the ladder and onto the long hand. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Mira. Son las dos en punto." The 12 at the top and the 6 at the bottom twinkle. Ask (signed, the top of the face): "¿Arriba o abajo?" He touches it: the long hand swings up with the frog on it and stands straight; the bell rings once, softly.
2. Don Tito turns a wheel, counting on his watch. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Un minuto, dos minutos... ¡treinta minutos! Ahora son las dos y media." Ask (signed, the bottom of the face): "¿Y ahora? ¿Arriba o abajo?" He touches it: the long hand swings down with the frog; the short hand creeps half way to the 3.
3. Don Tito turns the wheel again; the hand goes up by itself, the frog riding. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Treinta minutos más... Son las tres en punto." NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Las tres en punto?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Sí, Luis. En punto." The wheel again: the hand goes down. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Y en treinta minutos, las tres y media." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Las tres y media! ¡Luis, vamos!" Luis and Carmen run off across the square.

**The turn**

4. Don Tito opens a flap in the tower: wheels and a big spring. He taps the little door of the figures. The long hand slips to the side and points at the 3; the frog holds on. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Espera, Coquí. Ahora, con cuidado." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Tito reads his watch: an hour from four to eleven, and either "en punto" or "y media", drawn, each round on its own. The frog pushes the long hand to the top or to the bottom. Before round B don Tito turns the wheel and the hand goes back to the 3.

5. **Decision A, touch a place (the top of the face, the bottom; tests the words said).** NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las cinco y media." (Or, drawn: "Don Tito dice: Son las cinco en punto.")
   - Right: the long hand swings there; the works tick; the short hand takes its place. A star.
   - Wrong: the long hand swings the other way; the works grind; a spring jumps out of the flap, and a shutter of the little door sticks, crooked. A cross. NARR: "Don Tito dice: No, Coquí. Son las cinco y media." The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the hand swings there.
6. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; a later hour).** NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las siete en punto." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Don Tito shuts the flap and winds the key. The bell rings. The little door opens.
   - No shutter stuck: the rooster, the man with the guitar and the small frog come out one after the other, turn and bow; music. Stars and a heart.
   - One stuck (the man with the guitar's): two come out; the third knocks behind its shutter.
   - Two stuck: the small frog comes out alone, and bows twice.
8. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Mira, Coquí: un coquí pequeño." COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Me gusta!" NARR: "Don Tito dice: En media hora salen otra vez." **(media hora: heard)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** en punto 4 (steps 1, 3), y media 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: minuto 5 with minutos; treinta 3 (steps 2, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the words that were said (y media or en punto).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. The hand starts each round at the side, so neither way is the one that "moves".
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): bien; me gusta.

## Story 65: ¡Bingo!

- **Goal he can see:** a shelf of three prizes beside the bingo caller: a big basket of fruit, a pineapple, a hand of guineos. Abuela looks at the basket and rubs her hands.
- **At stake:** the prizes (3, best first). A bean on the wrong number: while abuela moves it another ball drops, a player at the next table fills his card, shouts, and takes the best prize left.
- **New words:** cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, cien.
- **Back again:** diez, veinte, treinta, habichuelas, tengo, no tengo, quiero, también, abuela, señor, señora, listo, toma, gracias, ahora, tú, mira, otro, minuto.

**Start:** late afternoon. Tables at the tower's foot. Abuela at one with her card: a row of five squares with the numerals 40, 60, 80, 90, 100. The frog on the table beside a saucer of dry habichuelas. Luis at her side with his own card: 30, 50, 70. The señor at the next table and don Tito at a third, each with a card. The señora stands at a wire cage of numbered balls: she is the caller. Beside her, the shelf of prizes.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "En la plaza hay bingo." **(bingo: heard; the picture carries it)** Abuela runs a finger along her card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Tengo el cuarenta, el sesenta, el ochenta, el noventa y el cien." Luis along his. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo tengo el treinta, el cincuenta y el setenta."
2. The señora holds the balls up in a row, in order, one after another. NARR: "La señora dice: Diez, veinte, treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa... ¡y cien! ¿Empezamos?" **(empezamos: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo, listos)]: "¡Listos!" She turns the cage.
3. A ball drops; she holds it up: 90. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Noventa! Nueve, cero. ¡El noventa!" Ask (signed, the 90 on abuela's card): "¿Dónde ponemos la habichuela?" He touches it: the frog lays a bean on it.
4. The next ball: 50. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Cincuenta! Cinco, cero." Abuela shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: No tengo el cincuenta." Luis lays a bean on his own. The next: 40. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Cuarenta! Cuatro, cero. ¡El cuarenta!" Ask (signed, the 40): "¿Y ahora? ¿Dónde está el cuarenta?" He touches it: a bean.
5. One more: 70. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Setenta! Siete, cero." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo el setenta!" At the next tables the señor and don Tito each lay a bean: each has four on his card, and one square empty.

**The turn**

6. The señor grins across at abuela and holds up one finger: one more. Abuela looks at her three empty squares. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora quiero el sesenta, el ochenta y el cien." She pushes the saucer to the frog and covers her eyes. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ahora tú. ¡Yo no miro!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Three of abuela's numbers are left: 60, 80 and 100. The señora calls them in a drawn order, one in each round and the last in the ending. From here she does not hold the ball up, and she does not spell it: only the number's name is heard. The frog lays a bean on a square.

7. **Decision A, touch a square (abuela's card, three squares empty; tests the number called).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Cien! ¡El cien!" (Drawn.)
   - Right: a bean on it. Abuela peeps through her fingers and nods. A star.
   - Wrong: a bean on another square. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. ¡El cien!" While she moves it, another ball drops. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Treinta!" The señor lays his last bean and jumps up. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Bingo!" He takes the best prize left from the shelf. A cross. The sign is on the right square; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a square (two squares empty; one of the two numbers left).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Sesenta! ¡El sesenta!" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A; after a wrong move the ball that drops is the 20, and it is don Tito who shouts and takes a prize: NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Veinte!" NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¡Bingo!" (If the señor has not won in round A, it is the 30 and the señor here.)

**The ending**

9. The last of her numbers is called. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Ochenta! ¡El ochenta!" (The one that is left.) Abuela opens her eyes and lays the bean herself. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Bingo!" She goes to the shelf.
   - Three prizes left: the big basket of fruit; she can hardly lift it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the pineapple.
   - One left: the hand of guineos.
10. She breaks off a guineo (from the basket, or from the hand; with the pineapple, she sets the frog on top of it). NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, gracias abuela)]: "¡Gracias, abuela!" NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Otro bingo en diez minutos!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** sesenta 3, ochenta 3, cien 3 before the game (steps 1, 2, 6), and twice more in the call itself. Not deciding: cuarenta 5, cincuenta 4, setenta 4, noventa 4.
**A miss is filed under:** the number that was called.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A, one in two in round B. No ball is shown and no digit is spelled in the scored calls.
**Never set against each other:** sesenta and setenta (abuela's card has no 70; the 70 is called for Luis, with no touch).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 10): listo; gracias.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 63: "¿Dónde son las tres?" means "where in the world is it three"; "Es la una. ¿Cuál es?" pointed at nothing: the asks are "¿En cuál reloj es la una?", "¿En cuál reloj son las dos?". The right card in the opening was always the lower hour: now it is not, and in the game the other card is as often earlier as later. A burnt pastelillo did not follow from a wrong bell: the vendedor drops it in and waits for the right count, so a wrong one leaves it in the oil. Three pastelillos went to four eaters, and the first was fried at no cost: now two are fried in the game and the last by the mended clock, and the shares work out. The closing line named an hour that is drawn: it is "El reloj de la plaza camina otra vez." A miss was filed under "son las", which is the same on both cards: it is filed under the number. People do not give themselves "don": "Yo soy Tito."
- 64: the first touch changed nothing (the hand was already up): the clock starts stopped at half past one. The asks were bare labels and asked where a time "is": "¿Arriba o abajo?", which also uses two old words. Step 3 asked the same two touches again: it is an exchange with Luis and Carmen while don Tito turns the wheel. In the game the hand sat at the bottom, so one answer was "the hand must move": it slips to the 3 before each round (the 3, not the 9: at the 9 the frog would be behind don Tito's head). "Espera un minuto... treinta minutos" is the idiom "hold on": he counts "Un minuto, dos minutos... ¡treinta minutos!". "¿Vienes?" was not a taught form: the turn answers "¿Cómo estás?". The closing line is the one people say: "En media hora salen otra vez."
- 65: rebuilt. The ball held up showed the numeral, the spelled digits gave the answer in old words, and round B had one empty square. Now abuela's card is a row of five; three are empty when the game starts; in the scored calls no ball is shown and nothing is spelled. Sesenta and cien had been heard twice: abuela says the three she still wants. The numbers on a card take the article ("Tengo el ochenta"). The turn "¡Listos!" echoed "¿Listos?": she asks "¿Empezamos?". "Toma, Coquí. ¿Cuál quieres?" made no sense with one prize in her hands: she gives, he thanks. The woman in the green dress came from nowhere: the two who can win are the señor and don Tito, each seen one short of a full card. The count starts at diez and veinte, which he knows, and their balls are the ones that drop for the others.

Left, and why:
- 63 tests no new word by itself: the numbers are old. What is new is the hour as a whole ("son las cinco" against a clock), and that is the skill the call needs.
- On the island, bingo runs from 1 to 75 with letters. This is a children's game of tens in a square, and nobody in it says otherwise.
- "Treinta minutos" is "media hora" in everyday speech. Treinta is the word being taught, so both are heard.
- Length: 65 is long (ten steps). The digits are spelled three times only; the real number comes from machine play.

## Needs (for the scene's designer and the code lane)

- States: `hora` mediodia, tarde, ocaso (the light); `mesas` (the bingo tables, the cage of balls and the prize shelf: there or not; story 65).

- Things he can touch: reloj (the face), arriba (the top of the face, at the 12), abajo (the bottom, at the 6), carta_a, carta_b (the two time cards), each numeral square of abuela's card (n40, n60, n80, n90, n100), each person (tito, vendedor, luis, carmen, abuela, senor, senora, coqui), pastelillos, puertita.

- Speaker keys: `tito`, `vendedor`; the others exist.

Acts (name: what it does on screen; story and step):
- `cartas` {a, b}: two time cards with the hours a and b appear; `pone63` {h}: the frog goes up the ladder and sets the short hand to h; `campana` {n}: the bell rings n times (63).
- `echa63`: the vendedor drops a pastelillo into the oil; `frie63` {ok}: he counts the rings on his fingers and lifts it out golden, or shakes his head and waits: smoke, a black one; `pastelillos` {n}: the raw ones left, and the plate of done ones (63). `final63` {n} (63: 8).
- `larga` {a}: the long hand, with the frog, swings to the top (punto), the bottom (media) or the side (lado); `corta` {h, media}: the short hand at an hour, or half way on (64). `resorte64`: the works grind, a spring jumps out, a shutter sticks (64: 5, 6). `figuras64` {n}: the ending (64: 7).

- Values: in 63 `h` the hour said (round A 4 to 8, round B later, to 12), `otra` the other card's hour, `lado` which card is at the left; in 64 `h` 4 to 11 and `dice` punto or media, each round; in 65 `orden` the order in which 60, 80 and 100 are called.

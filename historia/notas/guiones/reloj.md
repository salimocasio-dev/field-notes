# El reloj: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `reloj`). Stories 63 to 65. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 62.

Scene words (page 02): reloj, hora, ¿qué hora es?, es la una, son las, y media, en punto, minuto, the tens from 30 to 100.
How the tens are shared out: treinta in story 64 (with "y media"); cuarenta to cien in story 65.

The picture: the foot of the square's clock tower. One view. The tower's big round clock face fills the upper middle: twelve numerals, a short hand and a long hand (the hands are sprites). Under the face a small balcony with a rail, where somebody can stand and reach the hands, and a ladder up to it. Beside the face a little shuttered door for the tower's three wooden figures (story 64). At the tower's foot, at the right, the quiosco's counter with its fryer; at the left, tables (story 65: the bingo). Pigeons.

New person: don Tito, the clock man (old, a waistcoat, a pocket watch on a chain). The others: the vendedor of the quiosco (an apron, a paper hat), the family, Ana, the señora and the señor.

Time cards (story 63): two cards over the frog, each a small clock face with its hands; touching one makes the frog set the tower's clock so.
Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 63: ¿Qué hora es?

- **Goal he can see:** on the quiosco's counter, a plate of three raw pastelillos beside the fryer. The vendedor looks up at the tower's clock, which has stopped, and scratches his head: he fries by the bell.
- **At stake:** the pastelillos (3). The clock set to a wrong hour: the bell rings the wrong number, the vendedor counts on his fingers, forgets the fryer, and one comes out black.
- **New words:** reloj, hora, ¿qué hora es?, es la una, son las.
- **Back again:** uno to doce, me llamo, mucho gusto, ¿cómo te llamas?, no camina (camina), grande, viejo, come, caliente, huele, rico, sí, gracias, mira, ahora, después.

**Start:** midday. The clock's hands hang straight down. Don Tito on the balcony with an oil can; Luis, Carmen and the frog at the tower's foot; the vendedor at his fryer.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira el reloj de la plaza. Es un reloj grande y viejo. Y no camina." Ask (signed, the clock face): "¿Dónde está el reloj?" He touches it: the long hand swings and hangs again. Don Tito looks down. NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?" COQUÍ [TURN: me llamo (me llamo)]: "Me llamo Coquí." NARR: "Don Tito dice: Mucho gusto, Coquí. Yo soy don Tito."
2. Don Tito pulls out his pocket watch. NARR: "Luis dice: Don Tito, ¿qué hora es?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Es la una." Two time cards appear: one o'clock, and four o'clock. Ask (signed, the one o'clock card): "Es la una. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the frog hops up the ladder and pushes the short hand to the 1. The bell rings once. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Es la una! ¡A comer!" **(a comer: heard)**
3. The pigeons settle. The frog comes down; time goes by (the light moves a little). NARR: "Carmen dice: Don Tito, ¿qué hora es ahora?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las dos." Cards: two o'clock, and six o'clock. Ask (signed): "Son las dos. Toca el reloj de las dos." He touches it: the hand goes to the 2; the bell rings twice.
4. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Qué hora es? ¿Son las tres?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Sí. Ahora son las tres." Cards: three, and nine. Ask (signed): "¿Dónde son las tres?" He touches it: three rings. The vendedor counts three on his fingers and drops the first pastelillo into the fryer.

**The turn**

5. The pastelillo comes out golden; the vendedor lays it on a second plate and nods at the clock. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Con el reloj, los pastelillos salen bien." **(pastelillos, salen: heard; the picture carries them)** Don Tito hands the frog the oil can and sits down on the balcony's step with his watch. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Tito reads the hour from his watch: one of four to twelve, drawn, each round on its own (round B's is later than round A's). Two time cards: that hour, and another, drawn (never the pairs dos and doce, seis and siete). Which card is at the left is drawn.

6. **Decision A, touch a card (two cards; tests the number in "son las").** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Don Tito, ¿qué hora es?" NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las cinco." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog sets the short hand; the bell rings that many times; the vendedor counts, fries, and lifts out a golden pastelillo. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog sets the other hour; the bell rings that many times. The vendedor counts on his fingers, looks at don Tito, looks at the clock; smoke rises behind him: a black pastelillo. A cross. NARR: "Don Tito dice: No, Coquí. Son las cinco." The sign is on the right card; he touches it: the hand, the bell.
7. **Decision B, touch a card (the same game; a later hour).** NARR: "Don Tito dice: Ahora son las ocho." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Don Tito comes down the ladder, winds the clock with a big key, and the long hand starts to move by itself. NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¡Ya! El reloj camina." The vendedor brings the plate.
   - Three golden: one each for don Tito, Luis and Carmen, and the vendedor breaks his own in two for the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two golden, one black: the black one goes to a pigeon, which looks at it and walks away.
   - One golden, two black: the golden one is cut in four.
9. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Quieres un pastelillo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, gracias!" NARR: "Son las ocho en el reloj de la plaza."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** son las 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: reloj 4 before the game; ¿qué hora es? 4; hora counts with it; es la una 3 (step 2).
**A miss is filed under:** son las.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. The numbers one to twelve are El colmado's words; what is new is hearing them as an hour and finding it on a clock.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): me llamo; sí.

## Story 64: Y media

- **Goal he can see:** the little shuttered door beside the clock face, with three painted figures on its shutters: a rooster, a man with a guitar, a small frog. Don Tito taps it: they come out when the clock is right.
- **At stake:** the three figures. The long hand pushed the wrong way: the works grind, a spring jumps out, and one figure's shutter sticks.
- **New words:** y media, en punto, minuto, treinta.
- **Back again:** reloj, hora, ¿qué hora es?, son las, dos to doce, espera, arriba, abajo, voy, me gusta, mira, ahora, pequeño, gallo (heard).

**Start:** afternoon. Don Tito on the balcony with his watch and a screwdriver; the frog beside him on the rail. The clock shows two o'clock; the long hand points straight up.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Coquí, ¿vienes?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops onto the long hand. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Mira. Son las dos en punto." The 12 at the top twinkles. Ask (signed, the top of the face): "Las dos en punto. ¿Dónde está?" He touches it: the long hand, with the frog on it, stands straight up; the bell rings once, softly.
2. Don Tito turns a wheel; he counts on his watch. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Espera un minuto... treinta minutos. Ahora son las dos y media." The 6 at the bottom twinkles. Ask (signed, the bottom of the face): "Las dos y media. ¿Dónde está?" He touches it: the long hand swings down with the frog; the short hand creeps half way to the 3.
3. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Treinta minutos más. Son las tres en punto." Ask (signed, the top): "¿Y las tres en punto?" He touches it: up. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Y treinta minutos más: las tres y media." Ask (signed, the bottom): "¿Y las tres y media?" He touches it: down.

**The turn**

4. Don Tito opens a flap in the tower: wheels and a big spring. He taps the little door of the figures. NARR: "Don Tito dice: Un minuto, Coquí. Ahora, con cuidado." The frog holds on to the long hand. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Tito reads his watch: an hour from four to eleven, and either "en punto" or "y media", drawn, each round on its own. The frog pushes the long hand to the top or to the bottom.

5. **Decision A, touch a place (the top of the face, the bottom; tests the words said).** NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las cinco y media." (Or, drawn: "Don Tito dice: Son las cinco en punto.")
   - Right: the long hand swings there; the works tick; the short hand takes its place. A star.
   - Wrong: the long hand swings the other way; the works grind; a spring jumps out of the flap, and a shutter of the little door sticks, crooked. A cross. NARR: "Don Tito dice: No, Coquí. Las cinco y media." The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the hand swings there.
6. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; a later hour).** NARR: "Don Tito dice: Son las siete en punto." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Don Tito shuts the flap and winds the key. The bell rings. The little door opens.
   - No shutter stuck: the rooster, the man with the guitar and the small frog come out one after the other, turn and bow; music. Stars and a heart.
   - One stuck: two come out; the third knocks behind its shutter.
   - Two stuck: the small frog comes out alone, bows twice.
8. NARR: "Don Tito dice: ¿Te gusta el reloj, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" NARR: "Don Tito dice: En treinta minutos salen otra vez."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** en punto 3 (steps 1, 3), y media 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: minuto 4 with minutos; treinta 4.
**A miss is filed under:** the words that were said (y media or en punto).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): voy; me gusta.

## Story 65: ¡Bingo!

- **Goal he can see:** a shelf of three prizes beside the bingo caller: a big basket of fruit, a pineapple, a hand of guineos. Abuela looks at the basket and rubs her hands.
- **At stake:** the prizes (3, best first). A bean on the wrong number: while abuela moves it, the señor at the next table fills his card, shouts, and takes the best prize left.
- **New words:** cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, cien.
- **Back again:** treinta, veinte, diez, habichuelas, tengo, no tengo, mesa, abuela, señor, señora, piña, guineo, fruta, listo, mira, otro, ¿cuál?, por favor, gracias, minuto.

**Start:** late afternoon. Tables at the tower's foot. Abuela at one with her card: four squares with the numerals 40, 60, 90, 100. The frog on the table beside a saucer of dry habichuelas. Luis at her side with his own card: 30, 50, 70, 80. The señor at the next table with a card. The señora stands at a wire cage of numbered balls: she is the caller.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "En la plaza hay bingo. Abuela tiene cuatro números." **(bingo, números: heard; the picture carries them)** Abuela runs a finger along her card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Cuarenta, sesenta, noventa y cien." Luis along his. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo tengo treinta, cincuenta, setenta y ochenta."
2. The señora holds the balls up in a row, in order, one after another. NARR: "La señora dice: Treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa... ¡y cien! ¿Listos?" COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo, listos)]: "¡Listos!" She turns the cage.
3. A ball drops. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Noventa! Nueve, cero. ¡El noventa!" Ask (signed, the 90 on abuela's card): "Abuela tiene el noventa. Toca el noventa." He touches it: the frog lays a bean on it.
4. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Cincuenta! Cinco, cero. ¡El cincuenta!" Abuela shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: Cincuenta no tengo." Luis lays a bean on his own. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Cuarenta! Cuatro, cero. ¡El cuarenta!" Ask (signed, the 40): "¿Dónde está el cuarenta?" He touches it: a bean.
5. Two quick calls; the beans go on Luis's card. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Ochenta! ¡El ochenta! ... ¡Setenta! Siete, cero. ¡El setenta!" NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo ochenta! ¡Y tengo setenta!"

**The turn**

6. The señor at the next table has three beans on his card; he grins across at abuela, who has two. Abuela pushes the saucer to the frog and covers her eyes. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ahora tú. ¡Yo no miro!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two of abuela's numbers are left: 60 and 100. The señora calls them, in a drawn order, one in each round. The frog lays a bean on one of the two squares that are still empty (in round B one square only is empty, with a covered one beside it: a bean can go on any square).

7. **Decision A, touch a square (abuela's card; tests the number called).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Cien! Uno, cero, cero. ¡El cien!" (Or, drawn: "La señora dice: ¡Sesenta! Seis, cero. ¡El sesenta!")
   - Right: a bean on it. Abuela peeps through her fingers and nods. A star.
   - Wrong: a bean on another square. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. ¡El cien!" While she moves it, the señor lays his last bean and jumps up. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Bingo!" He takes the best prize left from the shelf. A cross. The sign is on the right square; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a square (the number that is left).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Sesenta! Seis, cero. ¡El sesenta!" (Or cien.) Right and wrong as in A; after a wrong move it is the señora with the green dress, at another table, who shouts and takes a prize.

**The ending**

9. Four beans. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Bingo!" She goes to the shelf.
   - Three prizes left: the big basket of fruit; she can hardly lift it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the pineapple.
   - One left: the hand of guineos.
10. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. ¿Cuál quieres?" She holds the prize out. COQUÍ [TURN: guineo (guineo)]: "¡Un guineo, por favor!" NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Otro bingo en diez minutos!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cien 3 and sesenta 3 before the game (steps 1, 2 and Luis's or abuela's reading), and three more in the call itself. Not deciding: cuarenta 6, cincuenta 6, setenta 5, ochenta 5, noventa 6 on a clean run.
**A miss is filed under:** the number that was called.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two in round A (two empty squares); in round B a learner who sees that one square is empty needs no Spanish. (To settle in the reviews: round B on Luis's card, with two squares empty, instead.)
**Never set against each other:** sesenta and setenta (abuela's card has no 70; the 70 is called for Luis, with no touch).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 10): listo; guineo.

# Los regalos: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `regalos`). Stories 114 to 116. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 113.

Scene words (page 02): regalo, cumpleaños, bizcocho, vela, da, recibe, para ti, sorpresa, ¡felicidades!, mes, año.
How they are shared out: 114 regalo, bizcocho, cumpleaños, sorpresa; 115 vela, año, mes, ¡felicidades!; 116 da, recibe, para ti.

The picture: a corner of the fort's courtyard at night, beside the dance floor. One view. At the back, the fort's inner wall with lanterns on it; at its left, a wide arch that opens onto the dance floor (its lights and its dancers show through). A line runs across the arch, and a curtain hangs from it on three clothes pegs. In the middle, in front of the wall, a long table with a cloth. Over the table, from the wall, a second line with pegs for photos. At the right, a bench against the wall. No roof.

People: the family (abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen); don Pepe, the maestra, the piragüero.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign.

## Story 114: La sorpresa

- **Goal he can see:** the curtain across the arch, on its three pegs. On the far side Carmen dances; on this side abuela lays a table for her. Luis holds a finger to his lips.
- **At stake:** the curtain's pegs (3). A wrong thing has to be carried back to the bench, past the curtain: the frog brushes it, and a peg flies off. The curtain sags, and more of the table shows from the dance floor.
- **New words:** regalo, bizcocho, cumpleaños, sorpresa.
- **Back again:** fiesta, hoy, baila, ve, flor, mesa, bonito, me gusta, trae, ¿quién?, viene, ahora, tengo, también, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The curtain is half open; through the arch, Carmen dances with her back to us. The table has its cloth and nothing on it. On the bench at the right, three things in a row: a cake under a net cover, a box wrapped in paper with a bow, a bunch of flowers in a jar. Abuela at the table, Luis at the arch, abuelo by the bench. The frog on the bench's end.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, hoy es el cumpleaños de Carmen." Luis lays a finger on his lips. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Shh! Es una sorpresa." (signed, the curtain.) He touches it: the frog pulls the curtain shut across the arch; the three pegs hold it. NARR: "Luis dice: Carmen baila. No ve la sorpresa."
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Para un cumpleaños, un bizcocho." (signed, the cake on the bench.) He touches it: the frog lifts its net cover: a cake, white, with a red flower of sugar. NARR: "Abuela dice: Este es el bizcocho de Carmen. Coquí, ¿te gusta el bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!"
3. Abuelo lifts the box with the bow, and shakes it by his ear: it rattles. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y yo tengo un regalo para Carmen." (signed, the box.) He touches it: the frog shakes it too; it rattles. NARR: "Luis dice: El regalo es una sorpresa también."
4. Don Pepe looks in round the curtain's end, with a small box of his own, and puts it in abuelo's hands. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Hola! Un regalo para Carmen." He goes back to the dance. Abuelo puts it with the first: one box, with one bow, on the bench. A shadow comes to the curtain from the far side: Carmen. Everybody stands still. The shadow goes away. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Carmen viene! ... No. Carmen baila."

**The turn**

5. Abuela shakes out a second cloth, a bright one, over the table, and smooths it with both hands. On the bench the three things wait: the cake under its cover, the box with the bow, the flowers. The frog on the bench's end. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, smoothing the cloth with her eyes on her hands, says what she wants on the table next (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): the cake, the present, the flowers. The frog carries it to the table.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the cake, the present, the flowers; tests regalo against bizcocho, with flores, which he has).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora, el regalo." (Or, drawn: "Ahora, el bizcocho." Or: "Ahora, las flores.")
   - Right: the frog carries it over; abuela sets it in its place on the cloth. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries another thing over. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. El regalo." He hurries it back to the bench, close by the curtain: it catches, and a peg flies off. The curtain sags at one corner; the dance floor's light comes through. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another thing).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora, el bizcocho." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis puts the last thing on the table. Everybody gets down behind it. The music stops; Carmen comes to the arch.
   - Three pegs: the curtain is whole. She pulls it open, and everybody jumps up. She jumps too. Stars and a heart.
   - Two pegs: she looks in at the sagging corner first, and smiles before she opens it.
   - One peg: the curtain hangs by a corner; she walks in laughing, with her hands over her eyes.
9. NARR: "Todos dicen: ¡Sorpresa!" **(todos: heard; a word of the last scene)** NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Un bizcocho! ¡Y un regalo! Coquí, ¿quién trae el bizcocho?" The frog hops to abuela. COQUÍ [TURN: abuela (abuela, la abuela)]: "¡Abuela!" NARR: "Hoy es el cumpleaños de Carmen. Hay un bizcocho, un regalo y una sorpresa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** bizcocho 3 (step 2), regalo 3 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: cumpleaños 2 before the game (steps 1, 2) and once in the ending; sorpresa 3 (steps 1, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for (regalo or bizcocho; for the flowers, flor).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A; one in two in round B if round A's thing is on the table.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): me gusta; abuela.

## Story 115: Las velas

- **Goal he can see:** the cake with seven candles, and abuela's long match. Over the table, the line with three pegs for Carmen's photos.
- **At stake:** the candles that burn (7). There is a wind. A wrong photo has to come down off the line again, and while abuelo changes it the wind blows out two candles.
- **New words:** vela, año, mes, ¡felicidades!
- **Back again:** cumpleaños, bizcocho, bebé, ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años, siete, tres, doce, uno, dos, pequeña, falta, un momento, viento, hoy, aquí, ahora, tiene, mira, sí, no.

**Start:** night; the curtain is open. Carmen sits at the table behind her cake; no candles on it yet. Abuela with a small box of candles. Abuelo beside the table with a big envelope. Luis; don Pepe and the maestra behind. The frog on the table's end. Over the table, the line with its three pegs, empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Felicidades, Carmen! ¿Cuántos años tienes hoy?" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo siete años!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Siete años, siete velas." (signed, the box of candles.) He touches it: the frog carries the candles to the cake one by one, and stands each in its place, while Luis counts. NARR: "Luis dice: Una vela, dos velas, tres velas... ¡siete velas!"
2. Abuelo takes a photo out of his envelope: a baby wrapped in a blanket. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Mira, Coquí. Aquí Carmen es un bebé. Tiene tres meses." **(meses: a form of mes)** (signed, the photo of the baby.) He touches it: the frog holds it up; abuelo hangs it on the line with a peg. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Tres meses? ¡Qué pequeña!"
3. A second photo: a small girl with a ball. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y aquí Carmen tiene tres años." (signed, the photo with the ball.) He touches it: abuelo hangs it beside the first. NARR: "Luis dice: Un año tiene doce meses. ¡Tres años son muchos meses!" A third photo: Carmen as she is tonight. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y aquí, siete años." He hangs it.
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿cuántas velas hay en el bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: siete (siete, hay siete, siete velas)]: "¡Siete!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Siete velas. Un año, una vela."

**The turn**

5. A gust: the three pegs let go, and the three photos flutter down onto the table's end, side by side, face up. Abuela, bent over the cake, has not seen: she strikes her long match and lights the seven candles. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Un momento! Faltan las fotos." Abuela cups her hands round the flames. Abuelo steps up on a stool, his face to the line and his hand held out behind him. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuelo, on his stool with his eyes on the line and its pegs, asks for a photo by how old Carmen is in it (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): three months, three years, seven years. The three photos lie on the table's end (their order is drawn). The frog hands one up.

6. **Decision A, touch a photo (three; tests mes against año: two of the photos have the same number).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Ahora, la foto de los tres meses." (Or, drawn: "Ahora, la foto de los tres años." Or: "Ahora, la foto de los siete años.")
   - Right: abuelo hangs it; it stays. A star.
   - Wrong: abuelo hangs it without a look, leans back, and looks. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡No, Coquí! La foto de los tres meses." He takes it down and puts it back on the table; the wind gets past abuela's hands, and two candles go out, with two threads of smoke. A cross. The sign is on the right photo; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a photo (the same game; another photo).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Ahora, la foto de los tres años." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuelo hangs the last photo himself, and comes down. Everybody stands round the cake.
   - Seven candles burn. Carmen blows them all out at once. Stars and a heart.
   - Five candles burn.
   - Three candles burn; Carmen blows them out, and laughs.
9. NARR: "Todos dicen: ¡Felicidades, Carmen!" **(todos: heard)** She blows. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, ¿quieres bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero, sí por favor)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" Abuelo cuts him a piece as big as he is. NARR: "Carmen tiene siete años. Hay siete velas en el bizcocho. ¡Felicidades!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** mes 4, all as meses (steps 2, 3); año 2 and años 5 (steps 1, 3, 4). Not deciding: vela 3 and velas 7 (steps 1, 4); ¡felicidades! once before the game (step 1) and twice in the ending.
**Forms:** meses with mes; años with año; velas with vela.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was asked for (mes or año).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A; one in two in round B if round A's photo is on the line. In one of the two rounds at least, the photo asked for is one of the two with "tres".
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): siete; quiero.

## Story 116: Para ti

- **Goal he can see:** three presents in a row on the table, each wrapped, each with a bow.
- **At stake:** the presents that are still a surprise (3). A present pushed to the wrong person is opened by the one who was giving it: the paper is torn, and everybody has seen what is inside.
- **New words:** da, recibe, para ti.
- **Back again:** regalo, cumpleaños, sorpresa, sombrero, ella, él, tiene, tengo, ¿quién?, viento, para mí, también, ahora, bonito, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The cake is cut. Three wrapped presents on the table, each with a paper tag on a thread. Carmen at the left of a low stool that stands in front of the table. Abuelo, with his box with the bow. Abuela with a sheet of paper (her list). Luis; don Pepe, the maestra and the piragüero wait at the right. The frog on the table's edge.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen, abuelo tiene un regalo." Abuelo puts his box on the stool, between himself and Carmen. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para ti, Carmen." (signed, Carmen.) He touches her: the frog pushes the box across the stool to her; she takes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo da el regalo. Carmen recibe el regalo."
2. Carmen opens it: a straw hat. She puts it on. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Un sombrero! ¡Gracias, abuelo!" She takes a small packet from under the table and puts it on the stool. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y yo tengo un regalo para ti, abuelo." (signed, abuelo.) He touches him: the frog pushes the packet across to abuelo; he opens it: a paper fan. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen da y abuelo recibe."
3. Luis puts a packet on the stool. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo también doy un regalo. Para ti, Carmen." **(doy: a form of da)** The frog pushes it across; Carmen takes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis da. ¿Y quién recibe?" COQUÍ [TURN: ella (ella, carmen)]: "¡Ella!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Sí! Carmen recibe."
4. A gust: the paper tags fly off the three presents on the table, and away over the wall. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, el viento!" She holds up her sheet of paper: it is all written down. Carmen puts three small packets of her own beside the three presents: one for each guest.

**The turn**

5. Abuela reads her sheet with her finger on the line. A guest comes to the right of the stool; Carmen stands at its left. Abuelo puts one thing on the stool between them: a guest's present for Carmen, or Carmen's packet for the guest; both are wrapped, and nothing says whose it is. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, her eyes on her sheet, says what the guest does (the guest is drawn from don Pepe, the maestra and the piragüero; what he does is drawn, each round on its own): he gives, or he receives. Carmen and the guest stand on either side of the stool, each with both hands held out, both looking at abuela. The frog pushes the thing to one of them: to Carmen if the guest gives, to the guest if he receives.

6. **Decision A, touch a person (Carmen, the guest; tests da against recibe).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Don Pepe da el regalo." (Or, drawn: "Don Pepe recibe el regalo." The guest is drawn too.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it across; the one who receives opens it, and holds it up: a surprise. The other claps. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes it to the one who was giving it. He opens it before abuela can speak: his own present; everybody has seen it. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Don Pepe da el regalo." One of the presents on the table loses its bow. A cross. The sign is on the right person; he touches him: the open thing goes across, with its torn paper.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "Abuela dice: La maestra recibe el regalo." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. One present is left on the table. Abuela reads the last line of her sheet, and looks up at the frog. Everybody looks at the frog.
   - Three presents were surprises: the frog's has its bow. Stars and a heart.
   - Two were.
   - One was: the frog's own.
9. Carmen puts the last present on the stool, in front of the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, este regalo es para ti." COQUÍ [TURN: para mí (para mí, ¿para mí?)]: "¿Para mí?" He opens it: a very small straw hat. He puts it on. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí, para ti!" NARR: "Carmen recibe regalos. Y Carmen da regalos también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** da 3 and doy once (steps 1, 2, 3), recibe 4 (steps 1, 2, 3). Not deciding: para ti 3 (steps 1, 2, 3) and twice in the ending.
**Forms:** doy with da.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (da or recibe).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the guest and what he does are drawn apart.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): ella; para mí.

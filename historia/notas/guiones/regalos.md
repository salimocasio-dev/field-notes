# Los regalos: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `regalos`). Stories 114 to 116. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 113.

Scene words (page 02): regalo, cumpleaños, bizcocho, vela, da, recibe, para ti, sorpresa, ¡felicidades!, mes, año.
How they are shared out: 114 regalo, bizcocho, cumpleaños, sorpresa; 115 vela, año, mes, ¡felicidades!; 116 da, recibe, para ti.

The picture: a corner of the fort's courtyard at night, beside the dance floor. One view. At the back, the fort's inner wall with lanterns on it; at its far left, a wide arch that opens onto the dance floor (its lights and its dancers show through). A line runs across the arch, and a curtain hangs from it on three clothes pegs. Against the wall, just to the right of the arch, a bench: the curtain's edge hangs beside its end. In the middle and at the right, in front of the wall, a long table with a cloth. Over the table, from the wall, a second line with pegs for photos. No roof.

People: the family (abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen); don Pepe, the maestra, the piragüero.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign. While he waits, something always moves: the lanterns' flames, the dancers beyond the arch, the curtain in the wind.

## Story 114: La sorpresa

- **Goal he can see:** the curtain across the arch, on its three pegs. On the far side Carmen dances; on this side abuela lays a table for her. Luis holds a finger to his lips.
- **At stake:** the curtain's pegs (3). A wrong thing has to go back to the bench, and the bench is beside the curtain: the frog, hurrying, runs into the curtain's edge, and a peg flies off. The curtain sags, and more of the table shows from the dance floor.
- **New words:** regalo, bizcocho, cumpleaños, sorpresa.
- **Back again:** fiesta, hoy, baila, ve, trae, flor, mesa, grande, bonito, me gusta, contento, ¿cómo estás?, viene, tengo, también, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The curtain is half open; through the arch, Carmen dances with her back to us. The table has its cloth and nothing on it. On the bench beside the arch, three things in a row: a cake under a net cover, a box wrapped in paper with a bow, a bunch of flowers in a jar. Abuela at the table, Luis at the arch, abuelo by the bench. The frog on the table's end.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, hoy es el cumpleaños de Carmen." Luis lays a finger on his lips. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Shh! Es una sorpresa." (signed, the curtain.) He touches it: the frog pulls the curtain shut across the arch; the three pegs hold it. NARR: "Luis dice: Carmen baila. No ve la sorpresa."
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Para el cumpleaños hay un bizcocho." (signed, the cake on the bench.) He touches it: the frog lifts its net cover off, for good: a cake, white, with a red flower of sugar. NARR: "Abuela dice: Este es el bizcocho de Carmen." NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿te gusta el bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!"
3. Abuelo lifts the box with the bow, and shakes it by his ear: it rattles. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y yo tengo un regalo para Carmen." (signed, the box.) He touches it: the frog shakes it too; it rattles. NARR: "Luis dice: El regalo es una sorpresa también." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué regalo grande!" Abuelo puts it back on the bench.

**The turn**

4. Abuela shakes out a second cloth, a bright one, over the table. A shadow comes to the curtain from the far side: Carmen. Everybody stands still. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ahí viene Carmen!" The shadow goes away. NARR: "Luis dice: No. Carmen baila." Abuela smooths the cloth fast, with both hands. On the bench the three things wait: the cake, the box with the bow, the flowers. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, smoothing the cloth with her eyes on her hands, says what she wants on the table next: one round the present, one round the cake (which comes first is drawn). The flowers can be touched in both rounds; they are never asked for. The frog carries the thing to the table. He starts each round on the table's end.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the cake, the present, the flowers; tests regalo against bizcocho).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, trae el regalo, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Coquí, trae el bizcocho, por favor.")
   - Right: the frog carries it over; abuela sets it in its place on the cloth. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries another thing over. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. El regalo." He hurries it back to the bench, and runs into the curtain's edge: a peg flies off. The curtain sags at one corner; the dance floor's light comes through. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the frog carries it to the table.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the other thing).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, trae el bizcocho, por favor." (The one that round A did not ask for.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Luis puts the flowers on the table. Everybody gets down behind it. The music stops; Carmen comes to the arch.
   - Three pegs: the curtain is whole. She pulls it open, and everybody jumps up. She jumps too. Stars and a heart.
   - Two pegs: she looks in at the sagging corner first, and smiles before she opens it.
   - One peg: the curtain hangs by a corner; she walks in laughing, with her hands over her eyes.
8. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Sorpresa!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Un bizcocho! ¡Y un regalo! Coquí, ¿cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: contento (contento, muy contento)]: "¡Contento!" NARR: "Hoy es el cumpleaños de Carmen. Hay un bizcocho, un regalo y una sorpresa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** bizcocho 3 (step 2: three lines), regalo 3 (step 3: three lines); each line is said while the thing is in the frog's or somebody's hands. Not deciding: cumpleaños 2 before the game (steps 1, 2) and once in the ending; sorpresa 3 (steps 1, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for (regalo or bizcocho).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A; one in two in round B if round A's thing is on the table.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 8): me gusta; contento.

## Story 115: Las velas

- **Goal he can see:** the cake with seven small candles, and abuela's long match. Over the table, the line with four pegs for Carmen's photos.
- **At stake:** the candles that burn (7). There is a wind. A wrong photo has to come down off the line again, and while abuelo changes it the wind gets past abuela's hands and blows out two candles.
- **New words:** vela, año, mes, ¡felicidades!
- **Back again:** cumpleaños, bizcocho, feliz, bebé, ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años, siete, tres, ¿quién?, tú, trae, falta, un momento, viento, un poco, aquí, ahora, tiene, mira, sí, no.

**Start:** night; the curtain is open. Carmen sits at the table behind her cake; no candles on it yet. Abuela beside her. Abuelo beside the table with a big envelope. Luis; don Pepe and the maestra behind. A small box of candles on the bench. The frog on the table's end. Over the table, the line with its four pegs, empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Feliz cumpleaños, Carmen! ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo siete años!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Felicidades, Carmen!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, trae las velas, por favor." **(velas: a form of vela)** (signed, the box of candles.) He touches it: the frog carries the candles to the cake one by one, and stands each in its place, while Luis counts on his fingers. NARR: "Luis dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis... ¡siete velitas!" **(velitas: a form of vela: the small candles of a cake)**
2. Abuelo takes two photos out of his envelope: a very small baby wrapped in a blanket; a small girl with a ball. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Mira, Coquí. Aquí Carmen es una bebé. Tiene tres meses." **(meses: a form of mes)** (signed, the photo of the baby in the blanket.) He touches it: the frog holds it up; abuelo hangs it on the line with a peg. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Y aquí Carmen tiene tres años." (signed, the photo with the ball.) He touches it: abuelo hangs it beside the first. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Tres meses? ¡Qué chiquita!"
3. Two more photos: a baby sitting up with a spoon; Carmen as she is tonight. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Aquí tiene siete meses. Y aquí, siete años." He hangs them: four photos on the line, from the smallest Carmen to the biggest. Carmen holds up the first photo's twin from the envelope. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí. ¿Quién es?" COQUÍ [TURN: tú (tú, eres tú)]: "¡Tú!"

**The turn**

4. A gust: the four pegs let go, and the four photos flutter down onto the table's end, side by side, face up. Abuela, bent over the cake, has not seen: she strikes her long match and lights the seven candles. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Un momento! Faltan mis fotos." Abuelo steps up on a stool, his face to the line and his hand held out behind him; abuela cups her hands round the flames. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuelo, on his stool with his eyes on the line and its pegs, asks for a photo by how old Carmen is in it. The four photos are two pairs with the same number: three months and three years; seven months and seven years. Round A asks one photo of one pair (drawn); round B asks one photo of the other pair (drawn), so both photos of the pair asked are always on the table: the number leaves two, and only "meses" or "años" says which. The four photos lie on the table's end (their order is drawn). The frog hands one up.

5. **Decision A, touch a photo (four; tests mes against año).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Ahora, la foto de los tres meses." (Or, drawn: "Ahora, la foto de los tres años." "Ahora, la foto de los siete meses." "Ahora, la foto de los siete años.")
   - Right: abuelo hangs it; it stays. A star.
   - Wrong: abuelo hangs it without a look, leans back, and looks. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡No, Coquí! La foto de los tres meses." He takes it down and puts it back on the table; the wind gets past abuela's hands, and two candles go out, with two threads of smoke. A cross. The sign is on the right photo; he touches it: abuelo hangs it.
6. **Decision B, touch a photo (the same game; a photo of the other pair).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Ahora, la foto de los siete años." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Abuelo hangs the last two photos himself, and comes down. Everybody stands round the cake.
   - Seven candles burn. Carmen blows them all out at once. Stars and a heart.
   - Five burn; two are black, each with its thread of smoke. She blows out the five.
   - Three burn; four are black. She blows out the three, and laughs.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Feliz cumpleaños, Carmen!" She blows. NARR: "La maestra dice: ¡Felicidades!" NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, ¿quieres bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco, sí un poco)]: "¡Sí, un poco!" Abuelo cuts him a piece as big as he is. NARR: "Carmen tiene siete años. Hay siete velitas en el bizcocho. ¡Felicidades!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** mes 3, all as meses (steps 2, 3: three lines); años 4 by line (steps 1, 2, 3). Año is not all new: he has "tengo … años"; mes is new, and the test is the one against the other. Not deciding: vela once as velas and once as velitas before the game (step 1), and once in the ending: three in all, with the candles in the picture each time; ¡felicidades! once before the game (step 1) and twice in the ending.
**Forms:** meses with mes; años with año; velas, velitas with vela.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was asked for (mes or año).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four in round A, one in three in round B; one in two for somebody who knows the numbers and not the two words.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): tú; un poco.

## Story 116: Para ti

- **Goal he can see:** three packets in a row on the table, all wrapped in the same paper, each with a bow. Abuela has a sheet of paper: who gives what to whom.
- **At stake:** the packets that are still a surprise (3). A packet pushed to the wrong person is opened by the one who was giving it: the paper is torn, the bow is off, and everybody has seen what is inside before the right person gets it.
- **New words:** da, recibe, para ti.
- **Back again:** regalo, cumpleaños, sorpresa, sombrero, ella, él, tiene, tengo, ¿quién?, viento, para mí, también, ahora, bonito, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The cake is cut. On the table, three packets in a row, each with a bow and a paper tag on a thread. In front of the table, a low stool. Carmen stands at the stool's left. Abuelo, with his box with the bow. Abuela with her sheet of paper. Luis; don Pepe, the maestra and the piragüero wait at the right. The frog on the table's edge.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen, abuelo tiene un regalo." Abuelo puts his box on the stool, between himself and Carmen. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para ti, Carmen." (signed, Carmen.) He touches her: the frog pushes the box across the stool to her; she takes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo le da el regalo a Carmen. Carmen recibe el regalo."
2. Carmen opens it: a straw hat. She puts it on. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Un sombrero! ¡Gracias, abuelo!" She takes a small thing from her pocket and puts it on the stool. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y yo tengo un regalo para ti, abuelo." (signed, abuelo.) He touches him: the frog pushes it across to abuelo; he opens it: a paper fan. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen da y abuelo recibe."
3. Luis puts a small thing on the stool. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo también te doy un regalo. Para ti, Carmen." **(doy: a form of da)** The frog pushes it across; Carmen takes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis da. ¿Y quién recibe?" The frog points. COQUÍ [TURN: ella (ella, carmen)]: "¡Ella!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Sí! Carmen recibe."

**The turn**

4. A gust: the paper tags fly off the three packets on the table, and away over the wall. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, el viento!" She holds up her sheet of paper: it is all written down. A guest comes to the right of the stool; Carmen stands at its left. Abuelo takes the first packet from the table and puts it on the stool between them. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The three packets look the same; nothing on them says whose they are. Abuela, her eyes on her sheet, says what the guest does (the guest is drawn from don Pepe, the maestra and the piragüero; what he does is drawn, each round on its own): he gives, or he receives. Carmen and the guest stand on either side of the stool, each with both hands held out, both looking at abuela. The frog pushes the packet to one of them: to Carmen if the guest gives, to the guest if he receives.

5. **Decision A, touch a person (Carmen, the guest; tests da against recibe).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Don Pepe da el regalo." (Or, drawn: "Don Pepe recibe el regalo." The guest is drawn too.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it across; the one who receives opens it, and holds it up: a surprise. The other claps. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes it to the one who was giving it. He opens it before abuela can speak: the paper is torn and the bow is off; everybody has seen it. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Don Pepe da el regalo." A cross. The sign is on the right person; he touches him: the open thing goes across, in its torn paper.
6. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game; another guest; abuelo puts the second packet on the stool).** NARR: "Abuela dice: La maestra recibe el regalo." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. One packet is left on the table. Abuela reads the last line of her sheet, and looks up at the frog. Everybody looks at the frog.
   - Three surprises: nothing torn anywhere. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: one torn paper lies by the stool.
   - One: two torn papers lie by the stool; the last packet is whole.
8. Carmen puts the last packet on the stool, in front of the frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, este regalo es para ti." COQUÍ [TURN: para mí (para mí, ¿para mí?)]: "¿Para mí?" He opens it: a very small straw hat. He puts it on. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí, para ti!" NARR: "Carmen recibe regalos. Y Carmen da regalos también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** da 3 and doy once (steps 1, 2, 3), recibe 4 (steps 1, 2, 3). Not deciding: para ti 3 (steps 1, 2, 3) and twice in the ending.
**Forms:** doy with da.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (da or recibe).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the guest and what he does are drawn apart, and every packet looks the same.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): ella; para mí.

## Reviews (draft 1 to draft 2)

A beginner's reviewer and a Puerto Rican Spanish reviewer read draft 1; each had only the script and the owner's rules.

- 114: the bench is beside the arch, so a thing hurried back to it meets the curtain's edge: the peg has a cause. Only the present and the cake are asked for; the flowers are a third thing to touch. Abuela asks in a whole sentence with a word he has ("Coquí, trae el regalo, por favor."). Three lines for each word, each with the thing in somebody's hands. One box only; the cake's cover stays off. The false alarm is the turn. "¡Ahí viene Carmen!" The last turn is not a quiz. One named person shouts "¡Sorpresa!".
- 115: four photos in two pairs with the same number, and a round never asks a photo whose twin has gone: the number leaves two photos, and only "meses" or "años" says which. "¡Feliz cumpleaños!" is what is said first and at the candles; "¡Felicidades!" comes from the guests and the narrator. "Una bebé." "¡Qué chiquita!" The candles on a cake are "velitas"; the box is "las velas". Luis counts, he does not label. The line about twelve months is gone. The turns are not echoes.
- 116: the three packets on the table are the ones that go to the stool, and they look the same, so a wrong push tears one of the three that are counted, and nothing on a packet says who gives it. Every ending has its picture. "Abuelo le da el regalo a Carmen." "Yo también te doy un regalo."
- Not taken: "¡Gracias!" for the frog's last turn ("¿Para mí?" is what anybody says, and it is a word he has); "¡Carmen!" for "¡Ella!" (a name is not a word of an earlier story; the frog points).

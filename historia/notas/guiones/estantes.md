# Los estantes: three stories (draft 1)

Place 4, El colmado. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `estantes`). Stories 39 to 41. He has finished stories 1 to 38.

Scene words (page 02): cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez, lata, botella, caja, ¿cuántos?, otro.

The picture: the shelves at the back of don Pepe's shop. One view, `wide`: three long shelves on the wall; a wooden ladder against them, with don Pepe on it when he works; the floor in front, where the delivery stands (crates and boxes); the shop window at the left, with a low table in it for a display; the street door at the right. The frog stands on a crate on the floor.

No numeral is ever on screen in this scene: counts are things to be counted. Answer bubbles for numbers speak their word and show nothing but a mouth.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as before. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places.

## Story 39: Latas y botellas

- **Goal he can see:** the middle shelf is bare, with six places marked by rings of dust: three round small ones and three round large ones. On the floor: a delivery of three cans and three bottles, and a small cardboard box with more inside. Fill the shelf.
- **At stake:** the six things for the shelf. Don Pepe, up the ladder, holds his hands ready for what he asked for. Tossed something else, he fumbles: a bottle breaks on the floor, a can rolls out of the door. Its place on the shelf stays bare, and at the end somebody goes without.
- **New words:** lata, botella, caja, ¿cuántos? (heard as ¿cuántas?).
- **Back again:** uno, dos, tres, hay, dame, tira, coge, aquí, colmado, necesito, aquí tiene, buenos días.

**Start:** morning. Don Pepe drags a big cardboard box in at the door; Luis and the frog beside the crates.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Don Pepe tiene una caja grande." He opens it; cans show. NARR: "En la caja hay latas." He lifts one can. NARR: "Esta es una lata." He lifts a bottle out of the crate beside it. NARR: "Y esta es una botella."
2. Don Pepe climbs the ladder and holds his hands out. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, dame una lata." Ask (signed, a can): "¿Dónde está la lata?" He touches it: the frog tosses it up; don Pepe catches it and stands it on the shelf, on a small ring. NARR: "Coquí tira la lata. ¡Don Pepe coge la lata!"
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Ahora, una botella." Ask (signed, a bottle): "Toca la botella." He touches it: up it goes, onto a large ring.
4. Luis counts what is on the floor, a finger on each. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Cuántas latas hay? Una, dos. Dos latas." NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y cuántas botellas hay? Una, dos. Dos botellas." COQUÍ [TURN: dos (dos)]: "¡Sí, dos!"

**The turn**

5. Don Pepe's glasses slip off his nose and fall into the big box. He feels for them and does not find them. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Ay! No veo. ¡Coquí, mira tú!" **(no veo: heard; the picture carries it)** From now on he looks straight ahead, hands out, and catches by ear. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two cans and two bottles are left on the floor. Each round don Pepe asks for one of the two kinds (drawn; round B is drawn again from what the shelf still needs).

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (a can or a bottle; tests the word asked).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Una lata!" or NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Una botella!"
   - Right: the frog tosses it; he catches it and stands it on its ring. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog tosses the other kind; don Pepe's hands are set for the wrong size and it slips through: a bottle breaks on the floor, a can rolls out of the door. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Esta es una lata." (Or: "…una botella.") The sign is on the right kind; he touches one; it goes up.
7. **Decision B, the same game.** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis finds the glasses in the box and hands them up. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Aquí tiene, don Pepe!" Don Pepe puts them on. The frog tosses up what is left on the floor, one by one. Don Pepe counts the shelf. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Cuántas latas hay? Una, dos, tres." (As many as there are; with two: "Una, dos." and he looks at the bare ring.) NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Y cuántas botellas hay? Una, dos, tres." (The same.)
9. The bell: the señora comes in. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días! Necesito tres botellas." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." COQUÍ [TURN: buenos días (buenos días, buenos dias)]: "¡Buenos días, señora!" (said as she comes in, before her line)
    - Nothing lost: she takes three bottles; the señor, behind her, three cans. Stars and a heart.
    - A bottle lost: she gets two and looks at her empty hand. NARR: "No hay más botellas."
    - A can lost: the señor gets two. NARR: "No hay más latas."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lata 5 (steps 1, 1, 2, 2, 4), botella 4 (steps 1, 3, 3, 4). Not deciding: caja 2 before the game and once more in the ending ("Luis busca en la caja." is added at step 8 if the checker counts fewer than three); ¿cuántas? 2 before the game, 2 after.
**A miss is filed under:** lata or botella, whichever was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two. Don Pepe's hands are the same for both lines.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): dos; buenos días.

## Story 40: Siete latas

- **Goal he can see:** on the low table in the window, don Pepe's drawing pinned to the wall: a pyramid of cans, four rows, with a little flag on top. On the floor, a crate full of cans.
- **At stake:** the cans. Each row is carried to the window as a pack: a row of cans held in a paper band. A pack of the wrong size does not fit its row: it slides off, and two of its cans roll out of the door and are gone. What is left in the crate at the end makes the top.
- **New words:** cuatro, cinco, seis, siete.
- **Back again:** uno, dos, tres, lata, ¿cuántas?, hay, dame, más, mira, otra vez, aquí.

**Start:** don Pepe at the window table; Luis and the frog at the crate of cans.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Don Pepe points at his drawing. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Mira! Latas, latas y más latas." He lays the bottom row, can by can, and counts. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete. ¡Siete latas!"
2. The second row, can by can. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis. Seis latas."
3. Luis bands two packs at the crate: a row of five and a row of four. NARR: "Luis dice: Aquí hay cinco latas. Y aquí hay cuatro." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, dame cinco latas." Ask (signed, the pack of five): "¿Dónde hay cinco latas?" He touches it: the frog and Luis carry it over; don Pepe sets it on. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Cinco!"
4. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y ahora, cuatro latas." Ask (signed, the pack of four): "Toca las cuatro latas." He touches it; it goes on top. Don Pepe sticks the flag in. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Siete, seis, cinco y cuatro. ¡Listo!"

**The turn**

5. The bell: Canelo runs in at the door, wagging, and bumps the table. The pyramid comes down; the cans roll back to the crate. NARR: "¡Ay, no! ¡Canelo!" Luis catches him by the collar. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Canelo, ven aquí!" Don Pepe looks at the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Otra vez, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si), otra vez (otra vez)]: "¡Sí, otra vez!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Pepe lays the bottom row again himself and counts it: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete." Then each round two packs stand on the crate: the row he asks for, and one with a can more or a can fewer (drawn; they stand side by side with a gap, so the cans can be counted).

6. **Decision A, touch a pack (two packs; tests seis).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Dame seis latas."
   - Right: the pack goes on. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Seis!" A star.
   - Wrong: the pack is set on, does not fit, slides off; two cans roll out of the door. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Aquí hay seis latas." Luis counts the right pack with his finger. The sign is on it; he touches it; it goes on.
7. **Decision B, touch a pack (two packs; tests cinco).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Dame cinco latas." Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Don Pepe tips the crate towards us: what is left. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Cuántas latas hay?"
    - Nothing lost, four left: he sets them on top, counting. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro. ¡Cuatro latas!" The flag goes in. Stars and a heart.
    - One miss, two left: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos. Dos latas." Two cans stand on top; no flag.
    - Two misses, none left: he shows the empty crate. NARR: "No hay más latas." The pyramid ends flat at its third row.
9. Canelo sits beside the frog, away from the table, good now. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está Canelo?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Canelo está aquí!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** seis 4 (steps 1, 2, 2, 4, and once more in the bottom row of the game), cinco 6, cuatro 6, siete 4. Every count is said while the cans appear one by one.
**A miss is filed under:** seis (A), cinco (B).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two: both packs stand ready; nothing marks the row's width on the table.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 9): sí and otra vez; aquí.

## Story 41: Diez botellas

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's wooden box for the party on the floor, with ten holes for ten bottles, seven of them filled. On the top shelf, above the ladder, don Pepe's last maltas in a row: seven bottles.
- **At stake:** the maltas on the top shelf. The old ladder wobbles: every time don Pepe has to climb down to look for himself, one bottle shakes off the shelf and breaks. Abuela's box needs three; the señora's and the señor's boxes need three more between them; one is over.
- **New words:** ocho, nueve, diez, otro (heard mostly as otra).
- **Back again:** cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, botella, caja, ¿cuántas?, hay, necesito, sí, por favor, más, aquí tiene, cuidado.

**Start:** don Pepe at the foot of the ladder; Luis with abuela's note (a drawing of the box with ten bottles); the frog beside abuela's box.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis shows the note. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela necesita diez botellas." **(the drink is malta; it is never named in a line that decides)** The frog hops along the box, bottle by bottle. NARR: "¿Cuántas botellas hay en la caja? Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete."
2. Don Pepe climbs; the ladder wobbles and the bottles on the top shelf rattle. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Ay! ¡Cuidado!" He takes a bottle down and hands it to Luis, who puts it in the box. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí hay otra botella." Ask (signed, the number bubble that says "ocho"; the other bubble says "siete"): "¿Cuántas hay ahora?" He touches it. NARR: "¡Ocho! Ocho botellas."
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Otra?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si), por favor (por favor)]: "¡Sí, por favor!" Another comes down. Ask (signed, the bubble "nueve"; the other says "ocho"): "¿Y ahora?" He touches it. NARR: "¡Nueve! Nueve botellas."
4. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y otra." It goes in: the box is full. NARR: "¡Diez! Diez botellas para abuela." Luis carries the box to the door and sets it down there.

**The turn**

5. The bell: the señora comes in with a box of her own, and the señor with his; each sets it on the floor by the frog. Bottles stand in them already. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días! Necesito diez botellas." NARR: "El señor dice: Y otra caja, don Pepe. Yo también necesito diez." Don Pepe goes up the ladder; from there he cannot see into the boxes. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One box holds eight bottles and the other nine (drawn which is whose). A box is seen from above: ten holes in two rows of five, so the bottles can be counted. Don Pepe asks from the ladder; the frog answers with two number bubbles: the right number and one beside it (eight: "ocho" and "nueve"; nine: "nueve" and "ocho" or "diez", drawn).

6. **Decision A, say a number (two bubbles; tests ocho or nueve).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, ¿cuántas botellas tiene la señora?"
   - Right: COQUÍ: "¡Ocho!" (or "¡Nueve!") Don Pepe hands down what is missing, one at a time. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Otra. Y otra. ¡Diez!" (For nine: "Otra. ¡Diez!") A star.
   - Wrong: don Pepe starts to hand down the wrong number; Luis shakes his head and holds up the box. Don Pepe climbs down to look: the ladder wobbles and a bottle falls off the top shelf and breaks. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho." (He counts with a finger; to nine if it holds nine.) The sign is on the right bubble; he touches it. Don Pepe climbs again and hands down what is missing.
7. **Decision B, the same game: the señor's box.** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Y cuántas botellas tiene el señor?" Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The señora and the señor lift their boxes. Don Pepe looks along the top shelf.
    - Nothing lost, one bottle left: he brings it down and opens it for the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y esta es para Coquí." COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, don Pepe!" Stars and a heart.
    - One miss, none left: both boxes are full; the shelf is bare. NARR: "No hay más botellas."
    - Two misses: the señor's box has nine. NARR: "El señor tiene nueve botellas. No hay más." He goes out looking at the empty hole.
9. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias! ¡Adiós!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós, señora!" Luis lifts abuela's box. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van a casa con diez botellas."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ocho 3 (step 2 three times, counting the bubble's own voice not at all: "ocho" is said by the narrator twice and is the signed answer once); nueve 3 (step 3); diez 4 (steps 1, 4, 4, 5). Not deciding: otra 5 before the game.
**A miss is filed under:** the number that was right (ocho or nueve).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two: both bubbles look the same and only speak.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): sí and por favor; adiós.

## Needs (for the scene's designer and the code lane)

The set `estantes`:
- View: `wide` (zoom 1).
- States: `gafas` (don Pepe's glasses on or off); the door shut or open.
- In the set: three shelves on the back wall (the middle one with six dust rings in story 39; the top one with a row of bottles in story 41); the ladder; the floor; the window with its low table and the pinned drawing of a pyramid (story 40); the street door.
- Things he can touch: lata, botella (the ones on the floor; any of a kind counts), caja (the big cardboard box); pack_a, pack_b (the two banded rows of cans on the crate); the number bubbles; pepe, luis, senora, senor, coqui, perro; estante (the shelf, a poke).
- Spots: cajon (the frog's crate), piso_izq, piso_der, escalera_pie, escalera_alto, vitrina (the window table), puerta, cola1, cola2.
- New sprites: can, bottle, banded pack of n cans (n from 4 to 7), the pyramid's rows, a box seen from above with ten holes and n bottles, broken bottle, don Pepe on the ladder (hands out; reaching up; climbing down), his glasses.
- Speaker keys: `pepe`, `luis`, `senora`, `senor`.

Acts (name: what it does; story and step):
- `sube`, `baja`: don Pepe up or down the ladder; `baja` with `tiembla` shakes a bottle off the top shelf (39: 2; 41: 2, 5, 6, 7).
- `saca` {k}: he lifts a can or a bottle out of the box to be seen (39: 1).
- `lanza` {k, ok}: the frog tosses a can or a bottle up; ok: caught and stood on its ring; not ok: it slips through, breaks or rolls out (39: 2, 3, 6, 7, 8).
- `cuenta` {k, n, quien}: a finger, or the frog, goes along n things, in time with the counting line (39: 4, 8; 40: 6; 41: 1, 6, 7).
- `gafas` {que}: they fall into the box; Luis finds them (39: 5, 8).
- `cliente` {quien, lleva, n}: comes in, takes n of a kind, goes (39: 9; 41: 5, 8, 9).
- `fila` {n, una}: don Pepe lays a row of n cans can by can, in time with the counting line (40: 1, 2, 6). `packs` {a, b}: two banded packs stand on the crate (40: 3, 6, 7). `pone` {n, ok}: a pack is carried over and set on; not ok: it slides off and two cans roll out (40: 3, 4, 6, 7). `bandera`: the flag (40: 4, 8). `tumba`: Canelo runs in, the pyramid falls, Luis holds him (40: 5). `resto` {n}: the crate tipped to show what is left; the top is made of it (40: 8).
- `cajade` {quien, n}: a box of ten holes with n bottles, seen from above, beside the frog (41: 1, 5). `pasa` {a, n}: don Pepe hands n bottles down, one at a time, into a box (41: 2, 3, 4, 6, 7). `lleva` {quien}: a box is carried to the door, or out (41: 4, 8, 9). `abre41`: he opens the last bottle for the frog (41: 8).
- `final`: stars and a heart when nothing was lost.

# Los estantes: three stories (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 4, El colmado. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `estantes`). Stories 39 to 41. He has finished stories 1 to 38.

Scene words (page 02): cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez, lata, botella, caja, ¿cuántos?, otro.

The picture: the shelves at the back of don Pepe's shop, further along the wall than his counter. One view, `wide`: a run of three long shelves on the wall; a wooden ladder against them, with don Pepe on it when he works; open floor in front, where the delivery stands (crates and boxes); a low table with a cloth at the left, for a display; a bunch of plantains hanging on the wall. The street door is out of the picture at the left: whoever comes in walks in from the left edge, and whoever waits stands there. The frog stands on a crate on the floor.

No numeral is ever on screen in this scene: counts are things to be counted. Answer bubbles for numbers speak their word and show nothing but a mouth.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as before. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places.

## Story 39: Latas y botellas

- **Goal he can see:** the middle shelf is bare, with six places marked by rings of dust: three small ones and three large ones. At the left edge the señora and the señor stand looking at it, waiting. On the floor: the delivery, a big cardboard box with three cans and three bottles in it.
- **At stake:** the six things for the shelf. Don Pepe, up the ladder without his glasses, catches by ear: his hands are set for what he asked for, and they look the same either way. Tossed the other thing, he fumbles: a bottle breaks on the floor, a can rolls out of the door. Its place on the shelf stays bare, and at the end somebody goes without.
- **New words:** lata, botella, caja, ¿cuántos? (heard as ¿cuántas?).
- **Back again:** uno, dos, tres, hay, dame, tira, coge, aquí, colmado, necesito, aquí tiene, buenos días.

**Start:** morning. Don Pepe drags the big cardboard box in at the door; Luis and the frog beside it. The señora and the señor at the left edge.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Don Pepe tiene una caja grande." He opens it; cans and bottles show. He lifts one can. NARR: "En la caja hay latas. Esta es una lata." He lifts a bottle. NARR: "Y esta es una botella."
2. Don Pepe climbs the ladder and holds his hands out. Ask (signed, a can): "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, dame una lata." He touches it: the frog tosses it up; don Pepe catches it and stands it on a small ring. NARR: "Coquí tira la lata. ¡Don Pepe coge la lata!"
3. Ask (signed, a bottle): "Don Pepe dice: Ahora, dame una botella." He touches it: up it goes, onto a large ring.
4. Luis counts what is left in the box, a finger on each. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Cuántas latas hay? Una, dos. Dos latas." His finger moves over the bottles and stops; he looks at the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y cuántas botellas hay?" COQUÍ [TURN: dos (dos)]: "¡Dos!"

**The turn**

5. Don Pepe's glasses slip off his nose and fall into the big box. He feels for them and does not find them. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Ay! No veo nada. ¡Ayúdame, Coquí!" **(no veo nada, ayúdame: heard; the picture carries them)** From now on he looks straight ahead, hands out. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two cans and two bottles are left. Each round don Pepe asks for a can or a bottle, drawn afresh.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (a can or a bottle; tests the word asked).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, dame una lata." or NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, dame una botella."
   - Right: the frog tosses it; he catches it and stands it on its ring. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog tosses the other kind; it slips through his hands: a bottle breaks on the floor, a can rolls out of the door. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Esta es una lata." (Or: "…una botella.") The sign is on the right kind; he touches one; it goes up.
7. **Decision B, the same game, drawn again.** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis looks in the box and finds the glasses. NARR: "Luis busca en la caja." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Aquí tiene, don Pepe!" Don Pepe puts them on. The frog tosses up what is left, one by one.
9. The bell: the señora and the señor come in. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenos días (buenos días, buenos dias)]: "¡Buenos días, señora!" NARR: "La señora dice: Necesito tres botellas." NARR: "El señor dice: Y yo necesito tres latas." Don Pepe counts the shelf with his finger. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Cuántas botellas hay?"
    - Nothing lost: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres. Aquí tiene." She takes three bottles; the señor three cans. Stars and a heart.
    - A bottle lost: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos. No hay más botellas." She gets two and looks at her empty hand. (Two bottles lost: "Don Pepe dice: Una. No hay más botellas.")
    - A can lost (one or two): the señor gets what there is. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: No hay más latas."
    - Each kind has its own line: with nothing lost of a kind, its customer gets three.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lata 5 (steps 1, 1, 2, 2, 4), botella 3 (steps 1, 3, 4). Not deciding: caja 3 (steps 1, 1, 8); ¿cuántas? 2 before the game, 1 after.
**A miss is filed under:** lata or botella, whichever was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, in each round on its own. Don Pepe's hands are the same for both lines, and what the shelf still lacks does not say what he will ask for.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): dos; buenos días.

## Story 40: Siete latas

- **Goal he can see:** the low table, and don Pepe's drawing pinned to the wall over it: a pyramid of cans, four rows, with a little flag on top. On the floor, a crate full of cans.
- **At stake:** the cans. Each row is carried to the window in a net: a bunch of cans, which have to be counted. A net with the wrong number does not make its row: don Pepe sends it back, and on the way two cans fall out of it and roll out of the door. What is left in the crate at the end makes the top.
- **New words:** cuatro, cinco, seis, siete.
- **Back again:** uno, dos, tres, lata, ¿cuántas?, hay, dame, más, mucho, mira, otra vez, aquí.

**Start:** don Pepe at the low table; Luis and the frog at the crate of cans.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Don Pepe points at his drawing. NARR: "Don Pepe tiene muchas latas." He lays the bottom row, can by can, and counts. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete. ¡Siete latas!"
2. The second row, can by can. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis. Seis latas."
3. Luis fills two nets at the crate: five cans in one, four in the other. NARR: "Luis dice: Aquí hay cinco latas. Y aquí hay cuatro." Ask (signed, the net of five): "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, dame cinco latas." He touches it: the frog and Luis carry it over; don Pepe lays the row. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Cinco!"
4. Ask (signed, the net of four): "Don Pepe dice: Y ahora, cuatro latas." He touches it; the row goes on top. Don Pepe sticks the flag in.

**The turn**

5. The bell: Canelo runs in at the door, wagging, and bumps the table. The pyramid comes down; the cans roll back to the crate; Canelo runs out again with the drawing in his mouth. NARR: "¡Ay, no! ¡Canelo!" Luis runs after him and brings him back by the collar, without the drawing. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Canelo, ven acá!" **(acá: heard)** Don Pepe looks at the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Otra vez, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: más (más, mas), lata (latas)]: "¡Sí, más latas!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Pepe lays the bottom row again himself; it appears can by can. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Siete latas aquí." There is no drawing now. Each round two nets stand on the crate: the number he asks for, and one that is two more or two fewer (A: six, and four; B: five, and seven), side by side with a gap, so the cans can be counted.

6. **Decision A, touch a net (two nets; tests seis).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Dame seis latas."
   - Right: the row goes on. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Seis!" A star.
   - Wrong: the net is carried over; don Pepe counts it with his finger and shakes his head; on the way back two cans fall out of it and roll out of the door. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Aquí hay seis latas." Luis counts the right net with his finger. The sign is on it; he touches it; the row goes on.
7. **Decision B, touch a net (two nets; tests cinco).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Dame cinco latas." Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Don Pepe tips the crate towards us: what is left. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Cuántas latas hay?"
    - Nothing lost, four left: he sets them on top, counting. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos, tres, cuatro. ¡Cuatro latas!" The flag goes in. Stars and a heart.
    - One miss, two left: NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Una, dos. Dos latas." Two cans stand on top; no flag.
    - Two misses, none left: he shows the empty crate. NARR: "No hay más latas." The pyramid ends flat at its third row.
9. Luis looks round the shop for the dog. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está Canelo?" Canelo sits beside the frog, away from the table, good now. COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Aquí está!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** seis 3 (steps 1, 2, 2), cinco 5 (steps 1, 2, 3, 3, 3). Not deciding: cuatro 5, siete 3. Every count is said while the cans appear one by one.
**A miss is filed under:** seis (A), cinco (B).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two. The drawing is gone, and the other net is once smaller and once larger, so neither "the bigger one" nor "one fewer than the row below" is right both times.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 9): más and lata; aquí.

## Story 41: Diez botellas

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's wooden box for the party on the floor, with ten holes for ten bottles, seven of them filled. On the top shelf, above the ladder, don Pepe's last maltas in a row.
- **At stake:** the maltas on the top shelf. The old ladder wobbles: every time don Pepe has to climb down to look for himself, one bottle shakes off the shelf and breaks. The shelf holds what abuela's box and the two customers' boxes need, and one over.
- **New words:** ocho, nueve, diez, otro (heard mostly as otra).
- **Back again:** cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, botella, caja, ¿cuántas?, hay, necesito, sí, por favor, más, también, cuidado, gracias.

**Start:** don Pepe at the foot of the ladder; Luis with abuela's note (a drawing of the box with ten bottles); the frog beside abuela's box.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis shows the note. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela necesita diez botellas." **(the drink is malta; it is never named in a line that decides)** The frog hops along the box, bottle by bottle. NARR: "¿Cuántas botellas hay en la caja? Una, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete."
2. Don Pepe climbs; the ladder wobbles and the bottles on the top shelf rattle. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Ay! ¡Cuidado!" He takes a bottle down and hands it to Luis, who puts it in the box. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí va otra botella." Ask (signed, the number bubble that says "ocho"; the other bubble says "siete"): "¿Cuántas hay ahora?" He touches it. NARR: "¡Ocho! Ocho botellas."
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Otra?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, por favor!" Another comes down. Ask (signed, the bubble "nueve"; the other says "ocho"): "¿Y ahora?" He touches it. NARR: "¡Nueve! Nueve botellas."
4. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y otra." It goes in: the box is full. NARR: "¡Diez! Diez botellas para abuela." Luis carries the box to the door and sets it down there.

**The turn**

5. The bell: the señora comes in with a box of her own, and the señor with his; each sets it on the floor and stands by it. Bottles stand in them already. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días! Necesito diez botellas." NARR: "El señor dice: Y yo también. Diez botellas." Don Pepe goes up the ladder; from there he cannot see into the boxes. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Each box holds eight bottles or nine, drawn on its own (so both may hold the same). A box is seen from above: ten holes in two rows of five, so the bottles can be counted. The box he is asked about twinkles once. The frog answers with two number bubbles: the right number and one beside it (eight: "ocho" and "nueve"; nine: "nueve" and "ocho" or "diez", drawn).

6. **Decision A, say a number (two bubbles; tests ocho or nueve).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, ¿cuántas botellas tiene la señora?"
   - Right: COQUÍ: "¡Ocho!" (or "¡Nueve!") Don Pepe hands down what is missing, one at a time. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Otra. Y otra. ¡Diez!" (For nine: "Otra. ¡Diez!") A star.
   - Wrong: don Pepe starts to hand down the wrong number; Luis shakes his head and holds up the box. Don Pepe climbs down to look: the ladder wobbles and a bottle falls off the top shelf and breaks. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Hay ocho." (Or: "Hay nueve.") Luis's finger runs along the bottles. The sign is on the right bubble; he touches it. Don Pepe climbs again and hands down what is missing.
7. **Decision B, the same game: the señor's box.** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Y cuántas botellas tiene el señor?" Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The señora and the señor lift their boxes. Don Pepe looks along the top shelf.
    - Nothing lost, one bottle left: he brings it down and opens it for the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y esta es para ti, Coquí." COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, don Pepe!" Stars and a heart.
    - One miss, none left: both boxes are full; the shelf is bare. NARR: "No hay más botellas."
    - Two misses: the señor's box, the second one served, is one short. NARR: "El señor tiene nueve botellas. No hay más." He goes out looking at the empty hole.
9. The señora waves from the door. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós, señora!" Luis lifts abuela's box. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van para la casa con diez botellas."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ocho 2 by the narrator and once as the signed answer (step 2); nueve the same (step 3); diez 5 (steps 1, 4, 4, 5, 5). Not deciding: otra 4 before the game. "Otro" and "ocho" are never next to each other: after "otra botella" the count comes as its own line.
**A miss is filed under:** the number that was right (ocho or nueve).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, in each round on its own: both bubbles look the same and only speak.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): sí; adiós.

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers saw draft 1 with only the owner's rules: one as a beginner, one for Puerto Rican Spanish.

Fixed after the beginner: no second round can be worked out from the first (39 and 41 draw each round on its own; 40's rows come in nets that must be counted, the drawing is gone before the game, and the other net is once smaller and once larger); the asks of the openings are said once, by don Pepe, not once by him and once by the narrator; 39's first turn answers a question nobody has answered yet, its customers are in the picture from the start and both say what they need, and don Pepe's hands give nothing away; 40's echo turn is "¡Sí, más latas!", its label line and its second count are gone; 41 says which box is asked about, shortens the count after a miss, and its last turn answers a thanks.

Fixed after the Spanish: "No veo nada. ¡Ayúdame, Coquí!" ("mira tú" is heard as "well, fancy that"); "¡Canelo, ven acá!"; "¡Aquí está!"; "Aquí va otra botella."; "Y esta es para ti, Coquí."; "van para la casa".

Left as written: the same pattern of don Pepe counting aloud (it is the teaching); "caja" for the cardboard box and for the bottle box, which is what people say.

## Needs (for the scene's designer and the code lane)

The set `estantes`:
- View: `wide` (zoom 1).
- States: `gafas` (don Pepe's glasses on or off); the door shut or open.
- In the set: three shelves on the back wall (the middle one with six dust rings in story 39; the top one with a row of bottles in story 41); the ladder; the floor; the window with its low table and the pinned drawing of a pyramid (story 40); the street door.
- Things he can touch: lata, botella (the ones on the floor; any of a kind counts), caja (the big cardboard box); red_a, red_b (the two nets of cans on the crate); the number bubbles; pepe, luis, senora, senor, coqui, perro; estante (the shelf, a poke).
- Spots: cajon (the frog's crate), piso_izq, piso_der, escalera_pie, escalera_alto, vitrina (the window table), puerta, cola1, cola2.
- New sprites: can, bottle, a net holding n cans in a bunch (n from 4 to 7), the pyramid's rows, a box seen from above with ten holes and n bottles, broken bottle, don Pepe on the ladder (hands out; reaching up; climbing down), his glasses.
- Speaker keys: `pepe`, `luis`, `senora`, `senor`.

Acts (name: what it does; story and step):
- `sube`, `baja`: don Pepe up or down the ladder; `baja` with `tiembla` shakes a bottle off the top shelf (39: 2; 41: 2, 5, 6, 7).
- `saca` {k}: he lifts a can or a bottle out of the box to be seen (39: 1).
- `lanza` {k, ok}: the frog tosses a can or a bottle up; ok: caught and stood on its ring; not ok: it slips through, breaks or rolls out (39: 2, 3, 6, 7, 8).
- `cuenta` {k, n, quien}: a finger, or the frog, goes along n things, in time with the counting line (39: 4, 8; 40: 6; 41: 1, 6, 7).
- `gafas` {que}: they fall into the box; Luis finds them (39: 5, 8).
- `cliente` {quien, lleva, n}: waits at the window; comes in, takes n of a kind, goes (39: start, 9; 41: 5, 8, 9).
- `fila` {n, una}: don Pepe lays a row of n cans can by can, in time with the counting line (40: 1, 2, 6). `redes` {a, b}: two nets of cans stand on the crate (40: 3, 6, 7). `pone` {n, ok}: a net is carried over and its row laid; not ok: don Pepe counts it, shakes his head, and two cans fall out on the way back and roll out of the door (40: 3, 4, 6, 7). `bandera`: the flag (40: 4, 8). `tumba`: Canelo runs in, the pyramid falls, he runs out with the drawing; Luis brings him back (40: 5). `resto` {n}: the crate tipped to show what is left; the top is made of it (40: 8).
- `cajade` {quien, n}: a box of ten holes with n bottles, seen from above, beside the frog (41: 1, 5). `pasa` {a, n}: don Pepe hands n bottles down, one at a time, into a box (41: 2, 3, 4, 6, 7). `lleva` {quien}: a box is carried to the door, or out (41: 4, 8, 9). `abre41`: he opens the last bottle for the frog (41: 8).
- `final`: stars and a heart when nothing was lost.
- `ya`: waits for an act that was started with `suelto: 1` (as in every scene). The counting acts (`cuenta`, `fila`, `pasa`) and `lanza`, `tumba` are started with `suelto: 1`, their line is said while they run, then `ya`.
- `abre39`: don Pepe opens the big box (39: 1). `busca39`: he feels for his glasses and does not find them (39: 5). `mira` {quien, a}: somebody looks at something: don Pepe at his drawing (40: 1), at the frog (40: 5), along the top shelf (41: 8); Luis round the shop (40: 9); a customer at an empty hand or an empty hole (39: 9; 41: 8). `nota41`: Luis shows abuela's note (41: 1). `tiembla`: the ladder wobbles and the bottles rattle (41: 2). `niega41`: don Pepe starts to hand down the wrong number; Luis shakes his head and holds up the box (41: 6, 7). `saluda` {quien}: somebody waves (41: 9). `brilla`: the engine's own `{"set": {"twinkle": key}}` (the box asked about, 41: 6, 7).
- The number bubbles are di_siete, di_ocho, di_nueve, di_diez; each speaks its number in the frog's voice ("¡Siete!", "¡Ocho!", "¡Nueve!", "¡Diez!").
- Values: `gafas` que caen or halla; `cuenta` quien luis, pepe or coqui; `lanza` ok 1 or 0; `lleva` quien the box's owner.

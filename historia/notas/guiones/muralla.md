# La muralla, de noche: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `muralla`). Stories 117 to 119. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 116. Story 119 is the last story of the level.

Scene words (page 02): porque, ¿por qué?, pero, todos, nadie, alguien, nos vemos, hasta pronto, gracias por venir.
How they are shared out: 117 todos, nadie, alguien; 118 ¿por qué?, porque, pero; 119 nos vemos, hasta pronto, gracias por venir.

The picture: the top of the fort's wall, at night. One view. At the back, the wall's low parapet, and beyond it the sea, the moon and the stars. On the walk, at the left and at the right, two sentry boxes of stone, each with a door (the left one red, the right one blue) and a small window with a light in it. Between the two, over the middle, a line with three lanterns. In the middle, against the parapet, a bench. At the left front, a small table with a chair beside it. At the right front, the head of the steps that go down to the gate. Far below, beyond the parapet at the right, the road. No roof.

People: the family (abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen); the two músicos; don Pepe, the maestra, the piragüero, the señor, the señora.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign. While he waits, something always moves: the lanterns sway, the sea glints, a pigeon stirs at a window, the guagua's lights blink.

## Story 117: Todos en la foto

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's camera on its three legs, facing the middle of the walk, where people gather under the three lanterns. She counts heads on her fingers, and shakes her head.
- **At stake:** the lanterns over the photo (3). Pigeons sleep in a sentry box when nobody is in it. Open the door of an empty one, and they fly out and knock a lantern off the line.
- **New words:** todos, nadie, alguien.
- **Back again:** puerta, rojo, azul, detrás, hay, no hay, falta, aquí, ¿dónde está?, pájaro, luz, viento, cuidado, otra vez, ahora, uno, dos, tres, por favor, sí, no.

**Start:** night; the moon over the sea. In the middle of the walk the family and the guests come together under the lanterns. Abuela at her camera, at the front. Both sentry boxes have their doors shut and their windows lit. The frog beside the camera.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Una foto! ¡Todos aquí, por favor!" People move closer together. Abuela counts on her fingers. NARR: "Abuela dice: Uno, dos, tres... No están todos. Falta alguien."
2. A giggle; the red box's window flickers. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Shh! Detrás de la puerta roja hay alguien." (signed, the red door.) He touches it: the frog pulls it open: Carmen, with her hands over her mouth. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Aquí estoy!" She runs to the others.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y detrás de la puerta azul? ¿Hay alguien?" (signed, the blue door.) He touches it: the frog pulls it open: an empty box; one pigeon on a ledge opens an eye, and a lantern on the line swings. NARR: "Luis dice: No. Aquí no hay nadie." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Nadie! Hay un pájaro." Luis shuts the door softly.
4. Abuela counts again, and nods. NARR: "Abuela dice: Puerta roja, nadie. Puerta azul, nadie. ¡Ahora sí están todos!" She looks about her feet. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿dónde estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, estoy aquí)]: "¡Aquí!" He hops in front of the others.

**The turn**

5. Abuela bends to her camera. A gust of wind: the three lanterns flicker, and for a moment the walk is dark. When the light comes back, one person is gone from the group, and both doors are shut. Abuela looks up, and counts. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Otra vez falta alguien." Luis runs to the red box's window and looks in, runs to the blue box's window and looks in, and comes back to the frog. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Each round, somebody is in one sentry box and nobody is in the other (drawn); pigeons sleep in whichever box is empty. Luis, back beside the frog with his eyes on abuela, says what is behind one of the two doors (which door he names is drawn, on its own): somebody, or nobody. The frog opens a door: the one with somebody in it.

6. **Decision A, touch a door (the red, the blue; tests alguien against nadie).** NARR: "Luis dice: Detrás de la puerta roja, nadie." (Or, drawn: "Detrás de la puerta roja, alguien." "Detrás de la puerta azul, nadie." "Detrás de la puerta azul, alguien.")
   - Right: the door opens: somebody blinks in the light, and goes to join the others. A star.
   - Wrong: the door opens on an empty box: pigeons burst out over the frog's head, and one knocks a lantern off the line: it goes out. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! Detrás de la puerta roja, nadie." A cross. The sign is on the other door; he touches it: it opens, and somebody comes out to the others. The pigeons settle again.
7. **Decision B, touch a door (the same game).** Another gust, another moment of dark: somebody else is gone, and the doors are shut. Luis looks in at both windows again. NARR: "Luis dice: Detrás de la puerta azul, alguien." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Everybody stands under the lanterns that are left.
   - Three lanterns: a bright photo, every face in it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two lanterns.
   - One lantern, and the moon: a dark photo, with white teeth in it.
9. Abuela bends to her camera, and lifts a finger for each count. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora sí. Uno, dos..." COQUÍ [TURN: tres (tres, y tres)]: "¡Tres!" A flash. The photo slides out. NARR: "En la foto están todos. No falta nadie."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** alguien 4 (steps 1, 2, 3, 5), nadie 3 (steps 3, 4: three lines). Not deciding: todos 3 (steps 1, 4: three lines) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (alguien or nadie).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the door he names and what is behind it are drawn apart, and the game's lines have no "hay" and no "no".
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): aquí; tres.

## Story 118: ¿Por qué no bailas?

- **Goal he can see:** the two músicos sit on the bench with the guitar and the drum. On the parapet beside them a tall candle burns in a glass, with three red rings painted round it. The músico with the hat points at it, and then at his guitar: one last song, as long as the candle.
- **At stake:** the candle's rings (3). Whoever does not dance is waited for; a wrong thing carried to him and back is time, and the candle burns down a ring.
- **New words:** ¿por qué?, porque, pero.
- **Back again:** baila, música, canción, tengo sed, tengo hambre, cansado, quiero, me gusta, jugo, silla, feliz, contento, ¿cómo estás?, ahora, tú, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The músicos on the bench; the candle burns, whole. People dance on the walk. On the small table at the left front: a glass of juice, a plate of food; beside the table, a chair. Abuelo leans on the parapet. The frog on the table.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen pulls at abuelo's hand. NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuelo, ¿por qué no bailas?" **(bailas: a form of baila)** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Porque estoy cansado." (signed, the chair.) He touches it: the frog pushes the chair to him; he sits down, and breathes out. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias, Coquí."
2. Luis stands apart with his tongue out. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis, ¿por qué no bailas?" NARR: "Luis dice: Porque tengo sed." (signed, the glass of juice.) He touches it: the frog carries it over; Luis drinks it off, and goes to dance.
3. Don Pepe stands with a hand on his stomach. NARR: "Abuela dice: Pepe, ¿y tú? ¿Por qué no?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Quiero bailar, pero tengo hambre." (signed, the plate.) He touches it: the frog carries it over; don Pepe eats, and goes to dance, with the plate in his hand.
4. Abuelo gets up from his chair. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Estoy cansado, pero quiero bailar." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Por qué, abuelo?" NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Porque me gusta la música!" He dances with abuela, slowly. Luis puts the chair, a new glass and a new plate back at the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tú estás cansado?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no, yo no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo estoy cansada, pero contenta."

**The turn**

5. The músico with the hat lifts his guitar: the last song begins, and the candle's flame stands up. Two guests do not dance: the maestra and the piragüero stand at the parapet, a little apart, with their hands at their sides. By the table: the glass of juice, the plate, the chair. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, dancing, calls to one who does not dance; he says why not (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): he is thirsty, he is hungry, he is tired. He stands still, with his hands at his sides and his eyes on the dancers. The frog takes him the juice, the plate, or the chair. A thing that has been used stays with the one who used it: round B has two things left.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the juice, the plate, the chair; tests what comes after porque).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Maestra, ¿y tú? ¿No bailas?" NARR: "La maestra dice: No, porque tengo sed." (Or, drawn: "No, porque tengo hambre." "No, porque estoy cansada.") **(cansada: a form of cansado)**
   - Right: she drinks (or eats a bite; or sits a moment), and goes to dance. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes her another thing; she looks at it, and shakes her head. NARR: "La maestra dice: No, Coquí. Tengo sed." The frog takes it back; the candle burns down past a ring. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: she drinks, and goes to dance.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the other guest; another reason; he says it with pero).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Por qué no bailas?" NARR: "El piragüero dice: Quiero bailar, pero tengo hambre." (Or, drawn: "Quiero bailar, pero tengo sed." "Quiero bailar, pero estoy cansado.")
   - Right and wrong as in A. Wrong: NARR: "El piragüero dice: No, Coquí. Tengo hambre."

**The ending**

8. Everybody dances; the song goes on for as long as the candle.
   - Three rings: a long song; the moon moves a little way across the sky, and everybody is still dancing. Stars and a heart.
   - Two rings: a song, and a bow at its end.
   - One ring: the flame gutters almost at once; the dancers have only just begun, and stop with a foot in the air. They laugh.
9. The candle goes out; the músicos put down the guitar and the drum. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué noche! Coquí, ¿cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: feliz (feliz, muy feliz)]: "¡Feliz!" NARR: "¿Por qué baila la gente? Porque está feliz."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿por qué? 4 (steps 1, 2, 3, 4), porque 3 (steps 1, 2, 4), pero 3 (steps 3, 4: three lines). What decides is the reason after porque or pero: tengo sed, tengo hambre, cansado (words he has; estoy, tiene and the other forms in these lines are forms he has heard since place 3); each is heard once with its thing in the opening.
**Forms:** bailas with baila; cansada with cansado.
**A miss is filed under:** porque in round A, pero in round B (the reason after it is what he missed).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A; one in two in round B (round A's thing has been used, and round B's reason is never round A's).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): no; feliz.

## Story 119: Nos vemos

- **Goal he can see:** far below, on the road, a guagua with its lights on and its door open; four of its windows are lit and empty. Its horn sounds once.
- **At stake:** the guests who catch the guagua (4 windows). Somebody who goes down the steps with the wrong thing has to climb them again; he sits down on the bench out of breath, and the guagua goes without him.
- **New words:** nos vemos, hasta pronto, gracias por venir.
- **Back again:** guagua, se va, ya, rápido, guitarra, bolsa, sombrero, caja, libro, mi, música, bonito, cansado, está bien, carro, gente, hasta luego, adiós, buenas noches, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** late night; the moon is low. The lanterns burn. At the head of the steps, at the right front, abuela and abuelo stand to see people off. On the small table, the guests' things in a row: a guitar, a hat, a cloth shopping bag, a box, a book. The frog on the table. Four guests sit on the bench. Below, the guagua waits.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The músico with the hat comes to the steps. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias por venir!" NARR: "El músico dice: ¡Gracias a ustedes! ¿Y mi guitarra?" (signed, the guitar.) He touches it: the frog pushes it to him; he slings it on his back. NARR: "El músico dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¡Hasta pronto!" He goes down the steps.
2. The other músico, with his drum under his arm. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias por venir. ¡Qué bonita la música! ¡Hasta pronto!" NARR: "El otro músico dice: ¡Nos vemos, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: hasta luego (hasta luego, adiós)]: "¡Hasta luego!" He goes down; below, the two músicos walk off along the road, away from the guagua.
3. The horn sounds below; the guagua's lights blink. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira, la guagua! ¡Ya mismo se va!" The four guests hurry over from the bench. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Rápido! Gracias por venir. ¡Cuídense!" **(cuídense: heard)** She kisses each of them on the cheek. Carmen waves. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Nos vemos!"

**The turn**

4. The four guests stand at the head of the steps, looking down over the parapet at the guagua, each with a hand held out behind him. On the table: the hat, the bag, the box, the book. The guagua's lights blink. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn from don Pepe, the señora, the señor and the maestra), his eyes on the guagua below, asks for his thing (each has his own: don Pepe the box, the señora the bag, the señor the hat, the maestra the book; nothing in the picture says whose is whose). The frog pushes a thing into his hand. The new words do not decide here: this last game is on words of the whole level.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the hat, the bag, the box, the book; a word he has decides).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Mi caja, por favor." (Or, drawn with the guest: "Mi bolsa, por favor." "Mi sombrero, por favor." "Mi libro, por favor.")
   - Right: he takes it. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Gracias! ¡Nos vemos!" (Each of the four says the same in his own voice.) He goes down the steps; below, a small figure gets into the guagua, and a face shows at one of its windows. A star.
   - Wrong: he takes it without a look and goes down the steps. A moment; he climbs back up with it, out of breath. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡No, Coquí! Mi caja." He puts the wrong thing back, and sits down on the bench, fanning himself. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the frog takes it to him on the bench; he hugs it, and waves down at the guagua.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "La señora dice: Mi bolsa, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The other two guests take their own things from the table, wave, and go down. The horn; the guagua shuts its door and goes off along the road.
   - Four faces at its windows, four hands waving. Stars and a heart.
   - Three faces; one guest waves from the bench.
   - Two faces; two wave from the bench.
   With somebody on the bench: NARR: "Abuelo dice: Está bien. Vamos en mi carro." He pats the guest's shoulder; they stay for the goodbyes. (With four faces abuelo only waves.)
8. The family and the frog stand at the parapet (and whoever stayed, beside them); below, the guagua's lights grow small. From its windows, small voices. NARR: "La gente dice: ¡Nos vemos, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adiós a todos)]: "¡Adiós!" He waves with both arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: Buenas noches, Coquí. Gracias por venir."
9. The lanterns go out one by one; the moon and the sea are left. NARR: "La fiesta en el fuerte: la música, los regalos, la gente. Nos vemos. ¡Hasta pronto!"

**Fewest hearings on a clean run:** gracias por venir 4 (steps 1, 2, 3, 8); nos vemos 6 (steps 2, 3, both right rounds, 8, 9), and 4 at the least on any path; hasta pronto 3 (steps 1, 2, 9). None of the three decides: the game is on words he has (caja, bolsa, sombrero, libro).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for (caja, bolsa, sombrero or libro).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four in round A; one in three in round B (round A's thing has gone).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 8): hasta luego; adiós.

## Reviews (draft 1 to draft 2)

A beginner's reviewer and a Puerto Rican Spanish reviewer read draft 1; each had only the script and the owner's rules.

- 117: the game's lines have no "hay" and no "no": "Detrás de la puerta roja, nadie." against "..., alguien.", so a word he has cannot decide it. One person slips away in a moment of dark before each round, and pigeons sleep in whichever box is empty, so both rounds have a hider and pigeons. "¡Ahora sí están todos!" The turns are not echoes: "¡Aquí!" to "¿dónde estás?", and "¡Tres!" to finish abuela's count.
- 118: abuela asks the grown-ups, and says "Pepe" (a child says usted to them; "don" with tú clashes). The question is not asked five times in the same words. The third "porque" is a new line ("¡Porque me gusta la música!"), not a recap. A used thing stays with its guest, and the chance note says what that leaves. With one ring the dancers have only just begun. The turns are not echoes.
- 119: the músicos walk home, so the guagua's four windows are for the four guests; four things, four guests, two of them served by the frog. Each guest who gets his thing says "¡Nos vemos!", so the counts hold on every path. The three new phrases come from different people. Somebody who missed the guagua stays for the goodbyes ("Está bien. Vamos en mi carro."), and the last touch after a miss gives him his thing on the bench. "¡Gracias a ustedes!" "¡Ya mismo se va!" The bag is a cloth shopping bag (a handbag would be a cartera).
- "Hasta pronto": on the island it is a sign-off, not what guests say at a door. It stays a word of the level, said by the two músicos and the hosts to them, and by the narrator in the level's last line; the guests say "nos vemos", and abuela says "cuídense".
- Not taken: a game on the new words in 118 and 119. In 118 the frame is the new thing and the reason after it decides (a miss is filed under porque or pero). 119 is the level's last story: its game is on words of the whole level, and its three phrases are goodbyes that are heard, and said back with words he has.

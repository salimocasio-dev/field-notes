# La muralla, de noche: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `muralla`). Stories 117 to 119. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 116. Story 119 is the last story of the level.

Scene words (page 02): porque, ¿por qué?, pero, todos, nadie, alguien, nos vemos, hasta pronto, gracias por venir.
How they are shared out: 117 todos, nadie, alguien; 118 ¿por qué?, porque, pero; 119 nos vemos, hasta pronto, gracias por venir.

The picture: the top of the fort's wall, at night. One view. At the back, the wall's low parapet, and beyond it the sea, the moon and the stars. On the walk, at the left and at the right, two sentry boxes of stone, each with a door (the left one red, the right one blue) and a small window with a light in it. Between the two, over the middle, a line with three lanterns. In the middle, against the parapet, a bench. At the left front, a small table. At the right front, the head of the steps that go down to the gate. Far below, beyond the parapet at the right, the road. No roof.

People: the family (abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen); the two músicos; don Pepe, the maestra, the piragüero, the señor, the señora.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign.

## Story 117: Todos en la foto

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's camera on its three legs, facing the middle of the walk, where people gather under the three lanterns. She counts heads on her fingers, and shakes her head.
- **At stake:** the lanterns over the photo (3). Pigeons sleep in a sentry box when nobody is in it. Open the door of an empty one, and they fly out and knock a lantern off the line.
- **New words:** todos, nadie, alguien.
- **Back again:** puerta, rojo, azul, detrás, hay, no hay, falta, aquí, pájaro, luz, viento, cuidado, también, listo, otra vez, ahora, uno, dos, tres, por favor, sí, no.

**Start:** night; the moon over the sea. In the middle of the walk the family and the guests come together under the lanterns. Abuela at her camera, at the front. Both sentry boxes have their doors shut and their windows lit. The frog beside the camera.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Una foto! ¡Todos aquí, por favor!" People move closer together. Abuela counts on her fingers. NARR: "Abuela dice: Uno, dos, tres... No están todos. Falta alguien."
2. A giggle; the red box's window flickers. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Shh! Detrás de la puerta roja hay alguien." (signed, the red door.) He touches it: the frog pulls it open: Carmen, with her hands over her mouth. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Aquí estoy!" She runs to the others.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y detrás de la puerta azul? ¿Hay alguien?" (signed, the blue door.) He touches it: the frog pulls it open: an empty box; one pigeon on a ledge opens an eye, and a lantern on the line swings. NARR: "Luis dice: No. Aquí no hay nadie." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Nadie! Hay un pájaro." Luis shuts the door softly. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado con la luz!"
4. Abuela counts again, and nods. NARR: "Abuela dice: Detrás de la puerta roja, nadie. Detrás de la puerta azul, nadie. ¡Ahora están todos! Coquí, ¿y tú?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (yo también, también)]: "¡Yo también!" He hops in front of the others.

**The turn**

5. Abuela bends to her camera. A gust of wind: hats fly; two people run for shelter, one after the other, behind the others' backs, and both doors are shut again. Abuela looks up, and counts. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Otra vez falta alguien." Luis runs to the red box's window and looks in, runs to the blue box's window and looks in, and comes back to the frog. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Each round, someone is in one sentry box and nobody is in the other (drawn). Luis, back beside the frog with his eyes on abuela, says what is behind one of the two doors (which door he names is drawn, on its own): somebody, or nobody. The frog opens a door: the one with somebody in it.

6. **Decision A, touch a door (the red, the blue; tests alguien against nadie).** NARR: "Luis dice: Detrás de la puerta roja no hay nadie." (Or, drawn: "Detrás de la puerta roja hay alguien." "Detrás de la puerta azul no hay nadie." "Detrás de la puerta azul hay alguien.")
   - Right: the door opens: somebody blinks in the light, and goes to join the others. A star.
   - Wrong: the door opens on an empty box: pigeons burst out over the frog's head, and one knocks a lantern off the line: it goes out. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! Detrás de la puerta roja no hay nadie." A cross. The sign is on the other door; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a door (the same game; Luis looks in at both windows again; somebody else).** NARR: "Luis dice: Detrás de la puerta azul hay alguien." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Everybody stands under the lanterns that are left.
   - Three lanterns: a bright photo, every face in it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two lanterns.
   - One lantern, and the moon: a dark photo, with white teeth in it.
9. Abuela counts once more. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora sí. Coquí, ¿listo?" COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo, sí listo)]: "¡Listo!" A flash. The photo slides out. NARR: "En la foto están todos. No falta nadie."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** alguien 4 (steps 1, 2, 3, 5), nadie 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: todos 3 (steps 1, 4) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (alguien or nadie).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the door he names and what is behind it are drawn apart.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): también; listo.

## Story 118: ¿Por qué no bailas?

- **Goal he can see:** the two músicos sit on the bench with the guitar and the drum. On the parapet beside them a tall candle burns in a glass, with three red rings painted round it. The músico with the hat points at it, and then at his guitar: one last song, as long as the candle.
- **At stake:** the candle's rings (3). Whoever does not dance is waited for; a wrong thing carried to him and back is time, and the candle burns down a ring.
- **New words:** ¿por qué?, porque, pero.
- **Back again:** baila, música, canción, tengo sed, tengo hambre, cansado, quiero, jugo, silla, feliz, contento, un poco, vela, ahora, tú, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The músicos on the bench; the candle burns, whole. People dance on the walk. On the small table at the left front: a jug with a glass of juice, a plate of food; beside the table, a chair. Abuelo leans on the parapet. The frog on the table.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen pulls at abuelo's hand. NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuelo, ¿por qué no bailas?" **(bailas: a form of baila)** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Porque estoy cansado." (signed, the chair.) He touches it: the frog pushes the chair to him; he sits down, and breathes out. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias, Coquí."
2. Luis stands apart with his tongue out. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis, ¿por qué no bailas?" NARR: "Luis dice: Porque tengo sed." (signed, the glass of juice.) He touches it: the frog carries it over; Luis drinks it off, and goes to dance. NARR: "Carmen dice: Luis no baila porque tiene sed. ¡Ahora sí!"
3. Don Pepe stands with a hand on his stomach. NARR: "Carmen dice: Don Pepe, ¿por qué no bailas?" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Quiero bailar, pero tengo hambre." (signed, the plate.) He touches it: the frog carries it over; don Pepe eats, and goes to dance, with the plate in his hand.
4. Abuelo gets up from his chair. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Estoy cansado, pero quiero bailar." He dances with abuela, slowly. Luis puts the chair, a new glass and a new plate back at the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tú estás cansado?" COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco, sí un poco)]: "¡Un poco!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Un poco cansado, pero contento."

**The turn**

5. The músico with the hat lifts his guitar: the last song begins, and the candle's flame stands up. Two guests do not dance: the maestra and the piragüero stand at the parapet, a little apart, with their hands at their sides. By the table: the glass of juice, the plate, the chair. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen, dancing, calls to one who does not dance; he says why not (drawn, each round on its own, and round B's is not round A's): he is thirsty, he is hungry, he is tired. He stands still, with his hands at his sides and his eyes on the dancers. The frog takes him the juice, the plate, or the chair.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the juice, the plate, the chair; tests what comes after porque).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Maestra, ¿por qué no bailas?" NARR: "La maestra dice: Porque tengo sed." (Or, drawn: "Porque tengo hambre." "Porque estoy cansada.") **(cansada: a form of cansado)**
   - Right: she drinks (or eats a bite; or sits a moment), and goes to dance. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes her another thing; she looks at it, and shakes her head. NARR: "La maestra dice: No, Coquí. Tengo sed." The frog takes it back; the candle burns down past a ring. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the other guest; another reason; he says it with pero).** NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Por qué no bailas?" NARR: "El piragüero dice: Quiero bailar, pero tengo hambre." (Or, drawn: "Quiero bailar, pero tengo sed." "Quiero bailar, pero estoy cansado.")
   - Right and wrong as in A. Wrong: NARR: "El piragüero dice: No, Coquí. Tengo hambre."

**The ending**

8. Everybody dances; the song goes on for as long as the candle.
   - Three rings: a long song; the moon moves a little way across the sky. Stars and a heart.
   - Two rings.
   - One ring: a short song; the flame gutters.
9. The candle goes out; the músicos put down the guitar and the drum. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿estás feliz?" COQUÍ [TURN: feliz (feliz, muy feliz, sí feliz)]: "¡Muy feliz!" NARR: "¿Por qué baila la gente? Porque está feliz."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿por qué? 3 (steps 1, 2, 3), porque 3 (steps 1, 2), pero 3 (steps 3, 4). What decides is the reason after porque or pero: tengo sed, tengo hambre, cansado (words he has); each is heard once with its thing in the opening.
**Forms:** bailas with baila; cansada with cansado.
**A miss is filed under:** the reason that was said (tengo sed, tengo hambre or cansado).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A; one in two in round B if round A's thing has been used (Luis puts a new one back in its place, so it is one in three by the picture; round B's reason is never round A's).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): un poco; feliz.

## Story 119: Nos vemos

- **Goal he can see:** far below, on the road, a guagua with its lights on and its door open; three of its windows are lit and empty. Its horn sounds once.
- **At stake:** the guests who catch the guagua (3). Somebody who goes down the steps with the wrong thing has to climb them again; he sits down on the bench out of breath, and the guagua goes without him.
- **New words:** nos vemos, hasta pronto, gracias por venir.
- **Back again:** guagua, se va, un momento, rápido, guitarra, bolsa, sombrero, caja, libro, mi, fiesta, cansado, carro, hasta luego, adiós, buenas noches, mañana, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** late night; the moon is low. The lanterns burn. At the head of the steps, at the right front, abuela and abuelo stand to see people off. On the small table, the guests' things in a row: a guitar, a hat, a bag, a box, a book. The frog on the table. Below, the guagua waits.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The músico with the hat comes to the steps. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias por venir!" NARR: "El músico dice: Gracias, abuela. ¿Y mi guitarra?" (signed, the guitar.) He touches it: the frog pushes it to him; he slings it on his back. NARR: "El músico dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¡Nos vemos!" He goes down the steps; below, a small figure gets into the guagua.
2. The other músico, with his drum under his arm. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias por venir. ¡Qué bonita la música!" NARR: "El otro músico dice: ¡Hasta pronto, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: hasta luego (hasta luego, adiós)]: "¡Hasta luego!" He goes down.
3. The horn sounds below. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira, la guagua! Se va en un momento." Three guests hurry over from the bench. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Rápido! Gracias por venir. ¡Nos vemos! ¡Hasta pronto!" She kisses each of them on the cheek.

**The turn**

4. The three guests stand at the head of the steps, looking down over the parapet at the guagua, each with a hand held out behind him. On the table: the hat, the bag, the box, the book. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest (drawn from don Pepe, the señora, the señor and the maestra), his eyes on the guagua below, asks for his thing (each has his own: don Pepe the box, the señora the bag, the señor the hat, the maestra the book; nothing in the picture says whose is whose). The frog pushes a thing into his hand.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the hat, the bag, the box, the book; a word he has decides).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Mi caja, por favor." (Or, drawn with the guest: "Mi bolsa, por favor." "Mi sombrero, por favor." "Mi libro, por favor.")
   - Right: he takes it. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Gracias! ¡Nos vemos!" (The señora and the maestra say: "¡Gracias! ¡Hasta pronto!") He goes down the steps; below, a small figure gets into the guagua, and a face shows at one of its windows. A star.
   - Wrong: he takes it without a look and goes down the steps. A moment; he climbs back up with it, out of breath. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡No, Coquí! Mi caja." He puts the wrong thing back, and sits down on the bench, fanning himself. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the frog takes it to him on the bench.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "La señora dice: Mi bolsa, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The third guest takes his own thing from the table, waves, and goes down. The horn; the guagua shuts its door and goes off along the road.
   - Three faces at its windows, three hands waving. Stars and a heart.
   - Two faces; one guest waves from the bench.
   - One face; two wave from the bench.
   Whoever is on the bench gets up. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Nos vemos mañana. ¡Vamos en mi carro!" (This line plays with two faces or one; with three, abuelo only waves.)
8. The walk is quiet. The family and the frog stand at the parapet; below, the guagua's lights grow small. From its windows, small voices. NARR: "Todos dicen: ¡Nos vemos, Coquí! ¡Hasta pronto!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adiós a todos)]: "¡Adiós!" He waves with both arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: Buenas noches, Coquí. Gracias por venir."
9. The lanterns go out one by one; the moon and the sea are left. NARR: "La fiesta en el fuerte: la música, los regalos, la gente. Nos vemos. ¡Hasta pronto!"

**Fewest hearings on a clean run:** gracias por venir 4 (steps 1, 2, 3, 8); nos vemos 2 before the game (steps 1, 3), at least once in the game, and twice in the ending; hasta pronto 2 before the game (steps 2, 3), and twice in the ending. None of the three decides: the game is on words he has (caja, bolsa, sombrero, libro).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for (caja, bolsa, sombrero or libro).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four in round A; one in three in round B if round A's thing has gone.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 8): hasta luego; adiós.

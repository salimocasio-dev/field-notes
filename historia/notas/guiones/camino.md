# El camino: three stories (draft 3, final: after two rounds of fresh reviews and a last gate)

Place 2, El parque. Scene 1 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (eleven stories).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Heard along the way: pájaro, bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, bola, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, tiene, entra, come, está, dónde, quién, mi, es, un, una, y, de, en, Luis, Carmen, dos, tres.

Scene words for El camino (from the 40-scene list): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres.

The picture: a park. A dirt path runs from the park gate on the left, past three small trees and a bench, to one very big tree on a small rise at the far right. Seven flat stones lie on the path, evenly spaced. Grass, sky, hills. Luis (the boy, green shirt) and Carmen (the girl, braids, pink dress) are here with abuela's dog, Canelo: a small tan dog with one ear down and a red collar. A grey cat lives in the park.

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Luis dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ). The dog and the cat only make their sounds.

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!". (Where the right move is to stand still, there is no hop.)
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a short "No." line that repeats the word; then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es un árbol." "Es el perro. Se llama Canelo.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Two kinds of answer bubble over the frog, both known from place 1:
- **Doing bubbles**: pictures of what the frog can do; he touches one. The pictures move. Which ones show, and where, is drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Which side each is on, and so which speaks first, is drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.

The doing pictures of this scene:
- walking: the frog stepping along, legs moving at an easy pace;
- running: the frog flat out, legs a blur, dust behind him;
- waiting: the frog standing quite still, feet together, nothing moving;
- going slowly (story 14): the frog creeping, one tiny step at a time, a snail next to him going just as fast;
- going fast (story 14): the frog stepping along with long quick steps, speed lines behind him.

## Story 12: Uno, dos, tres

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** Luis stands by the big tree at the far end of the path. Carmen, the frog and Canelo stand at the park gate, at the start of the path. Get along the path, stone by stone, to Luis.
- **At stake:** his place on the path. The rule, shown once before the game: do what Luis calls and you go one stone on; do something else and Luis shakes his head and you walk back to the stone you were on. Carmen always does what is called, so after each wrong move he is one stone further behind her.
- **New words:** parque, perro, camina, corre, espera, uno, dos, tres.
- **Back again:** hola, mira, vamos, ahora, sí, no, juega, también.
- **Heard along the way:** árbol, se llama, este, a jugar.

**Start:** the gate. Canelo pulls at his leash, tail going. Far up the path, Luis waves from the big tree.

1. NARR: "Este es el parque." NARR: "Mira. Luis y Carmen están en el parque."
2. NARR: "Y este es el perro de abuela." (Canelo twinkles.) NARR: "El perro se llama Canelo." Canelo barks twice. COQUÍ: "¡Hola, Canelo!"

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "¿Puedes tocar al perro?"
   - He touches Canelo. Right signal. Canelo licks the frog's face.
4. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos a jugar! ¡Uno, dos, tres!" He holds up one, two, three fingers.
5. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Camina!" Doing bubbles: walking, running. The big sign is on walking. He touches it. The frog and Carmen walk to the first stone. NARR: "Coquí camina. Carmen camina."
6. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Corre!" Doing bubbles: running, waiting. The sign on running. He touches it. They run to the second stone, Canelo running beside them. NARR: "Coquí corre. El perro corre también."
7. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Espera!" Doing bubbles: waiting, walking. The sign on waiting. He touches it. The frog and Carmen stand quite still. Luis looks hard at them, then laughs and waves them on; they go to the third stone. NARR: "Coquí espera. Carmen espera."
8. **A call for each of them, taught.** They stay where they are. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Carmen, corre! ¡Coquí, espera!" All three doing bubbles show. The sign is on waiting. He touches it. Carmen runs to the next stone; the frog stands still; Luis laughs and waves the frog on to it too.
9. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Coquí, camina! ¡Carmen, espera!" (His name comes first this time.) All three doing bubbles; the sign on walking. He touches it. Carmen stands still, the frog walks to the next stone, then Luis waves Carmen on.

**The turn**

10. They stay where they are for the real game. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora sí. ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Espera!" Carmen and the frog stand still. Canelo cannot: he runs on to the next stone. Luis shakes his head. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Canelo! ¡Espera!" Canelo walks back, ears down, and sits. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Three rounds. Every call is drawn fresh. Round 3 never repeats round 1's call; apart from that a call can come twice.

11. **Round 1, Do (one call, two pictures).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Corre!" (Or "¡Camina!" or "¡Espera!") Two doing bubbles: the call and one other.
   - Right: the frog does it, and Carmen with him; both end one stone on. (After a right "espera" Luis laughs and waves them one stone on.)
   - Wrong: the frog does the other thing, Carmen does the right one. Luis shakes his head. NARR: "No. ¡Corre!" (the call). If the frog moved, he walks back to the stone he was on. The sign shows the right bubble; he touches it; the frog does it on the spot. He is one stone behind Carmen.
12. **Round 2, Do (a call for each of them, three pictures).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Uno, dos, tres! ¡Carmen, espera! ¡Coquí, corre!" The two names come in an order drawn fresh; the two calls are different and drawn fresh. All three doing bubbles show.
   - Right: the frog does his, Carmen does hers; both end one stone on.
   - Wrong: Luis shakes his head. NARR: "No. Coquí, ¡corre!" The frog walks back if he moved. The sign; he does it on the spot.
13. **Round 3, Do (one call, three pictures).** NARR: "Luis dice: Uno. Dos. Tres. ¡Camina!" (drawn fresh from the two calls round 1 did not use; said slowly.) All three doing bubbles show.
   - Right and wrong as in round 1.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

14. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ven, Coquí! ¡Ven, Canelo!" Canelo runs beside the frog, and they go up the last of the path to the tree. By his place:
   - No wrong moves: the frog and Carmen get there together, and the frog touches the trunk first. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two wrong: Carmen is at the tree first; the frog and Canelo come in after her.
   - Three wrong: Carmen and Luis are sitting under the tree when the frog and Canelo come in.
15. They all sit under the big tree. Canelo chases his tail. NARR: "Canelo juega en el parque." COQUÍ: "¡Uno, dos, tres!" Canelo barks three times.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** camina 4 (steps 5 and 9), corre 4 (steps 6 and 8), espera 7 (steps 7, 8, 9 and 10). Parque is heard 3 times (steps 1 and 15). Uno, dos, tres: 6 before the game; they are not staked here (story 13 stakes them).

**A miss is filed under:** the word that was called for the frog.

## Story 13: ¿Dónde está el gato?

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen holds a small basket with a blanket in it. The grey cat sits on the bench. Get the cat into its basket.
- **At stake:** three fish biscuits, one a round, shown in the corner. A right move and the cat eats that round's biscuit: a heart takes its place and stays. A wrong move and Canelo gobbles it: a paw mark takes its place.
- **New words:** gato, árbol, hay, uno, dos, tres (now staked), ¿dónde está?
- **Back again:** perro, corre, espera, pájaro, no, mira, aquí, está, con.
- **Heard along the way:** en, gatito.

This story is at the left end of the path; the big tree is out of the picture. There are exactly three trees. Birds sit in them: one tree has one bird, one has two, one has three. Before each round the birds fly up and settle again, so which tree has how many is drawn fresh, and so is the tree the cat is in.

**Start:** the cat on the bench under the middle tree, washing a paw. On the left, Luis holds Canelo's leash. Carmen stands beside him with the basket. The frog is in front, in the middle.

1. NARR: "Mira. Un gato." (The cat twinkles.) NARR: "El gato está en el parque."
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ven, gatito! ¡Ven!" She shows it the basket. The cat looks.

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "¿Puedes tocar al gato?"
   - He touches the cat. Right signal. The cat purrs; a small heart.
4. Canelo sees the cat and barks. The cat jumps off the bench and runs up a tree. Its tail hangs out of the leaves. NARR: "¡El gato corre! El gato está en el árbol."
5. NARR: "Mira los árboles." NARR: "Un árbol." (The first tree lights up and stays lit.) NARR: "Dos árboles." (The second lights up too.) NARR: "Tres árboles." (All three are lit.)
6. NARR: "En este árbol hay un pájaro. Uno." (That tree twinkles; its one bird chirps.) NARR: "Aquí hay dos pájaros." (The tree with two; they chirp one after the other.) NARR: "Y aquí hay tres pájaros." (The tree with three.)
7. **Where it is, taught.** NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está el gato?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Está en el árbol con dos pájaros." (The tree it ran up.) The big sign is on that tree. He touches it. The frog hops to it. The cat's face comes out of the leaves. Right signal.
8. **Where it is not, taught.** The birds fly up and settle again. The cat jumps to another tree; its tail shows. NARR: "Luis dice: El gato no está en el árbol con un pájaro." NARR: "Luis dice: Y no está en el árbol con tres pájaros." (The two trees it is not in.) The big sign is on the third tree. He touches it. The frog hops there; the cat's face comes out.
9. **Telling them, taught.** The birds fly up and settle again; the cat jumps again; its tail shows. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¿dónde está el gato?" Two speaking bubbles: "Está en el árbol con tres pájaros." (the tree with the tail) and one with another count. The big sign is on the right one. He touches it. COQUÍ: "Está en el árbol con tres pájaros." Carmen walks to that tree; the cat's face comes out.

**The turn**

10. Canelo barks again and pulls loose from Luis. The birds all fly up. The cat jumps from tree to tree, too fast to follow, and is gone in the leaves. The birds settle again. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Canelo! ¡Espera!" Luis catches the leash. NARR: "Un pájaro. Dos pájaros. Tres pájaros." (The three trees twinkle in that order: the one with one bird, the one with two, the one with three.) The three biscuits show in the corner. The signs go away. From here on the cat's tail does not show, except in decision C.

**The game** (no sign). Before every decision the cat's face goes back into the leaves, all three trees shake at once, and the birds fly up and settle again. Where the cat goes is never seen. The counts and the cat's tree are drawn fresh each time. A touch on a bird is a free poke ("Es un pájaro."); only a touch on the leaves or the trunk picks a tree. In step 6 and at the turn the counts never fall one, two, three from left to right.

11. **Decision A, Do (the number).** NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Dónde está el gato?" NARR: "Luis dice: Está en el árbol con tres pájaros." (Or "con un pájaro", or "con dos pájaros".) He can touch any of the three trees.
   - Right: the frog hops to that tree and holds up this round's biscuit. The cat's face comes out and it eats. Right signal. A heart in the corner.
   - Wrong: the frog holds the biscuit up at the wrong tree. Nothing comes out. Canelo jumps and gobbles it. NARR: "No. Tres pájaros." A paw mark in the corner. The sign shows the right tree; he touches it; the cat's face comes out and the frog pats it.
12. **Decision B, Do ("no" twice).** NARR: "Luis dice: El gato no está en el árbol con un pájaro." NARR: "Luis dice: Y no está en el árbol con tres pájaros." (The two trees it is not in, in an order drawn fresh.) He can touch any of the three trees.
   - Right and wrong as in A. The "No." line names where it is: "No. Dos pájaros."
13. **Decision C, Say (he tells them).** This time the frog is the only one who can see: the cat's tail hangs out of one tree. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¿dónde está el gato?" Two speaking bubbles: "Está en el árbol con dos pájaros." (the tree with the tail) and the same line with another count.
   - Right: COQUÍ says it. Carmen walks to that tree with the basket and the biscuit. The cat jumps down into the basket and eats. Right signal. A heart.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other count. Carmen walks to that tree and looks up; nothing. Canelo gobbles the biscuit. NARR: "No. Dos pájaros." A paw mark. The sign shows the right bubble; it speaks again; he touches it. COQUÍ says it. Carmen walks to the right tree; the cat jumps down into the basket.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

14. The cat is in its basket. By the corner:
   - Three hearts: the cat purrs loudly, a big heart over the basket. Stars.
   - One or two hearts: the cat washes its face.
   - No hearts: the cat looks at Canelo, who is licking his lips, and turns its back on him.
15. Canelo comes and sniffs the basket. The cat puts a paw on his nose. He lies down beside it. NARR: "El gato y el perro están aquí." NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora son amigos." COQUÍ: "¡Amigos!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost, on the worst draw:** un / uno 4 (step 5 "un árbol", step 6 "un pájaro. Uno.", the turn); dos 3 (steps 5 and 6, the turn); tres 3 (the same). Steps 7 to 9 add one more hearing each for whichever count they name. The count-in of story 12 comes before all of these. Gato 8. Árbol 9. Hay 3 (step 6).

**A miss is filed under:** A, the number said; B, "no"; C, the number he had to say.

## Story 14: Rápido, despacio

- **Goal he can see:** the end of the walk. The frog holds Canelo's leash. On the frog's head is a little basket with three guineos for abuela. Abuela, Luis and Carmen wait at the park gate, at the other end of the path. Walk the dog to the gate.
- **At stake:** the three guineos. After a wrong move the leash jerks, one guineo falls out, and Canelo eats it.
- **New words:** rápido, despacio.
- **Back again:** perro, camina, corre, parque, ven, vamos, no, abuela, gracias, tres, ¿dónde está?
- **Heard along the way:** muy, guineos, para abuela.

Canelo's ways of going, always drawn the same:
- he walks (camina): an easy trot;
- he walks slowly (camina despacio): he creeps, and a snail on the path keeps up with him;
- he walks fast (camina rápido): long quick steps, speed lines;
- he runs (corre): flat out, ears back, dust, the leash tight, the frog pulled off his feet;
- he does not walk (no camina): he stands still, nose in a flower.

**Start:** the big tree. The frog, the basket on his head, the leash, Canelo sitting. Far off at the gate, abuela, Luis and Carmen.

1. NARR: "Coquí y Canelo están en el parque." NARR: "Mira. Tres guineos para abuela." (The basket twinkles.) NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos, Coquí! ¡Ven con el perro!"
1b. NARR: "¿Dónde está el perro?" The big sign is on Canelo. He touches him. Right signal. Canelo wags his tail.

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. Canelo trots off, the frog behind him. NARR: "El perro camina. Coquí camina."
3. Canelo sees a butterfly and bolts. The leash goes tight; the frog is pulled off his feet, the basket wobbling. NARR: "¡El perro corre! ¡Corre!" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Despacio, Canelo!" and "¡Rápido, Canelo!" The big sign is on "¡Despacio, Canelo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Despacio, Canelo!" Canelo drops to a slow creep, the snail keeping up with him. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el perro camina despacio."
4. Canelo finds a smell and stops dead, nose in a flower. The frog taps his foot. NARR: "El perro no camina." The two speaking bubbles again. The big sign is on "¡Rápido, Canelo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Rápido, Canelo!" Canelo lifts his head and steps out with long quick steps. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el perro camina rápido."
5. **The pace, taught.** Luis shouts from the gate. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí! ¡Camina despacio!" Two doing bubbles: going slowly, going fast. The big sign is on going slowly. He touches it. The frog and Canelo creep to the next stone, the snail beside them.
6. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Camina rápido!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on going fast. He touches it. They step out quickly to the next stone.

**The turn**

7. The frog trips on the stone. The basket tips, the guineos jump in it, and one nearly goes over the edge; Canelo watches it with great interest. The three guineos show in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Decisions A and B come in an order drawn fresh; C is last.

8. **Decision A, Say (he sees what the dog is doing; nothing is said).** Canelo does one of two things, drawn fresh: he bolts, the leash tight, the frog pulled off his feet; or he stops dead, nose in a flower. The narrator says nothing. The two speaking bubbles: "¡Despacio, Canelo!" and "¡Rápido, Canelo!"
   - Right: the frog says the one that puts it right. Canelo walks on. Right signal.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other. Canelo does even more of what he was doing (faster still; or he lies down). The leash jerks, a guineo falls, and Canelo eats it. NARR: "No. ¡Despacio!" (Or "No. ¡Rápido!") The sign shows the right bubble; he touches it; the dog walks on.
9. **Decision B, Do (Luis calls the pace).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí! ¡Camina despacio!" (Or "¡Camina rápido!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: going slowly, going fast. Canelo sits and waits for him.
   - Right: Canelo goes at the pace that was called and the frog with him, to the next stone. Right signal.
   - Wrong: Canelo goes at the pace that was called; the frog goes at the other. The leash jerks, a guineo falls, Canelo eats it. NARR: "No. ¡Despacio!" (Or "No. ¡Rápido!") The sign; he does it.
10. **Decision C, Say, last (he only hears what the dog is doing).** The path goes behind a big bush. Canelo goes behind it; only the leash shows, the same either way. NARR: "¡El perro corre!" (Or: "El perro no camina." Drawn fresh. Only words he knows: the answer is not the word he has just heard.) The two speaking bubbles.
   - Right: the frog says the one that puts it right. Canelo comes out from behind the bush, walking. Right signal.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other. Canelo comes out even faster (or does not come out at all, and the frog has to pull). The leash jerks, a guineo falls, Canelo eats it. NARR: "No. ¡Despacio!" (Or "No. ¡Rápido!") The sign; he says it; the dog walks on.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

11. At the gate. By the guineos left:
   - Three: abuela takes the full basket. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two: she takes the basket with what is left.
   - None: the basket is empty; Canelo looks very pleased with himself; abuela laughs.
12. Abuela takes the leash and pats Canelo. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hola, Canelo! Gracias, Coquí." COQUÍ: "De nada, abuela." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos a casa!" They walk out of the gate, Canelo at his easy trot.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** rápido 5, despacio 5 (steps 3 to 6: the bubbles twice, the frog, the narrator saying what the dog does now, Luis).

**A miss is filed under:** the word he had to pick (rápido or despacio).

## What the wordless read changed (after the build)

- The dog stands in front of the frog, in plain sight, not behind him.
- Story 12 never goes back to the start; the path has seven stones, one for every round.
- Canelo stands a little ahead of the frog on the path, and Carmen a little below it, so the three do not overlap.
- The walking picture has footprints behind the frog and an arrow ahead of him; the waiting picture has a raised hand beside the frog.
- The snail is bigger and has a shell and eye stalks; the biscuits are fish-shaped; the cat's tail is thicker and striped.
- The bush of story 14 is in the picture from the start.
- A person who runs leans forward. The espera pair is Carmen standing still against Carmen running.

## For the owner, one line each

- The dog is abuela's and is called Canelo. The cat has no name.
- Story 12 is a "do what Luis calls" game. It is not "Un, dos, tres, pescao"; the title is "Uno, dos, tres".
- "Espera" is drawn as standing still, not sitting, so it is not mixed up with "se sienta".
- "Uno, dos, tres" are counted aloud in story 12 and decide things in story 13.

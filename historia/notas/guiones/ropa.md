# La ropa: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `ropa`). Stories 78 to 80. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 77.

Scene words (page 02, as changed on Nov 21): camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos, chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla, se pone, se quita, tiene puesto.
The plan had "lleva" for what somebody is wearing. On the island that is "tiene puesto" ("lleva" is first heard as "carries"). The everyday one wins (page 16).
How they are shared out: 78 camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos; 79 chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla; 80 se pone, se quita, tiene puesto.

The picture: where the family changes, at the back of the beach. One view. A line between two palms with clothes pegged on it; under it a bench with abuela's bag; at the left a little wooden cabin with a curtain for a door (somebody goes in wearing one thing and comes out wearing another). In front, sand; at the top, a strip of wet sand and the sea, where things wash up (story 79). Far off at the right, the road with the guagua's stop (story 80).

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. In story 80, two more children of the beach, seen from behind: a boy who looks like Luis and a girl who looks like Carmen.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 78: La ropa seca

- **Goal he can see:** on the bench, a plate of three sandwiches; abuela lifts the cloth off it for a moment and lays it back beside the plate: for after they are dressed. Luis and Carmen, wet from the sea and wrapped in towels, look at the plate.
- **At stake:** the sandwiches (3). A wrong piece of clothing is handed back, slips, and falls on the sand; abuela shakes the sand out of it, and the sand lands on a sandwich. That one goes to the gulls.
- **New words:** camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos.
- **Back again:** seco, mojado, rojo, azul, amarillo, blanco, dame, toma, gracias, come, quiero, quieres, ¿dónde está?, ¿cuál?, arena, sí, no, después, ropa (heard), sándwich (heard).

**Start:** late afternoon. Abuela stands at the line with her bag of dry clothes; the frog on the line's first peg. Luis and Carmen are still in the sea, far off. The line is empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Carmen están en el agua. Abuela tiene la ropa seca." **(ropa: heard; the clothes carry it)** Abuela takes a red shirt out of the bag and pegs it up. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: una camisa. La camisa roja de Luis." Ask (signed, the shirt): "¿Dónde está la camisa?" He touches it: it swings on its pegs.
2. She pegs up blue trousers beside it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y un pantalón. El pantalón azul." Then a yellow dress. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un vestido. El vestido amarillo de Carmen." Ask (signed, the trousers): "¿Cuál es el pantalón?" He touches it. Ask (signed, the dress): "¿Y el vestido?" He touches it: it swings.
3. She sets two pairs of shoes on the bench's end. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y los zapatos. Zapatos blancos y zapatos negros." Ask (signed, the shoes): "¿Dónde están los zapatos?" He touches them. NARR: "Abuela dice: Camisa, pantalón, vestido y zapatos: ¡la ropa está seca! Coquí, ¿me ayudas?" **(me ayudas: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!"

**The turn**

4. Luis and Carmen come running out of the sea, dripping, and wrap themselves in towels. Abuela lifts the cloth off the plate of sandwiches, and lays it down beside the plate. NARR: "Abuela dice: Primero la ropa. Y después comemos." **(primero, comemos: heard)** She sits down on the bench, by the cabin's curtain. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** On the line hang the shirt, the trousers and the dress, and on the bench's end stand the shoes. Abuela asks for one of the four, drawn; in round B, one of the three that are left. The frog brings it; she hands it in through the curtain to whichever child is changing.

5. **Decision A, touch a piece of clothing (four; tests the word).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, dame el pantalón." (Drawn: "... la camisa.", "... el vestido.", "... los zapatos.")
   - Right: the frog brings it; abuela passes it through the curtain. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings another. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Dame el pantalón." She hands it back; it slips and falls on the sand. She picks it up and shakes it: a cloud of sand over the bench, and onto a sandwich. She throws that one to the gulls. A cross. The frog pegs the piece up again. The sign is on the right piece; he touches it: the frog brings it.
6. **Decision B, touch a piece of clothing (three are left).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y ahora, dame los zapatos." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The curtain opens: Luis in his shirt and trousers, Carmen in her dress, both in their shoes (abuela has passed in the rest herself).
   - Three sandwiches: one for Luis, one for Carmen, one for abuela, who gives the frog a corner of hers. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: Luis and Carmen; each gives the frog a corner.
   - One: abuela cuts it in four.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres un sándwich, Coquí?" **(sándwich: heard; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "Luis y Carmen están secos. Y Coquí come."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** camisa 4, pantalón 4, vestido 4, zapatos 5 (steps 1 to 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the piece that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four in round A, one in three in round B. Abuela asks, so who is changing gives nothing away.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): sí; quiero.

## Story 79: ¡Mi sombrero!

- **Goal he can see:** at the water's edge, things rocking on the small waves, coming in and going out: abuela's sun hat, dark glasses, a pair of sandals, a swimming suit, a towel. A big wave has taken the family's bag. Abuela stands with her hands on her head.
- **At stake:** the things still out in the water. The frog hops in for one thing at a time; if he brings the wrong one, the next wave carries the one that was asked for out to sea.
- **New words:** chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla.
- **Back again:** mar, ola, playa, mojado, seco, mi, busca, ¿dónde está?, mira, viene, se va, grande, cuidado, gracias, toma, sí, no, abuela, allá, está, ahí (heard).

**Start:** afternoon. The bench, the line (one dry towel pegged on it) and the cabin. The bag lies open and empty at the water's edge; five things float in the shallow water. Abuela, Luis and Carmen at the edge; the frog on the wet sand.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "¡Una ola grande!" The wave slides back; the things rock in the water. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! ¡Mi sombrero! Coquí, busca mi sombrero." Ask (signed, the sun hat): "¿Dónde está el sombrero?" He touches it: the frog hops in and pushes the hat to the edge with his nose; abuela shakes it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. ¡El sombrero está mojado!" A small wave takes it out of her hand again.
2. Luis points. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: una toalla. La toalla está allá." Ask (signed, the floating towel): "¿Dónde está la toalla?" He touches it: the frog drags the towel in; Luis wrings it out and hangs it on the line beside the dry one.
3. Abuela points at each thing in turn. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, ahí están las chancletas." **(ahí: heard)** Ask (signed, the sandals): "¿Dónde están las chancletas?" He touches them: they bob. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahí están las gafas." Ask (signed, the glasses): "¿Y las gafas?" He touches them. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y ahí está el traje de baño. Las chancletas, las gafas, el traje de baño y mi sombrero."
4. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Avanza, Coquí!" **(avanza: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops to the edge.

**The turn**

5. Out past the things a big wave is building. Four things float in a row, in a new order: the hat, the sandals, the glasses, the swimming suit. Abuela holds the bag open. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Viene una ola. Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Four things are in the water, in a drawn order. Abuela asks for one of them, drawn; in round B for one of those still afloat.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing in the water (four things; tests the word).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí, busca las chancletas!" (Drawn: "... las gafas!", "... el traje de baño!", "... el sombrero!")
   - Right: the frog brings it in; it goes into the bag. A star. The wave comes and goes; the other things rock.
   - Wrong: the frog brings in another thing (it stays in the bag). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No! ¡Las chancletas!" The wave comes, and when it slides back the sandals are far out. A cross. The sign is on them, out there; he touches them: the frog swims out, and a second wave carries them out of sight.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing in the water (those still afloat: three, or two).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí, busca las gafas!" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The last wave brings whatever is still afloat up onto the sand by itself. Abuela looks into the bag.
   - Nothing lost: hat, sandals, glasses and swimming suit all on the line, dripping, beside the towel. Stars and a heart.
   - One thing lost: three on the line; abuela looks out to sea, and shrugs.
   - Two things lost: two on the line; abuela waves goodbye to the sea, and the children laugh.
9. Abuela takes the dry towel from the line. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Una toalla seca." She wraps him up. COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" NARR: "El sombrero está mojado. Y Coquí está seco."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** chancletas 4, gafas 4, traje de baño 3, sombrero 6 (steps 1, 3). Not deciding: toalla 3 (step 2), and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four in round A; one in three or one in two in round B. Abuela asks every time, so who owns what gives nothing away.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): voy; gracias.

## Story 80: Se pone y se quita

- **Goal he can see:** far off at the right, the guagua at its stop, its door open; the driver leans out and holds up three fingers. Abuela, with the bag packed, looks along the beach: two boys and two girls play at the water's edge with their backs to her, and she has not got her glasses.
- **At stake:** the driver's patience (his three fingers). Each time the frog fetches the wrong child, the driver sounds his horn and puts a finger down.
- **New words:** se pone, se quita, tiene puesto.
- **Back again:** camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos, chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla, rojo, amarillo, guagua, se va, espera, ¿dónde está?, ¿quién?, busca, allá, ven, voy, aquí, ahora, tarde, sol, no hay, mira.

**Start:** the sun low. Abuela at the bench with the bag. Luis and Carmen at the cabin, in swimming things. The frog on the bench. At the right, the road and the guagua.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es tarde. La guagua está esperando." **(esperando: a form of espera)** The driver holds up three fingers. Luis pulls his red shirt over his head; we see him do it. NARR: "Luis se pone la camisa." Then he puts on a straw hat. NARR: "Y se pone el sombrero." Ask (signed, Luis): "¿Quién tiene puesto un sombrero?" He touches him: Luis tips his hat.
2. Carmen pulls her yellow dress on over her swimming suit. NARR: "Carmen se pone el vestido." She puts her dark glasses on, too. NARR: "Carmen tiene puestas las gafas." Abuela shakes her head and points at the low sun. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen, ya no hay sol." Carmen takes them off. NARR: "Carmen se quita las gafas." Ask (signed, Carmen): "¿Quién tiene puesto un vestido?" He touches her: she spins.
3. Luis takes his hat off and fans himself; puts it on again. NARR: "Luis se quita el sombrero... y se pone el sombrero." NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿vienes?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" But the two run off along the sand for one last game.

**The turn**

4. At the water's edge, far off, four children with their backs to us: two boys in red shirts with straw hats in their hands, two girls in yellow dresses with straw hats in their hands. Abuela shades her eyes; the driver looks at his watch. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Dónde está Luis? ¿Dónde está Carmen? Coquí, búscalos." **(búscalos: a form of busca)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two children who look alike stand side by side with their backs to us. One puts his hat on while the other takes his off (or one pulls his shirt on while the other pulls his off; or, for the girls, the dress); they go on doing it, slowly, until he chooses. The narrator says what abuela's child is doing. Which thing, which of the two does what, and which of the two is hers are drawn, each round on its own.

5. **Decision A, touch a child (the two boys; tests se pone against se quita).** NARR: "Luis se quita el sombrero." (Drawn: "Luis se pone el sombrero.", "Luis se pone la camisa.", "Luis se quita la camisa.")
   - Right: the frog hops along the sand and pulls at his leg: Luis turns round and runs back with him. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pulls at the other one's leg: a strange boy turns round, and laughs. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Luis se quita el sombrero." The horn sounds; the driver puts a finger down. A cross. The sign is on the right child; he touches him: Luis comes.
6. **Decision B, touch a child (the two girls).** NARR: "Carmen se pone el sombrero." (Drawn: "... se quita el sombrero.", "... se pone el vestido.", "... se quita el vestido.") Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The four of them run for the road.
   - Three fingers: the driver waits, smiling; they get on and sit at the front. Stars and a heart.
   - Two fingers: he waits, tapping the wheel; they get on.
   - One finger: the guagua has started to roll; abuela waves the towel, it stops, and they climb on, out of breath.
8. Abuela counts heads at the guagua's door, and looks about her. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y Canelo? ¿Dónde está Canelo?" Canelo is under the frog, with abuela's sun hat on. COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Aquí!" NARR: "Canelo tiene puesto el sombrero de abuela. Y la guagua se va."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se pone 4 (steps 1, 2, 3), se quita 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: tiene puesto 3 with "tiene puestas" (steps 1, 2), and once in the ending.
**Forms:** tiene puestas, tiene puesta count with tiene puesto.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se pone or se quita).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two are alike and do opposite things with the same thing.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): voy; aquí.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 78: Luis never asked for the dress and Carmen never for the trousers, so the child who asked ruled a piece out: abuela asks, and hands each piece in through the curtain. The turn blew more clothes onto the line, so "el pantalón" had two answers; and the children were dressed, then in swimming things again, with nothing said: now the opening only hangs the clothes up, and the children come out of the sea once. Sand on a sandwich under a cloth: the cloth is off, and that sandwich goes to the gulls. The frog eats in every ending. A plate of sandwiches is not "pan": "un sándwich". A child asking for his clothes says "dame", not "necesito". The closing line named clothes the draw might not have given: "Luis y Carmen están secos."
- 79: after a wrong round A only one thing was left, so round B was no choice, and the two-lost ending could not happen: four things float in the game. "Luis dice: mis gafas" gave the answer by its speaker: abuela asks every time. Three words that could cost were only said in a list: each is pointed at ("Ahí están las chancletas"), two of them touched. After a miss he was never shown the right thing: the sign is on it, out at sea. The frog was wrapped in a "dry" towel that had just been fished out: a dry one hangs on the line from the start.
- 80: rebuilt. Glasses cannot be seen on a child with his back to us; Luis's hat was on the frog when abuela said Luis wore one; "lleva" was the same in every line and decided nothing; a cap is "una gorra", not "un sombrero". Now two children who look alike do opposite things with the same thing, the narrator says which Luis does, and "se pone" against "se quita" is what decides. Luis has a straw hat. "Carmen se pone el vestido" was said of a dress already on: she is seen pulling it on. The ending's question is about Canelo, and the answer is true.

Left, and why:
- 78's round B has three pieces left (one in three): said in the script.
- "Tiene puesto" decides nothing. It is heard four times with its picture, and it is what the call needs ("what are you wearing").
- "Vestido": older island speech says "traje"; with "traje de baño" in the same scene, vestido is the clearer word.
- How does the narrator know what Luis is doing when abuela cannot see? The narrator tells the story; abuela, without her glasses, cannot tell the two apart. Her correction after a miss is said once she has seen him turn round.

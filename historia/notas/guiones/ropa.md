# La ropa: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `ropa`). Stories 78 to 80. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 77.

Scene words (page 02): camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos, chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla, se pone, se quita, lleva.
How they are shared out: 78 camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos; 79 chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla; 80 se pone, se quita, lleva.

The picture: where the family changes, at the back of the beach. One view. A line between two palms with clothes pegged on it; under it a bench with abuela's bag; at the left a little wooden cabin with a curtain for a door (somebody goes in wearing one thing and comes out wearing another). In front, sand; at the top, a strip of wet sand and the sea, where things wash up (story 79). Far off at the right, the road with the guagua's stop (story 80).

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. In story 80, other children of the beach, seen from far off or from behind: they are told apart only by what they wear.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 78: La ropa seca

- **Goal he can see:** on the bench, a plate of three sandwiches under a cloth that abuela lifts for a moment: for after they are dressed. Luis and Carmen, in their wet swimming things, look at the plate and at the clothes on the line.
- **At stake:** the sandwiches (3). A piece of clothing taken to the wrong person is handed back and falls on the sand; Carmen shakes the sand out of it, and the sand lands on a sandwich.
- **New words:** camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos.
- **Back again:** ropa (heard), seco, mojado, rojo, azul, amarillo, blanco, mi, tu, necesito, dame, toma, gracias, es de, come, pan, tengo hambre, quiero, quieres, ¿dónde está?, ¿cuál?, arena, sí, no.

**Start:** late afternoon. On the line: a red shirt, blue trousers, a yellow dress, and under the line two pairs of shoes on the bench's end. Luis and Carmen in wet swimming things, each wrapped in a towel. Abuela on the bench beside the covered plate. The frog on the bench's arm.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Carmen están mojados. La ropa está seca." **(ropa: heard; the line of clothes carries it)** Luis points up at the line. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira mi camisa. Mi camisa roja." Ask (signed, the red shirt): "¿Dónde está la camisa?" He touches it: the frog runs along the line and unpegs it; it drops into Luis's arms. NARR: "Luis dice: Gracias. Y necesito el pantalón."
2. Ask (signed, the blue trousers): "¿Cuál es el pantalón?" He touches it: the frog unpegs the trousers. Luis goes into the cabin with the shirt and the trousers; the curtain shakes; he comes out dressed. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Camisa y pantalón!"
3. Carmen points. NARR: "Carmen dice: El vestido es mío. Dame el vestido amarillo, Coquí." **(mío: heard)** Ask (signed, the yellow dress): "¿Dónde está el vestido?" He touches it: the dress comes down; Carmen goes in and comes out in it, and spins. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Y mis zapatos?" Luis holds up his own pair. NARR: "Luis dice: Estos zapatos son míos. Tus zapatos son blancos." Ask (signed, the white shoes): "¿Dónde están los zapatos de Carmen?" He touches them: the frog pushes them to her.
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes hambre?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre, sí tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí, tengo hambre!" She lifts the cloth: three sandwiches. NARR: "Abuela dice: Después. La ropa, y después el pan."

**The turn**

5. A wave of wind: the washing of the family at the next palm blows across and catches on the line beside theirs: more shirts, more trousers, a dress, shoes in a heap under the bench. Luis and Carmen have gone back into the water; they come out wet again, laughing, and stand in their towels. Abuela shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Otra vez mojados!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** On the line hang a shirt, a pair of trousers and a dress, and on the bench's end stands a pair of shoes (dry ones, from the bag). Luis or Carmen (drawn) asks for one of the four, drawn, each round on its own. The frog brings it.

6. **Decision A, touch a piece of clothing (the four; tests the word).** NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, necesito el pantalón." (Drawn: "... la camisa.", "... los zapatos."; Carmen: "... el vestido.", "... la camisa.", "... los zapatos.")
   - Right: the frog brings it; the child goes into the cabin and comes out with it on. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings another. NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. El pantalón." He hands it back; it slips and falls on the sand. Carmen picks it up and shakes it: a cloud of sand over the bench, and onto a sandwich. A cross. The sign is on the right piece; he touches it: the frog brings it.
7. **Decision B, touch a piece of clothing (the other child).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, necesito los zapatos." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Both are dressed and dry. Abuela lifts the cloth.
   - Three sandwiches: one for Luis, one for Carmen, and one for abuela, who gives the frog its corner. Stars and a heart.
   - Two clean, one sandy: Luis and Carmen eat; abuela brushes the third off for Canelo.
   - One clean: abuela cuts it in three; Canelo gets two.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres pan, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "Luis tiene camisa y pantalón. Carmen tiene vestido y zapatos. Y Coquí come."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** camisa 4 (steps 1, 2), pantalón 3 (steps 1, 2), vestido 3 (step 3), zapatos 4 (step 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the piece that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own. (A learner may think a dress is Carmen's: Carmen asks for a shirt as often as for the dress.)
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): tengo hambre; quiero.

## Story 79: ¡Mi sombrero!

- **Goal he can see:** at the water's edge, a line of things rocking on the small waves, coming in: abuela's sun hat, Luis's dark glasses, Carmen's sandals, a swimming suit, a towel. A big wave has taken the family's bag. Abuela stands with her hands on her head.
- **At stake:** the three things that are still out in the water after the opening. The frog hops in for one thing at a time; if he brings the wrong one, the next wave carries the one that was asked for out to sea.
- **New words:** chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla.
- **Back again:** mar, ola, playa, mojado, seco, mi, tu, dame, busca, ¿dónde está?, mira, viene, se va, grande, cuidado, rápido, gracias, toma, sí, no, abuela.

**Start:** afternoon. The bench, the line (empty) and the cabin. The bag lies open and empty at the water's edge; five things float in the shallow water. Abuela, Luis and Carmen at the edge; the frog on the wet sand.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "¡Una ola grande!" The wave slides back; the things rock in the water. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! ¡Mi sombrero! Coquí, busca mi sombrero." Ask (signed, the sun hat): "¿Dónde está el sombrero?" He touches it: the frog hops in, pushes the hat to the edge with his nose; abuela puts it on, dripping. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. ¡Un sombrero mojado!"
2. Luis points. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: una toalla. La toalla de abuela." Ask (signed, the towel): "¿Y la toalla?" He touches it: the frog drags the towel in; Luis hangs it on the line. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mis chancletas! Y las gafas de Luis. Y mi traje de baño."
3. A small wave lifts the things and sets them down a little further out. NARR: "Luis dice: Las gafas, las chancletas y el traje de baño. ¡Rápido, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops to the edge. Abuela points at each in turn; each twinkles as she names it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las chancletas. Las gafas. El traje de baño."

**The turn**

4. Out past the things a big wave is building. Abuela holds the bag open. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Viene una ola. Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Three things are still in the water: the sandals, the glasses, the swimming suit; they lie in a drawn order. One of the family (drawn) asks for one of them, drawn; in round B one of the two that are left (if round A's thing was lost, of the two that are left all the same).

5. **Decision A, touch a thing in the water (three things; tests the word).** NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Coquí, mis chancletas!" (Drawn: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, mis gafas!", "Carmen dice: ¡Coquí, mi traje de baño!")
   - Right: the frog brings it in; it goes into the bag. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings in another thing. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No! ¡Mis chancletas!" The wave comes, and when it slides back the sandals are far out, two small dots; then gone. A cross. (The thing he brought stays in the bag.) The sign is not shown: there is nothing left to touch.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing in the water (what is left).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, mis gafas!" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The last wave brings whatever is still afloat up onto the sand by itself. Abuela looks into the bag.
   - Nothing lost: sandals, glasses and swimming suit all on the line, with the towel; abuela in her wet hat. Stars and a heart.
   - One thing lost: its owner shrugs; abuela gives him her hat to wear.
   - Two things lost: Luis and Carmen walk home in one sandal each... (to settle in the reviews: a kinder picture).
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Una toalla seca." She wraps him up. COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" NARR: "El sombrero está mojado. Y Coquí está seco."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** chancletas 3, gafas 3, traje de baño 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: sombrero 4; toalla 4.
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A; one in two in round B.
**To settle in the reviews:** after a wrong move there is no second touch (the thing is gone): the kit's signed touch after a wrong move is missing here. And "stake 3" with two rounds: the third thing always comes in.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): voy; gracias.

## Story 80: ¿Qué lleva?

- **Goal he can see:** far off at the right, the guagua at its stop, its door open; the driver leans out and holds up three fingers. Abuela, with the bag packed, looks along the beach: it is full of children, and she cannot see which are hers.
- **At stake:** the driver's patience (his three fingers). Each time the frog fetches the wrong child, the driver sounds his horn and puts a finger down.
- **New words:** se pone, se quita, lleva.
- **Back again:** camisa, pantalón, vestido, zapatos, chancletas, sombrero, gafas, traje de baño, toalla, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, guagua, se va, se monta, espera, ¿dónde está?, busca, niño, niña, allá, lejos, ven, vamos, rápido, ahora, tarde.

**Start:** the sun low. Abuela at the bench with the bag. Luis and Carmen at the cabin, in swimming things. The frog on the bench. Along the wet sand, far off and small, six or seven children playing with their backs to us. At the right, the road and the guagua.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es tarde. La guagua espera." The driver holds up three fingers. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Rápido! Luis, la camisa." Luis pulls his shirt over his head. NARR: "Luis se pone la camisa." Then the cap. NARR: "Y se pone el sombrero." Ask (signed, Luis): "¿Quién lleva sombrero?" He touches him: Luis tips his cap.
2. Carmen comes out of the cabin in her dress, with her dark glasses on. NARR: "Carmen se pone el vestido. Y lleva gafas." Abuela shakes her head and points at the low sun. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen, es tarde. No hay sol." NARR: "Carmen se quita las gafas." Ask (signed, Carmen): "¿Quién lleva un vestido?" He touches her: she spins.
3. Luis takes his cap off, and puts it on the frog. NARR: "Luis se quita el sombrero. ¡Ahora Coquí lleva sombrero!" NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿vienes?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" But the two run off along the sand for one last game, in among the other children.

**The turn**

4. Abuela shades her eyes: seven small children, far off, with their backs to her. The driver looks at his watch. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Dónde está Luis? ¿Dónde está Carmen? Coquí, busca." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Among the children far off, two are alike but for one thing they wear (drawn each round: a colour of shirt or dress, or with a hat or without, with glasses or without). Abuela says what her own child is wearing. The frog fetches one.

5. **Decision A, touch a child (the two who are alike, among the others; tests the description).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis lleva una camisa roja y un sombrero." Two boys in red shirts stand side by side, one in a hat. (Drawn: "... una camisa azul y gafas."; and so on.)
   - Right: the frog hops along the sand and pulls at his leg: Luis turns round and runs back with him. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pulls at the other one's leg: a strange boy turns round, and laughs. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Luis lleva sombrero." The horn sounds; the driver puts a finger down. A cross. The sign is on the right child; he touches him: Luis comes.
6. **Decision B, touch a child (two girls who are alike).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen lleva un vestido amarillo. No lleva gafas." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The four of them run for the road.
   - Three fingers: the driver waits, smiling; they get on and sit at the front. Stars and a heart.
   - Two fingers: he waits, tapping the wheel; they get on.
   - One finger: the guagua has started to roll; abuela waves the towel, it stops, and they climb on, out of breath.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y mi sombrero?" The frog still has Luis's cap on; abuela's sun hat is on Canelo. COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Aquí!" NARR: "Coquí se quita el sombrero. Y la guagua se va."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lleva 5 (steps 1, 2, 3). Not deciding: se pone 3 (steps 1, 2); se quita 2 before the game and once in the ending (to fix: one more in the opening).
**A miss is filed under:** lleva.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two for a learner who has found the two who are alike; one in seven as the children go.
**What the rounds test:** the words of stories 78 and 79 inside "lleva ...". Lleva is the same in both of abuela's sentences, so by itself it decides only in round B ("No lleva gafas").
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): voy; aquí.

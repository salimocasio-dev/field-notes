# La mesa: three stories (draft 3, after two rounds of reviews)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 1 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (stories 1 to 11), and place 2, El parque (stories 12 to 23).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, otra, su, luna, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for La mesa (from the 40-scene list): mesa, plato, taza, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, cocina, pone, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo.

The picture: abuela's kitchen. On the left, a counter with a cupboard over it. In the middle, the table, seen a little from above so that what lies on it can be seen; four chairs stand behind it, facing us. On the right, the stove, and a window over it. The floor in front of the table is where the frog, Luis and abuela walk: they stand lower in the picture than the table top, so they never hide what lies on it.

A place at the table is named by the person sitting at it; that person twinkles when the line is said. A place holds two things side by side at most; a second plate goes on top of the first, a second cup into the first. A mess (crumbs, a puddle, a stain) lies in front of the place, near our edge of the table, and stays to the end.

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Abuela dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said; then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the story goes on without the signal.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named in a sentence ("Es la mesa." "Es un plato." "Es abuela.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
A line that says what somebody is doing is spoken while they are doing it, never after.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Answer bubbles, as before:
- **Picture bubbles**: each shows a thing; he touches one. Places drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
In this scene the bubbles stand in the upper part of the picture, clear of the table and the people.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game: a star for a round done right at the first try, a red cross for a round that went wrong. Both stay.

The three games open differently: 24 with a touch on a thing, 25 with a touch on a person, 26 with something he says.

## Story 24: La mesa

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a table with a cloth and nothing on it, four chairs, a bizcocho on the stove, a jug of hot chocolate beside it, and one little cake with a cherry. Get a plate and a cup to each place, so that abuela can serve.
- **At stake:** the merienda. After the turn abuela has exactly three slices on her board and three cups of chocolate in the jug: one of each for each bare place. She serves as soon as a thing is set down, with her eyes on the baby: the slice goes onto what she asked for, the chocolate into what she asked for. A slice put on a cup tips over and breaks on the table. Chocolate poured on a plate runs off into a puddle. A plate put where a plate already stands knocks the slice off it, and the slice breaks on the table; a cup put where a cup stands knocks it over, and the chocolate is a puddle. The mess stays, and somebody's plate or cup stays empty to the end, because there is no spare slice and no spare chocolate. A full cup steams; an empty one does not. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** mesa, plato, taza, pone.
- **Back again:** abuela, abuelo, mamá, papá, bebé, familia, bizcocho, para, tiene, hay, aquí, no, mira, otra vez, gracias, ¿quién?, se sienta.
- **Heard along the way:** chocolate, la mesa está lista, a comer.

**Start:** the table has its cloth and nothing else. On the counter: a stack of plates and a row of cups. Abuela stands at the stove. Luis stands at the counter. The frog stands in front of the table. Nobody sits yet.

1. NARR: "Esta es la mesa." (The table twinkles.) NARR: "Aquí hay un plato." (One plate lifts off the stack and twinkles.) NARR: "Y aquí hay una taza." (One cup lifts out of the row and twinkles.)
2. Abuelo, papá and mamá come in and sit down; mamá has the baby on her lap. While they sit down: NARR: "La familia se sienta." NARR: "El bebé está con mamá." (The baby twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Abuelo twinkles. Abuela comes to the front of the table with a slice of bizcocho on a server in one hand and the jug in the other. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un plato para abuelo." The big sign is on the stack of plates. He touches it. The frog takes a plate, hops to the table and puts it down in front of abuelo; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone el plato en la mesa." Right signal. Abuela puts the slice on the plate; while she does: NARR: "Abuela pone el bizcocho en el plato."
4. **He calls for what is missing, taught.** Abuelo's place has a plate and no cup. Abuelo twinkles. Luis stands at the counter, one hand over the plates and one over the cups. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Un plato!" and "¡Una taza!" The big sign is on "¡Una taza!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Una taza!" Luis brings a cup and puts it down; while he does: NARR: "Luis pone la taza en la mesa." Right signal. Abuela fills the cup; while she does: NARR: "Y el chocolate, en la taza."
5. **Who has none, taught.** Luis goes along the table with a plate in one hand and a cup in the other, and deals them out: the plate to mamá, the cup to papá. Abuela stands at the counter. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene taza?" The big sign is on mamá. He touches her. The frog takes her a cup. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora mamá tiene un plato y una taza."
6. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene plato?" The big sign is on papá. He touches him. The frog takes him a plate. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora papá tiene una taza y un plato."
7. Carmen runs in and sits down on the last chair. Her place is bare. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una taza para Carmen." The big sign is on the cups. He touches them. The frog puts a cup at her place; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone la taza en la mesa." Right signal.
8. Carmen's place has a cup and no plate. Carmen twinkles; Luis is at the counter. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un plato!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un plato!" Luis brings a plate; while he puts it down: NARR: "Luis pone el plato en la mesa." Right signal.

**The turn**

9. Abuela is at the stove, cutting three slices onto her board. The baby takes hold of the tablecloth and pulls. The plates and cups of mamá, papá and Carmen slide along the table and go over the edge with the cloth; they clatter on the floor in a heap. Nothing breaks. Abuelo has lifted his plate and his cup in time and holds them up. NARR: "¡Ay, no! ¡El bebé!" Luis carries the cloth and the heap out. The table is bare wood; abuelo puts his plate and cup down again. Mamá holds the baby with both arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Otra vez!" The frog hops to the counter; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone la mesa." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Three places are bare: mamá's, papá's and Carmen's. Which of them is first, second and third is drawn fresh.

10. **Decision A, Touch (which thing).** The first of the three twinkles. Abuela stands in front of the table with the board of slices in one hand and the jug in the other; she is looking at the baby. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un plato para papá." (Or: "Una taza para papá." Drawn fresh; the name is the one who twinkles.) He touches the plates or the cups.
   - Right: the frog puts it at that place, and abuela serves it at once: the slice onto the plate (or the chocolate into the cup). Right signal. A star. Luis brings the other thing, and abuela serves that too.
   - Wrong: the frog puts the other thing at that place, and abuela, looking at the baby, serves what she asked for: the slice lands on the cup, tips over and breaks on the table (or: the chocolate is poured on the plate and runs off into a puddle on the table). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá no tiene plato." (Or: "no tiene taza". True: the place has the other thing only.) The sign is on the right pile; he touches it; the frog brings it. It stays empty: the slices on the board are for the other two (and so is the chocolate). Abuela serves the thing that was brought first.
11. **Decision B, Touch (who has none).** Luis deals again: a plate to one of the two places that are still bare and a cup to the other (which gets which is drawn fresh). Abuela serves both: a slice on the plate, chocolate in the cup. Then she stands in front of the table with the board and the jug, looking at the baby. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene taza?" (Or: "¿Quién no tiene plato?" Drawn fresh; but after a miss in round A she asks for the other thing than in round A, so that every place holds one that is full.) He touches one of the four at the table.
   - Right: the frog takes it to that one, and abuela serves it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes it to the one he touched, who has that thing already. The cup knocks over the cup that stands there, and the chocolate is a puddle on the table (or: the plate knocks the slice off the plate that stands there, and the slice breaks on the table). Abuela, looking at the baby, serves the new one. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen no tiene taza." (The true one; she twinkles.) The sign; he touches her; the frog takes her a cup. It stays empty: the jug is empty now (or: the board is empty now).
12. **Decision C, Say, last (what is missing).** One place is still short of one thing: the place that got only a plate from Luis has no cup, or the other way round. That person twinkles. Luis stands at the counter, one hand over the plates and one over the cups. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Un plato!" and "¡Una taza!"
   - Right: Luis brings it; abuela serves it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis brings what the frog called and puts it where that thing already stands: the plate knocks the slice off, and it breaks (or: the cup knocks the full cup over, and the chocolate is a puddle). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá no tiene taza." (The true one.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis brings it; abuela serves it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. NARR: "La mesa está lista." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A comer!" Then everybody eats and drinks what he has. By the messes on the table:
   - None (three stars): four neat places. Abuela puts the little cake with the cherry, which has stood on the stove since the first frame, on the floor in front of the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - One, two or three: the crumbs and the puddles lie where they fell. Whoever has an empty plate or an empty cup looks into it.
14. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** plato 9 (step 1; step 3 three times; steps 4 and 8, the bubble; step 5; step 6 twice; step 8: the frog, the line after), taza 9 (step 1; step 4: the bubble, the frog, twice after; step 5 twice; step 6; step 7 twice; step 8, the bubble), "no tiene" 2, each time under the sign with the answer shown. Not deciding, but heard: mesa 8, pone 7.

**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that was asked. B: the thing that was asked. C: the thing that was missing.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in four (one in two for someone who sees that only two places are short). C one in two. C's answer is always the word that round B did not ask; to use that he must still tell the two bubbles apart by ear.

## Story 25: La cuchara

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** lunch. The family sits at a table with a white cloth, abuela has the food on the stove, and in the tray on the counter there are just three things to eat with: one spoon, one fork, one knife. Get the right one to whoever is eating.
- **At stake:** the white cloth. Whoever gets the wrong thing tries to eat with it all the same, and the food drops off it onto the cloth: soup runs through a fork and off a knife, spaghetti slides off a spoon and off a knife, butter drops off a spoon and off a fork. The stain stays in front of that place to the end, in the colour of the dish. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida.
- **Back again:** mesa, plato, pone, tiene, come, para, con, hay, aquí, ahora, más, pan, ¿quién?, no, mira.
- **Heard along the way:** nothing new.

The three dishes, each with one thing to eat it with: a bowl of soup (spoon), a plate of spaghetti in red sauce (fork), a bread roll with butter (knife). No line names a dish except "pan".

Spoon, fork and knife are drawn big: a spoon with a round bowl, a fork with three prongs, a knife with a straight blade. There is only one of each, and all through the story they go from hand to hand. Whoever has one and is not eating holds it up over his head, twice the size. Nothing is ever left lying beside a dish.

**Start:** abuelo, mamá, papá and Carmen sit at the table. Abuela stands at the stove with the pot. The tray lies on the counter; Luis stands beside it. The frog stands in front of the table.

1. NARR: "Abuela tiene la comida." (The pot twinkles.) She brings a bowl of soup to mamá and a plate of spaghetti to papá; while she does: NARR: "Abuela pone la comida en la mesa."
2. The three rise out of the tray, one at a time, and twinkle. NARR: "Aquí hay una cuchara." NARR: "Aquí hay un tenedor." NARR: "Y aquí hay un cuchillo."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Mamá twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una cuchara para mamá." Three picture bubbles: a spoon, a fork, a knife. The big sign is on the spoon. He touches it. The frog takes the spoon from the tray to mamá. Right signal. While she eats her soup: NARR: "Mamá come con la cuchara."
4. Papá twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un tenedor para papá." The three picture bubbles; the sign is on the fork. He touches it. The frog takes him the fork. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Papá come con el tenedor."
5. **Who has it, taught.** Luis takes the knife out of the tray, the last thing in it, and holds it up. Abuela puts a bread roll with butter in front of abuelo. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién tiene el cuchillo?" The big sign is on Luis. He touches him. The frog takes the knife from Luis to abuelo. Right signal. While abuelo butters his bread: NARR: "Ahora abuelo tiene el cuchillo." When he bites: NARR: "Abuelo come pan."
6. **He calls for what is missing, taught.** Mamá's bowl is empty; abuela takes it away, and mamá holds her spoon up. Abuela puts a bowl of soup in front of Carmen. Carmen looks at it; she has nothing to eat it with. She twinkles. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Una cuchara!", "¡Un tenedor!", "¡Un cuchillo!" The big sign is on "¡Una cuchara!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Una cuchara!" Mamá passes her spoon along to Carmen. Right signal. While Carmen eats: NARR: "Carmen come con la cuchara."
7. Papá's plate is empty; abuela takes it away, and papá holds his fork up. Abuela puts a plate of spaghetti in front of mamá. She twinkles. The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un tenedor!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un tenedor!" Papá passes his fork to mamá. Right signal. While she eats: NARR: "Mamá come con el tenedor."
8. Abuelo's bread is eaten; abuela takes the plate, and abuelo holds the knife up. Abuela puts a bread roll with butter in front of papá. He twinkles. The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un cuchillo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un cuchillo!" Abuelo passes the knife to papá. Right signal. While papá butters his bread: NARR: "Ahora papá tiene el cuchillo."

**The turn**

9. The dishes are empty. Abuela takes them away, and Luis takes the empty tray out. Abuela lifts the lid of the pot; steam. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hay más comida!" Carmen holds up the spoon, mamá the fork, papá the knife. Abuelo has nothing in his hands. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Touch (who has it).** Abuela stands at the stove, filling a dish we cannot see, for abuelo; he twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién tiene la cuchara?" (Or: "el tenedor", "el cuchillo". Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the three who hold something up.
   - Right: the frog takes it from that one to abuelo. Abuela brings the dish that goes with it. Abuelo eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes over the thing of the one he touched. Abuela brings the dish, and abuelo tries to eat with what he has: the food drops onto the cloth. A stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen tiene la cuchara." (The true one; she twinkles and holds it up.) The sign; he touches her; the frog takes it over, and takes the wrong thing back to the one it came from. Abuelo eats.
11. **Decision B, Say (what is missing).** Two at the table still hold a thing up. Abuela brings one of them (drawn fresh) a dish, and it is the dish for the thing the other one holds: spaghetti in front of the one with the knife, when the other one holds the fork. That one looks at the dish and at the thing in his hand, and twinkles. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Una cuchara!", "¡Un tenedor!", "¡Un cuchillo!"
   - Right: the two change over: whoever holds what the frog called passes it along and gets the other thing back. That one eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: whoever holds what the frog called changes with him (if the frog called the thing he has already, he holds it up, shrugs, and keeps it). He tries to eat with it: the food drops onto the cloth. A stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá no tiene tenedor." (The true one.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; they change over; that one eats; and if abuelo's thing went round, it goes back to abuelo.
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (which one).** Abuelo and the one from round B have eaten; abuela takes their dishes, and they hold their things up. So three hold something up again, and the one who gave his away in round A sits with empty hands; he twinkles. Abuela is at the stove, filling a dish we cannot see. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un tenedor para Carmen." (Or: "Una cuchara", "Un cuchillo". Drawn fresh; the name is the one who twinkles.) Three picture bubbles: a spoon, a fork, a knife.
   - Right: the frog fetches it from whoever holds it and takes it to that one. Abuela brings the dish. That one eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog fetches the one he touched. Abuela brings the dish; that one tries to eat with it. A stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen no tiene tenedor." The sign shows the right picture; he touches it; the frog fetches it, and takes the wrong thing back. That one eats.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Abuela goes along the table with a basket and gives everybody a bread roll; all four eat. By the stains on the cloth:
   - None (three stars): a clean white cloth. Abuela gives the frog a roll too. Stars and a heart.
   - One, two or three: the stains lie in front of the places where they were made.
14. NARR: "La familia come." COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡La comida de abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cuchara 7 (step 2; step 3 twice; step 6: the bubble, the frog, the line after; steps 7 and 8, the bubble), tenedor 7 (step 2; step 4 twice; step 6, the bubble; step 7: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 8, the bubble), cuchillo 7 (step 2; step 5 twice; steps 6 and 7, the bubble; step 8: the bubble, the frog, the line after). Not deciding, but heard: comida 3 before the game, 4 with the last line.

**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked, or was missing.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in three. B one in three. C one in three.

**Not under the sign in every way:** six signed touches cannot show each of the three things in each of the three ways. The knife is never the signed picture, and the spoon and the fork are never the signed "who has it".

## Story 26: Tengo hambre

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** abuela's food is ready, and the family comes in from the yard one after the other, some hungry, some thirsty. Bring each one what he asks for.
- **At stake:** lunch. From the turn on there are exactly four plates of food and four glasses of water on the counter, one of each for abuelo, mamá, papá and the frog; the pot and the jug are empty. Whoever gets the wrong thing puts it aside at the edge of the table, and Canelo, who lies in his corner and watches every plate, has it: he eats the food, or knocks the glass over and laps the water up. That plate or glass is gone: at the end the one it was for sits without it, and the empty plate or the fallen glass lies beside Canelo. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo, cocina.
- **Back again:** comida, agua, plato, vaso, toma, gracias, come, bebe, ¿y tú?, ahora, perro, contento, familia, entra.
- **Heard along the way:** tiene hambre, tiene sed, tienes, la comida está lista, esa.

A thought bubble with a picture in it (a plate of food, or a glass of water) shows what somebody wants. It is taught on the frog in the opening.

**Start:** abuela at the stove with the pot. The table is bare wood. On the counter: a jug of water. The frog stands by the counter. The door to the yard is at the left edge. Nobody else is in the room.

1. NARR: "Esta es la cocina de abuela." NARR: "Abuela está en la cocina." Abuela lifts the lid of the pot; steam. NARR: "La comida está lista."
2. Abuela fills two plates from the pot and two glasses from the jug and sets them on the counter: plates on the left, glasses on the right. When the last one stands: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo!"

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Luis runs in and sits down at the table; while he comes: NARR: "Luis entra a la cocina." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" The big sign is on the plates of food. He touches them. The frog takes Luis a plate. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Luis tiene hambre. Luis come."
4. Carmen comes in and sits down. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo sed!" The sign is on the glasses. He touches them. The frog takes her a glass of water. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Carmen tiene sed. Carmen bebe agua."
5. **He says it himself, taught.** A thought bubble over the frog shows a glass of water. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Tengo hambre!" and "¡Tengo sed!" The big sign is on "¡Tengo sed!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Tengo sed!" Right signal. Abuela puts a glass on the floor in front of him; while she does: NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma agua, Coquí." He drinks.
6. A thought bubble over the frog shows a plate of food. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y ahora?" The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Tengo hambre!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Tengo hambre!" Right signal. Abuela puts a little plate in front of him; while she does: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo!" He eats. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!"
7. **Who said it, taught.** Luis's plate and Carmen's glass are empty. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo sed!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" Abuela holds out a plate of food; it twinkles. The big sign is on Carmen. He touches her. The frog takes her the plate. Right signal. While she eats: NARR: "Carmen tiene hambre. Carmen come." Abuela takes Luis a glass.
8. Luis and Carmen run out to the yard again.

**The turn**

9. Abuela fills four plates and four glasses and stands them on the counter. She tips the pot towards us: it is empty. The jug is empty too. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo!" Canelo comes in at the door, sits down in front of the counter and looks up at the plates. He whines. NARR: "Canelo tiene hambre." NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Canelo. Esa es la comida de la familia." Canelo goes to his corner by the stove, lies down and watches. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Say (what he wants himself).** A thought bubble over the frog shows a plate of food, or a glass of water (drawn fresh). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Tengo hambre!" and "¡Tengo sed!"
   - Right: abuela puts it in front of him; he eats (or drinks). Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuela puts in front of him what he said. He does not touch it; the thought bubble is still there. Canelo comes and has it. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Tienes sed." (Or: "Tienes hambre." The thought bubble twinkles.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; abuela gives it to him.
11. **Decision B, Touch (food or water).** Abuelo comes in and sits down, hands on the table. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Tengo sed!" (Or: "¡Tengo hambre!" Drawn fresh. He sits the same way either time.) He touches the plates or the glasses.
   - Right: the frog takes it to him. He eats (or drinks). Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuelo shakes his head and puts it aside at the edge of the table. Canelo has it. A cross. A thought bubble over abuelo shows the glass (or the plate), and twinkles. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Abuelo tiene sed." (Or: "tiene hambre".) The sign is on the right ones; he touches them; abuelo drinks (or eats).
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (who said it).** Mamá and papá come in together and sit down, both the same way, hands on the table. Abuela holds out a plate of food. (Or: a glass of water. Drawn fresh.) Then each says one thing, one after the other (who says which, and who speaks first, is drawn fresh). NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Tengo sed!" The thing in abuela's hand twinkles. He touches mamá or papá.
   - Right: the frog takes it to that one. Right signal. A star. Abuela takes the other thing to the other one.
   - Wrong: the frog takes it to the other one, who puts it aside. Canelo has it. A cross. A thought bubble over the right one shows the thing, and twinkles. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá tiene hambre." (The true line for the thing he carried.) The sign; he touches her; the frog takes her another from the counter. Abuela takes the other thing to the other one.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Abuela hands out what is left on the counter, so that abuelo, mamá, papá and the frog each have a plate and a glass, as far as it goes; when the last is set down: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo!" Everybody eats and drinks what he has. By what lies beside Canelo:
   - Nothing (three stars): everybody has a plate and a glass. Abuela gives Canelo a bone. Stars and a heart.
   - One, two or three: whoever's plate or glass Canelo had sits without it. Canelo lies beside the empty plates and the fallen glasses, wagging his tail.
14. NARR: "La familia come en la cocina." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y Canelo?" COQUÍ: "¡Canelo está contento!"

## For the owner, one line each

- "Pone" is your word; the command to one person is "pon". The stories use "pone" only in what the narrator says ("Coquí pone la mesa"), never as a command.

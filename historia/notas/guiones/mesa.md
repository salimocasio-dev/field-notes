# La mesa: three stories (draft 2, after the first two reviews)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 1 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (stories 1 to 11), and place 2, El parque (stories 12 to 23).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, otra, su, luna, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for La mesa (from the 40-scene list): mesa, plato, taza, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, cocina, pone, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo.

The picture: abuela's kitchen. On the left, a counter with a cupboard over it. In the middle, the table, seen a little from above so that what lies on it can be seen; four chairs stand behind it, facing us. On the right, the stove, and a window over it. The floor in front of the table is where the frog, Luis and abuela walk: they stand lower in the picture than the table top, so they never hide what lies on it.

A place at the table is named by the person sitting at it; that person twinkles when the line is said. A place holds two things side by side at most; a second plate goes on top of the first, a second cup into the first. A mess (crumbs, a puddle, a stain) lies in front of the place, near our edge of the table, and stays to the end.

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Abuela dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said; then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the story goes on without the signal.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named in a sentence ("Es la mesa." "Es un plato." "Es abuela.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
A line that says what somebody is doing is spoken while they are doing it, never after.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Answer bubbles, as before:
- **Picture bubbles**: each shows a thing; he touches one. Places drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
In this scene the bubbles stand in the upper part of the picture, clear of the table and the people.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game: a star for a round done right at the first try, a red cross for a round that went wrong. Both stay.

The three games open differently: 24 with a touch on a thing, 25 with a touch on a person, 26 with something he says.

## Story 24: La mesa

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a table with a cloth and nothing on it, four chairs, a bizcocho on the stove and a jug of hot chocolate beside it. Get a plate and a cup to each place, so that abuela can serve.
- **At stake:** the merienda. Abuela serves as soon as a thing is set down, with her eyes on the baby: the slice goes onto what she asked for, the chocolate into what she asked for. A slice put on a cup tips over and breaks on the table. Chocolate poured on a plate runs off into a puddle. A plate put where a plate already stands goes on top of the slice and squashes it; a cup put where a cup stands goes into it, and the chocolate runs over. The mess stays, and the plate or cup that comes afterwards stays empty to the end. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** mesa, plato, taza, pone.
- **Back again:** abuela, abuelo, mamá, papá, bebé, familia, bizcocho, para, tiene, hay, aquí, no, mira, otra vez, gracias, ¿quién?, se sienta.
- **Heard along the way:** chocolate, la mesa está lista, a comer.

**Start:** the table has its cloth and nothing else. On the counter: a stack of plates and a row of cups. Abuela stands at the stove. Luis stands at the counter. The frog stands in front of the table. Nobody sits yet.

1. NARR: "Esta es la mesa." (The table twinkles.) NARR: "Aquí hay un plato." (The stack of plates twinkles.) NARR: "Y aquí hay una taza." (The row of cups twinkles.)
2. Abuelo, papá and mamá come in and sit down; mamá has the baby on her lap. While they sit down: NARR: "La familia se sienta." NARR: "El bebé está con mamá." (The baby twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Abuelo twinkles. Abuela comes to the front of the table with a slice of bizcocho on a server in one hand and the jug in the other. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un plato para abuelo." The big sign is on the stack of plates. He touches it. The frog takes a plate, hops to the table and puts it down in front of abuelo; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone el plato en la mesa." Right signal. Abuela puts the slice on the plate; while she does: NARR: "Abuela pone el bizcocho en el plato."
4. **He calls for what is missing, taught.** Abuelo's place has a plate and no cup. Abuelo twinkles. Luis stands at the counter, one hand over the plates and one over the cups. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Un plato!" and "¡Una taza!" The big sign is on "¡Una taza!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Una taza!" Luis brings a cup and puts it down; while he does: NARR: "Luis pone la taza en la mesa." Right signal. Abuela fills the cup; while she does: NARR: "Y el chocolate, en la taza."
5. **Who has none, taught.** Luis goes along the table with a plate in one hand and a cup in the other, and deals them out: the plate to mamá, the cup to papá. Abuela stands at the counter. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene taza?" The big sign is on mamá. He touches her. The frog takes her a cup. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora mamá tiene un plato y una taza."
6. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene plato?" The big sign is on papá. He touches him. The frog takes him a plate. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora papá tiene una taza y un plato."
7. Carmen runs in and sits down on the last chair. Her place is bare. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una taza para Carmen." The big sign is on the cups. He touches them. The frog puts a cup at her place; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone la taza en la mesa." Right signal.
8. Carmen's place has a cup and no plate. Carmen twinkles; Luis is at the counter. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un plato!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un plato!" Luis brings a plate; while he puts it down: NARR: "Luis pone el plato en la mesa." Right signal.

**The turn**

9. Abuela is at the stove, cutting the bizcocho. The baby takes hold of the tablecloth and pulls. The plates and cups of mamá, papá and Carmen slide along the table and go over the edge with the cloth; they clatter on the floor in a heap. Nothing breaks. Abuelo has lifted his plate and his cup in time and holds them up. NARR: "¡Ay, no! ¡El bebé!" Luis carries the cloth and the heap out. The table is bare wood; abuelo puts his plate and cup down again. Mamá holds the baby with both arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Otra vez!" The frog hops to the counter; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone la mesa." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Three places are bare: mamá's, papá's and Carmen's. Which of them is first, second and third is drawn fresh.

10. **Decision A, Touch (which thing).** The first of the three twinkles. Abuela stands in front of the table with a slice on the server in one hand and the jug in the other; she is looking at the baby. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un plato para papá." (Or: "Una taza para papá." Drawn fresh; the name is the one who twinkles.) He touches the plates or the cups.
   - Right: the frog puts it at that place, and abuela serves it at once: the slice onto the plate (or the chocolate into the cup). Right signal. A star. Luis brings the other thing, and abuela serves that too.
   - Wrong: the frog puts the other thing at that place, and abuela, looking at the baby, serves what she asked for: the slice lands on the cup, tips over and breaks on the table (or: the chocolate is poured on the plate and runs off into a puddle on the table). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá no tiene plato." (Or: "no tiene taza". True: the place has the other thing only.) The sign is on the right pile; he touches it; the frog brings it. It stays empty. Abuela serves the thing that was brought first.
11. **Decision B, Touch (who has none).** Luis deals again: a plate to one of the two places that are still bare and a cup to the other (which gets which is drawn fresh). Abuela serves both: a slice on the plate, chocolate in the cup. Then she stands in front of the table with a slice and the jug. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene taza?" (Or: "¿Quién no tiene plato?" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the four at the table.
   - Right: the frog takes it to that one, and abuela serves it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes it to the one he touched, who has that thing already. The cup goes into the cup that stands there, and the chocolate runs over into a puddle (or: the plate goes on top of the slice and squashes it flat; crumbs). Abuela serves the new one. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen no tiene taza." (The true one; she twinkles.) The sign; he touches her; the frog takes her a cup. It stays empty: the jug is empty now (or: there is no slice left for the plate).
12. **Decision C, Say, last (what is missing).** One place is still short of one thing: the place that got only a plate from Luis has no cup, or the other way round. That person twinkles. Luis stands at the counter, one hand over the plates and one over the cups. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Un plato!" and "¡Una taza!"
   - Right: Luis brings it; abuela serves it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis brings what the frog called and puts it where that thing already stands: the plate goes on the slice and squashes it (or: the cup goes into the full cup, and the chocolate runs over). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá no tiene taza." (The true one.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis brings it; abuela serves it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. NARR: "La mesa está lista." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A comer!" Then everybody eats and drinks what he has. By the messes on the table:
   - None (three stars): four neat places. Abuela puts a small plate with the last slice on the floor in front of the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - One, two or three: the crumbs and the puddles lie where they fell. Whoever has an empty plate or an empty cup looks into it.
14. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** plato 9 (step 1; step 3 three times; steps 4 and 8, the bubble; step 5; step 6 twice; step 8: the frog, the line after), taza 9 (step 1; step 4: the bubble, the frog, twice after; step 5 twice; step 6; step 7 twice; step 8, the bubble), "no tiene" 2, each time under the sign with the answer shown. Not deciding, but heard: mesa 8, pone 7.

**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that was asked. B: the thing that was asked. C: the thing that was missing.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in four (one in two for someone who sees that only two places are short). C one in two.

## Story 25: La cuchara

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** lunch. The family sits at a table with a white cloth, abuela has the food on the stove, and the spoons, forks and knives are all in a tray on the counter. Get each one the thing to eat with.
- **At stake:** the white cloth. Whoever gets the wrong thing tries to eat with it all the same: soup runs through a fork, spaghetti slides off a spoon, butter flies off a fork. It lands on the cloth, and the stain stays to the end. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida.
- **Back again:** mesa, plato, pone, tiene, come, para, con, hay, aquí, más, pan, ¿quién?, no, mira.
- **Heard along the way:** nothing new.

The three dishes, each with one thing to eat it with: a bowl of soup (spoon), a plate of spaghetti in red sauce (fork), a bread roll with butter (knife). No line names a dish except "pan".

Spoon, fork and knife are drawn big: as long as a plate is wide, a spoon with a round bowl, a fork with three prongs, a knife with a straight blade. Held up, one stands beside its holder's head. On the table it lies beside the dish.

**Start:** abuelo, mamá, papá and Carmen sit at the table. Abuela stands at the stove with the pot. The tray lies on the counter; Luis stands beside it. The frog stands in front of the table.

1. NARR: "Abuela tiene la comida." (The pot twinkles.) She brings a bowl of soup to mamá and a plate of spaghetti to papá; while she does: NARR: "Abuela pone la comida en la mesa."
2. One of each kind rises out of the tray, one at a time, and twinkles. NARR: "Aquí hay una cuchara." NARR: "Aquí hay un tenedor." NARR: "Y aquí hay un cuchillo."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Mamá twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una cuchara para mamá." Three picture bubbles: a spoon, a fork, a knife. The big sign is on the spoon. He touches it. The frog takes a spoon from the tray to mamá. Right signal. While she eats her soup: NARR: "Mamá come con la cuchara."
4. Papá twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un tenedor para papá." The three picture bubbles; the sign is on the fork. He touches it. The frog takes him a fork. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Papá come con el tenedor."
5. **Who has it, taught.** Luis has taken a knife out of the tray and holds it up. Abuela puts a bread roll with butter in front of abuelo. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién tiene el cuchillo?" The big sign is on Luis. He touches him. The frog takes the knife from Luis to abuelo. Right signal. While abuelo butters his bread: NARR: "Ahora abuelo tiene el cuchillo. Abuelo come pan."
6. **He calls for what is missing, taught.** Abuela puts a bowl of soup in front of Carmen. Carmen looks at it; she has nothing to eat it with. Luis stands at the tray. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Una cuchara!", "¡Un tenedor!", "¡Un cuchillo!" The big sign is on "¡Una cuchara!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Una cuchara!" Luis brings her a spoon. Right signal. While she eats: NARR: "Carmen come con la cuchara."
7. Mamá's bowl is empty. Abuela takes it away and puts a plate of spaghetti in front of her. Mamá lays her spoon down and looks at Luis. The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un tenedor!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un tenedor!" Luis brings it. Right signal. While she eats: NARR: "Mamá come con el tenedor."
8. Papá's plate is empty. Abuela takes it away and puts a bread roll with butter in front of him. Papá lays his fork down. The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un cuchillo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un cuchillo!" Luis brings it. Right signal. While papá butters his bread: NARR: "Papá tiene el cuchillo."

**The turn**

9. The dishes are empty. Abuela takes them away. Luis goes along the table with the tray, and everybody's spoon, fork and knife go into it; while he walks: NARR: "Luis tiene las cucharas, los tenedores y los cuchillos." He trips. The tray flies; spoons, forks and knives clatter all over the floor. NARR: "¡Ay, Luis!" Three of the four at the table pick up one each from beside their chair, wipe it and hold it up: one a spoon, one a fork, one a knife (who holds which, and which one of the four holds none, is drawn fresh). Luis sweeps the rest into the tray and carries it out to be washed. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hay más comida!" The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Touch (who has it).** Abuela stands at the stove, filling a dish we cannot see, for the one at the table who holds nothing; that one twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién tiene la cuchara?" (Or: "el tenedor", "el cuchillo". Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the three who hold something up.
   - Right: the frog takes it from that one to the one who had nothing. Abuela brings the dish: the soup. That one eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes over the thing of the one he touched. Abuela brings the dish, and that one tries to eat with what he has: the soup runs through the fork onto the cloth. A stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen tiene la cuchara." (The true one; she twinkles and holds it up.) The sign; he touches her; the frog takes it over; that one eats. The wrong thing lies beside the dish.
11. **Decision B, Touch (which one).** Luis comes back with the washed tray and puts it on the counter. The one who gave his thing away in round A sits with empty hands, and twinkles. Abuela is at the stove again, filling a dish we cannot see. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un tenedor para abuelo." (Or: "Una cuchara", "Un cuchillo". Drawn fresh; the name is the one who twinkles.) Three picture bubbles: a spoon, a fork, a knife.
   - Right: the frog takes it from the tray to that one. Abuela brings the dish. That one eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes over the one he touched. Abuela brings the dish; that one tries to eat with it. A stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Abuelo no tiene tenedor." The sign shows the right picture; he touches it; the frog takes it over; that one eats.
12. **Decision C, Say, last (what is missing).** One who still holds up the thing he picked from the floor gets his dish, and it is not the dish for that thing: abuela puts spaghetti in front of the one with the knife (the dish is drawn fresh among the two that do not fit). That one lays his thing down beside the dish and looks at Luis, who stands at the tray. He twinkles. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Una cuchara!", "¡Un tenedor!", "¡Un cuchillo!"
   - Right: Luis brings it. That one eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis brings what the frog called; that one tries to eat with it. A stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá no tiene tenedor." (The true one.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis brings it and takes the wrong one back; that one eats.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Whoever has no dish yet gets one from abuela, with the right thing from Luis. Everybody eats. By the stains on the cloth:
   - None (three stars): a clean white cloth. Abuela puts a small bowl and a small spoon on the floor in front of the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - One, two or three: the stains lie in front of the places where they were made.
14. NARR: "La familia come." COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Comida!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cuchara 7 (step 2; step 3 twice; step 6: the bubble, the frog, the line after; steps 7 and 8, the bubble; the turn), tenedor 7 (step 2; step 4 twice; step 6, the bubble; step 7: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 8, the bubble; the turn), cuchillo 7 (step 2; step 5 twice; steps 6 and 7, the bubble; step 8: the bubble, the frog, the line after; the turn). Not deciding, but heard: comida 3 before the game, 4 with the last line.

**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked, or was missing.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in three. B one in three. C one in three.

## Story 26: Tengo hambre

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** abuela's food is ready, and the family comes in from the yard one after the other, some hungry, some thirsty. Bring each one what he asks for.
- **At stake:** what abuela has made for the family. Whoever gets the wrong thing puts it aside at the edge of the table, and Canelo, who sits in front of the table and watches every plate, has it: he eats the food, or knocks the glass over and laps the water up. The empty plate or the glass lies beside him to the end. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo, cocina.
- **Back again:** comida, agua, plato, vaso, quiero, toma, gracias, come, bebe, ¿y tú?, perro, contento, familia, entra.
- **Heard along the way:** tiene hambre, tiene sed, la comida está lista.

**Start:** abuela at the stove with the pot. The table is bare wood. On the counter: a jug of water. The frog stands by the counter. The door to the yard is at the left edge. Nobody else is in the room.

1. NARR: "Esta es la cocina de abuela." NARR: "Abuela está en la cocina." Abuela lifts the lid of the pot; steam. NARR: "La comida está lista."
2. Abuela fills two plates from the pot and two glasses from the jug and sets them on the counter: plates on the left, glasses on the right. When the last one stands: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo!"

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Luis runs in and sits down at the table; while he comes: NARR: "Luis entra en la cocina." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" The big sign is on the plates of food. He touches them. The frog takes Luis a plate. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Luis tiene hambre. Luis come."
4. Carmen comes in and sits down. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo sed!" The sign is on the glasses. He touches them. The frog takes her a glass of water. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Carmen tiene sed. Carmen bebe agua."
5. **He says it himself, taught.** A thought bubble over the frog shows a glass of water. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Tengo hambre!" and "¡Tengo sed!" The big sign is on "¡Tengo sed!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Tengo sed!" Abuela puts a glass on the floor in front of him; he drinks. Right signal. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Agua."
6. A thought bubble over the frog shows a plate of food. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y ahora?" The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Tengo hambre!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Tengo hambre!" Abuela gives him a little plate; he eats it up. Right signal. COQUÍ: "¡Listo!"
7. **Who said it, taught.** Luis's plate and Carmen's glass are empty. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo sed!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" Abuela fills a plate and holds it out; it twinkles. The big sign is on Carmen. He touches her. The frog takes her the plate. Right signal. While she eats: NARR: "Carmen tiene hambre. Carmen come." Abuela takes Luis a glass.
8. Luis and Carmen run out to the yard again.

**The turn**

9. Canelo comes in at the door, sits down in front of the table and looks at the pot. He whines. NARR: "Canelo tiene hambre." NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Canelo. Es la comida de la familia." Canelo lies down in front of the table and watches. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Abuela fills a plate from the pot or a glass from the jug whenever one is needed; there is always one of each on the counter.

10. **Decision A, Say (what he wants himself).** A thought bubble over the frog shows a plate of food, or a glass of water (drawn fresh). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Tengo hambre!" and "¡Tengo sed!"
   - Right: abuela gives him what he wanted. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuela gives him what he said. He does not touch it; the thought bubble is still there. Canelo comes and has it: he eats the food (or knocks the glass over and laps the water up). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Coquí tiene sed." (Or: "Coquí tiene hambre." The thought bubble twinkles.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; abuela gives it to him.
11. **Decision B, Touch (food or water).** Abuelo comes in and sits down, hands on the table. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Tengo sed!" (Or: "¡Tengo hambre!" Drawn fresh. He sits the same way either time.) He touches the plates or the glasses.
   - Right: the frog takes it to him. He eats (or drinks). Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuelo shakes his head and puts it aside at the edge of the table. Canelo has it. A cross. Abuelo fans himself (or lays a hand on his belly). NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Abuelo tiene sed." (Or: "tiene hambre".) The sign is on the right ones; he touches them; abuelo drinks (or eats).
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (who said it).** Mamá and papá come in together and sit down, both the same way, hands on the table. Each says one thing, one after the other (who says which, and who speaks first, is drawn fresh). NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Tengo sed!" Then abuela holds out a plate of food, and it twinkles. (Or: a glass of water. Drawn fresh.) He touches mamá or papá.
   - Right: the frog takes it to that one. Right signal. A star. Abuela takes the other thing to the other one.
   - Wrong: the frog takes it to the other one, who puts it aside. Canelo has it. A cross. The right one lays a hand on her belly (or fans himself). NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá tiene hambre." (The true line for the thing he carried.) The sign; he touches her; the frog takes her a new one. Abuela takes the other thing to the other one.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Everybody at the table eats and drinks. By what lies beside Canelo:
   - Nothing (three stars): abuela gives Canelo a bone. Stars and a heart.
   - One, two or three empty plates and glasses: Canelo lies beside them with a round belly, wagging his tail.
14. NARR: "La familia come en la cocina." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y Canelo?" COQUÍ: "¡Canelo está contento!"

## For the owner, one line each

- "Pone" is your word; the command to one person is "pon". The stories use "pone" only in what the narrator says ("Coquí pone la mesa"), never as a command.
- In story 24 the cups are for hot chocolate: in Puerto Rico a "taza" is for something hot, and juice or water goes in a "vaso".
- In story 25 the fork dish is spaghetti, not rice: many people eat rice with a spoon, so rice could not show that a spoon is wrong.
- "Mesa", "pone", "comida", "cocina" and "listo" are heard many times but decide nothing in their stories; they are tested the next day in the picture pairs.
- "Tengo hambre" and "tengo sed" are taught as whole phrases, with "tiene hambre" / "tiene sed" heard for somebody else.

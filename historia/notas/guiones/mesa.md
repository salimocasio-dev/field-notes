# La mesa: three stories (draft 1, before reviews)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 1 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (stories 1 to 11), and place 2, El parque (stories 12 to 23).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, otra, su, luna, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for La mesa (from the 40-scene list): mesa, plato, taza, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, cocina, pone, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo.

The picture: abuela's kitchen. On the left, a counter with a cupboard over it: a stack of plates, a row of cups, and a tray with spoons, forks and knives lie on the counter. In the middle, the table, seen a little from above so that what lies on it can be seen; four chairs stand behind it, facing us. On the right, the stove with a big pot, and a window over it. The floor in front of the table is where the frog walks; he reaches the table top with a hop.

Who sits where never matters to a decision: a place at the table is named by the person sitting at it, and that person twinkles when the line is said.

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Abuela dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ).

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a line that says what was right ("No, Coquí." and then a whole sentence, true to the eye at the moment it is said); then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays without its reward.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es la mesa." "Es un plato." "Es abuela.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
A line that says what somebody is doing is spoken while they are doing it, never after.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Answer bubbles, as before:
- **Picture bubbles**: each shows a thing; he touches one. Places drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Sides drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
In this scene the bubbles stand in the upper part of the picture, clear of the table and the people.

In the corner of the screen, from the turn on, a card with three places, one for each round of the game: a star for a round done right at the first try, a red cross for a round that went wrong. Both stay.

## Story 24: La mesa

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** an empty table, four chairs, a bizcocho cooling on the stove and a pot of hot chocolate beside it. Set a plate and a cup at each place before abuela serves.
- **At stake:** the merienda. Abuela serves into whatever stands at a place. If a place has two cups and no plate, the slice of bizcocho goes into a cup; if it has two plates and no cup, the chocolate is poured onto a plate. It stays like that to the end. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** mesa, plato, taza, pone.
- **Back again:** abuela, abuelo, mamá, papá, bizcocho, para, tiene, hay, no, mira, toma, gracias, ¿quién?
- **Heard along the way:** chocolate, la mesa está lista.

**Start:** the table is bare. Abuela stands at the stove. Luis stands at the counter. The frog stands in front of the table. Nobody sits yet.

1. NARR: "Esta es la mesa." (The table twinkles.) NARR: "Mira. Un plato." (The stack of plates twinkles.) NARR: "Y una taza." (The row of cups twinkles.)
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A la mesa!" Abuelo, mamá, papá and Carmen come in and sit down, one on each chair.

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Abuelo twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un plato para abuelo." The big sign is on the stack of plates. He touches it. The frog takes a plate, hops to the table and puts it down in front of abuelo. Right signal. While he puts it down: NARR: "Coquí pone el plato en la mesa."
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una taza para abuelo." The sign is on the cups. He touches them. The frog puts a cup at abuelo's place. Right signal. NARR: "Coquí pone la taza en la mesa."
5. **He calls for what is missing, taught.** Luis has put a plate at mamá's place and gone back to the counter. Her place has a plate and no cup. Mamá twinkles. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Un plato!" and "¡Una taza!" The big sign is on "¡Una taza!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Una taza!" Luis brings a cup and puts it down. Right signal. While he puts it down: NARR: "Luis pone la taza en la mesa."
6. Luis has put a cup at papá's place; it has no plate. Papá twinkles. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un plato!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un plato!" Luis brings a plate. Right signal. NARR: "Luis pone el plato."
7. **Who has none, taught.** Carmen's place has a cup and no plate. The other three places are complete. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene plato?" The big sign is on Carmen. He touches her. The frog brings her a plate. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora Carmen tiene un plato y una taza."
8. Abuela comes round with the bizcocho and puts a slice on each plate, then with the pot and fills each cup. NARR: "Abuela pone el bizcocho en el plato." NARR: "Y el chocolate en la taza."

**The turn**

9. The baby, on mamá's lap, reaches out and sweeps her plate and her cup off the table. They land on the floor; the slice is under the table, the chocolate a puddle. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El plato de mamá. La taza de mamá." Luis wipes the floor. Everybody gets up; the plates and cups go back to the counter; the table is bare again. NARR: "Abuela dice: Otra vez. Coquí pone la mesa." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

10. **Decision A, Touch (which thing).** The four sit down again. One of them twinkles (drawn fresh). NARR: "Abuela dice: Una taza para papá." (Or: "Un plato para papá." Drawn fresh; the name is the one who twinkles.) He touches the plates or the cups.
   - Right: the frog puts it at that place. Right signal. A star. Luis brings the other thing for that place and one of each for two more places.
   - Wrong: the frog puts the other thing at that place. Luis, who has not been listening, brings what he thinks is missing: the same thing again. The place now has two cups and no plate (or two plates and no cup). Abuela comes by and serves it: the slice goes into a cup (or the chocolate is poured onto a plate). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá no tiene plato." (Or: "no tiene taza".) The sign is on the right pile; he touches it; the frog brings it. It stands empty beside the mess. Luis lays two more places.
11. **Decision B, Say (what is missing).** The last place has been laid by Luis with one thing only: a plate and no cup, or a cup and no plate (drawn fresh). That person twinkles. Luis is back at the counter with his hand over the plates and the cups. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Un plato!" and "¡Una taza!"
   - Right: Luis brings it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis brings what the frog called: a second one of what is already there. Abuela serves the place as it is. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen no tiene taza." (The true one.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis brings it.
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (who has none).** Abuelo knocks his cup over reaching for the sugar, and Carmen's plate has gone to the baby, who is licking it: so one person has a plate and no cup, and another a cup and no plate (who is which is drawn fresh among the four); the other two places are complete. Abuela stands at the counter with one clean plate in one hand and one clean cup in the other. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién no tiene taza?" (Or: "¿Quién no tiene plato?" Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the four.
   - Right: the frog carries it to that one. Right signal. A star. Abuela brings the other thing to the other one herself.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to the one he touched, who has one already and now has two; the one who needed it has none, and abuela, coming with the pot, serves that place as it is. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Abuelo no tiene taza." (The true one; he twinkles.) The sign; he touches him; the frog brings the thing from the place that has two.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. Abuela serves whatever is still unserved. Everybody eats and drinks. By the places that went wrong:
   - None (three stars): four neat places. Abuela puts a fifth plate and a small cup on the table for the frog, with the biggest slice. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two: somebody eats bizcocho out of a cup with his fingers, or drinks chocolate off a plate, holding it in both hands.
   - Three: three of them do. Abuela looks at the table and laughs.
14. NARR: "La mesa está lista." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A comer!" COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** plato 9 (step 1; step 3 twice; steps 5 and 6, the bubble; step 6, the frog and the line after; step 7 twice; step 8; the turn), taza 9 (step 1; step 4 twice; step 5: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 6, the bubble; step 7; step 8; the turn). Not deciding, but heard: mesa 6, pone 7.

**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that was asked. B: the thing that was missing. C: the thing that was asked.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in four (one in two for someone who sees that only two places are short).

## Story 25: La cuchara

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** lunch on the table: a bowl of soup, a plate of chicken and rice, and a bread to be cut, and no spoon, fork or knife anywhere. Get each person the thing to eat with.
- **At stake:** the tablecloth. Whoever gets the wrong tool tries to eat with it all the same: soup with a fork, rice with a knife. It ends on the cloth, and the stain stays. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida.
- **Back again:** mesa, plato, pone, tiene, come, para, con, dame, ¿quién?, pan, no.
- **Heard along the way:** sopa, arroz, corta.

**Start:** the family sits at the table, a white cloth on it. Abuela stands at the stove with the pot. The tray of spoons, forks and knives lies on the counter. The frog stands by the counter.

1. NARR: "Mira la comida." (The pot and the plates twinkle.) NARR: "Abuela pone la comida en la mesa." She puts a bowl of soup in front of mamá and a plate of rice and chicken in front of papá.
2. The tray twinkles, one kind at a time. NARR: "Una cuchara." NARR: "Un tenedor." NARR: "Y un cuchillo."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Mamá twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Una cuchara para mamá." Three picture bubbles: a spoon, a fork, a knife. The big sign is on the spoon. He touches it. The frog takes a spoon to mamá. Right signal. While she eats her soup: NARR: "Mamá come con la cuchara."
4. Papá twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Un tenedor para papá." The three picture bubbles; the sign is on the fork. He touches it. The frog takes him a fork. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Papá come con el tenedor."
5. Abuela holds the loaf of bread. NARR: "Abuela dice: Dame el cuchillo, por favor." The three picture bubbles; the sign is on the knife. He touches it. The frog takes it to her. Right signal. While she cuts: NARR: "Abuela corta el pan con el cuchillo."
6. **He calls for what is missing, taught.** Luis stands at the tray. Abuelo has a bowl of soup in front of him and looks at it; he has nothing to eat it with. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Una cuchara!", "¡Un tenedor!", "¡Un cuchillo!" The big sign is on "¡Una cuchara!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Una cuchara!" Luis brings abuelo a spoon. Right signal. NARR: "Abuelo come con la cuchara."
7. Carmen has a plate of rice in front of her and nothing to eat it with. The three speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Un tenedor!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Un tenedor!" Luis brings it. Right signal. NARR: "Carmen come con el tenedor."

**The turn**

8. The baby, in mamá's arms, takes hold of the edge of the tray that Luis has carried to the table, and tips it. Spoons, forks and knives clatter to the floor. Everybody's own tool lies on the floor too, as they jump up. NARR: "¡Ay, no! Las cucharas. Los tenedores. Los cuchillos." Abuela washes them and lays them back in the tray on the counter. Nobody has a tool. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Touch (which tool).** Everybody sits with food in front of them. One of them twinkles (drawn fresh). NARR: "Abuela dice: Un cuchillo para Luis." (Or "Una cuchara", "Un tenedor". Drawn fresh. What is on that person's plate is under a cover, so the food does not say which.) Three picture bubbles: a spoon, a fork, a knife.
   - Right: the frog takes it over; the cover comes off; that one eats. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog takes over the one he touched. The cover comes off; that one tries to eat with it: soup runs through the fork, rice falls off the knife. A stain on the cloth. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Luis no tiene cuchillo." The sign shows the right picture; he touches it; the frog takes it over.
10. **Decision B, Say (what is missing).** Another one (drawn fresh) has two of the three tools beside the plate; one is missing (which is drawn fresh). That one twinkles. Luis stands at the tray. Three speaking bubbles: "¡Una cuchara!", "¡Un tenedor!", "¡Un cuchillo!"
   - Right: Luis brings it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis brings what the frog called; that one now has two of it and still lacks the other, and makes do: a stain. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá no tiene tenedor." (The true one.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Luis brings it.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (who has it).** Abuela stands at the stove with her back to us. Abuelo, Carmen and papá each hold one tool up, cleaning it with a napkin: one a spoon, one a fork, one a knife (who holds which is drawn fresh). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién tiene el cuchillo?" (Or: "la cuchara", "el tenedor". Drawn fresh. Abuela stands at the stove with her back to us; what she wants it for is not in the picture until she uses it.) He touches one of the three.
   - Right: the frog fetches it from that one and takes it to abuela. She uses it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog fetches the tool of the one he touched and takes it to abuela, who uses it without looking: she saws at the bread with a spoon and squashes it flat (or stirs with a knife; or lifts the chicken with a spoon, and it slides onto the cloth). A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Papá tiene el cuchillo." (The true one; he twinkles.) The sign; he touches him; the frog fetches it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Everybody eats. By the stains on the cloth:
   - None (three stars): a clean white cloth. Abuela puts a small bowl in front of the frog, with a small spoon. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two stains: mamá lays a napkin over each.
   - Three: abuela takes the cloth off altogether; the table is bare wood.
13. NARR: "La familia come." NARR: "Mamá come con la cuchara. Papá come con el tenedor." COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡La comida!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cuchara 6 (step 2; step 3 twice; step 6: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 7, the bubble), tenedor 6 (step 2; step 4 twice; step 6, the bubble; step 7: the bubble, the frog, the line after), cuchillo 5 (step 2; step 5 twice; steps 6 and 7, the bubble). Not deciding, but heard: comida 3.

**A miss is filed under:** the tool that was asked, or was missing.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in three. B one in three. C one in three.

## Story 26: Tengo hambre

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** the family comes in from the yard, hot and hungry, one after the other. On the counter: plates of food, and glasses of water. Bring each one what he asks for.
- **At stake:** abuela's food. A plate brought to somebody who is only thirsty is pushed away, and Canelo, under the table, has it. One plate fewer for the family. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo, cocina.
- **Back again:** comida, agua, plato, mesa, quiero, toma, gracias, come, bebe, ¿y tú?, perro.
- **Heard along the way:** tiene hambre, tiene sed, la comida está lista.

**Start:** abuela at the stove. Four plates of food and four glasses of water stand on the counter. The table is laid. Canelo lies under the table. The frog stands by the counter. The door to the yard is on the left.

1. NARR: "Esta es la cocina de abuela." NARR: "Abuela está en la cocina." Abuela lifts the lid of the pot; steam. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo! La comida está lista."
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A la mesa!"

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. Luis runs in and sits down, a hand on his belly. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" The big sign is on the plates of food. He touches them. The frog takes Luis a plate. Right signal. While he eats: NARR: "Luis tiene hambre. Luis come."
4. Carmen comes in, fanning herself, and sits down. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo sed!" The sign is on the glasses. He touches them. The frog takes her a glass of water. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Carmen tiene sed. Carmen bebe agua."
5. **He says it himself, taught.** A thought bubble over the frog shows a glass of water. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Tengo hambre!" and "¡Tengo sed!" The big sign is on "¡Tengo sed!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Tengo sed!" Abuela gives him a glass; he drinks. Right signal. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma. Agua."
6. A thought bubble over the frog shows a plate of food. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y ahora?" The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Tengo hambre!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Tengo hambre!" Abuela gives him a little plate; he eats. Right signal. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias!"

**The turn**

7. Canelo comes out from under the table and lays his head on abuelo's empty chair, looking at the counter. He whines. NARR: "Canelo tiene hambre." Abuela shakes her head: NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Canelo. Es la comida de la familia." Canelo goes back under the table, where only his nose shows. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

8. **Decision A, Touch (food or water).** Abuelo comes in and sits down. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Tengo sed!" (Or: "¡Tengo hambre!" Drawn fresh. He sits the same way either time, hands on the table.) He touches the plates or the glasses.
   - Right: the frog takes it to him. He eats (or drinks). Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong, a plate for a thirsty man: abuelo pushes the plate away; it slides off the edge, and Canelo has it under the table. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Abuelo tiene sed." The sign is on the glasses; he touches them; abuelo drinks.
   - Wrong, a glass for a hungry man: abuelo drinks the water, puts the glass down, and his belly rumbles. He takes a plate from the counter himself, and on the way back it tips: Canelo has it. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Abuelo tiene hambre." The sign is on the plates; he touches them; abuelo eats.
9. **Decision B, Touch (who said it).** Mamá and papá come in together and sit down. Each says one thing, one after the other (who says which, and who speaks first, is drawn fresh). NARR: "Mamá dice: Tengo hambre." NARR: "Papá dice: Tengo sed." Then abuela puts a plate of food on the frog's head. (Or: a glass of water. Drawn fresh.) He touches mamá or papá.
   - Right: the frog takes it to that one. Right signal. A star. Abuela takes the other thing to the other one.
   - Wrong: the frog takes it to the other one. A plate for the thirsty one is pushed away and Canelo has it; a glass for the hungry one is drunk, and the plate that one then fetches tips. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mamá tiene hambre." (The true line for the thing he carried.) The sign; he touches the right one.
10. **Decision C, Say, last (what he wants himself).** Everybody is eating. A thought bubble over the frog shows a plate of food, or a glass of water (drawn fresh). NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Tengo hambre!" and "¡Tengo sed!"
   - Right: abuela gives him what he wanted. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: abuela gives him what he said. A glass when he wanted food: he drinks it, and his belly rumbles. A plate when he wanted water: he cannot eat; he pants; Canelo, under his stool, has the plate. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira." (The thought bubble twinkles.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; abuela gives it to him.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

11. By the plates of food that are left for the family:
   - All four (three stars): everybody has a full plate, and abuela puts a bone in Canelo's bowl. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two lost: abuela shares what there is; the plates are half full. Canelo sleeps, round as a ball.
   - Three lost: one plate between four, and four spoons. Canelo sleeps on his back.
12. NARR: "La familia come en la cocina." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y Canelo?" COQUÍ: "¡Canelo no tiene hambre!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tengo hambre 5 (step 3; step 5, the bubble; step 6: the bubble, the frog; and "tiene hambre" twice more), tengo sed 5 (step 4; step 5: the bubble, the frog; step 6, the bubble; and "tiene sed" once more). Not deciding, but heard: listo 2, cocina 3.

**A miss is filed under:** tengo hambre or tengo sed (the one that was said, or was true).

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two.

## For the owner, one line each

- "Pone" is your word; the command to one person is "pon". The stories use "pone" in what the narrator and abuela say ("Coquí pone la mesa"), never as a command.
- In story 24 the cups are for hot chocolate: in Puerto Rico a "taza" is for something hot, and juice or water goes in a "vaso".
- "Mesa", "pone", "comida", "cocina" and "listo" are heard many times but decide nothing in their stories; they are tested the next day in the picture pairs.
- "Tengo hambre" and "tengo sed" are taught as whole phrases, with "tiene hambre" / "tiene sed" heard for somebody else.

# La orilla: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `orilla`). Stories 72 to 74. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 71.

Scene words (page 02): playa, mar, arena, ola, barco, flota, puedo, puedes, hace calor, hondo, mojado, seco.

The picture: the shore. One view. Dry sand in front; a strip of wet, darker sand at the water's edge; the sea behind, light where it is shallow and dark blue where it is deep, with a line of small waves coming in; far out, a fishing boat at anchor. Palms at the left. Everybody stands on the sand, at the water's edge or in the shallow water.

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. Nobody new.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 72: La playa

- **Goal he can see:** on the sand, the flat base of a sand castle with three marked rounds for three towers, and a little paper flag stuck beside it. Luis and Carmen kneel at it with a bucket.
- **At stake:** the castle's towers (3). What a tower needs is wet sand: sea water on a tower that needed sand turns it to mud; dry sand on one that needed water makes it crumble. Either way that tower is a heap.
- **New words:** playa, mar, arena, ola.
- **Back again:** agua, necesito, hay, mucho, más, grande, mira, me gusta, cuidado, viene, se va, vamos, voy, ven, aquí, foto (heard), hace calor (heard).

**Start:** morning. Abuela, Luis, Carmen and the frog walk in from the palms with a bag, a bucket and Canelo. The sea rolls in and out.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira: la playa. Hace calor." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡La playa! ¡Me gusta la playa!" She runs to the water's edge. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira el mar, Coquí. El mar es grande." Ask (signed, the sea): "¿Dónde está el mar?" He touches it: a small wave runs up the sand and over the frog's feet. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Una ola!"
2. Luis kneels and lets sand run through his fingers. NARR: "Luis dice: Aquí hay arena. ¡Mucha arena!" He fills the bucket, pats it, and turns it over on the first round of the base: a tower. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Al mar!" COQUÍ [TURN: vamos (vamos)]: "¡Vamos!" The frog and Carmen run to the water with the frog's own small bucket.
3. Carmen marks the second round. NARR: "Carmen dice: Necesito agua del mar." Ask (signed, the sea): "¿Dónde busca agua Coquí?" He touches it: the frog fills his bucket in the sea and brings it; Carmen wets her sand and turns out a second tower. NARR: "Luis dice: Y yo necesito más arena." Ask (signed, the heap of dry sand): "¿Dónde está la arena?" He touches it: the frog fills his bucket at the heap and brings it.
4. A bigger wave comes up the beach and stops just short of the castle. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! ¡Una ola grande!" The children lift the bucket; the wave slides back. NARR: "Luis dice: Viene una ola... y se va."

**The turn**

5. Luis smooths the base flat again: three empty rounds, and the flag waiting. Abuela takes out her camera and sits down on the sand to wait. NARR: "Abuela dice: Tres torres. Y después, la foto." **(torres: heard; the picture carries it)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A tower is being built; Luis or Carmen (drawn) says what he needs: sea water, or sand (drawn), each round on its own. The frog fills his bucket at the sea or at the heap. The first tower is built in the turn, with no touch, by Luis alone; the two rounds build the second and the third.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the sea, the heap of sand; tests mar against arena).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, necesito agua del mar." (Or, drawn: "Carmen dice: Coquí, necesito arena."; or Luis.)
   - Right: the frog brings it; the tower is turned out, straight and sharp. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings the other. Water on the dry tower: it sinks into mud. (Sand on the wet one: it crumbles.) NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. Necesito agua del mar." A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog brings it, and Carmen pats the heap into a small round hill with a shell on top.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the other child).** NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, necesito arena." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis plants the flag. A wave comes up the beach, and stops at the castle's foot.
   - Three towers: a castle with three towers and its flag; the wave fills its moat. Stars and a heart.
   - Two towers and a hill.
   - One tower and two hills: the flag on the tower.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ven aquí. ¡La foto!" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops onto the highest tower; the camera clicks. NARR: "Hay mucha arena en la playa. Y el mar es grande."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** mar 5 (steps 1, 2, 3), arena 4 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: ola 3 (steps 1, 4); playa 3 (step 1).
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (mar or arena).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. Nothing in the picture says what the tower needs.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): vamos; voy.

## Story 73: ¿Flota?

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen stands out in the shallow water with a string in her hands and three empty knots on it: a necklace waiting for three shells. On the sand beside the frog, three shells.
- **At stake:** the shells (3). A toy boat that does not float fills and goes down under the frog: he swims back, and the shell he carried is gone where the water is deep.
- **New words:** barco, flota, hondo.
- **Back again:** mar, ola, playa, agua, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, ¿cuál?, tengo, dos, grande, pequeño, cuidado, aquí, allá, lejos, quiero, quieres, gracias, toma, mira, sí, no, es.

**Start:** midday. Carmen wades out. Luis sits on the wet sand with two small boats he has made of wood and paper. The frog beside three shells. Abuela on the sand. Far out, the fishing boat.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis points out to sea. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí: un barco. Un barco grande. Está lejos." Ask (signed, the fishing boat): "¿Dónde está el barco?" He touches it: the boat rocks on a wave and a gull flies up from it.
2. Carmen wades; the water is at her ankles. NARR: "Carmen dice: Aquí no es hondo." She goes on; the water comes to her knees. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Uy! Aquí es hondo." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Carmen, cuidado! Allá es hondo." Carmen stops and holds up her string.
3. Luis holds up his two little boats: a green one and a white one. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo tengo dos barcos. El barco verde flota." He sets it on the water: it rides the ripples. NARR: "Luis dice: El barco blanco no flota." He sets it down: it fills and sits on the bottom; he lifts it out and pours the water away. Ask (signed, the green boat): "¿Cuál barco flota?" He touches it. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿quieres un barco?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" The frog hops in with a shell; Luis gives the boat a push; it floats out to Carmen, who takes the shell and ties it on.

**The turn**

4. The frog rides a wave back in. Luis brings two more boats out of his bag and sets them side by side on the wet sand: they look alike, but for their colour. Two shells are left. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: dos barcos más." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two toy boats on the wet sand, their colours drawn each round from rojo, azul, verde, amarillo and blanco. Luis says which one floats and which does not (which is said first is drawn; which colour floats is drawn). The frog gets into one with a shell.

5. **Decision A, touch a boat (two boats; tests flota against no flota).** NARR: "Luis dice: El barco rojo flota. El barco azul no flota." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hops in with a shell; Luis pushes; the boat floats out to Carmen. She ties the shell on. A star.
   - Wrong: the boat rides out a little way, fills, and goes down. The frog swims back; the shell is gone. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ay! Aquí es hondo." NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. El barco azul no flota." A cross. The sign is on the right boat; he touches it: the frog rides it out to Carmen with empty hands, and rides back.
6. **Decision B, touch a boat (two more boats).** NARR: "Luis dice: El barco amarillo no flota. El barco verde flota." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Carmen wades back in with her string.
   - Three shells on it: a necklace; she puts it round the frog's neck, and it hangs to his feet. Stars and a heart.
   - Two shells: she ties it round the frog's wrist.
   - One shell: she hangs it on Canelo's collar; Canelo shakes.
8. NARR: "Carmen dice: Toma, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" Far out, the fishing boat pulls up its anchor. NARR: "El barco grande flota. Y se va."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** flota 4 with "no flota" (step 3: twice by Luis, the ask, and the boats seen doing it). Not deciding: barco 7; hondo 3 (step 2), and once more after each wrong move.
**A miss is filed under:** flota.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two boats differ only in colour, and both colours are named in every round.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): quiero; gracias.

## Story 74: ¿Puedo?

- **Goal he can see:** on abuela's blanket, a pile of three dry towels, folded, and the bag packed for going home. Abuela pats the pile: one each for the way back.
- **At stake:** the dry towels (3). A child who does what abuela said he cannot comes back dripping, or knocks something over: a towel is used up, wet.
- **New words:** puedo, puedes, mojado, seco, hace calor.
- **Back again:** playa, mar, arena, ola, barco, hondo, nada, juega, bola, come, pan, jugo, corre, abuela, sí, no, cuidado, ahora, después, tengo, quiero, toma, gracias, bien, lejos, toalla (heard).

**Start:** afternoon, the sun high. Abuela sits on her blanket beside the pile of three towels, a ball, a swimming ring and a basket with bread and juice. The frog on the blanket. Luis and Carmen stand in front of her, dry, fanning themselves.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Hace calor en la playa. ¡Hace mucho calor!" Luis points at the water. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, hace calor. ¿Puedo nadar?" **(nadar: heard; "nada" is a word of El lago)** NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí, puedes." Ask (signed, the swimming ring): "Luis puede nadar. ¿Qué necesita?" He touches it: the frog rolls the ring to Luis, who runs into the sea with it.
2. Carmen points far out, at the fishing boat. NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuela, ¿puedo ir al barco?" NARR: "Abuela dice: No, no puedes. Es hondo." Carmen sits down. NARR: "Abuela dice: Puedes comer. Toma." She gives her bread from the basket.
3. Luis comes back dripping; water runs off him onto the sand. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Estoy mojado!" Abuela holds out a towel from her own shoulders, not from the pile. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma. Esta toalla está seca." He rubs himself. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora la toalla está mojada. Y yo estoy seco." He hangs it on the palm. Ask (signed, the pile of three): "¿Dónde están las toallas secas?" He touches it: the pile twinkles.
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes calor?" **(tienes calor: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" She fans him with her hat.

**The turn**

5. Abuela lies back and puts her hat over her face. The ball, the ring and the basket are beside the frog; the pile of dry towels beside her. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ahora tú. Tres toallas secas, ¿sí?" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child asks abuela two things; she says what he can do and what he cannot (drawn from nadar, jugar bola and comer; which is said first is drawn). The frog gives the child the thing for what he CAN do: the ring, the ball, or the bread.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two of the three things twinkle when touched; tests puedes against no puedes).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuela, ¿puedo nadar? ¿Puedo jugar bola?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Puedes jugar bola. No puedes nadar." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog gives her the thing; she goes off with it, and abuela nods under her hat. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog gives her the other thing, and she is off with it before abuela can sit up. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Carmen! No puedes nadar." She comes back dripping (or, with the ball: the ball knocks the juice over the blanket; with the bread: gulls come down and she drops it in the water, and wades after it). A towel from the pile goes round her, wet. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: she takes it and goes.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; Luis).** NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, ¿puedo comer? ¿Puedo nadar?" NARR: "Abuela dice: No puedes nadar. Puedes comer." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The sun is low. Abuela packs the basket. The children come out of the water for the last time, dripping.
   - Three dry towels: one round Luis, one round Carmen, one round the frog, who vanishes in it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: Luis and Carmen; abuela puts the frog under her hat.
   - One: Luis and Carmen share it; the frog under the hat.
9. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, ¿puedo venir mañana?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí, puedes. ¿Y tú, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (también, tambien, yo también)]: "¡Yo también!" NARR: "Coquí está seco. Y hace calor."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** puedes 3 (steps 1, 2 twice), with "no puedes" once and "puede" once; puedo 2 before the game and twice in each round. Not deciding: mojado 2 and mojada 1; seco 1, seca 2, secas 2; hace calor 4.
**To fix in the reviews if they agree:** "puedo" is heard twice before the game (it is the child's word and never decides).
**Forms:** puede counts with puedes; mojada with mojado; seca, secas with seco.
**A miss is filed under:** puedes.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both things are named in what abuela says, and only "puedes" and "no puedes" say which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): sí; también.

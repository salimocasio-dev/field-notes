# La orilla: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `orilla`). Stories 72 to 74. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 71.

Scene words (page 02, as changed on Nov 21): playa, mar, arena, ola, bote, flota, puedo, puedes, hace calor, hondo, mojado, seco.
The plan had "barco". What floats off this beach is a small fishing boat, and on the island that is "un bote"; "barco" is a ship. The everyday word wins (page 16).

The picture: the shore. One view. Dry sand in front; a strip of wet, darker sand at the water's edge; the sea behind, light where it is shallow and dark blue where it is deep, with a line of small waves coming in; far out, a fishing boat at anchor. Palms at the left, a rope between two of them. Everybody stands on the sand, at the water's edge or in the shallow water.

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. Nobody new.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 72: La playa

- **Goal he can see:** a sand castle with three towers, built by Luis and Carmen; beside it three big pink shells in a row, one for the top of each tower. Luis holds one up over a tower and grins: a game first.
- **At stake:** the pink shells (3). Luis hides one, in the sea's edge or in the sand. If the frog looks in the wrong place, the shell is lost while he looks: a wave takes it from the water's edge, or Canelo digs it out of the sand and runs off with it.
- **New words:** playa, mar, arena, ola.
- **Back again:** agua, hay, mucho, grande, mira, me gusta, cuidado, viene, se va, vamos, voy, ven, aquí, busca, ¿dónde está?, está, no, perro, concha (heard), torre (heard), foto (heard), hace calor (heard).

**Start:** morning. Abuela, Luis, Carmen and the frog walk in from the palms with a bag, a bucket and Canelo. The sea rolls in and out.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira: la playa. Hace calor." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡La playa! ¡Me gusta la playa!" She runs to the water's edge. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira el mar, Coquí. El mar es grande." Ask (signed, the sea): "¿Dónde está el mar?" He touches it: a small wave runs up the sand and over the frog's feet. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Una ola!"
2. Luis kneels and lets sand run through his fingers. NARR: "Luis dice: Aquí hay arena. ¡Mucha arena!" He fills the bucket, pats it and turns it over: a tower. NARR: "Luis dice: Una torre de arena." **(torre: heard; the picture carries it)** Carmen turns out two more. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Al agua!" COQUÍ [TURN: vamos (vamos)]: "¡Vamos!" The frog and Carmen splash at the edge; a wave comes and goes. NARR: "Carmen dice: Viene una ola... y se va."
3. Luis shows the frog the three pink shells, and puts a hand over the frog's eyes. He pushes one shell into the dry sand in front of the castle and smooths it over. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, busca. La concha está en la arena." **(concha: heard)** Ask (signed, the patch of smooth sand): "¿Dónde hay arena?" He touches it: the frog digs, and comes up with the shell. Luis sets it on the first tower.
4. The hand over his eyes again; Luis wades two steps and drops a shell into the shallow water. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora la concha está en el mar." Ask (signed, the sea's edge): "¿Dónde está el mar?" He touches it: the frog ducks under and comes up with the shell on his head. It goes on the second tower. A wave runs up after him. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! ¡Una ola grande!"

**The turn**

5. Two towers have their shells; the third is bare. Luis takes the two shells back off, grinning, and shows three in his hands: again, and this time for good. Canelo sits up and watches his hands; out on the water a wave is building. Abuela takes out her camera. NARR: "Abuela dice: Tres torres. Y después, la foto." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A hand over the frog's eyes; Luis hides a shell in the sand or in the sea's edge (drawn, each round on its own); nothing shows where. Carmen says where it is. The frog looks in one of the two places. The first tower's shell goes on in the turn, by Luis, with no touch.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the patch of sand, the sea's edge; tests mar against arena).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, la concha está en el mar." (Or, drawn: "Carmen dice: Coquí, la concha está en la arena.")
   - Right: the frog finds it; it goes on a tower. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog looks in the other place: nothing. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Está en el mar." A wave runs in and, when it slides back, the shell is rolling away out to sea. (Or, hidden in the sand: Canelo digs it out and runs off with it down the beach.) A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog looks there: only the mark where it was.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora está en la arena." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis stands back from the castle.
   - Three shells: every tower has its pink shell. Stars and a heart.
   - Two shells: two towers with shells; on the third, Carmen sets a small white one.
   - One shell: one pink shell on the first tower; Canelo comes back with sand on his nose and lies down beside the castle.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ven acá. ¡La foto!" **(acá: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops onto the highest tower; the camera clicks. NARR: "Hay mucha arena en la playa. Y el mar es grande."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** mar 5 (steps 1, 4), arena 5 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: ola 3 (steps 1, 2, 4); playa 3 (step 1).
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (mar or arena).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. Nothing shows where the shell is, and both of Carmen's lines are alike but for the one word.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): vamos; voy.

## Story 73: ¿Flota?

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen stands out in the water, up to her knees, with a string in her hands and three empty knots on it: a necklace waiting for three shells. On the sand beside the frog, three shells.
- **At stake:** the shells (3). A toy boat that does not float fills and goes down under the frog just short of Carmen: he swims back, and the shell he carried is gone in the deep water.
- **New words:** bote, flota, hondo.
- **Back again:** mar, ola, playa, agua, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, ¿cuál?, tengo, dos, grande, cuidado, aquí, allá, lejos, quiero, quieres, gracias, toma, mira, sí, no, es, está, hay.

**Start:** midday. Carmen wades out. Luis sits on the wet sand with two small boats he has made of wood and paper. The frog beside three shells. Abuela on the sand. Far out, the fishing boat.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis points out to sea. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí: allá hay un bote. Es grande. Está lejos." Ask (signed, the fishing boat): "¿Dónde está el bote?" He touches it: the boat rocks on a wave and a gull flies up from it.
2. Carmen wades; the water is at her ankles. NARR: "Carmen dice: Aquí no está hondo." She goes on; the water comes to her knees. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Uy! Aquí está hondo." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Carmen, cuidado! Allá está hondo." Carmen stops and holds up her string.
3. Luis holds up his two little boats: a green one and a white one. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo tengo dos botes. El bote verde flota." He sets it on the water: it rides the ripples. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira! ¡Flota!" He sets the white one down: it fills and sits on the bottom. NARR: "Luis dice: El bote blanco no flota. No flota." He lifts it out and pours the water away. Ask (signed, the green boat): "¿Cuál bote flota?" He touches it. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¿quieres ir en el bote?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" The frog hops in with a shell; Luis gives the boat a push; it floats out to Carmen, who takes the shell and ties it on.

**The turn**

4. The frog rides a wave back in. Luis brings two more boats out of his bag and sets them side by side on the wet sand: they look alike, but for their colour. Two shells are left. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: dos botes más." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two toy boats on the wet sand, their colours drawn each round from rojo, azul and amarillo (never the opening's green and white). Luis says which one floats and which does not (which is said first is drawn; which colour floats is drawn). The frog gets into one with a shell.

5. **Decision A, touch a boat (two boats; tests flota against no flota).** NARR: "Luis dice: El bote rojo flota. El bote azul no flota." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hops in with a shell; Luis pushes; the boat floats out to Carmen. She ties the shell on. A star.
   - Wrong: the boat rides out, fills, and goes down just short of Carmen. The frog swims back; Carmen feels for the shell with her foot, and shakes her head. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ay! Aquí está hondo." NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. El bote azul no flota." A cross. The sign is on the right boat; he touches it: the frog rides it out to Carmen with empty hands, and rides back.
6. **Decision B, touch a boat (two more boats).** NARR: "Luis dice: El bote amarillo no flota. El bote rojo flota." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Carmen wades back in with her string.
   - Three shells on it: a necklace; she puts it round the frog's neck, and it hangs to his feet. Stars and a heart.
   - Two shells: she ties it round the frog's wrist.
   - One shell: she unties it and puts the shell in the frog's hand.
8. NARR: "Carmen dice: Toma, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" Far out, the fishing boat pulls up its anchor. NARR: "El bote grande flota. Y se va."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** flota 5, of which "no flota" twice (step 3). Not deciding: bote 6 with botes; hondo 3 (step 2), and once more after each wrong move.
**Forms:** botes counts with bote.
**A miss is filed under:** flota.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two boats differ only in colour, both colours are named in every round, and no colour means the same from round to round.
**What decides:** "flota" against "no flota". The "no" is an old word; "flota" is the new one that the "no" is put to.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): quiero; gracias.

## Story 74: ¿Puedo?

- **Goal he can see:** on abuela's blanket, a pile of three dry towels, folded, and the bag packed for going home. Abuela pats the pile: one each for the way back.
- **At stake:** the dry towels of the pile (3). A child who is handed a wet towel is still wet: abuela has to give him a dry one from the pile.
- **New words:** puedo, puedes, mojado, seco, hace calor.
- **Back again:** playa, mar, agua, bote, hondo, rojo, azul, amarillo, verde, come, pan, abuela, sí, no, cuidado, ahora, mañana, tengo, toma, gracias, también, allá, lejos, toalla (heard), ir (heard).

**Start:** afternoon, the sun high. Abuela sits on her blanket beside the pile of three towels and a basket with bread. The frog on the blanket. Luis and Carmen stand in front of her, dry, fanning themselves. On the rope between the palms hang two more towels, one red and one blue.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Hace calor en la playa. ¡Hace mucho calor!" Luis points at the water. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, hace calor. ¿Puedo ir al agua?" **(ir: heard)** NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí, puedes." Luis runs into the sea. Carmen points far out, at the fishing boat. NARR: "Carmen dice: Abuela, ¿puedo ir al bote?" NARR: "Abuela dice: No, no puedes. Allá está hondo." Carmen sits down. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Puedo comer?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí, puedes. Toma." She gives her bread from the basket.
2. Luis comes back dripping; water runs off him onto the sand. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Estoy mojado!" Abuela takes the red towel from the rope and feels it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma. Esta toalla está seca." He rubs himself. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora la toalla está mojada. Y yo estoy seco." He hangs it back on the rope, where it drips. Ask (signed, the pile of three): "¿Dónde hay toallas secas?" He touches it: the pile twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿quieres ir al agua?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" The frog jumps in and comes out shaking himself. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Coquí está mojado!"
3. Abuela feels the two towels on the rope, the red one and the blue one. NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla azul está seca. La toalla roja está mojada." Ask (signed, the blue towel): "¿Cuál toalla está seca?" He touches it: abuela rubs the frog dry with it, and hangs it back.

**The turn**

4. Abuela lies back and puts her hat over her face. On the rope hang two folded towels again, side by side; they look alike, but for their colour. Luis and Carmen run into the water. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ahora tú. Quiero tres toallas secas." She pats the pile. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child comes out of the water, dripping. On the rope hang two towels, their colours drawn each round from rojo, azul, amarillo and verde; one has dried in the sun and one is still wet, and nothing shows which. Abuela, from under her hat, says which is which (which is said first is drawn). The frog brings the child a towel from the rope.

5. **Decision A, touch a towel (the two on the rope; tests seco against mojado).** Carmen comes out, dripping. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Estoy mojada!" NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla amarilla está seca. La toalla verde está mojada." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog brings it; she rubs herself dry and hangs it back. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog brings the other: she puts it round her, and jumps. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Uy! ¡Está mojada!" Abuela lifts her hat, sighs, and gives her a dry towel from the pile. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. La toalla verde está mojada." A cross. The sign is on the right towel; he touches it: it twinkles on the rope.
6. **Decision B, touch a towel (the same game; Luis).** Luis comes out. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Estoy mojado!" NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla roja está mojada. La toalla azul está seca." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The sun is low. Abuela packs the basket and counts the pile.
   - Three dry towels: one round Luis, one round Carmen, one round the frog, who vanishes in it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: Luis and Carmen; abuela puts the frog under her hat.
   - One: Luis and Carmen share it; the frog under the hat.
8. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, ¿puedo venir mañana?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí, puedes. ¿Y tú, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (también, tambien, yo también)]: "¡Yo también!" NARR: "Coquí está seco. Y hace calor."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** seco 5 with seca, secas (steps 2, 3), mojado 5 with mojada (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: puedo 3, puedes 3 with "no puedes" (step 1); hace calor 3 (step 1).
**Forms:** mojada counts with mojado; seca, secas with seco.
**A miss is filed under:** seco.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two towels differ only in colour, and both colours are named.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 8): sí; también.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 72: the game ("Necesito agua del mar" or "Necesito arena") was settled by "agua", an old word, and the opening taught that Carmen wants water and Luis sand. The opening built two towers and then smoothed them away. Rebuilt: Luis hides a shell in the sea's edge or in the sand, Carmen says where, and only "mar" or "arena" differs. The towers stay. "¿Dónde busca agua Coquí?" spoke of the learner in the third person; "¿Dónde está la arena?" on a beach had every touch for an answer. "Ven aquí" is "ven acá".
- 73: the boat is "un bote" (see above). "Es hondo" is "está hondo" of water you stand in. The game's colours could contradict the opening's (green floats, white does not): the game never uses those two. "Flota" had been heard three times, and "no flota" once: five, two of them "no flota". The wrong boat sank short of Carmen where it was shallow: it goes down beside her, where she has said it is deep. In the one-shell ending "Toma, Coquí" gave him nothing: he gets the shell.
- 74: rebuilt. "Puedes jugar bola. No puedes nadar." is settled by "no" alone, and the three wrong outcomes (dripping, spilt juice, gulls) did not all cost a towel. Now the game is the wet towel and the dry one, said by colour, which only "seca" and "mojada" settle, and a wet towel handed over costs a dry one from the pile. "Puedo" and "puedes" are the opening's talk with abuela, three times each. "¿Puedo nadar?" also means "do I know how": "¿Puedo ir al agua?". "Luis puede nadar. ¿Qué necesita?" argued against its own answer: gone.

Left, and why:
- 73 and 74 are the same kind of game (two things of two colours, one of which has the property). The property is the new word each time, and it is the plainest fair test of it.
- In 73 the "no" of "no flota" is an old word. A learner who knows only "no" still has to hear which colour it goes with; said in the script.
- "Puedes" never decides. Whatever is set against it ("no puedes") is settled by "no".
- Length: the reviewer counts more lines than the budget in every opening. 72's wave step is merged; the real number comes from machine play.

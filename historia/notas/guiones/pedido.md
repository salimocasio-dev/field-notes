# El pedido: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `pedido`). Stories 87 to 89. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 86.

Scene words (page 02): sopa, carne, pescado, ensalada, tostones, mofongo, bebida, refresco, limonada, para mí, para él, me da (the plan had quisiera: see the reviews).
How they are shared out: 87 sopa, carne, pescado, ensalada; 88 tostones, mofongo, me da; 89 bebida, refresco, limonada, para mí, para él (and para ella, made of words he has).

The picture: the family's table, close, inside the fonda, in the evening. One view. In front, a long table; the family sits in a row along its far side, facing us; the frog stands on the table. Behind them, in the back wall, the kitchen's hatch: a wide opening with a shelf, where the cocinero puts the plates and rings a bell; his head and his pans show in it. At the left of the hatch, on the wall, two boards with pictures: the food, and the drinks. At the right of the hatch, a hook with green plantains. Under the shelf's end, a low stool; the fonda's cat.

People: the cocinero (a white hat), the mesera, the mesero; abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; guests at the next table (the señor, the señora, the joven, the mujer).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 87: ¿Qué hay de comer?

- **Goal he can see:** at the end of the hatch's shelf, a small plate with three golden fried rounds: the cocinero sets it down, points at the frog, and winks: for the helper. Under it, the fonda's cat looks up.
- **At stake:** the three golden rounds. A wrong plate goes out to a table and comes back; while the frog is busy with it, the cat takes a round.
- **New words:** sopa, carne, pescado, ensalada.
- **Back again:** mesera, cocinero, menú, quiero, hay, come, huele, rico, gato, mira, ven, para, por favor, gracias, buen provecho, yo, esto, no.

**Start:** evening. The family along the table: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; the frog on the table. The mesera with her pad. On the wall, the board with four pictures: a bowl of soup, a plate of meat, a fish on a plate, a bowl of salad. The cocinero in the hatch: he sets the small plate with three golden rounds on the shelf's end, and winks at the frog. The cat sits under it.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Qué quieren comer?" **(quieren, comer: forms of quiere and come)** Abuela points at the board; each picture lights up as she says its word. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira el menú, Coquí. Hay sopa. Hay carne. Hay pescado. Y hay ensalada." Ask (signed, the soup's picture): "¿Dónde está la sopa en el menú?" He touches it: the picture grows for a moment: a bowl, and steam.
2. Each one orders; a small picture of the dish shows over the one who speaks, and stays. NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo quiero sopa." NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo, pescado." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Carne! Yo quiero carne." NARR: "Carmen dice: Y yo quiero ensalada." Ask (signed, the fish's picture on the board): "Abuelo quiere pescado. ¿Dónde está el pescado?" He touches it: it grows; abuelo nods.
3. The mesera reads from her pad, towards the hatch. NARR: "La mesera dice: Una sopa, un pescado, una carne y una ensalada." Flames in the hatch. A bell: a plate of meat on the shelf. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡La carne!" Ask (signed, the plate): "¿Dónde está la carne de Luis?" He touches it: the frog carries it on his head to Luis. It steams. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mmm. ¿Cómo huele, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: rico (rico, qué rico)]: "¡Rico!"
4. The bell, three times: the mesera carries three plates at once, and sets them down one by one. NARR: "La mesera dice: La sopa para abuela, el pescado para abuelo y la ensalada para Carmen." The small pictures over their heads go out.

**The turn**

5. The fonda fills up; the mesera runs from table to table. She lifts the frog onto the hatch's shelf. NARR: "La mesera dice: Coquí, ven acá, por favor." The cocinero taps the small plate of golden rounds. The cat looks up at it. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A bell: three plates stand on the hatch's shelf: the dish that will be called and two of the other three (which, and their order, are drawn each round; the two rounds call different dishes). The mesera, at a table across the room with her back to the hatch, calls for one. The frog pushes a plate to the hatch's edge, where she takes it.

6. **Decision A, touch a plate (three plates; tests the four dishes).** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Coquí! El pescado, por favor." (Drawn.)
   - Right: she takes it to the table; the guest eats. A star.
   - Wrong: she takes it; the guest shakes his head; she brings it back and holds it up. NARR: "La mesera dice: No, Coquí. Esto es carne. ¡El pescado!" While the frog pushes the plate back into place, the cat's paw comes up: a golden round is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a plate (the same game; a bell, three new plates, another dish).** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Coquí! La sopa, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The last plates go out. The frog hops down to the family's table with his small plate.
   - Three rounds: he eats them, one, two, three. Stars and a heart.
   - Two rounds: he eats two; the cat licks a paw.
   - One round: he eats it slowly; the cat sleeps, round as a ball.
9. NARR: "La familia come. Abuela come sopa. Abuelo come pescado." COQUÍ [TURN: buen provecho (buen provecho)]: "¡Buen provecho!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ensalada 4, sopa 5, carne 5, pescado 5 (steps 1 to 4; a word said twice in one line counts once).
**A miss is filed under:** the dish that was called.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): rico; buen provecho.

## Story 88: Tostones y mofongo

- **Goal he can see:** on the hook by the hatch, green plantains: a bunch, and three more tied with a red ribbon. On the shelf's end, the frog's own plate, empty. The cocinero holds up the three with the ribbon, and points at the plate: for you.
- **At stake:** the three plantains with the ribbon. A wrong plate comes back from the table, and the cocinero has to make the right one again: with one of the three.
- **New words:** tostones, mofongo, me da.
- **Back again:** carne, pescado, con, plátano, cocinero, mesera, menú, plato, caliente, cuidado, rico, me gusta, mucho, otro, familia, come, esto, eso, mira, ahora, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** evening. The frog on the hatch's shelf beside the bell, his empty plate at the shelf's end. The cocinero in the hatch with a green plantain in his hand; on the hook, the bunch, and the three with the ribbon. The family at its table; at the next table the señor and the señora. On the wall, the board shows four plates: meat with golden rounds, meat with a dome, fish with golden rounds, fish with a dome.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Mira, Coquí. Un plátano." He slices it, fries the pieces, flattens each one under a wooden press, and fries them again: golden rounds on a plate. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Tostones!" Ask (signed, the plate): "¿Dónde están los tostones?" He touches it: the cocinero gives him one; it is hot, and the frog juggles it. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Cuidado! Los tostones están calientes." The frog eats it; his eyes go wide. COQUÍ [TURN: otro (otro, otro por favor)]: "¡Otro, por favor!" The cocinero laughs and gives him one more.
2. The cocinero mashes fried plantain in a wooden mortar and turns it out: a dome on a plate. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Y esto es mofongo." Ask (signed, the dome): "¿Dónde está el mofongo?" He touches it: the frog tastes a little, and nods. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Tostones y mofongo. Los dos son de plátano."
3. The mesera at the next table. NARR: "El señor dice: Me da carne con tostones, por favor." The mesera taps the board: the plate of meat with golden rounds lights up; the cocinero makes it. NARR: "La señora dice: Y a mí me da pescado con mofongo." Ask (signed, the board's fish with a dome): "La señora quiere pescado con mofongo. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the cocinero makes it; the bell; both plates go out. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Tostones! Gracias." NARR: "La señora dice: Mmm. El mofongo está rico."

**The turn**

4. The mesera is called away to another table. NARR: "La mesera dice: Ahora tú, Coquí. Mira el menú. ¿Qué plato quieren?" The cocinero holds up the three plantains with the ribbon, and points at the frog's empty plate. New guests sit down at the next table. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest at the next table (drawn), looking at the frog, asks for meat or fish, with tostones or with mofongo (both drawn). The board shows the four plates (their places are drawn each round). The frog touches one; the cocinero makes that plate, and it goes out.

5. **Decision A, touch a plate on the board (four; tests tostones against mofongo, with carne and pescado from the last story).** NARR: "El joven dice: Me da pescado con tostones, por favor." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the plate goes out; the guest eats. A star.
   - Wrong: the plate goes out; the guest shakes his head. NARR: "El joven dice: No, eso no. Pescado con tostones, por favor." The plate comes back. The cocinero takes a plantain from the three with the ribbon, and makes the right plate. A cross. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it.
6. **Decision B, touch a plate on the board (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "La mujer dice: Me da carne con mofongo, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The cocinero takes down the plantains with the ribbon that are left, fries them, and fills the frog's plate. The frog carries it to the family's table.
   - Three plantains: a mountain of tostones. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: a full plate.
   - One: four tostones.
8. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Tostones para Coquí." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Te gustan los tostones, Coquí?" **(te gustan: a form of me gusta)** COQUÍ [TURN: mucho (mucho, sí mucho)]: "¡Sí, mucho!" NARR: "Coquí come tostones con la familia."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tostones 6 (steps 1, 2, 3), mofongo 6 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: me da 2 before the game (step 3) and once in each round: four on a clean run.
**A miss is filed under:** tostones or mofongo, if that half was wrong; if only the other half was wrong, carne or pescado.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four; one in two for a learner who has carne and pescado from story 87.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): otro; mucho.

## Story 89: Las bebidas

- **Goal he can see:** the mesero makes a drink for his helper: a tall glass of orange juice, full to the brim, with a straw and a paper umbrella. He sets it on the tray's corner and taps the frog's nose: afterwards.
- **At stake:** the juice (three thirds of the glass). At each mix-up the mesero, hot and flustered, takes a long pull at the straw: a third is gone.
- **New words:** bebida, refresco, limonada, para mí, para él (and para ella).
- **Back again:** mesero, jugo, china, café, agua, hay, ella, él, quiero, quieres, toma (tomar), también, otro, tengo sed, hace calor, me gusta, después, ahora, aquí, esto, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** a hot evening; a fan turns. At the table, three of the family: abuelo, abuela, Luis (Carmen is away at the vellonera, out of the picture). The frog on the table. The mesero with his pad. On the wall, the second board: four pictures, a bottle of soda with a straw, a glass of lemonade with a slice of green lemon on its rim, a glass of orange juice, a cup of coffee.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hace calor! Tengo sed." The mesero points at the second board; each picture lights up as he says its word. NARR: "El mesero dice: Aquí están las bebidas. Hay refresco, hay limonada, hay jugo de china y hay café." Ask (signed, the second board): "¿Dónde están las bebidas?" He touches it: the four pictures light up together.
2. The mesero comes back with a tray: a soda, a coffee, a lemonade. He lifts the bottle. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿El refresco?" NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Para mí, para mí! Me gusta el refresco." Ask (signed, Luis): "¿Para quién es el refresco?" **(quién: heard; the sign carries it)** He touches him: the frog pushes the bottle to Luis.
3. The mesero lifts the cup. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿El café?" Luis nods towards abuelo. NARR: "Luis dice: Para él." Ask (signed, abuelo): "El café es para él. ¿Quién es él?" He touches him: the frog pushes the cup across. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Sí, para mí. Gracias."
4. The mesero lifts the glass. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿Y la limonada?" Abuelo nods towards abuela. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para ella." Ask (signed, abuela): "La limonada es para ella. ¿Quién es ella?" He touches her: the frog pushes the glass across. NARR: "Abuela dice: Para mí. Mmm, limonada." Luis points at each in turn, and at himself. NARR: "Luis dice: El café es para él, la limonada es para ella... ¡y el refresco es para mí!"
5. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿Y para ti, Coquí? ¿Qué quieres de tomar?" COQUÍ [TURN: jugo de china (jugo de china, un jugo de china, jugo)]: "Jugo de china, por favor." The mesero brings the tall glass of orange juice, and sets it on the tray's corner. NARR: "El mesero dice: Una bebida para Coquí. Ahora no. Después."

**The turn**

6. The glasses on the table are empty already. The mesero comes back with a full tray; he fans himself, looks at the tall glass of juice, and then from the drinks to the faces, lost. He taps the frog's nose. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two rounds, two games. Whoever speaks keeps his hands in his lap and his eyes on the frog.

7. **Decision A, touch a drink (three on the tray: a soda, a lemonade and a coffee, in a drawn order; tests refresco against limonada).** One of the three (drawn, on its own) asks for the soda or the lemonade (drawn, on its own). NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para mí, un refresco, por favor." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it across; a long drink, a sigh. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes another one across; he holds it up. NARR: "Abuelo dice: No, Coquí. Esto es limonada. Un refresco, por favor." The mesero takes it back, and pulls at the straw of the tall glass: a third of the juice is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right drink; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a person (three: abuelo, abuela, Luis; tests para mí against para él and para ella).** The mesero lifts one drink. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿La limonada?" Abuelo or Luis (drawn) answers. NARR: "Luis dice: Para ella." (Drawn: para mí, para él or para ella.)
   - Right: the frog pushes the drink to that one. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes it to another; it is pushed back. NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. Para ella." The mesero pulls at the straw: a third is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right person; he touches her.

**The ending**

9. The mesero puts the tall glass down in front of the frog.
   - Full: the frog drinks it to the bottom; a hiccup. Stars and a heart.
   - Two thirds full: he drinks it.
   - One third: one long pull at the straw, and it is gone; he looks into the glass.
10. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Yo quiero otro refresco!" COQUÍ [TURN: también (yo también, también)]: "¡Yo también!" NARR: "Abuela dice: No, no. Ahora, agua."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** refresco 5 (steps 1, 2, 4), limonada 5 (steps 1, 4); para mí 4 (steps 2, 3, 4), para él 3 (steps 3, 4), para ella 3 (step 4). Not deciding: bebida 3 (steps 1, 5).
**A miss is filed under:** A: the drink that was asked for; B: para mí or para él as said; for para ella, ella.
**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in three, and one in two for a learner who has café; B one in three (él and ella are words he has: mí is the new one).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): jugo de china; también.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- All three: the plan's "quisiera" is a textbook's and a formal restaurant's word; in a fonda people say "Me da ..., por favor". "Me da" is story 88's new word in its place, the frog's turn in 89 is "Jugo de china, por favor", and page 02's list follows. A guest who gets the wrong plate does not repeat his order's formula: "No, eso no. Pescado con tostones, por favor." Whoever holds the wrong thing up says "esto", not "eso". The goal's thing is in the first picture of 87 and 88.
- 87: abuela's soup and abuelo's fish were never brought, and the last line had them eating: the mesera brings three plates in step 4. The orders were only spoken: a small picture of the dish shows over each one. Four brand-new words on four plates: three plates a round, a bell and a new shelf each round, and the two rounds call different dishes. "Tú, aquí" is how one orders a pet about: "ven acá, por favor". Abuela answered "buen provecho" with the same words.
- 88: "Todo con plátano" said "with plantain on the side": "Los dos son de plátano". The mesera's "La gente dice: quisiera carne..." put a second "dice" inside a line and drilled: one plain line, and the pencil that did nothing is gone. The spare plantains looked like the others, and it was not clear who lost them: they are tied with a ribbon, and they fill the frog's own plate. The last line restated his answer.
- 89: a mesero with drinks asks whose they are ("¿El refresco?"); he does not announce them. "¿Qué bebida quieres?" is not said: "¿Qué quieres de tomar?" (bebida is the board's word). "¿Dónde está él?" at a table where everyone is in sight: "¿Quién es él?". The frog asked for juice and got a striped drink: he gets a tall glass of orange juice, and the stake is its three thirds. Jugo and café stood on the tray and were never called: the tray has three drinks, and the chance is stated honestly. Who asks is drawn apart from what was his in the opening. Para él and para ella had three hearings within seconds of each other: Luis's line at the end of step 4 spreads them. The closing narration had no speaker and repeated abuela. "Las bebidas." alone was a label.

Left, and why:
- Three or four things to choose from (87, 88, 89): his rule asks for two real options, and every round has at least that; earlier places have threes and fours too. 87 went from four plates to three.
- 89 B: él and ella are words he has, so only "mí" is new there. It is practice of the phrase, and it is said so.
- "¿Qué quieren comer?" (87): the mesera's usual "¿Qué van a comer?" needs "van a", which comes in place 10.
- "Carne" alone: a real board says carne guisada or carne frita. The picture is fried chunks; the word stays plain.
- "El joven", "la mujer" as speakers' names: as in La entrada.
- Two speaking turns a story: the same as places 4 to 7.

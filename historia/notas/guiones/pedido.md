# El pedido: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `pedido`). Stories 87 to 89. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 86.

Scene words (page 02): sopa, carne, pescado, ensalada, tostones, mofongo, bebida, refresco, limonada, para mí, para él, quisiera.
How they are shared out: 87 sopa, carne, pescado, ensalada; 88 tostones, mofongo, quisiera; 89 bebida, refresco, limonada, para mí, para él (and para ella, made of words he has).

The picture: the family's table, close, inside the fonda, in the evening. One view. In front, a long table; the family sits in a row along its far side, facing us; the frog stands on the table. Behind them, in the back wall, the kitchen's hatch: a wide opening with a shelf, where the cocinero puts the plates and rings a bell; his head and his pans show in it. At the left of the hatch, on the wall, two boards with pictures: the food, and the drinks. At the right of the hatch, a hook with a bunch of green plantains. Under the hatch's end, a low stool; the fonda's cat.

People: the cocinero (a white hat), the mesera, the mesero; abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; guests at the next table (the señor, the señora, the joven, the mujer).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 87: ¿Qué hay de comer?

- **Goal he can see:** at the end of the hatch's shelf, a small plate with three golden fried rounds: the cocinero sets it down, points at the frog, and winks: for the helper. Under it, the fonda's cat looks up.
- **At stake:** the three golden rounds. A wrong plate goes out to a table and comes back; while the frog is busy with it, the cat takes a round.
- **New words:** sopa, carne, pescado, ensalada.
- **Back again:** fonda, mesera, cocinero, menú, quiero, hay, come, comida, plato, huele, rico, caliente, gato, mira, por favor, gracias, buen provecho, yo, eso, no.

**Start:** evening. The family along the table: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; the frog on the table. The mesera with her pad. On the wall, the board with four pictures: a bowl of soup, a plate of meat, a fish on a plate, a bowl of salad. The cocinero's head in the hatch.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Qué quieren comer?" **(comer: heard; a form of come)** Abuela points at the board; the pictures light up in turn. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira el menú, Coquí. Hay sopa. Hay carne. Hay pescado. Y hay ensalada." Ask (signed, the soup's picture): "¿Dónde está la sopa?" He touches it: the picture grows for a moment: a bowl, and steam.
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo quiero sopa." NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo, pescado." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Carne! Yo quiero carne." NARR: "Carmen dice: Y yo quiero ensalada." Ask (signed, the fish's picture): "Abuelo quiere pescado. ¿Dónde está el pescado?" He touches it: the fish's picture grows; abuelo nods.
3. The mesera reads from her pad, towards the hatch. NARR: "La mesera dice: Una sopa, un pescado, una carne y una ensalada." Flames in the hatch. A bell: a plate of meat on the shelf. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡La carne!" Ask (signed, the plate): "¿Dónde está la carne?" He touches it: the frog carries it on his head to Luis. It steams. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mmm. ¿Cómo huele, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: rico (rico, qué rico)]: "¡Rico!"
4. The bell again: a bowl of salad. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡La ensalada!" The frog carries it to Carmen. NARR: "Carmen dice: Gracias, Coquí. Me gusta la ensalada."

**The turn**

5. The fonda fills up; the mesera runs from table to table. She lifts the frog onto the hatch's shelf. NARR: "La mesera dice: Coquí, por favor. Tú, aquí." The cocinero sets down the small plate with the three golden rounds, and winks. The cat looks up at it. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Four plates stand on the hatch's shelf: soup, meat, fish, salad (their order is drawn each round). The mesera, at a table across the room, calls for one (drawn). The frog pushes a plate to the hatch's edge, where she takes it.

6. **Decision A, touch a plate (four plates; tests the four dishes).** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Coquí! El pescado, por favor." (Drawn.)
   - Right: she takes it to the table; the guest eats. A star.
   - Wrong: she takes it; the guest shakes his head; she brings it back. NARR: "La mesera dice: No, Coquí. Eso es carne. ¡El pescado!" While the frog pushes the plate back into place, the cat's paw comes up: a golden round is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a plate (the same game; the plates in a new order).** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Coquí! La sopa, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The last plates go out. The frog hops down to the family's table with his small plate.
   - Three rounds: he eats them, one, two, three. Stars and a heart.
   - Two rounds: he eats two; the cat licks a paw.
   - One round: he eats it slowly; the cat sleeps, round as a ball.
9. NARR: "La familia come. Abuela come sopa. Abuelo come pescado." COQUÍ [TURN: buen provecho (buen provecho)]: "¡Buen provecho!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias, Coquí. ¡Buen provecho!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** sopa 4 (steps 1, 2, 3), carne 6, pescado 5, ensalada 5 (steps 1 to 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the dish that was called.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): rico; buen provecho.

## Story 88: Tostones y mofongo

- **Goal he can see:** on the hook by the hatch, a bunch of green plantains. The cocinero counts them off: these for the orders, and three more. He points at the three, and at the family's table: for you.
- **At stake:** the three spare plantains. A wrong plate comes back from the table, and the cocinero has to make the right one again: with one of the three.
- **New words:** tostones, mofongo, quisiera.
- **Back again:** sopa, carne, pescado, con, plátano, cocinero, mesera, menú, caliente, cuidado, me gusta, mucho, otro, familia, para, esto, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** evening. The frog on the hatch's shelf beside the bell. The cocinero in the hatch with a green plantain in his hand. The family at its table; at the next table the señor and the señora. On the wall, the board; under the four dishes it now shows four plates: meat with golden rounds, meat with a dome, fish with golden rounds, fish with a dome.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Mira, Coquí. Un plátano." He slices it, fries the pieces, flattens each one under a wooden press, and fries them again: golden rounds on a plate. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Tostones!" Ask (signed, the plate): "¿Dónde están los tostones?" He touches it: the cocinero gives him one; it is hot, and the frog juggles it. NARR: "El cocinero dice: ¡Cuidado! Los tostones están calientes." The frog eats it, and holds out his hand. COQUÍ [TURN: otro (otro, otro por favor)]: "¡Otro, por favor!" The cocinero laughs and gives him one more.
2. The cocinero mashes fried plantain in a wooden mortar and turns it out: a dome on a plate. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Y esto es mofongo." Ask (signed, the dome): "¿Dónde está el mofongo?" He touches it: the frog tastes a little, and nods. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Tostones y mofongo. Todo con plátano."
3. The mesera at the next table; the señor looks at the board. NARR: "El señor dice: Quisiera carne con tostones, por favor." The mesera taps the board: the plate of meat with golden rounds lights up; the cocinero makes it. NARR: "La señora dice: Y yo quisiera pescado con mofongo." Ask (signed, the board's fish with a dome): "Pescado con mofongo. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the cocinero makes it; the bell; both plates go out. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Tostones! Gracias." NARR: "La señora dice: Mmm. El mofongo está rico."
4. The mesera puts her pencil in the frog's hand. NARR: "La mesera dice: Ahora tú, Coquí. La gente dice: quisiera carne, quisiera pescado... Tú tocas el menú." **(tocas: a form of toca)**

**The turn**

5. The cocinero counts the plantains on the hook: the orders', and three more; he points at the three, and at the family. New guests sit down at the next table. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A guest at the next table (drawn) asks for meat or fish, with tostones or with mofongo (both drawn). The board shows the four plates (their places are drawn each round). The frog touches one; the cocinero makes that plate, and it goes out.

6. **Decision A, touch a plate on the board (four; tests tostones against mofongo, and carne against pescado).** NARR: "El joven dice: Quisiera pescado con tostones, por favor." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the plate goes out; the guest eats. A star.
   - Wrong: the plate goes out; the guest shakes his head. NARR: "El joven dice: No. Quisiera pescado con tostones." The plate comes back. The cocinero takes a plantain from the three, and makes the right plate. A cross. The sign is on the right plate; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a plate on the board (the same game; another guest).** NARR: "La mujer dice: Quisiera carne con mofongo, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The cocinero takes down the plantains that are left, and fries them.
   - Three plantains: a mountain of tostones in the middle of the family's table. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: a plate of tostones.
   - One: four tostones: one each for abuela, abuelo, Luis and Carmen; the frog gets the crumbs.
9. NARR: "El cocinero dice: Tostones para la familia." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Te gustan los tostones, Coquí?" **(te gustan: a form of me gusta)** COQUÍ [TURN: mucho (mucho, sí mucho)]: "¡Sí, mucho!" NARR: "A Coquí le gustan los tostones. Y el mofongo también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tostones 6 (steps 1, 2, 3), mofongo 5 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: quisiera 4 before the game (steps 3, 4), and once in each round.
**A miss is filed under:** tostones or mofongo, if that half was wrong; if only the other half was wrong, carne or pescado.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): otro; mucho.

## Story 89: Las bebidas

- **Goal he can see:** the mesero has made a drink for his helper: a tall glass in three layers, red, yellow and green, with a straw and a paper umbrella. He sets it on the tray's corner and taps the frog's nose: afterwards.
- **At stake:** the three layers. At each mix-up the mesero, to steady himself, takes a long pull at the straw: a layer is gone.
- **New words:** bebida, refresco, limonada, para mí, para él (and para ella).
- **Back again:** quisiera, mesero, menú, jugo, china, café, agua, vaso, taza, hay, ella, él, quiero, también, otro, tengo sed, hace calor, aquí tiene, por favor, gracias, sí, no, eso, ahora.

**Start:** a hot evening; a fan turns. At the table, three of the family: abuelo, abuela, Luis (Carmen is away at the vellonera). The frog on the table. The mesero with his pad. On the wall, the second board: four pictures, a bottle of soda with a straw, a glass of lemonade with a slice of lemon on its rim, a glass of orange juice, a cup of coffee.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hace calor! Tengo sed." The mesero points at the second board. NARR: "El mesero dice: Las bebidas. Hay refresco, hay limonada, hay jugo de china y hay café." Ask (signed, the second board): "¿Dónde están las bebidas?" He touches it: its four pictures light up in turn.
2. The mesero comes back with a tray: a soda, a coffee, a lemonade. NARR: "El mesero dice: Un refresco." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Para mí, para mí! Me gusta el refresco." Ask (signed, Luis): "El refresco es para Luis. ¿Dónde está Luis?" He touches him: the frog pushes the bottle to Luis.
3. NARR: "El mesero dice: Un café." Luis nods towards abuelo. NARR: "Luis dice: Para él." Ask (signed, abuelo): "Para él. ¿Dónde está él?" He touches him: the frog pushes the cup across. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿Para él? Aquí tiene." NARR: "Abuelo dice: Sí, para mí. Gracias."
4. NARR: "El mesero dice: Y una limonada." Abuelo nods towards abuela. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para ella." Ask (signed, abuela): "Para ella. ¿Dónde está ella?" He touches her: the frog pushes the glass across. NARR: "El mesero dice: Para ella. Aquí tiene." NARR: "Abuela dice: Para mí. Mmm, limonada."
5. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¿Y para ti, Coquí? ¿Qué bebida quieres?" COQUÍ [TURN: quisiera jugo (quisiera jugo, quisiera un jugo, jugo)]: "Quisiera jugo, por favor." The mesero brings the tall glass in three layers, and sets it on the tray's corner. NARR: "El mesero dice: Una bebida para Coquí. Ahora no. Después."

**The turn**

6. The glasses on the table are empty already. The mesero comes back with a full tray, and looks from the glasses to the faces, lost. He taps the frog's nose, and the tall glass. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two rounds, two games.

7. **Decision A, touch a drink (four on the tray: soda, lemonade, orange juice, coffee, in a drawn order; tests refresco and limonada among the drinks he has).** One of the three (drawn) asks. NARR: "Abuela dice: Quisiera una limonada, por favor." (Drawn; in this round the drink is always the soda or the lemonade.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it across; a long drink, a sigh. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes another one across; a sip, a face. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Eso es jugo. Una limonada." The mesero takes it back, and pulls at the straw of the tall glass: a layer is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right drink; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a person (three: abuelo, abuela, Luis; tests para mí, para él and para ella).** The mesero holds up one drink. NARR: "El mesero dice: Un refresco." Abuelo or Luis (drawn) answers, with his hands in his lap and his eyes on the drink. NARR: "Luis dice: Para ella." (Drawn: para mí, para él or para ella.)
   - Right: the frog pushes the drink to that one. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes it to another; it is pushed back. NARR: "Luis dice: No, Coquí. Para ella." The mesero pulls at the straw: a layer is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right person; he touches her.

**The ending**

9. The mesero puts the tall glass down in front of the frog.
   - Three layers: red, yellow, green; the frog drinks it to the bottom; a hiccup. Stars and a heart.
   - Two layers. One layer: one long pull, and it is gone.
10. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Yo quiero otro refresco!" COQUÍ [TURN: también (yo también, también)]: "¡Yo también!" NARR: "Abuela dice: No, no. Ahora, agua." NARR: "Una limonada para ella, un café para él... y agua para Luis y Coquí."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** refresco 4 (steps 1, 2), limonada 3 (steps 1, 4); para mí 4 (steps 2, 3, 4), para él 3 (step 3), para ella 3 (step 4). Not deciding: bebida 4 (steps 1, 5).
**A miss is filed under:** A: the drink that was asked for; B: the one that was said (para mí, para él, or ella for para ella).
**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in four; B one in three.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): quisiera jugo; también.

# El cuarto: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `cuarto`). Stories 96 to 98. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 95.

Scene words (page 02): cuarto, cama, almohada, ropa, luz, se despierta, se levanta, se viste, la mañana, a las siete, primero, tiene sueño.
How they are shared out: 96 cuarto, cama, almohada, luz, tiene sueño; 97 se despierta, se levanta, la mañana, a las siete; 98 se viste, ropa, primero.

The picture: the children's bedroom, early in the morning. One view. The back wall faces us: a window (the dawn, then the sun), a wall clock, the door at the right. Two beds stand side by side with their heads to the back wall: Luis's (blue) at the left, Carmen's (pink) at the right; between them a night table with a small brass bell. At the far left, a standing lamp. At the right front, a chair. On the floor at the left front, a rug. In the right back corner (stories 97), a cradle. No ceiling, nothing over anyone's head.

People: Luis, Carmen, abuela, the baby (asleep in the cradle), Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 96: Buenos días

- **Goal he can see:** abuela sets a plate on the night table: three pieces of bread with butter, for Luis when he is up. Under the bed, Canelo's nose comes out, towards the plate.
- **At stake:** the three pieces of bread. While the frog looks in the wrong place, Canelo takes one.
- **New words:** cuarto, cama, almohada, luz, tiene sueño.
- **Back again:** buenos días, hola, busca, cerca, gafas, zapatos, duerme, perro, pan, mantequilla, mira, ¿dónde está?, mi, tu, yo, no, sí, gracias, por favor, ahora.

**Start:** dawn; the window is pale, the room dim. Luis sleeps in his bed, Carmen in hers. The frog on the rug. Abuela in the door with a plate.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Buenos días, Coquí. Mira: el cuarto de Luis y Carmen. Luis duerme en la cama." Ask (signed, Luis's bed): "¿Dónde está la cama de Luis?" He touches it: the frog hops onto the bed; Luis groans and pulls the blanket over his head. NARR: "Luis dice: No... Tengo sueño."
2. Abuela sets the plate on the night table. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis tiene sueño. Coquí, la luz, por favor." Ask (signed, the standing lamp): "¿Dónde está la luz del cuarto?" He touches it: the lamp comes on, and the room is bright. Carmen sits up in her bed. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ay, la luz! ¡Hola, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenos días (buenos días, buenos dias)]: "¡Buenos días!"
3. Carmen throws her big pillow at Luis; it bounces off him and lands on the floor, at the right. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Luis! ¡La almohada!" NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ay! ¡Tu almohada!" Ask (signed, the big pillow on the floor): "¿Dónde está la almohada de Carmen?" He touches it: the frog bounces on it. Luis sits up, and feels about with his hands. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde están mis gafas? No están en la cama."
4. NARR: "Carmen dice: Yo sé dónde están. Yo tengo sueño... Coquí busca." She lies back, her eyes half shut. Abuela shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Este cuarto! Vengo ahora." **(vengo: a form of viene)** She goes.

**The turn**

5. Canelo comes out from under the bed and sits by the night table, his eyes on the plate. Luis feels about, blind without his glasses. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Something of Luis's is lost (round A his glasses, round B his shoes). It lies out of sight near one of three things: the bed (under its edge), the big pillow on the floor (under its corner), the lamp (behind its foot). Carmen, from her bed with her eyes half shut, says where to look (drawn, each round on its own). The frog looks there.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the bed, the pillow, the lamp; tests cama, almohada and luz).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde están mis gafas?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Busca cerca de la luz." (Or, drawn: "Busca cerca de la cama." Or: "Busca cerca de la almohada.")
   - Right: the frog pulls them out. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mis gafas! Gracias, Coquí." A star.
   - Wrong: the frog looks; nothing there. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. Cerca de la luz." Meanwhile Canelo's nose goes up over the plate: a piece of bread is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; his shoes).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y mis zapatos?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Busca cerca de la cama." (Drawn.) Right: NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mis zapatos! Gracias." Wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis, with his glasses and his shoes, sits down on his bed with the plate.
   - Three pieces: he eats two and gives one to the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two pieces: he eats them; the frog gets the crumbs.
   - One piece: he breaks it in two with the frog; Canelo licks his nose.
9. Abuela comes back. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Tienes sueño?" **(tienes: a form of tiene)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" The frog lies down on the big pillow, and his eyes shut. NARR: "Coquí tiene sueño. Duerme en la almohada."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cama 3 (steps 1, 3), almohada 3 (step 3), luz 3 (step 2). Not deciding: cuarto 3 (steps 1, 2, 4); tiene sueño 4 with tengo sueño (steps 1, 2, 4).
**Forms:** tengo sueño and tienes sueño count with tiene sueño.
**A miss is filed under:** the place that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): buenos días; sí.

## Story 97: A las siete

- **Goal he can see:** in the corner, the cradle: the baby sleeps, with three small Z's over it. Abuela puts a finger to her lips.
- **At stake:** the baby's sleep (three Z's). A wrong move makes a noise: a Z pops.
- **New words:** se despierta, se levanta, la mañana, a las siete.
- **Back again:** cuarto, cama, tiene sueño, bebé, duerme, despacio, reloj, ¿qué hora es?, son las, y media, siete, toca, ahora, ya, buenos días, cuidado, no, sí.

**Start:** morning; the sun is in the window; the wall clock shows seven. Luis and Carmen sleep. The baby sleeps in the cradle. The frog on the night table, beside the small bell. Abuela in the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Son las siete de la mañana, Coquí." Ask (signed, the clock): "Son las siete de la mañana. ¿Dónde está el reloj?" He touches it: it ticks. Abuela puts a finger to her lips and points at the cradle. NARR: "Abuela dice: El bebé duerme. Despacio, Coquí."
2. She points at the little bell. NARR: "Abuela dice: A las siete, Carmen se despierta." Ask (signed, the bell): "Carmen se despierta. ¿Qué toca Coquí?" He touches it: a soft tinkle; Carmen opens her eyes and stretches, in her bed. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mmm. Buenos días."
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen se levanta." Ask (signed, Carmen's blanket): "Carmen se levanta. ¿Qué toca Coquí?" He touches it: the frog pulls the blanket off; Carmen stands up on the floor. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ya! Me levanto." **(me levanto: a form of se levanta)**
4. Luis snores. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis tiene sueño. ¿Qué hora es, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: son las siete (son las siete)]: "Son las siete." NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. A las siete Luis se despierta, y a las siete y media se levanta."

**The turn**

5. Abuela goes to the kitchen, on tiptoe. The baby's three Z's float over the cradle. Luis sleeps on. The frog stands between the bell and Luis's blanket. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela calls from the kitchen (her voice comes in at the door) what Luis does now: he wakes, or he gets up (drawn, each round on its own). The frog rings the little bell, or pulls the blanket off. Before round B, Luis has flopped back into bed, asleep again.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the bell, the blanket; tests se despierta against se levanta).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí! Ahora Luis se despierta." (Or, drawn: "¡Coquí! Ahora Luis se levanta.")
   - Right: for "se despierta" a soft tinkle, and Luis opens his eyes and sits up; for "se levanta" the blanket comes off, and he stands up, yawning. A star.
   - Wrong: the blanket comes off when she said he wakes: Luis rolls out of bed with a thump. (Or the bell tinkles when she said he gets up: he opens his eyes, and yawns out loud, long.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Se despierta!" (or "¡Se levanta!") Over the cradle a Z pops; the baby stirs. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; Luis is asleep in his bed again).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí! Ahora Luis se levanta." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The clock shows half past seven. Luis and Carmen are up.
   - Three Z's: the baby sleeps on; they tiptoe out. Stars and a heart.
   - Two Z's: the baby turns over, and sucks a thumb.
   - One Z: the baby sits up and laughs; abuela comes and carries it off to breakfast.
9. Luis runs for the door, his feet loud. COQUÍ [TURN: despacio (despacio, despacio luis)]: "¡Despacio, Luis!" He goes on tiptoe. NARR: "Son las siete y media de la mañana. Luis y Carmen se levantan." **(se levantan: a form of se levanta)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se despierta 4 (steps 2, 4), se levanta 3 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: a las siete 3 (steps 2, 4); la mañana 2 before the game (step 1) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se despierta or se levanta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): son las siete; despacio.

## Story 98: Se viste

- **Goal he can see:** abuela holds up her camera: a photo of Luis for the first day of school. Luis's hair is combed flat and his clothes are neat.
- **At stake:** how neat he is for the photo (3 steps). A thing put on in the wrong order has to come off again: first a tuft of hair stands up, then his shirt tail hangs out.
- **New words:** se viste, ropa, primero.
- **Back again:** después, camisa, pantalón, zapatos, sombrero, gafas, reloj, falta, cuarto, rápido, guagua, escuela, bonito, mira, ahora, no, sí.

**Start:** morning. Luis stands on the rug in his pyjamas, his hair combed. On the chair, his clothes. Carmen sits on her bed. The frog on the rug. Abuela in the door with a camera round her neck.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: la ropa de Luis." Ask (signed, the chair): "¿Dónde está la ropa de Luis?" He touches it: the frog hops onto the chair. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Luis se viste."
2. Luis, half asleep, pulls his shoes on, and then his trousers: they stick at the shoes; he hops, and falls onto the bed. Carmen laughs. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Luis! Primero el pantalón. Después los zapatos." Ask (signed, the trousers): "¿Qué se pone Luis primero?" He touches them: the shoes come off, the trousers go on. NARR: "Carmen dice: Después, los zapatos." The frog pushes the shoes over; Luis puts them on.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se viste: primero el pantalón, después los zapatos. ¿Qué falta, Coquí?" Luis still has his pyjama top on. COQUÍ [TURN: camisa (la camisa, camisa)]: "¡La camisa!"

**The turn**

4. Abuela lifts her camera, and lowers it: not yet. Carmen takes charge: she stands on her bed like a captain. New things lie on the chair. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two things lie on the chair (round A: the shirt and the glasses; round B: the hat and the watch). Carmen names one of them and says whether it goes on first or afterwards (drawn, each round on its own). The frog pushes over the thing that goes on first.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (two on the chair; tests primero against después).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Primero, la camisa." (Or, drawn: "La camisa, después." Or: "Primero, las gafas." Or: "Las gafas, después.")
   - Right: Luis puts it on, and then the other. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis puts the other one on first. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No! Primero, la camisa." It has to come off again, over his head: a tuft of his hair stands up (the second time: his shirt tail hangs out). A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the hat and the watch).** NARR: "Carmen dice: El sombrero, después." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Abuela lifts her camera. A flash; the photo shows on its screen.
   - Neat: hair flat, shirt in. Stars and a heart.
   - A tuft of hair stands up.
   - A tuft of hair, and his shirt tail out; he grins.
8. A horn outside. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bonito! ¡La guagua! Vamos." COQUÍ [TURN: rápido (rápido, rapido)]: "¡Rápido, Luis!" NARR: "Por la mañana Luis se viste: primero la ropa, después la escuela."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** primero 4 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: ropa 2 (step 1) and once in the ending; se viste 2 before the game (steps 1, 3) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (primero or después).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both things lie on the chair, and neither has an order of its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): camisa; rápido.

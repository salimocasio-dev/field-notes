# El cuarto: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `cuarto`). Stories 96 to 98. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 95.

Scene words (page 02): cuarto, cama, almohada, ropa, luz, se despierta, se levanta, se viste, la mañana, a las siete, primero, tiene sueño.
How they are shared out: 96 cuarto, cama, almohada, luz, tiene sueño; 97 se despierta, se levanta, la mañana, a las siete; 98 se viste, ropa, primero.

The picture: the children's bedroom, early in the morning. One view. The back wall faces us: a window (the dawn, then the sun), a wall clock, a wall light with its switch, the door at the right. Two beds stand side by side with their heads to the back wall: Luis's (blue) at the left, Carmen's (pink) at the right; between them a night table with a small brass bell. At the far left, an armchair. At the right front, a chair. On the floor at the left front, a rug. In the right back corner (story 97), a cradle. No ceiling, nothing over anyone's head.

People: Luis, Carmen, abuela, abuelo (asleep in the armchair, story 97), the baby (asleep in the cradle), Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask: the sign shows what to touch, and the voice does not ask again.

## Story 96: Buenos días

- **Goal he can see:** abuela sets a plate on the night table: three pieces of bread with butter, for Luis when he is up. Under the bed, Canelo's nose comes out, towards the plate.
- **At stake:** the three pieces of bread. While the frog looks in the wrong place, Canelo takes one.
- **New words:** cuarto, cama, almohada, luz, tiene sueño.
- **Back again:** buenos días, hola, busca, cerca, silla, camisa, zapatos, duerme, perro, quiero, mira, ¿dónde está?, mi, tu, yo, tú, no, sí, gracias, por favor, ahora, viene (vengo).

**Start:** dawn; the window is pale, the room dim. Luis sleeps in his bed, Carmen in hers. The frog on the rug. Abuela in the door with a plate; she sets it on the night table. Under Luis's bed, Canelo's nose.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Buenos días, Coquí. Mira: el cuarto de Luis y Carmen. Luis duerme en la cama." Ask (signed, Luis's bed): "¿Dónde está la cama de Luis?" He touches it: the frog hops onto the bed; Luis groans and pulls the blanket over his head. NARR: "Luis dice: No... Tengo sueño. ¡Mi cama!"
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis tiene sueño. Coquí, la luz, por favor." (signed, the wall light's switch.) He touches it: the wall light comes on, and the room is bright. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No! ¡La luz no!" Carmen sits up in her bed. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ay, la luz! ¡Hola, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenos días (buenos días, buenos dias)]: "¡Buenos días!"
3. Carmen throws her big pillow at Luis; it bounces off him and lands on the floor, at the right. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Luis, toma mi almohada!" NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ay! ¡No quiero tu almohada!" Ask (signed, the big pillow on the floor): "¿Dónde está la almohada de Carmen?" He touches it: the frog bounces on it. Luis sits up, his eyes still shut, and feels about with his hands. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está mi camisa?"
4. NARR: "Carmen dice: Yo sé dónde está. Yo tengo sueño... Busca tú, Coquí." She lies back, her eyes half shut. Abuela shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Este cuarto! Vengo ahora." **(vengo: a form of viene)** She goes.

**The turn**

5. Canelo comes out from under the bed and sits by the night table, his eyes on the plate. Luis sits on his bed in his pyjamas, his eyes shut. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Something of Luis's is lost (round A his shirt, round B his shoes). It lies out of sight near one of three things: the bed (under its edge), the big pillow on the floor (under it), the chair (behind it). Carmen, from her bed with her eyes half shut, says where to look (drawn; round B's place is not round A's). The frog looks there.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the bed, the pillow, the chair; tests cama against almohada, with silla, which he has).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está mi camisa?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Busca cerca de la almohada." (Or, drawn: "Busca cerca de la cama." Or: "Busca cerca de la silla.")
   - Right: the frog pulls it out. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mi camisa! Gracias, Coquí." A star.
   - Wrong: the frog looks; nothing there. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. Cerca de la almohada." Meanwhile Canelo's nose goes up over the plate: a piece of bread is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; his shoes, another place).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y mis zapatos?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Busca cerca de la cama." (Drawn.) Right: NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mis zapatos! Gracias." Wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Luis, with his shirt and his shoes, sits down on his bed with the plate.
   - Three pieces: he eats two and gives one to the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two pieces: he eats them; the frog gets the crumbs.
   - One piece: he breaks it in two with the frog; Canelo licks his nose.
9. Abuela comes back. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Tienes sueño?" **(tienes: a form of tiene)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" The frog lies down on the big pillow, and his eyes shut. NARR: "Coquí tiene sueño. Duerme en la almohada, en el cuarto de Luis y Carmen."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cama 3 (step 1), almohada 3 (step 3). Not deciding: luz 3 (step 2), cuarto 2 before the game (steps 1, 4) and once in the last line, tiene sueño 3 with tengo sueño (steps 1, 2, 4) and 2 more in the ending.
**Forms:** tengo sueño and tienes sueño count with tiene sueño.
**A miss is filed under:** the place that was said (cama, almohada or silla).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): buenos días; sí.

## Story 97: A las siete

- **Goal he can see:** in the corner, the cradle: the baby sleeps, with three small Z's over it. Abuela puts a finger to her lips.
- **At stake:** the baby's sleep (three Z's). A wrong move ends in a noise: a Z pops.
- **New words:** se despierta, se levanta, la mañana, a las siete.
- **Back again:** cuarto, cama, tiene sueño, bebé, duerme, despacio, reloj, ¿qué hora es?, son las, y media, siete, toca (tocas), ahora, ya, buenos días, no, sí.

**Start:** morning; the sun is in the window; the wall clock shows seven. Luis and Carmen sleep in their beds. Abuelo sleeps in the armchair, a storybook on his chest and a blanket over his knees. The baby sleeps in the cradle. The frog on the night table, beside the small bell. Abuela in the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela puts a finger to her lips and points at the cradle. NARR: "Abuela dice: Shh. El bebé duerme. Despacio, Coquí." Ask (signed, the clock): "¿Dónde está el reloj, Coquí?" He touches it: it ticks. NARR: "Abuela dice: Son las siete de la mañana."
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: A las siete, Carmen se despierta." (signed, the bell.) He touches it: a soft tinkle; Carmen opens her eyes and sits up in her bed. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mmm. Buenos días."
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen se levanta." (signed, Carmen's blanket.) He touches it: the frog pulls the blanket off; Carmen stands up on the floor. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ya me levanto! ¡Qué bonita la mañana!" **(me levanto: a form of se levanta)**
4. Luis snores. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis duerme. ¿Qué hora es, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: son las siete (son las siete)]: "Son las siete." NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. A las siete Luis se despierta." The frog rings the bell: Luis opens his eyes, and shuts them again. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y a las siete y media se levanta." Carmen points at her own two feet on the floor. NARR: "Carmen dice: Luis se despierta. Yo me levanto."

**The turn**

5. Abuela goes to the kitchen, on tiptoe. The baby's three Z's float over the cradle. Luis sleeps on in his bed; abuelo in the armchair. The frog stands by the bell. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela calls from the kitchen (her voice comes in at the door) what a sleeper does now: he wakes, or he gets up (drawn, each round on its own: the two rounds may be the same). Round A is Luis in his bed; round B is abuelo in the armchair. The frog rings the little bell, or pulls the sleeper's blanket off. The bell always wakes a sleeper: he opens his eyes and sits up. The blanket always gets him up: he stands, cold.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the bell, Luis's blanket; tests se despierta against se levanta).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí! Ahora Luis se despierta." (Or, drawn: "¡Coquí! Ahora Luis se levanta.")
   - Right: for "se despierta" a soft tinkle, and Luis opens his eyes and sits up, quiet; for "se levanta" the blanket comes off, and he stands up, quiet. A star.
   - Wrong: the blanket comes off when she said he wakes: Luis stands, cold, and howls. (Or the bell tinkles when she said he gets up: he sits up in bed and stays there, and abuela has to call again, loud.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Se despierta!" (or "¡Se levanta!") Over the cradle a Z pops; the baby stirs. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; abuelo, his blanket).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí! Ahora abuelo se levanta." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.
8. Luis jumps down and runs for the door, his feet loud. COQUÍ [TURN: despacio (despacio, despacio luis)]: "¡Despacio, Luis!" He stops, and goes on tiptoe.

**The ending**

9. The clock shows half past seven. Abuela comes to the door and claps her hands without a sound: whoever is still sitting gets up. All of them are on their feet.
   - Three Z's: the baby sleeps on; they tiptoe out. Stars and a heart.
   - Two Z's: the baby turns over, and sucks a thumb; they tiptoe out.
   - One Z: the baby sits up and laughs; abuela carries it out with them.
10. NARR: "Son las siete y media de la mañana. Luis y Carmen se levantan." **(se levantan: a form of se levanta)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se despierta 3 (steps 2, 4), se levanta 2 and me levanto twice (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: a las siete 3 (steps 2, 4); la mañana 2 before the game (steps 1, 3) and 1 in the ending.
**Forms:** me levanto, se levantan count with se levanta.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se despierta or se levanta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 8): son las siete; despacio.

## Story 98: Se viste

- **Goal he can see:** abuela holds up her camera: a photo of Luis for the first day of school. Luis's hair is combed flat and his clothes are neat.
- **At stake:** how neat he is for the photo (3 steps). A thing put on out of turn has to come off again and go on a second time: first a tuft of hair stands up, then his shirt tail hangs out.
- **New words:** se viste, ropa, primero.
- **Back again:** después, se pone, camisa, pantalón, zapatos, sombrero, reloj, falta, cuarto, rápido, listo, guagua, escuela, mira, ahora, no, sí.

**Start:** morning. Luis stands on the rug in his pyjamas, his hair combed. On the chair, his clothes. Carmen sits on her bed. The frog on the rug. Abuela in the door with a camera round her neck.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: la ropa de Luis está en la silla." (signed, the chair.) He touches it: the frog hops onto the chair. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Luis se viste."
2. Luis, half asleep, pulls his shoes on, and then his trousers: they stick at the shoes; he hops, and falls onto the bed. Carmen laughs. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Luis! Primero el pantalón, después los zapatos." Ask (signed, the trousers): "¿Qué se pone Luis primero?" He touches them: the shoes come off, the trousers go on, and then the shoes.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se viste. ¿Qué falta, Coquí?" Luis still has his pyjama top on. COQUÍ [TURN: camisa (la camisa, camisa)]: "¡La camisa!" NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. Primero la ropa, después la foto."

**The turn**

4. Abuela lifts her camera, and lowers it: not yet. Carmen takes charge: she stands on her bed like a captain. Luis takes his shoes off again and waits in his socks. New things lie on the chair. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two things lie on the chair (round A: the shirt and the shoes; round B: the hat and the watch): either can go on before the other. Carmen names one of them and says whether Luis puts it on first or afterwards (drawn, each round on its own). The frog pushes over the thing that goes on first.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (two on the chair; tests primero against después).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Primero Luis se pone la camisa." (Or, drawn: "Luis se pone la camisa después." Or: "Primero Luis se pone los zapatos." Or: "Luis se pone los zapatos después.")
   - Right: Luis puts it on, and then the other. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis puts the other one on first. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No! Primero la camisa." (She always names the thing that goes first.) She makes him take it off and put both on again in her order: a tuft of his hair stands up (the second time: his shirt tail hangs out). A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the hat and the watch).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Luis se pone el sombrero después." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Abuela lifts her camera. A flash; the photo shows on its screen.
   - Neat: hair flat, shirt in. Stars and a heart.
   - A tuft of hair stands up.
   - A tuft of hair, and his shirt tail out; he grins.
8. A horn outside. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Listo! ¡La guagua! ¡Vámonos!" COQUÍ [TURN: rápido (rápido, rapido)]: "¡Rápido, Luis!" NARR: "Por la mañana Luis se viste: primero la ropa, después la escuela."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** primero 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: ropa 2 before the game (steps 1, 3) and once in the ending; se viste 2 before the game (steps 1, 3) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (primero or después).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both things lie on the chair, and neither has an order of its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): camisa; rápido.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- Words. On the island "gafas" are sunglasses, and glasses to see with are "espejuelos": Luis has no glasses in these stories (he looks for his shirt and his shoes, with his eyes still shut), so the earlier word is not used wrongly. "La luz" is the light, and a floor lamp is "la lámpara": the thing he touches is the wall light's switch, and nobody looks "cerca de la luz" (the third place is the chair). The voice says tú to him: "¿Dónde está el reloj, Coquí?", "Busca tú, Coquí." A boy who snores "duerme"; he does not "tiene sueño". "¡Ya! Me levanto." is "¡Ya me levanto!". Leaving the house: "¡Vámonos!". "Despacio" is slowly, so abuela's hush is "Shh."
- 96: Luis said his things were not in the bed, and a round could then send the frog to the bed. The bare cries "¡La almohada!", "¡Tu almohada!" are sentences. Round B never repeats round A's place.
- 97: a right first round was undone (Luis flopped back to sleep) so that the second could be played on him: the second round is abuelo, asleep in the armchair. The same touch gave opposite pictures by whether it was right: now the bell always wakes and the blanket always gets a sleeper up, and what is wrong is the noise that follows. The last picture said they were all up when a draw could leave Luis sitting in bed: abuela gets everyone up in the ending. The frog's "¡Despacio, Luis!" came after endings in which the baby was already awake or gone: it comes before them. "Son las siete de la mañana" was said twice running, as a line and as an ask.
- 98: a watch does not come off over the head, and a shirt tail cannot come out because of a hat: the cost is that Carmen makes him take it off and dress again in her order, and it is the doing twice that ruffles him. "La camisa, después." was a fragment: "Luis se pone la camisa después." Abuela said "¡Qué bonito!" over a ruffled photo: "¡Listo!". Step 3 repeated step 2.

Left, and why:
- 98: "después" is his already, so a learner can play the rounds on that word and on the names alone; "primero" is taught here and is never the only thing that decides. No line was found that makes it so without giving the answer by the order of the words.
- The plain "duerme" for what is happening now ("está durmiendo" is what is said): one form for A1.
- "Sombrero": the picture is a hat with a brim (a cap is "una gorra").

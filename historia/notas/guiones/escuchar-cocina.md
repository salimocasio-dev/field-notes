# Escuchar for La cocina: first set (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 3, La cocina. The listening set that goes with its twelve stories (24 to 35), built like El parque's (historia/notas/guiones/escuchar-parque.md):

- **Otra vez**: each of the twelve map stories told straight through, nothing to touch. Already works; nothing to write.
- **Cuentos con dibujos**: five new picture stories in the four scenes already drawn. He only watches and hears.
- **Haz esto**: four do-this routines, one for each scene. He does what he is told. Nothing is lost.

Nothing in Escuchar is scored. It only adds minutes listened. No speaking turns: here he listens.

The old site is frozen, so this set is content only: every beat is staged with acts and states the four scenes already have (their sheets: docs/proyecto/kit/). Nothing new is built. A beat that cannot be staged that way is restaged in this script, not coded.

Words he has met by now: all of places 1 and 2, and La cocina's: mesa, plato, taza, pone, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo, cocina, arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace, pollo, huevo, sal, huele, caliente, frío, cuidado, ya, leche, queso, no hay, busca, fruta, guineo, manzana, pan, china, mantequilla, lleno, vacío, quieres, ¿qué quieres?, me gusta, no me gusta, buen provecho, también, rico, todo, café, azúcar, un poco, mucho.
Heard along the way in La cocina: chocolate, nevera, a comer, lista, tiene hambre, tiene sed, huele a, no hay más, ponle, le pone, sí hay, fo, van, ya está, otra, se levanta, estoy, se come.

His rule for listening: "they hear a bunch of words and focus on the words their learning." So a picture story is not held to the lists. Where it uses another word the picture carries it; those are marked **(new)**. The routines stay inside the lists, because there he has to act on what he hears.

A picture story does not retell a map story: each has its own small turn.

Voices as in the stories: the narrator; "X dice:" for what a person says; the frog in his own voice (COQUÍ).

## Cuentos con dibujos

Each runs a little over a minute. Stage directions are in English and are not heard. Every line is said while the thing it tells is in the picture.

### 1. La cuchara de abuelo (La mesa)

The table as in story 25: abuelo, mamá and papá sit; Luis stands with the tray (one spoon, one fork, one knife); abuela at the stove; the frog in front.

1. A thought of food over each of the three at the table. NARR: "La familia tiene hambre." Abuela turns from the stove with a dish. NARR: "Abuela tiene la comida."
2. She sets a bowl of soup down for abuelo. NARR: "Aquí hay sopa para abuelo." **(new: sopa)** She sets a plate of spaghetti down for mamá. NARR: "Aquí hay espaguetis para mamá." **(new: espaguetis)** She sets bread down for papá. NARR: "Y aquí hay pan para papá."
3. Luis goes round fast: the knife to abuelo, the spoon to mamá, the fork to papá. NARR: "Luis pone un cuchillo, una cuchara y un tenedor. Luis va rápido."
4. Abuelo holds the knife up and looks at his soup. NARR: "Abuelo tiene sopa. Y tiene un cuchillo." He shakes his head. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Y mi cuchara?"
5. Mamá eats with the spoon: the spaghetti slides off onto the cloth. NARR: "Mamá tiene la cuchara. ¡Ay! Los espaguetis se caen." Papá pokes his bread with the fork; it skids on the plate. NARR: "Y papá tiene pan y un tenedor."
6. The frog hops up. COQUÍ: "¡Mamá! La cuchara es para abuelo." Mamá passes the spoon along to abuelo and gets his knife. NARR: "Ahora abuelo tiene la cuchara." He eats. NARR: "Abuelo come con la cuchara."
7. Mamá looks at the knife in her hand. COQUÍ: "¡Papá! El tenedor es para mamá." Papá passes the fork along and gets the knife. NARR: "Ahora mamá tiene el tenedor. Y papá tiene el cuchillo."
8. All three eat; papá cuts his bread. NARR: "Mamá come con el tenedor. Papá come pan." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Muy bien, Coquí!" NARR: "La familia come." A heart.

### 2. La comida de Coquí (La mesa)

The kitchen as in story 26: abuelo, mamá and papá at the table with their plates and glasses; abuela at the counter with a pot, plates and glasses; the frog in front, on the floor.

1. NARR: "La familia come en la cocina."
2. Canelo comes in, sits down in front of the counter and whines. NARR: "Canelo entra. Canelo tiene hambre."
3. Abuela sets a plate of food on the floor in front of the frog. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Tu comida."
4. The frog turns to her. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!" Behind him Canelo eats the plate clean. NARR: "¡Ay, no! Canelo se come la comida de Coquí."
5. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Canelo! ¡Esa es la comida de Coquí!" She sets a second plate down. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Más comida."
6. Canelo looks at the plate. The frog hops round to stand between Canelo and the plate. NARR: "Canelo mira la comida. Y Coquí mira a Canelo."
7. The frog eats it all at once. NARR: "¡Ahora Coquí come rápido!" COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Qué rico!"
8. Canelo whines again, his tongue out. Abuela sets a glass of water on the floor. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y agua para Coquí." Canelo drinks it. NARR: "¡Ay! Canelo se bebe el agua de Coquí."
9. The frog looks at the empty glass and laughs. COQUÍ: "¡Canelo tiene sed!" Abuela sets another glass down; the frog drinks; Canelo lies down beside him. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma más agua, Coquí." NARR: "Coquí bebe agua. Y Canelo está contento." A heart.

### 3. El chocolate de Luis (La estufa)

The stove as in story 29: abuela at the pan on the left; Luis on the right with the pot of hot chocolate (steam), the jug of cold chocolate and the clean cups; the frog in front. A hot cup steams; a cold one does not.

1. NARR: "Abuela hace la comida." NARR: "Luis tiene chocolate caliente. Y chocolate frío."
2. Papá comes in at the door. A thought over his head: the cold jug. NARR: "Papá dice: Tengo sed. Quiero chocolate frío, por favor."
3. Luis fills a cup at the hot pot and holds it out, steaming. NARR: "Luis dice: Toma, papá." Papá shakes his head. NARR: "Papá dice: No, Luis. Ese chocolate está caliente." Luis drinks it himself and lays the empty cup on its side. NARR: "Luis se bebe el chocolate caliente."
4. Mamá comes in. A thought over her head: the hot pot. NARR: "Mamá dice: Yo quiero chocolate caliente, por favor." Luis fills a cup at the cold jug and holds it out. She shakes her head. NARR: "Mamá dice: No, Luis. Ese chocolate está frío." Luis drinks that one too. NARR: "Luis se bebe el chocolate frío."
5. Abuela turns round from the pan. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, Luis!" Luis smiles, two empty cups on their sides beside him. NARR: "Luis ya no tiene sed. Luis está contento."
6. The frog hops to the cups. COQUÍ: "¡Ahora yo!" He fills a cup at the jug and carries it to papá. NARR: "Este chocolate frío es para papá." He fills a cup at the pot and carries it to mamá. NARR: "Y este chocolate caliente es para mamá."
7. NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" They drink. NARR: "Papá bebe chocolate. Y mamá también."
8. Luis holds an empty cup out to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y para mí?" Abuela shakes her head. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Luis. ¡No más chocolate!" A heart.

### 4. Comida para el parque (La nevera)

The fridge as in story 31: the tray on the counter under the window, the picnic basket beside it, the drawer of fruit; abuela, Luis and Carmen; the frog in front; the yard wall outside the window.

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos al parque." The picnic basket twinkles. NARR: "Abuela busca comida para el parque."
2. Abuela takes a guineo and a manzana out of the drawer; Luis holds the guineo, Carmen the manzana. NARR: "Aquí hay un guineo y una manzana." NARR: "Abuela dice: La fruta es para el parque."
3. Carmen lays her manzana in the basket. NARR: "Carmen pone la manzana en la canasta." **(canasta: heard in El parque's set)**
4. Luis eats his guineo. NARR: "¿Y Luis? ¡Luis se come el guineo!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Luis! El guineo es para el parque."
5. Abuelo comes to the window from the yard and lays a guineo and a manzana on the sill. NARR: "Abuelo tiene más fruta." He hands the guineo in and abuela lays it in the basket; he bites the manzana. NARR: "Abuela pone el guineo en la canasta. Y abuelo se come la manzana." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, abuelo!"
6. Abuela sets a roll on the tray. NARR: "Aquí hay pan para el parque." A bird lands on the yard wall. The frog hops onto the counter and tosses the roll out to it. NARR: "¡Coquí le tira el pan al pájaro! El pájaro se come el pan."
7. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí! ¡El pan es para el parque!" COQUÍ: "¡El pájaro tiene hambre!"
8. Abuela looks into the basket. NARR: "En la canasta hay una manzana y un guineo." NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay mucha comida."
9. Luis takes the basket; Luis and Carmen go out at the door. NARR: "Luis y Carmen van al parque." The frog waves from his stool. COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, Luis! ¡Adiós, Carmen!" A heart.

### 5. El café de abuela (A comer)

The kitchen table as in story 35: abuelo and papá sit with their cups; Carmen at her place with her cup; the coffee pot, the sugar bowl and the little jug of milk on the table; abuela's own cup at her place; the frog on his stool.

1. Abuela lifts the coffee pot off the stove; steam. NARR: "Abuela hace café." NARR: "¡Mmm! Huele a café."
2. She pours a cup for herself; steam over it. She sits down. NARR: "Este café es para abuela." NARR: "Abuela se sienta."
3. She lifts her cup. Abuelo holds his cup up and tips it towards us: empty. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Más café, por favor." She puts her cup down, gets up and pours for him. NARR: "Abuela le pone más café a abuelo."
4. She sits down and lifts her cup. Papá points at the sugar bowl. NARR: "Papá dice: Más azúcar, por favor." She puts her cup down, gets up and spoons sugar into his cup. NARR: "Abuela le pone azúcar al café."
5. She sits down and lifts her cup. Carmen holds her cup up: empty. NARR: "Carmen dice: Tengo sed. Quiero leche, por favor." Abuela puts her cup down, gets up and pours milk for her. NARR: "Abuela tiene leche para Carmen."
6. Abuela sits down at last. No steam over her cup now. She drinks and makes a face. NARR: "¡Ay! El café de abuela está frío."
7. The frog looks at her cup and hops to the coffee pot. COQUÍ: "¡Espera, abuela!" He brings the pot and pours; steam over her cup again. NARR: "Coquí le pone café caliente a abuela."
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" She drinks. NARR: "Abuela toma café caliente." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡El café está rico!" A heart.

## Haz esto

How a routine works (as in La casa and El parque): a line is said; he touches or does it; the thing happens. There is no sign and nothing twinkles before he touches: what stands in brackets after a line is what happens after the right touch. No praise after each line; one "¡Muy bien!" at the end. Nothing is lost on a wrong touch: "No, esas son las tazas." (what he touched), then the big sign shows the right one. If he waits, the line is said again. Where two touches are fair, both count. Each routine ends with a line in two parts, which can be done in either order. A touch on something that is not one of the answers is only a poke: it is named, and nothing else happens.

In each group of lines, not every answer is asked for, and one may be asked for twice: so the last line of a group is never the only one left.

### A. En la mesa

The table as in story 24: abuelo, mamá (with the baby) and papá sit at the bare table; the plates and the cups stand on the counter; Luis and abuela at the counter; the frog in front.

1. "¿Dónde están los platos?" (One plate is lifted off the pile to be seen.) The cups and the table are the other answers.
2. "Toca las tazas." (One cup is lifted to be seen.) The plates and the table are the other answers. The table is never asked for.
3. "Abuela dice: Un plato para abuelo." He touches the plates. (The frog sets a plate down at abuelo's place.) The cups are the other answer.
4. "Abuela dice: Una taza para mamá." He touches the cups. (The frog sets a cup down at mamá's place.) The plates are the other answer.
5. "Abuela dice: Un plato para papá." He touches the plates. (The frog sets a plate down at papá's place.) The cups are the other answer. A cup is asked for once, a plate twice.
6. "¿Quién tiene una taza?" (Mamá waves.) Abuelo and papá, who have a plate each, are the other answers.
7. Two parts: "Toca al bebé y a papá." (The baby claps; papá waves.) Abuelo is the other answer; a touch on mamá is only a poke. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

Wrong-touch lines: "No. Esa es la mesa." (the line already recorded for El balcón's routine) "No, esos son los platos." "No, esas son las tazas." For people, the lines already recorded.

### B. En la estufa

The stove as in story 27: two pots on the stove, both lit, Luis at the left one and abuela at the right one (the scene's own sides); the sack of rice and the jar of beans on the counter, with the cups; Carmen beside them; the frog in front.

1. "¿Dónde está el arroz?" (The sack twinkles.) The jar of beans and the pots are the other answers.
2. "Toca las habichuelas." (The jar twinkles.) The sack and the pots are the other answers. A pot is not asked for here.
3. "Abuela dice: Dame arroz, por favor." He touches the sack. (The frog tips a cup of rice into abuela's pot.) The jar is the other answer.
4. "Luis dice: Dame habichuelas, por favor." He touches the jar. (The frog tips a cup of beans into Luis's pot.) The sack is the other answer.
5. "Abuela dice: Más arroz, por favor." He touches the sack. (Another cup into abuela's pot.) The jar is the other answer. More beans are never asked for.
6. The flame under Luis's pot goes out, and its steam stops; abuela's pot still steams over its flame. "¿Cuál olla está caliente?" He touches abuela's pot. (Abuela lifts a hand. "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Está caliente.", the line of story 29, said of the pot.) Luis's pot is the other answer.
7. Two parts: "Toca a abuela y a Luis." (Each waves.) Carmen is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

Wrong-touch lines: "No, esa es una olla." "No, ese es el arroz." "No, esas son las habichuelas." "No, esa olla está fría." (line 6).

### C. En la nevera

The fridge as in story 32: the four metal cups on the counter (green, yellow, red, blue), the big jug of juice, the net of chinas, the butter on its plate; the fridge; abuela; the frog in front. From line 5 on, a round picture over the red cup and over the blue cup shows each from above: the red one full, the blue one empty.

1. "¿Dónde están las chinas?" (The net twinkles.) The butter and the jug are the other answers.
2. "Toca la mantequilla." (It twinkles.) The net and the jug are the other answers. The jug is never asked for.
3. "Toca el vaso verde." (It twinkles.) The other three cups are the other answers.
4. "¿Cuál es el vaso amarillo?" (It twinkles.) The same.
5. The two round pictures come on. "¿Cuál vaso está vacío?" (The blue cup twinkles.) The red cup is the other answer.
6. "Abuela dice: Coquí, ponle jugo al vaso vacío." He touches the blue cup again. (The frog tips the jug over it: its round picture shows it full.) The red cup is the other answer. The full cup is never the answer.
7. Two parts: "Toca la nevera y el vaso verde." (The fridge door opens and shuts; the cup twinkles.) The yellow cup is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

Wrong-touch lines: "No, esa es la nevera." "No, esas son las chinas." "No, esa es la mantequilla." "No, ese es el jugo." "No, ese es el vaso verde." "No, ese es el vaso amarillo." "No, ese es el vaso rojo." "No, ese es el vaso azul." "No, ese vaso está lleno." (lines 5 and 6).

### D. A comer

The kitchen table as in story 35: abuelo and papá sit with their cups, both empty; the coffee pot, the sugar bowl and the little jug of milk on the table; abuela beside the table; the frog on his stool with the sugar spoon. A touch on a cup is only a poke.

1. "¿Dónde está el azúcar?" (The sugar bowl twinkles.) The coffee pot and the milk are the other answers.
2. "Toca la leche." (The little jug twinkles.) The coffee pot and the sugar bowl are the other answers.
3. "Papá dice: Más café, por favor." He touches the coffee pot. (Abuela pours a little coffee into papá's cup.) The sugar bowl is the other answer.
4. "Abuelo dice: Más azúcar, por favor." He touches the sugar bowl. (The frog spoons sugar into abuelo's cup.) The coffee pot is the other answer.
5. "Abuelo dice: Más café, por favor." He touches the coffee pot. (Abuela fills abuelo's cup to the brim.) The sugar bowl is the other answer. Milk is never asked for here.
6. Papá drinks; his cup is nearly empty. Abuelo's is full to the brim. "¿Quién tiene mucho café?" (Abuelo lifts his cup.) Papá is the other answer.
7. Two parts: "Toca el café y el azúcar." (The pot steams; the bowl twinkles.) The milk is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

Wrong-touch lines: "No, ese es el café." "No, ese es el azúcar." "No, esa es la leche." For people, the lines already recorded.

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers saw draft 1 with only the owner's rules: one as a beginner, one for Puerto Rican Spanish.

Fixed after the beginner: bare labels are whole sentences now ("Aquí hay sopa para abuelo."); papá's beat in story 1 has its own small trouble; Canelo is seen to be thirsty and ends content beside the frog (story 2); the second mirror beat of story 3 is one step, and "No hay más" (which needed empty pots) is "¡No más chocolate!"; abuelo's two fruit beats are one (story 4); abuelo's and Carmen's cups are shown empty and one repeated line is gone (story 5). Routines: nothing twinkles before the touch; in each opening group one answer is never asked for; A line 5 is a plate for papá and line 6 asks who has a cup; B's cold pot goes out just before its question; C asks for the empty cup twice and never the full one; D's line on "mucho café" has one nearly empty cup against a full one.

Fixed after the Spanish: "Ese chocolate está caliente." (a bare "Está caliente." after "No, Luis." can be heard as said of Luis, which is vulgar); "se come", "se bebe" for finishing one whole thing; "le pone más café a abuelo", "le pone azúcar al café" (a bare "le pone café" hangs); "Toma, Coquí. Más comida." for "Otra vez."; plural lines for the piles of plates and cups; "una olla" where there are two; the frog no longer calls "¡Adiós, pájaro!" ("pájaro" called at somebody is an insult): he says goodbye to the children; "le tira el pan al pájaro"; "Luis ya no tiene sed."; spaghetti is named.

Left as written: abuela's "Un plato para abuelo." and "Una taza para mamá." are her own lines from story 24, already recorded; they are what she says, not labels. "pone" stays his word where a Puerto Rican speaker would say "echa" (café, jugo): told to the owner. "Más arroz" in routine B leans on which cook has the rice; small. Two of the reviewer's notes were mistaken: "pájaro" and "tira" are words of place 2.

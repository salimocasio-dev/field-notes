# El balcón: two stories (draft 4, after four outside reviews)

Place 1, La casa. Scene 2 of 4. The learner is the little frog (the coquí). Abuela is his grandmother.
He has already played three stories at the front of the house (La lluvia, El bizcocho, ¿Dónde está el pájaro?).
Words he has met so far: casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós.
Heard along the way so far: mira, puedes, dónde está, está cerrada, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, tiene, toma, gracias, bien, cómo estás, entra, pájaro, bizcocho, palma, lluvia, tú, o.

Scene words for El balcón (from the 40-scene list): jugo, agua, vaso, silla, toma, gracias, de nada, por favor, quiero, ¿cómo estás?, bien, se sienta.

The picture: abuela's porch, seen from the yard. A teal wall with a window in the middle and a door on the right. A big plain wooden chair (abuela's; no rockers) on the left, a small round table in the middle, a small yellow chair on the right. A hot, sunny day.

Voices: the narrator (a woman) speaks to the learner. Abuela's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with "Abuela dice:"; her bubble shows. The frog has his own voice (marked COQUÍ). The bird only chirps.

The signal for a right move is the same as in stories 1 to 3: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!". It plays after every right answer below, also where it is not written out.

Poking is free, as always: anything he touches outside an answer is named.

## Story 4: El jugo

- **Goal he can see:** a cold drink on a hot day. The frog is sweating (three drops). A bird keeps getting to the drinks first.
- **At stake:** three drinks, one a round. Each round abuela fills two glasses: one is his, one is hers, and she says which is his. Take his and he drinks it. Take hers, and she takes it back and the bird drinks his. Two scores show in the top corners: glasses the frog drank, glasses the bird drank. One drop of sweat goes away with every glass he drinks.
- **New words:** silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma.
- **Back again:** abuela, hola, grande, pequeña (here "pequeño", with vaso), entra, no.
- **Heard along the way:** hace calor, qué calor, ven, balcón, dos, un, y, de, pone, mesa, gracias, de nada.

**Start:** the frog is in the yard, sweating. Abuela stands at the open door. Her big chair is empty.

1. NARR: "Hace calor." (A sun in the frog's bubble.)
2. COQUÍ: "¡Ay, qué calor!"
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hola! Ven al balcón." The frog hops up the steps onto the porch.

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

4. NARR: "Esta es la silla de abuela." Then: "¿Puedes tocar la silla de abuela?"
   - He touches the empty big chair. Right signal. Abuela walks over and sits down in it. NARR: "Abuela se sienta en la silla."
5. NARR: "Mira. Esta silla es pequeña." Then: "¿Dónde está la silla pequeña?"
   - He touches the small chair. Right signal. The frog hops up and sits on it.
6. Abuela gets up and goes in through the door. NARR: "Abuela entra a la casa." She comes back with two glasses. NARR: "Mira. Abuela tiene dos vasos." She puts them on the table. NARR: "Abuela pone los vasos en la mesa. Un vaso de jugo. Y un vaso de agua." (Each glass twinkles as it is named: orange with a straw, clear blue with ice.) She goes back to her chair. NARR: "Abuela se sienta."
7. NARR: "¿Puedes tocar el vaso de agua?"
   - He touches the water. Right signal. NARR: "El agua está en la mesa."
8. NARR: "¿Y el jugo? ¿Dónde está el jugo?"
   - He touches the juice. Right signal. The frog hops to the table and reaches for it.

**The turn**

9. A bird swoops in, lands by the juice and drinks it all. NARR: "¡Mira! Un pájaro. El pájaro toma el jugo." COQUÍ: "¡No, pájaro, no!"
10. The bird flies to the frog's small chair and plops down on the seat, like a person. NARR: "El pájaro se sienta en la silla pequeña."
11. The signs go away. The two scores appear, both empty (the stolen juice does not count). The picture moves in closer. The bird flies up to the window sill and watches from there.

**The game** (no sign; one guess a round; a touch on a glass before abuela finishes her line does not count)

Abuela stays in her chair. Each round two fresh glasses pop into place with a pouring sound, and she holds out her hand as she offers. When she takes hers, it pops into her hand. Her picture bubble stays empty, so the picture never says which glass. Where each glass stands is drawn fresh.

12. **Round 1, Find (jugo or agua).** Two glasses the same size on the table, juice and water. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma el agua." (Or "Toma el jugo." Drawn fresh.)
   - Right: the frog picks it up. Right signal. He drinks it; a drop of sweat goes. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!" One glass on his score. Abuela takes the other glass and drinks it. NARR: "Abuela toma el jugo."
   - Wrong: the frog picks up the other glass. Abuela shakes her head (a red X in her bubble). NARR: "No. Eso es jugo." She takes her glass back and drinks it. The bird flies down to his glass and drinks it. NARR: "El pájaro toma el agua." One glass on the bird's score. The bird goes back to the window.
13. **Round 2, Find (silla or mesa).** Two glasses of water, the same in every way. One stands on the small chair, one on the table. NARR: "Un vaso en la silla. Y un vaso en la mesa." (Each twinkles.) NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma el vaso de la silla." (Or "el vaso de la mesa". Drawn fresh.)
   - Right: as round 1. "Abuela toma el vaso de la mesa."
   - Wrong: "No. Ese es el vaso de la mesa." She takes it back. "El pájaro toma el vaso de la silla."
14. **Round 3, Find (grande or pequeño).** Two glasses of juice on the table, one big, one small. NARR: "Un vaso grande. Y un vaso pequeño." (Each twinkles.) NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma el vaso pequeño." (Or "el vaso grande". Drawn fresh.)
   - Right and wrong as before. "Abuela toma el vaso grande." / "No. Ese es el vaso grande." "El pájaro toma el vaso pequeño."

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

15. The two scores go away. Two last glasses of water. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma." The frog drinks one; one more drop of sweat goes, if any is left (no glasses in the game ends with two drops, one glass with one, two or three with none). The bird drinks the other. NARR: "El pájaro toma el agua."
16. By the scores:
   - Three for the frog: stars and a heart. The frog hops back onto his small chair.
   - Two: a heart. The frog gets his chair.
   - One or none: the bird takes the frog's chair, round and pleased. The frog stays on the floor and shrugs.
   - If he fed the bird in El bizcocho and the bird won, the bird gives the chair back to him.
17. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!" NARR: "Abuela dice: De nada."

**What things are called when he pokes them:** "Es la silla de abuela." "Es la silla pequeña." "Es la mesa." "Es el jugo." "Es el agua." "Es el vaso de la silla." "Es el vaso de la mesa." "Es el vaso grande." "Es el vaso pequeño." "Esta es abuela." "Es un pájaro." "Es la puerta." "Es la ventana." "Es el balcón." The frog: "¡Coquí!"

**Fewest hearings of each new word on a run with no mistakes:** silla 8, se sienta 3, vaso 13, jugo 5, agua 5, toma 9 (five as "drinks", three as abuela's offer, one as "here you go").

## Story 5: ¡Qué calor!

- **Goal he can see:** help abuela cool down. She sits in her chair, sweating, fanning herself with a paper fan. A thermometer shows in the corner.
- **At stake:** the heat. The thermometer has five marks and starts on the third. A drink for abuela brings it down a mark. Every wrong move makes her wait: she looks puzzled, the sun flares, and the thermometer climbs a mark.
- **New words:** quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien.
- **Back again:** agua, jugo, toma, no.
- **Heard along the way:** qué calor, y tú, muy, o.

**Start:** abuela sits in her big chair with a paper fan. On the table: a jug of juice (orange) and a jug of water (clear blue, with ice). The frog hops up onto the porch. He is not sweating.

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?" (Her bubble: happy face, question mark, sad face.)
2. COQUÍ: "Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú, cómo estás?" (His bubble: a happy face.)
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, qué calor!" (A sun in her bubble.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Quiero agua, por favor." (A glass of water in her bubble.) The frog, eager, hops to the table and holds up the juice jug. COQUÍ: "¿Jugo?" NARR: "Abuela dice: No. No quiero jugo. Quiero agua, por favor." (Her bubble: juice with a red X, then water.) He puts it down.
5. NARR: "¿Puedes tocar el agua?"
   - He touches the water jug. Right signal. The frog carries the jug to her and fills her glass. COQUÍ: "Toma, abuela." She drinks. The thermometer comes down a mark. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias."
6. **The speaking bubbles, taught.** Two bubbles appear over the frog. They hold no words. Each one says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other, and wiggles only while it speaks: "De nada." ... "Por favor." The big sign is on "De nada". He touches it. COQUÍ: "De nada." Right signal.

**The turn**

7. Her paper fan tears in two. The halves fall to the floor. She looks at her empty hand. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, qué calor!" The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). The two Say decisions come in an order drawn fresh; the drink decision is always last.

8. **Say decision A: she thanks him.** The frog picks up the two halves of the fan and gives them to her. She fans herself with one half; it stays with her. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias." Two speaking bubbles: "De nada." and one other, drawn fresh from "Por favor." and "Gracias." Which side each is on, and so which speaks first, is drawn fresh. A touch while they are speaking does nothing.
   - Right: COQUÍ: "De nada." Right signal. She hands him the other half of the fan. He keeps it and fans himself to the end.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other line. A question mark over abuela; she waits; the sun flares; the thermometer climbs a mark. The sign shows the right bubble. It speaks again. He touches it. COQUÍ: "De nada." The other half of the fan is left on the table.
9. **Say, no wrong answer** (always right before decision B). Two picture bubbles over the frog: a glass of water, a glass of juice. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Quieres agua o jugo?"
   - Juice: COQUÍ: "Quiero jugo, por favor." Water: COQUÍ: "Quiero agua, por favor."
   - Abuela fills a glass with his pick and holds it out.
10. **Say decision B: she hands him his drink.** NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma." Two speaking bubbles: "Gracias." and one other, drawn fresh from "De nada." and "Por favor." Sides are drawn fresh, as in A.
   - Right: COQUÍ: "Gracias." Right signal. He gets the glass. NARR: "Abuela dice: De nada."
   - Wrong: the frog says the other line. She keeps holding the glass, puzzled; the sun flares; the thermometer climbs a mark. The sign shows the right bubble; it speaks; he touches it. COQUÍ: "Gracias." He gets the glass. NARR: "Abuela dice: De nada."
   - Then the two picks end differently. Juice: he drinks it. COQUÍ: "¡Mmm!" Water: he drinks it and squirts a little fountain. COQUÍ: "¡Ah, qué bien!"
   - His pick carries into the first story in La sala.
11. **Do decision, last (quiero or no quiero).** Both drinks are named, and which is named first is drawn fresh. NARR: "Abuela dice: Quiero jugo, por favor. No quiero agua." (Or "No quiero agua. Quiero jugo, por favor." Or the same two with the drinks swapped.) Her bubble stays empty.
   - Right: the frog carries that jug over and fills her glass. Right signal. COQUÍ: "Toma, abuela." She drinks. The thermometer comes down a mark.
   - Wrong: he fills her glass from the other jug. She does not drink it. NARR: "No. Eso es agua." She puts that glass on the table, where it stays, and holds out a fresh empty one. The thermometer climbs a mark. The sign shows the right jug. He fills the fresh glass from it, and the right side plays.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. COQUÍ: "¿Cómo estás, abuela?"
13. By the thermometer:
   - At the bottom (no wrong moves): abuela leans back, cool and smiling. Stars and a heart.
   - One mark up: she smiles and fans herself with the half fan.
   - Two marks up: she fans hard, one drop of sweat left, and smiles.
   - Three marks up (three wrong): she tips the water jug over her own head, and laughs. It is funny. It is not a fail screen.
14. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Bien, muy bien! ¿Y tú?" COQUÍ: "¡Bien!"

**What things are called when he pokes them:** "Es el agua." "Es el jugo." (the jugs) "Es la mesa." "Es la silla de abuela." "Es la silla pequeña." "Esta es abuela." "Es la puerta." "Es la ventana." "Es el balcón." The frog: "¡Coquí!"

**Fewest hearings of each new word on a run with no mistakes:** quiero 6 (two of them "no quiero"), por favor 5, gracias 5, de nada 5, cómo estás 3, bien 3 (not counting "¡Muy bien!").

**New in this story:** the speaking bubbles of the Say move. Sí and no could be drawn. "Gracias", "de nada" and "por favor" cannot, so each bubble says its line aloud and he picks by ear. The bubbles show no words. They are taught once with the big sign before they can cost anything. If he waits, abuela's line and both bubbles play again, free. Touching the frog plays the bubbles again too.

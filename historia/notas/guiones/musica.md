# La música: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 10, El fuerte. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `musica`). Stories 111 to 113. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 110.

Scene words (page 02): música, canción, baila, canta, guitarra, tambor, ríe, feliz, divertido, ¿quieres bailar?, claro, ¡qué bueno!
How they are shared out: 111 música, canción, guitarra, tambor; 112 baila, canta, ¿quieres bailar?, claro; 113 se ríe (the plan's ríe: people say "se ríe"), feliz, divertido, ¡qué bueno!

The picture: the fort's courtyard at night, with the party. One view. At the back, the fort's inner wall with lanterns on it. In front of the wall, at the left, a low stage of boards: two stools, and at its right end a rack that holds the instruments; on the floor beside the stage, a microphone on a stand. In the middle and at the right, the open floor where people dance. At the right, a bench against the wall, and a stool beside the floor. No roof.

People: the two músicos (one with a hat, one with a beard); the family; the people of every place.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign. While he waits, something always moves: the lanterns' flames, a foot that taps, a head that nods.

## Story 111: La música

- **Goal he can see:** on the open floor, three couples wait to dance; they tap their feet and look at the stage.
- **At stake:** the dancers (3 couples). The wrong instrument in a músico's hands makes a sour noise: a couple covers its ears, and walks off to the bench.
- **New words:** música, canción, guitarra, tambor.
- **Back again:** fiesta, noche, dame, para mí, quiere, otro, me gusta, mucho, bonito, ahora, yo, mira, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night; lanterns. On the stage the two músicos sit on their stools with empty hands; the instruments (a guitar, a barrel drum that is played with the hands) hang on their stand. Three couples on the open floor. The frog on the stage's edge. Abuela beside the stage.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, en una fiesta hay música. Mira." (signed, the stage.) He touches it: the músico with the hat waves. NARR: "El músico dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Quieres música?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!"
2. NARR: "El músico dice: Dame la guitarra, por favor." (signed, the guitar.) He touches it: the frog pushes the guitar off its stand into his hands; he strums: small notes float up; the couples sway. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bonita la guitarra!"
3. The músico with the beard taps his knees. NARR: "El otro músico dice: Y para mí, el tambor." (signed, the drum.) He touches it: the frog pushes the drum over; he beats it with his hands: bigger notes; the couples step. NARR: "Abuela dice: Me gusta el tambor."
4. They play together, and stop. NARR: "El músico dice: Esa es una canción. ¿Te gusta la canción?" COQUÍ [TURN: mucho (mucho, sí mucho)]: "¡Sí, mucho!" The two change over: each holds out his instrument to the other. NARR: "El otro músico dice: Ahora la guitarra, para mí." NARR: "El músico dice: Y yo, el tambor." They play again, the other way round. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bonita la música!" They hang the two instruments back on the stand.

**The turn**

5. The músicos sit with their hands open. NARR: "El músico dice: Ahora, otra canción." The three couples stand ready, and tap their feet; the músicos nod to the beat. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Before each song both instruments hang on the stand and both músicos have empty hands. The two músicos shut their eyes to think of the song, and one of them asks for the instrument it begins with: in round A the músico with the hat, in round B the other (his bubble says "El otro músico"). The instrument is drawn, each round on its own: either may ask for either. The frog pushes the guitar or the drum into the hands of the one who asked. After each round the instrument goes back on the stand.

6. **Decision A, touch an instrument (the guitar, the drum; tests guitarra against tambor).** NARR: "El músico dice: Para esta canción, el tambor." (Or, drawn: "Para esta canción, la guitarra.")
   - Right: he plays; notes float up; the couples dance. A star. He hangs it back.
   - Wrong: he plays what he gets, his eyes shut: the guitar is beaten like a drum (or the drum is strummed): a sour noise, in jagged notes. A couple covers its ears and walks off to the bench. NARR: "El músico dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡El tambor!" He hangs the wrong one back. A cross. The sign is on the right instrument; he touches it: the frog pushes it over; he plays a few notes, and hangs it back.
7. **Decision B, touch an instrument (the same game; the other músico; drawn again).** NARR: "El otro músico dice: Para esta canción, la guitarra." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The song goes on; the couples that are left dance.
   - Three couples. Stars and a heart.
   - Two couples; one watches from the bench.
   - One couple; two watch from the bench, and tap their feet.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bonita canción! Coquí, ¿quieres otra?" COQUÍ [TURN: otro (otra, otra por favor, otro)]: "¡Otra, por favor!" NARR: "En la fiesta hay música: una guitarra, un tambor y una canción."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** guitarra 3 (steps 2, 4), tambor 3 (steps 3, 4); each time the instrument is the one in somebody's hands, or going into them. Not deciding: música 3 (steps 1, 4), canción 2 before the game (steps 4, 5), and in every round's line.
**A miss is filed under:** the instrument that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 4, 9): sí; mucho; otro.

## Story 112: ¿Quieres bailar?

- **Goal he can see:** over the stage hang three big paper notes, not yet lit: the long song's three parts. The músicos point up at them.
- **At stake:** the song's parts (3 notes, lit when the long song begins). Someone at the wrong place trips on the microphone's cable, or sings where nobody hears: the music stops for a moment, and a note goes dark.
- **New words:** baila, canta, ¿quieres bailar?, claro.
- **Back again:** música, canción, guitarra, quiero, también, cansado, ahora, yo, tú, por favor, gracias, sí, no.

**Start:** night. The músicos play softly. The microphone stands at the stage's front. Over the stage, three paper notes, dark. On the open floor, nobody yet. Abuelo, abuela, Luis and Carmen stand at the right. The frog between the stage and the floor.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuelo bows to abuela. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Quieres bailar?" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Claro!" (signed, the open floor.) He touches it: the frog leads them onto the floor; they dance. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira, Coquí. Abuela baila con abuelo."
2. Carmen runs to the microphone. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Yo canto!" **(canto: a form of canta)** NARR: "El músico dice: ¡Claro, Carmen!" (signed, the microphone.) He touches it: the frog tips it down to her height; she sings: notes. NARR: "Luis dice: Carmen canta la canción." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bonito canta Carmen!"
3. Carmen calls from the microphone. NARR: "Carmen dice: Luis, ¿quieres bailar?" Luis shakes his head, and looks at his feet. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Claro que sí! Tú bailas y yo canto." **(bailas: a form of baila)** She pulls him onto the floor; he dances, stiff, then well.
4. Abuelo comes off the floor to the microphone; the frog tips it up to his height; he sings, and Carmen dances with abuela. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Abuelo canta y Carmen baila! Coquí, ¿quieres bailar?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" He hops in a circle. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí baila!"

**The turn**

5. The song ends; everybody comes off the floor, and abuelo comes away from the microphone; it stands upright again. The three paper notes over the stage light up, one after another: the long song begins. Abuelo, Luis and Carmen stand at the right, ready. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The músico with the hat, his eyes on his guitar, says what one of the people does now (the person is drawn from abuelo, Luis and Carmen; what he does is drawn on its own: any of them may dance, any of them may sing; the opening has shown abuelo and Carmen do both). The frog leads that one to the open floor, or to the microphone. After each round he walks back to the right, and the microphone stands upright.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the open floor, the microphone; tests baila against canta).** NARR: "El músico dice: Ahora el abuelo canta." (Or, drawn: "Ahora el abuelo baila." "Ahora Carmen canta." "Ahora Carmen baila." "Ahora Luis canta." "Ahora Luis baila.")
   - Right: he sings into the microphone (notes), or dances on the floor. A star.
   - Wrong: he dances at the microphone, and his foot catches its cable: the music stops. (Or he sings on the floor, far from the microphone: nobody hears, and the músicos stop to listen.) NARR: "El músico dice: ¡No, Coquí! El abuelo canta." One of the paper notes goes dark. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog leads him across.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; another person).** NARR: "El músico dice: Ahora Carmen baila." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The song comes to its end.
   - Three notes lit: everybody dances, and the one at the microphone sings. Stars and a heart.
   - Two notes lit.
   - One note lit: a short song.
9. Abuelo sits down on the bench, and fans himself with his hat. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Ay! Estoy cansado. ¿Y tú, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: yo (yo no, no)]: "¡Yo no!" The frog hops onto the stage and sings into the microphone: "¡Coquí! ¡Coquí!" NARR: "En la fiesta, la gente baila y canta. Coquí canta también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** baila 3 and bailas once (steps 1, 3, 4), canta 3 and canto twice (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: ¿quieres bailar? 3 (steps 1, 3, 4); claro 3 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**Forms:** bailas with baila; canto with canta.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (baila or canta).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: who is named does not say where he goes.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): quiero; yo.

## Story 113: ¡Qué bueno!

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's camera makes paper photos. Three spare sheets of photo paper stick out of her pocket; she taps them, and hangs the camera round the frog's neck.
- **At stake:** the spare sheets (3). A photo of the wrong person is a sheet wasted: abuela has to load a spare one.
- **New words:** se ríe, feliz, divertido, ¡qué bueno!
- **Back again:** baila, canta, come, ¿quién?, fiesta, quiero, puedes, puedo, claro, ¿quieres bailar?, mira, también, mucho, ahora, tú, sí, no.

**Start:** night; the party is at its height. Luis and Carmen beside abuela, at the floor's edge. Abuela with her camera, and the frog.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Luis tells Carmen something in her ear; Carmen laughs, bent double. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: Carmen se ríe." (signed, Carmen.) He touches her: she laughs harder. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen se ríe mucho. ¡Está feliz!"
2. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Qué divertido!" He laughs too. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se ríe también. La fiesta está divertida." Luis wipes his eyes, and goes off to the bench to eat. Carmen is still laughing.
3. Abuela lifts her camera. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién se ríe? Quiero una foto." (signed, Carmen.) He touches her: a flash; a paper photo slides out of the camera: Carmen, laughing. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bueno! Mira la foto: Carmen se ríe."
4. Abuela hangs the camera round the frog's neck. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora tú, Coquí. ¿Puedes?" COQUÍ [TURN: puedo (puedo, sí puedo)]: "¡Sí, puedo!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bueno! Coquí está feliz."

**The turn**

5. Abuela sits down on the stool beside the floor with an album open on her knees; the three spare sheets stick out of her pocket. Round the floor, five people (drawn from the family and the people of every place), each doing one thing: one laughs (bent, a hand on his stomach), one sings at the microphone, one yawns, one dances, one eats on the bench. Three of the five have their mouths wide open. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, her eyes on her album, asks who does one of four things, for a photo (drawn; round B's is not round A's; one of the two rounds, drawn, is "se ríe"; the yawn is never asked for). The five people are in the picture, each doing his thing; between the rounds they change places and things. The frog touches one: a flash.

6. **Decision A, touch a person (five; tests se ríe among canta, baila and come).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién se ríe? Quiero una foto." (Or, drawn: "¿Quién canta? Quiero una foto." "¿Quién baila? Quiero una foto." "¿Quién come? Quiero una foto.")
   - Right: a flash; the photo slides out; abuela puts it in her album. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bueno! ¡Qué divertido!" A star.
   - Wrong: a flash; the photo slides out; abuela looks at it. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. ¿Quién se ríe?" The photo goes under her stool; she takes a spare sheet from her pocket and loads the camera. A cross. The sign is on the right person; he touches him: a flash, and the photo goes into the album.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game; the five have changed places and things).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quién baila? Quiero una foto." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela takes the camera back, and uses the spare sheets that are left.
   - Three sheets: three more photos: the músicos, everybody on the floor, and the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two sheets: everybody on the floor, and the frog.
   - One sheet: the frog.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿quieres bailar?" COQUÍ [TURN: claro (claro, claro que sí)]: "¡Claro!" They dance. NARR: "La fiesta está divertida. Todos se ríen. Coquí está feliz. ¡Qué bueno!" **(todos, se ríen: heard)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se ríe 5 (steps 1, 2, 3). Not deciding: feliz 2 (steps 1, 4) and once in the ending; divertido once and divertida once (step 2), divertido in each right round, divertida in the ending; ¡qué bueno! 2 (steps 3, 4), in each right round, and in the ending.
**Forms:** divertida with divertido; se ríen with se ríe.
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for (se ríe, canta, baila or come).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in five, each round on its own. In the round that asks who laughs, the three known things rule out three people; two are left with their mouths open, the one who laughs and the one who yawns.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): puedo; claro.

## Reviews (draft 1 to draft 2)

A beginner's reviewer and a Puerto Rican Spanish reviewer read draft 1; each had only the script and the owner's rules.

- 111: before every round both instruments hang on the stand and both pairs of hands are empty; after a miss the wrong one goes back first. Each instrument's three hearings come while it is in somebody's hands, not in a list. The músicos change over in the opening, so either may ask for either; who asks is fixed by round and named in his bubble. "Y para mí, el tambor", not "toco" (he knows toca as "touch"). "Dame la guitarra, por favor." The drum is a barrel drum played with the hands; "tambor" stays (the island's other words each name one drum).
- 112: the paper notes are dark until the long song begins. After each round the person walks back and the microphone stands upright; abuelo sings once in the opening, so a name does not say who sings. The músico says "el abuelo". "Tú bailas y yo canto." The last turn is not an echo, and not 111's.
- 113: the word is "se ríe" in every line (nobody on the island says "ríe" alone). The ask is "¿Quién se ríe? Quiero una foto.": no "alguien que", and it reuses ¿quién? The stake is abuela's three spare sheets: a right photo costs nothing, a wrong one costs a sheet, and the ending uses what is left. Five people, three with their mouths open, and a yawn that is never asked for, so the laugher is not found by ruling out the others. Luis stops laughing before the first ask. "La fiesta está divertida." The turn in step 4 is not an echo.
- Not taken: both rounds asking who laughs (the picture would then answer it: he would only look for the laugher); "¡Qué chévere!" (kept as a word for later; divertido is the plan's word and is used everywhere).

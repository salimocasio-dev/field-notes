# La nevera: three stories (draft 2, after one round of reviews)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 3 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa (stories 1 to 11), El parque (12 to 23) and two scenes of La cocina: La mesa (24 to 26) and La estufa (27 to 29).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, otra, su, luna, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.
Words he has met (place 3, La mesa): mesa, plato, taza, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, cocina, pone, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo. Heard there: chocolate, lista, a comer, tiene hambre, tiene sed, tienes, esa.
Words he has met (place 3, La estufa): arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace, pollo, huevo, sal, huele, caliente, frío, cuidado, ya (as "ya está"). Heard there: listas, huele a, no hay más, ponle.

Scene words for La nevera (from the 40-scene list): leche, queso, pan, mantequilla, fruta, guineo, manzana, china, no hay, lleno, vacío, busca.

The picture: abuela's fridge stands at the left. It can be open towards us (we see in: two shelves, and a drawer with a white front that hides what is in it), or shut; when abuela opens it a little and looks in, only she sees in. To its right a counter with a sink at its left end. Over the counter a wide window to the yard, with a sill things can stand on; whoever stands in the yard is seen in it from the waist up, and the top of the yard wall shows behind. The frog stands on a stool between the fridge and the counter. People in the kitchen stand on the floor in front of the counter, lower than its top, so nobody hides what is on it.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La mesa and La estufa. In short: a wrong move costs something in the picture that stays; then "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true at that moment; then the big sign shows the right one and he does it, with no star. Nobody points at or looks toward the answer in a decision. The sides of answer bubbles are drawn fresh every time.
A poke names people, the fridge and the kitchen only. It never names milk, cheese, bread, fruit, a cup or a coin.

The three games open differently: 30 with something he says, 31 with a touch on a thing, 32 with a touch on a thing that stands for a person, and their rounds differ: 30 is say, picture bubbles, say; 31 is touch, picture bubbles, say; 32 is touch (two lines), touch (one line), carry to the sink (one line). The three losses differ: in 30 the frog's own piragua money is spent on what was already there, in 31 good food is thrown to the birds, in 32 juice runs over or goes down the sink. The three turns differ: the piragua man's bell; abuelo with his hat on; the last chinas.

## Story 30: No hay leche

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** the open fridge holds one bottle of milk and one wedge of cheese; the family eats bread with cheese and drinks milk. On the sill: a tray with nine coins in nine dents (abuela's shopping money), and the frog's own clear jar, a frog and a piragua drawn on it, with three coins in it. Keep the fridge stocked, and keep the three coins for a piragua.
- **At stake:** the frog's three coins. Everything papá fetches from the colmado costs one coin. Abuela's tray pays for one errand a round. If the frog sends papá for the wrong thing, the second errand is paid from the frog's jar.
- **New words:** leche, queso, no hay, busca.
- **Back again:** hay, sí, aquí, ahora, mira, come, toma, bebé, pan (heard before), con, espera, piragua, tira, coge, gracias.
- **Heard along the way:** nevera.

**Start:** the fridge is open: a bottle of milk in its holder, a wedge of cheese on its blue dish. Abuela stands at the counter with a loaf. At the right of the kitchen, by the window: Luis, Carmen, and mamá with the baby and its bottle. Papá stands in the yard at the window. On the sill: the tray of nine coins, the frog's jar with three coins. By the window hang two cards, one with a bottle of milk on it, one with a wedge of cheese.

1. NARR: "Esta es la nevera." (the fridge twinkles) NARR: "Aquí hay leche." (the bottle twinkles) NARR: "Y aquí hay queso." (the cheese twinkles) Mamá fills the baby's bottle from the milk and gives papá the empty one; abuela lays the cheese on slices of bread for Luis and Carmen; during it: NARR: "El bebé toma leche." NARR: "Luis come pan con queso." The milk holder in the fridge is empty; the cheese dish still has a piece.

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **He says it, taught.** Abuela goes to the sink and turns the tap on, her back to the fridge. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera. ¿Hay leche? ¿Hay queso?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Papá, no hay leche!" / "¡Papá, no hay queso!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡Papá, no hay leche!" The frog gives papá a coin from the tray; papá goes off along the wall and is back with a bottle; abuela stands it in the fridge. During it: NARR: "Papá busca la leche." NARR: "Ahora hay leche."
3. Abuela lays the last cheese on a slice for Carmen; the dish in the fridge is empty. NARR: "Carmen come pan con queso." Abuela is at the sink again. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera. ¿Hay leche? ¿Hay queso?" Sign on "¡Papá, no hay queso!" COQUÍ: "¡Papá, no hay queso!" A coin from the tray; papá goes and is back. NARR: "Papá busca el queso." NARR: "Ahora hay queso."
4. **Picture bubbles, taught.** Abuela shuts the fridge. The family goes on eating and drinking. Later abuela opens the door a little and looks in; we cannot see in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay queso. No hay leche." She goes to the sink. Two picture bubbles: the milk card, the cheese card. Sign on the milk card. He touches it; the frog holds the card up at the window and gives papá a coin from the tray. NARR: "Papá busca la leche."
5. Later: NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay queso. Sí hay leche." Sign on the cheese card. He touches it. A coin from the tray. NARR: "Papá busca el queso."
6. **One line only, taught.** Abuela looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche." She goes to the sink. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Papá, busca leche!" / "¡Papá, busca queso!" Sign on the second. COQUÍ: "¡Papá, busca queso!" A coin from the tray; papá goes and is back with a wedge. NARR: "Papá busca el queso."
7. Abuela looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay leche." Sign on "¡Papá, busca leche!" COQUÍ: "¡Papá, busca leche!" A coin from the tray; papá goes and is back with a bottle. NARR: "Papá busca la leche." Three coins are left in the tray.

**The turn**

8. A bell. The piragua man's cart stops at the yard wall, at the far end of the window. COQUÍ: "¡Una piragua!" The frog hops to his jar. NARR: "Abuela dice: Espera, Coquí." Papá walks over to the cart and stands there talking with the piragua man, too far to hear abuela. The family goes on eating bread with cheese and drinking milk. The card shows. The signs go.

**The game.** In every round one of the two has run out (drawn fresh). The family eats both all the time, so nothing outside the fridge says which. Abuela speaks into the fridge and then stands at the sink with the tap running and her back turned: she does not hear what the frog calls. Papá hears only the frog, who is at the window.

9. **Decision A, Say (he sees in).** Abuela opens the fridge wide and goes to the sink. The milk holder is empty, or the cheese dish is (drawn). NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera. ¿Hay leche? ¿Hay queso?" Bubbles: "¡Papá, no hay leche!" / "¡Papá, no hay queso!"
   - Right: the frog tosses papá a coin from the tray; papá goes and is back with it; abuela dries her hands and puts it in the fridge. During it: NARR: "Papá busca la leche." (Or: "…el queso.") A star.
   - Wrong: the frog tosses papá a coin from the tray; papá is back with what the frog called and hands it in at the window to Luis and Carmen, who finish it at once; the empty bottle, or the rind, lies on the counter. Abuela turns round and looks into the fridge. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay leche." (Or: "No hay queso.") The sign is on the right bubble; he says it; the frog takes a coin out of his own jar and tosses it; papá goes again.
10. **Decision B, Picture bubbles (he hears both).** The fridge is shut. Abuela opens it a little and looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche. No hay queso." (Which one has run out, and which half she says first, drawn.) She goes to the sink. Picture bubbles: the milk card, the cheese card. The frog holds the card up and tosses the coin.
   - Right and wrong as in A. After a wrong one abuela opens the fridge wide to us: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay queso." (Or: "No hay leche.")
11. **Decision C, Say (one line).** Abuela opens the fridge a little and looks in. She says one line of four (drawn): NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche." / "No hay leche." / "Sí hay queso." / "No hay queso." She goes to the sink. Bubbles: "¡Papá, busca leche!" / "¡Papá, busca queso!"
   - Right and wrong as in A. After a wrong one abuela opens the fridge wide to us: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay queso." (Or: "No hay leche.")

**Count:** the tray has nine coins: six pay for the opening, three for the first errand of rounds A, B and C; it is always empty at the end. The jar has three coins: each miss takes one for the second errand; three, two, one or none are left. A redo always has its coin. Milk and cheese at the colmado do not run out. What a wrong errand brought is finished at once, so it never stocks the fridge for a later round.

**The ending.** NARR: "Ahora hay leche y queso." The frog tosses papá the coins left in his jar; papá buys a piragua from the cart and hands it in: one coin, one scoop; two coins, two scoops; three coins, three scoops, red, yellow and blue.
- Nothing wrong (three stars): a piragua three scoops high. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, papá!" Stars and a heart.
- One or two misses: a smaller piragua; the empty bottles or rinds lie on the counter. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, papá!"
- Three misses: the jar is empty. NARR: "No hay piragua para Coquí." The frog looks at the cart.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost** (the frog's own line counts once; a bubble's voice is not counted): leche 13 (step 1 twice; step 2 four times; steps 3, 5 and 6 once each; steps 4 and 7 twice and three times), queso 12, no hay 5 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5, 7), busca 8 (steps 2 to 7). "Sí hay" against "No hay" side by side: steps 4 and 5; alone: steps 6 and 7.
**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that had run out. B and C: no hay.
**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two: saying back the word abuela said is right for "No hay…" and wrong for "Sí hay…".

## Story 31: Para los pájaros

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** an open picnic basket on the counter, with three hollows in it. Three bread rolls in a basket on top of the fridge; three plates of fruit on the shelves of the open fridge. Two birds sit on the yard wall. Fill the picnic basket with good things for the park.
- **At stake:** the picnic. After the turn there are three lots of two things; in each lot one thing is old and one is good. Abuela smells them and says which is old; the old one is for the birds. A good thing pushed out of the window is eaten by the birds at once, and its hollow in the basket stays empty.
- **New words:** fruta, guineo, manzana, pan.
- **Back again:** huele, bien, tira, busca, pájaro, come, hay, aquí, vamos, parque, para, comida, este, esta, mira, adiós.
- **Heard along the way:** fo, van.

**Start:** the fridge is open: three plates of fruit on its shelves (a guineo and a manzana on each); its drawer is shut. On top of the fridge, a basket with three bread rolls. On the counter, the open picnic basket. On the sill, a tray. Two birds on the yard wall. Abuela at the fridge; Luis and Carmen by the counter; the frog on his stool.

1. NARR: "Vamos al parque." NARR: "Abuela busca comida para el parque." The basket of rolls twinkles: NARR: "Aquí hay pan." The plates twinkle: NARR: "Y aquí hay fruta." Abuela pulls the drawer out and tips it towards us: guineos and manzanas. NARR: "Hay guineos y manzanas." (the guineos twinkle, then the manzanas) She pushes it shut.

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **Touch a thing, taught.** Abuela sets a roll and a plate of fruit on the tray. She bends over the tray and smells the roll without lifting it; during it: NARR: "Abuela huele el pan." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Fo! Este pan no huele bien." NARR: "Abuela dice: Este pan es para los pájaros." Sign on the roll. He touches it; the frog pushes it out of the window onto the yard wall; during it: NARR: "Coquí tira el pan." A bird lands on it. NARR: "El pájaro come pan." Abuela gives the plate of fruit to Carmen. NARR: "Carmen come fruta."
3. Abuela sets a new roll and a new plate on the tray, bends over and smells the plate. NARR: "Abuela huele la fruta." NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta fruta no huele bien." Sign on the plate. He touches it. NARR: "Coquí tira la fruta." NARR: "El pájaro come fruta." Abuela gives the roll to Luis. NARR: "Luis come pan."
4. **Picture bubbles, taught.** Abuela pulls the drawer out a little and bends over it; we cannot see in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Este guineo no huele bien." She pushes it shut and goes to the sink to wash her hands, her back turned. Two picture bubbles: a guineo, a manzana. Sign on the guineo. He touches it; the frog hops to the drawer, comes out with a guineo and pushes it out of the window. NARR: "Coquí tira el guineo." NARR: "El pájaro come guineo."
5. The same. NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta manzana no huele bien." Sign on the manzana. NARR: "Coquí tira la manzana." NARR: "El pájaro come manzana."
6. **He says it, taught.** Luis takes a guineo and a manzana out of the drawer without looking at them and lays them on the tray; the guineo has brown spots, towards us. Abuela is at the picnic basket, her back turned. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el guineo y la manzana." Two speaking bubbles: "¡El guineo no huele bien!" / "¡La manzana no huele bien!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡El guineo no huele bien!" The frog pushes it out; a bird has it. Carmen takes the manzana. NARR: "Carmen come manzana."
7. Carmen lays a guineo and a manzana on the tray; the manzana has brown spots. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el guineo y la manzana." Sign on "¡La manzana no huele bien!" COQUÍ: "¡La manzana no huele bien!" The frog pushes it out. Luis takes the guineo. NARR: "Luis come guineo."

**The turn**

8. Abuelo comes in with his hat on and the ball under his arm. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Vamos al parque!" Three more birds land on the wall. Abuela takes down the last roll and the last plate of fruit. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay pan y fruta para el parque." The card shows. The signs go.

**The game**

9. **Decision A, Touch (bread or fruit).** The last roll and the last plate stand on the tray. From our side both look good. Abuela, the picnic cloth in both hands, bends over the tray and smells one, then the other, without lifting them; she turns back to the picnic basket and speaks over her shoulder. NARR: "Abuela dice: El pan no huele bien." (Or: "La fruta no huele bien." Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it out; a bird has it. Abuela puts the other one into the picnic basket. A star.
   - Wrong: the good one goes out, and the birds are on it at once. Abuela turns round, throws a hand up, and turns over the one that is left: green spots underneath. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pan no huele bien." (Or: "La fruta no huele bien.") The sign is on it; he touches it; the frog pushes it out too. Nothing from this lot goes into the picnic basket.
10. **Decision B, Picture bubbles (in the drawer).** Abuela pulls the drawer out a little, bends over it and smells. NARR: "Abuela dice: La manzana no huele bien." (Or: "El guineo no huele bien." Drawn.) She pushes it shut and goes to the sink to wash her hands, her back turned. Picture bubbles: a guineo, a manzana. The frog takes the one he chose out of the drawer and pushes it out of the window.
   - Right: a bird has it. Abuela takes the other one out of the drawer and puts it into the picnic basket. A star.
   - Wrong: the birds are on the good one at once. Abuela takes the other one out of the drawer and holds it up: brown spots. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. La manzana no huele bien." (Or: "El guineo…") The sign is on its bubble; he touches it; the frog pushes it out.
11. **Decision C, Say (he sees).** Abuelo takes the last guineo and the last manzana out of the drawer without looking at them and lays them on the tray; one of them has brown spots, towards us (drawn). Abuela is at the picnic basket, her back turned. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el guineo y la manzana." Bubbles: "¡El guineo no huele bien!" / "¡La manzana no huele bien!" The frog pushes out the one he named.
   - Right: a bird has it. Abuela puts the other one into the picnic basket. A star.
   - Wrong: the good one goes out; the birds are on it. Abuela turns round. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El guineo no huele bien." (Or: "La manzana…") The sign is on the right bubble; he says it; the frog pushes the spotted one out.

**Count:** rolls: three (steps 2 and 3, and A). Plates of fruit: three (steps 2 and 3, and A). In the drawer: five guineos and five manzanas (steps 4, 5, 6, 7, B, C); the drawer front hides them. Good things of the opening are eaten by Luis and Carmen (one plate, one roll, one manzana, one guineo). Three lots in the game: the three old things always go out. Into the picnic basket: three, less one for each miss. The birds get three to six things.

**The ending.** Abuela shuts the picnic basket's lid half; what is in it shows. Abuelo carries it. NARR: "Luis y Carmen van al parque." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, abuela!"
- Nothing wrong (three stars): three things in the basket. Stars and a heart.
- Otherwise: a hollow is empty for each miss; the birds still peck at the good roll or fruit on the wall; Luis looks into the basket.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** pan 7 (step 1; step 2 five times; step 3), fruta 7 (step 1; step 2; step 3 four times; step 8 comes after), guineo 7 (steps 1, 4 three times, 6 twice, 7), manzana 7 (steps 1, 5 three times, 6, 7 twice).
**A miss is filed under:** A and B: the thing abuela named. C: the thing with the spots.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two each. "El" and "la" go with the noun (el pan, el guineo; la fruta, la manzana): a second cue in A and B, for somebody who already knows which is which.

## Story 32: Jugo de china

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** on the counter a net of chinas, a glass jug of juice with four marks on its side, and four metal cups: a green one, a yellow one, a red one, a blue one. Nobody can see into a metal cup from the side. On top of the fridge, two clear glasses, kept for abuela and the frog. Papá, mamá and abuelo sit in the yard. Give everybody juice.
- **At stake:** the last jug: four cups' worth. Juice poured into a cup that is already full runs over, and the jug is a mark lower. A full cup handed to abuela at the sink goes into the dishwater.
- **New words:** china, mantequilla, lleno, vacío.
- **Back again:** vaso, jugo, hace calor, hace, pone, pan, toma, mi, quiero, más, por favor, ahora, familia, verde, amarillo, rojo, azul, gracias, no hay.
- **Heard along the way:** ponle, le pone.

**Start:** the fridge is shut; two clear glasses stand on top of it. On the counter: the net of chinas, the jug, full to its fourth mark, and the four metal cups; abuela has already filled the green one and the red one. On the sill: a plate of bread and the butter dish. Abuela at the counter; Luis and Carmen beside her. Papá, mamá and abuelo in the yard, seen through the window.

When a cup is spoken of in the opening, a round picture over it shows it from above: orange to the brim, or empty. In the game there is no such picture, except in a correction.

1. NARR: "Hace calor." Abuela squeezes a china; during it: NARR: "Abuela hace jugo de china." The net twinkles: NARR: "Aquí hay chinas." The butter dish twinkles: NARR: "Y aquí hay mantequilla." Abuela butters a slice; during it: NARR: "Abuela le pone mantequilla al pan." NARR: "Luis come pan con mantequilla."

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **Touch a cup, two lines, taught.** The green cup and the yellow cup stand in front. NARR: "El vaso verde está lleno." NARR: "El vaso amarillo está vacío." NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, un vaso está vacío. Ponle jugo de china." Sign on the yellow cup. He touches it; the frog pours from the jug: three marks. NARR: "Ahora el vaso amarillo está lleno."
3. **The children say it, taught.** Luis takes the green cup and Carmen the yellow one; Luis drinks his up. They set the cups on the counter and turn to abuela, who butters bread for them. NARR: "Luis dice: Mi vaso está vacío." NARR: "Carmen dice: Mi vaso está lleno." Sign on the green cup. He touches it (a touch on Luis counts as his cup); the frog pours: two marks. NARR: "Luis toma jugo de china."
4. **One line, taught.** Mamá, at the window, takes the red cup and the blue cup to the sill and looks down into them. NARR: "Mamá dice: El vaso rojo está lleno." She turns away to the yard table. Sign on the blue cup. He touches it; the frog pours: one mark. Mamá carries the two cups out to papá and abuelo.
5. **To the sink, taught.** Luis has drunk his up again. The green cup and the yellow cup stand on the counter; Luis and Carmen are in the yard with their bread. Abuela looks into both cups, goes to the sink and puts her hands in the dishwater, her back turned. NARR: "Abuela dice: El vaso verde está vacío." Sign on the green cup. He touches it; the frog carries it to abuela, who dips it in the dishwater without looking: nothing runs out. She washes it and stands it back. Carmen comes for her cup. NARR: "Carmen dice: Quiero pan con mantequilla y jugo de china."

**The turn**

6. Abuela pours the last mark into the green cup; the jug is empty. Luis and Carmen take their cups and their bread out to the yard. NARR: "Carmen come pan con mantequilla." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Más pan con mantequilla, por favor!" Abuela squeezes the last chinas: the jug is full to its fourth mark again, and the net is empty. NARR: "Abuela hace más jugo de china." NARR: "La familia toma jugo de china." The card shows. The signs go.

**The game**

7. **Decision A, Touch a cup (two lines).** Luis and Carmen come in for more bread, each with his cup; one has drunk his up, one has not (drawn). NARR: "Luis dice: Mi vaso está vacío." NARR: "Carmen dice: Mi vaso está lleno." (Who speaks first, drawn.) They set the cups on the counter and turn to abuela and the bread. He touches a cup, or its child; the frog pours into that cup.
   - Right: the jug is one mark down. A star.
   - Wrong: juice runs over the cup onto the counter and down the front of the cabinet: a puddle with drops. The jug is one mark down. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El vaso verde está vacío." (Or: "…amarillo…"; the round picture shows it.) The sign is on that cup; he touches it; the frog pours; a second mark down.
8. **Decision B, Touch a cup (one line).** Mamá comes to the window with the red cup and the blue cup, sets them on the sill and looks down into them; one is full and one is empty (drawn). She says one line of four (drawn): NARR: "Mamá dice: El vaso rojo está lleno." / "…está vacío." / "El vaso azul está lleno." / "…está vacío." She turns away to the yard table. He touches a cup; the frog pours into it.
   - Right: one mark down. Mamá carries the cups out. A star.
   - Wrong: juice runs over the sill and down the wall under it. One mark down. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El vaso azul está vacío." (The empty one; the round picture shows it.) The sign is on it; he touches it; the frog pours; a second mark down.
9. **Decision C, To the sink (one line).** Luis and Carmen have left the green cup and the yellow cup on the counter and gone to play in the yard; one cup is empty and one is still full (drawn). Abuela looks into both, goes to the sink and puts her hands in the dishwater, her back turned. She says one line of four (drawn): NARR: "Abuela dice: El vaso verde está vacío." / "…está lleno." / "El vaso amarillo está vacío." / "…está lleno." He touches a cup; the frog carries it to abuela, who dips it in the dishwater without looking.
   - Right: nothing runs out. She washes it. A star.

## For the owner, one line each

- "Busca" decides nothing in story 30, nor "china" and "mantequilla" in story 32; each is heard six times or more and tested the next day by its pair.
- "Nevera" and "fo" (what abuela says at a bad smell) are heard as helper words. They never decide.
- "No hay" is taught against "sí hay": a bare "Hay leche." sounds like "¡Ay, leche!". It turns on the old word "no"; that cannot be avoided.
- "Leche", like "huevo", is never said with mi, tu or su.
- Old bread and fruit "no huele bien": the stories do not say "viejo" of food.
- The three fruit pairs use each other's fruit. The pair for "leche" has juice in the other glass, and "jugo" is an old word.
- In all three stories it is the frog who goes without first. In 30 it is his own money.

Beginner (all three "not ready") and Puerto Rican Spanish (thirteen lines).

# La nevera: three stories (draft 3, after two rounds of reviews and a final Spanish check)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 3 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa (stories 1 to 11), El parque (12 to 23) and two scenes of La cocina: La mesa (24 to 26) and La estufa (27 to 29).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.

Words he has met (place 3, La estufa): arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace, pollo, huevo, sal, huele, caliente, frío, cuidado, ya (as "ya está"). Heard there: listas, huele a, no hay más, ponle.

Scene words for La nevera (from the 40-scene list): leche, queso, pan, mantequilla, fruta, guineo, manzana, china, no hay, lleno, vacío, busca.

The picture: abuela's fridge stands at the left. It can be open towards us (we see in: two shelves and a drawer), or shut; when abuela opens it a little and looks in, only she sees in. To its right a counter with a sink at its left end. Over the counter a wide window to the yard, with a sill things can stand on; whoever stands in the yard is seen in it from the waist up, and the top of the yard wall shows behind. The door to the rest of the house is past the right edge. The frog stands on a stool between the fridge and the counter. People in the kitchen stand on the floor in front of the counter, lower than its top, so nobody hides what is on it.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La mesa and La estufa. In short: a wrong move costs something in the picture that stays; then "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true at that moment; then the big sign shows the right one and he does it, with no star. Nobody points at or looks toward the answer in a decision. The sides of answer bubbles, and the sides things stand on, are drawn fresh every time.
A poke names people, the fridge and the kitchen only. It never names milk, cheese, bread, fruit, a cup or a coin.

The three games differ in their rounds: 30 is say (he sees), picture cards (he hears both), picture cards (he hears one line); 31 is touch a thing, touch a person, say (he sees); 32 is touch a cup (two lines), touch a cup (one line), a cup to the sink (one line). The three losses differ: in 30 the frog's own piragua money is spent on what was already there, in 31 good food goes to the birds, in 32 juice runs over or goes into the dishwater. The three turns differ: the piragua man's bell; abuelo at the window with his hat on; the last chinas.

## Story 30: No hay leche

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** the open fridge has one holder for the milk bottle and one blue dish for the cheese, both full. On the sill stands the frog's own clear jar, a frog and a piragua drawn on it, with three coins in it. Keep the fridge stocked, and keep the three coins for a piragua.
- **At stake:** the frog's three coins. Everything papá fetches from the colmado costs one coin. Each time something runs out, abuela lays one coin on the sill and leaves the kitchen. If the frog sends papá for the wrong thing, that coin is spent, abuela is not there, and the only money left is in the frog's jar.
- **New words:** leche, queso, no hay, busca.
- **Back again:** hay, sí, aquí, ahora, mira, come, toma, bebé, pan (heard before), con, piragua, tira, coge, gracias.
- **Heard along the way:** nevera.

**Start:** the fridge is open: a bottle of milk in its holder, a wedge of cheese on its blue dish. Abuela stands at the counter with a plate of bread. In the kitchen, at the right: Luis, Carmen, and mamá with the baby and its bottle. Papá stands in the yard at the window. On the sill: the frog's jar with three coins. By the window hang two cards, one with a bottle of milk on it, one with a wedge of cheese. Abuelo is in the sala, past the door; abuela carries his food out to him.

1. NARR: "Esta es la nevera." (the fridge twinkles) NARR: "Aquí hay leche." (the bottle twinkles) NARR: "Y aquí hay queso." (the cheese twinkles) Mamá fills the baby's bottle from the milk and hands papá the empty one over the sill; abuela lays cheese on slices of bread for Luis and Carmen; during it: NARR: "El bebé toma leche." NARR: "Luis come pan con queso." The milk holder in the fridge is empty; the cheese dish still has a piece.

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **He says it, taught.** Abuela lays a coin from her apron on the sill, takes her plate of bread and goes out of the door; as she goes: NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera. ¿Hay leche? ¿Hay queso?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Papá, no hay leche!" / "¡Papá, no hay queso!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡Papá, no hay leche!" The frog tosses papá the coin from the sill; papá goes off along the wall; as he goes: NARR: "Papá busca la leche." He is back with a bottle and hands it in to Luis, who carries it to the fridge and stands it in the holder. NARR: "Ahora hay leche." Abuela comes back.
3. Abuela takes the last cheese for her plate; the dish in the fridge is empty. She lays a coin on the sill and goes out. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera." Sign on "¡Papá, no hay queso!" COQUÍ: "¡Papá, no hay queso!" The coin; papá goes. NARR: "Papá busca el queso." Luis lays the wedge on the dish. NARR: "Ahora hay queso." Abuela comes back and shuts the fridge.
4. **Picture cards, she says both, taught.** Later abuela opens the fridge a little and looks in; we cannot see in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay queso. No hay leche." She shuts it, lays a coin on the sill and goes out with her plate. The two cards twinkle. Sign on the milk card. He touches it; the frog holds the card up at the window and tosses the coin; papá goes. NARR: "Papá busca la leche." Luis opens the fridge wide and stands the bottle in the empty holder, and shuts it. Abuela comes back.
5. NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay queso. Sí hay leche." A coin; she goes out. Sign on the cheese card. He touches it. NARR: "Papá busca el queso." Luis lays the wedge on the empty dish. Abuela comes back.
6. **Picture cards, one line, taught.** Abuela looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche." A coin; she goes out. Sign on the cheese card. He touches it; papá goes and is back with a wedge. NARR: "Papá busca el queso." Luis lays it on the empty dish.
7. Abuela looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay leche." A coin; she goes out. Sign on the milk card. He touches it. NARR: "Papá busca la leche." Luis stands the bottle in the empty holder. Abuela comes back.

**The turn**

8. A bell. The piragua man's cart stops at the yard wall, at the far end of the window. COQUÍ: "¡Una piragua!" The frog hops to his jar and shakes it: three coins. Abuela turns her apron pocket out: one coin after another, three in all, lie in her hand. The card shows. The signs go.

**The game.** In every round one of the two has run out (drawn fresh). Abuela says what she has to say, lays one coin on the sill, and goes out of the door with her plate; she comes back when papá is back. Papá hears only the frog. Whatever papá brings, he hands in to Luis, who carries it to the fridge: there is one holder and one dish.

9. **Decision A, Say (he sees in).** Abuela opens the fridge wide: the milk holder is empty, or the cheese dish is (drawn). She lays a coin on the sill and goes out. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera." Bubbles: "¡Papá, no hay leche!" / "¡Papá, no hay queso!"
   - Right: the frog tosses papá the coin; papá goes and is back with it; Luis puts it in its empty place. As papá goes: NARR: "Papá busca la leche." (Or: "…el queso.") Abuela comes back. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog tosses papá the coin; papá is back with what the frog called. Luis carries it to the fridge: its place is taken. He throws a hand up and stands it on the counter. The sill is bare. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay leche." (Or: "No hay queso.") The sign is on the right bubble; he says it; the frog takes a coin out of his own jar and tosses it; papá goes again, and Luis puts the right thing in its place. Abuela comes back. Mamá and the children share out what stands on the counter; its empty bottle, or its rind, stays there.
10. **Decision B, Picture cards (she says both).** The fridge is shut. Abuela opens it a little and looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche. No hay queso." (Which one has run out, and which half she says first, drawn.) She shuts it, lays a coin on the sill and goes out. He touches a card; the frog holds it up and tosses the coin.
   - Right and wrong as in A. Luis opens the fridge wide to put the thing in, so after a wrong one we see in: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay queso." (Or: "No hay leche.")
11. **Decision C, Picture cards (one line).** Abuela opens the fridge a little and looks in. She says one line of four (drawn): NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche." / "No hay leche." / "Sí hay queso." / "No hay queso." She shuts it, lays a coin on the sill and goes out. He touches a card.
   - Right and wrong as in B.

**Count:** abuela's apron holds nine coins: six for the opening, three for the game, one a round. The frog's jar holds three: each miss takes one; three, two, one or none are left. A redo always has its coin. Milk and cheese at the colmado do not run out. The bottle papá takes back each time is the empty one; what a wrong errand brought is shared out before the next round, so it never stocks the fridge.

**The ending.** NARR: "Ahora hay leche y queso." The frog tosses papá the coins left in his jar; papá buys a piragua from the cart and hands it in: one coin, one scoop; two coins, two scoops; three coins, three scoops, red, yellow and blue.
- Nothing wrong (three stars): a piragua three scoops high. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, papá!" Stars and a heart.
- One or two misses: a smaller piragua; the empty bottles or rinds stand on the counter. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, papá!"
- Three misses: the jar is empty. NARR: "No hay piragua para Coquí." The frog looks at the cart.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost** (the frog's own line counts once; a bubble's voice is not counted): leche 12 (step 1 twice; step 2 four times; steps 4 and 7 twice each; steps 5 and 6 once each), queso 11 (step 1 twice; step 2 once; step 3 three times; step 4 once; steps 5 and 6 twice each), no hay 5 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5, 7), busca 6 (steps 2 to 7). "Sí hay" against "No hay" side by side: steps 4 and 5; alone: steps 6 and 7.
**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that had run out. B: no hay, and the thing. C: no hay.
**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two: the card of the thing abuela named is right for "No hay…" and wrong for "Sí hay…". "Sí" and "no" are old words, but in B and C the answer is a picture: the learner has to know which thing "leche" is.

## Story 31: Para los pájaros

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** an open picnic basket on the counter, with three hollows in it. Three bread rolls in a basket on top of the fridge; three plates of fruit on the shelves of the open fridge; the drawer of the fridge. Two birds sit on the yard wall, and abuelo stands in the yard at the window. Fill the picnic basket with good things for the park.
- **At stake:** the picnic. After the turn there are three lots of two things, one lot for each hollow; in each lot one thing is old and one is good. The old one is for the birds. A good thing that goes to the birds is eaten at once, and its hollow in the basket stays empty.
- **New words:** fruta, guineo, manzana, pan.
- **Back again:** huele, bien, tira, busca, pájaro, come, hay, aquí, vamos, parque, para, comida, este, esta, mira, adiós.
- **Heard along the way:** fo, van.

**Start:** the fridge is open: three plates of fruit on its shelves (a guineo and a manzana on each); its drawer is shut. On top of the fridge, a basket with three bread rolls. On the counter, the open picnic basket. On the sill, a tray. Two birds on the yard wall; abuelo in the yard at the window. Abuela at the fridge; Luis and Carmen by the counter; the frog on his stool.

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos al parque." NARR: "Abuela busca comida para el parque." The basket of rolls twinkles: NARR: "Aquí hay pan." The plates twinkle: NARR: "Y aquí hay fruta." Abuela pulls the drawer out and tips it towards us: three guineos and three manzanas. NARR: "Aquí hay guineos y manzanas." (the guineos twinkle, then the manzanas) She pushes it shut.

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **Touch a thing, taught.** Abuela sets a roll and a plate of fruit on the tray. She bends over the tray and smells the roll without lifting it; during it: NARR: "Abuela huele el pan." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Fo! Este pan no huele bien." NARR: "Abuela dice: Este pan es para los pájaros." Sign on the roll. He touches it; the frog tosses it out of the window onto the yard wall; during it: NARR: "Coquí tira el pan." A bird lands on it. NARR: "El pájaro come pan." Abuela gives the plate of fruit to Carmen. NARR: "Carmen come fruta."
3. Abuela sets a new roll and a new plate on the tray, bends over and smells the plate. NARR: "Abuela huele la fruta." NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta fruta no huele bien." Sign on the plate. He touches it. NARR: "Coquí tira la fruta." NARR: "El pájaro come fruta." Abuela gives the roll to Luis. NARR: "Luis come pan."
4. **Touch a person, taught.** Abuela takes a guineo and a manzana out of the drawer and gives the guineo to Luis and the manzana to Carmen to hold. She bends to Luis's hands and smells, then to Carmen's. NARR: "Abuela dice: Este guineo no huele bien." She turns to the picnic basket. Sign on Luis. He touches him; the frog hops over, takes the guineo out of Luis's hands and tosses it out of the window. NARR: "El guineo es para los pájaros." NARR: "Carmen come la manzana."
5. The same, with the manzana in Luis's hands and the guineo in Carmen's. NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta manzana no huele bien." Sign on Luis. NARR: "La manzana es para los pájaros." NARR: "Carmen come el guineo."
6. **He says it, taught.** Abuelo comes to the window with a guineo and a manzana and lays them on the tray; from the yard he sees their backs. On our side the guineo has black spots. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, mira la fruta." Two speaking bubbles: "¡El guineo no huele bien!" / "¡La manzana no huele bien!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡El guineo no huele bien!" Abuelo tosses the guineo to the birds. NARR: "Abuelo come la manzana."
7. Abuelo lays another guineo and manzana on the tray; the manzana has black spots on our side. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, mira la fruta." Sign on "¡La manzana no huele bien!" COQUÍ: "¡La manzana no huele bien!" Abuelo tosses the manzana to the birds. NARR: "Abuelo come el guineo."

**The turn**

8. Abuelo puts his hat on. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Vamos al parque!" Three more birds land on the wall. The three last lots come out, one for each hollow of the picnic basket: abuela sets the last roll and the last plate of fruit on the tray; she tips the drawer towards us, and Luis and Carmen take the last guineo and the last manzana out of it, one each: the drawer is empty; abuelo holds a guineo and a manzana up at the window. NARR: "Abuela dice: Aquí hay pan y fruta para el parque." The card shows. The signs go.

**The game**

9. **Decision A, Touch a thing (bread or fruit).** The last roll and the last plate stand on the tray (which stands left and which right, drawn). From our side both look good. Abuela, the picnic cloth in both hands, bends over the tray and smells one, then the other (the order drawn), with the same face; she turns back to the picnic basket and speaks over her shoulder. NARR: "Abuela dice: El pan no huele bien." (Or: "La fruta no huele bien." Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog tosses it out; a bird has it. Abuela puts the other one into the picnic basket. A star.
   - Wrong: the good one goes out, and the birds are on it at once. Abuela turns round, throws a hand up, and turns over the one that is left: green spots underneath. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pan no huele bien." (Or: "La fruta no huele bien.") The sign is on it; he touches it; the frog tosses it out too. Nothing from this lot goes into the picnic basket.
10. **Decision B, Touch a person (guineo or manzana).** Luis and Carmen hold the two fruits from the drawer, one each (who holds which, drawn). Abuela bends to the hands of one and smells, then of the other, with the same face, and turns to the picnic basket. NARR: "Abuela dice: La manzana no huele bien." (Or: "El guineo no huele bien." Drawn.) He touches a child; the frog hops over, takes the fruit out of that child's hands and tosses it out of the window.
   - Right: a bird has it. The other child lays his fruit in the picnic basket. A star.
   - Wrong: the birds are on the good fruit at once. The other child looks at the fruit in his hands and turns it over: black spots. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. La manzana no huele bien." (Or: "El guineo…") The sign is on that child; he touches him; the frog tosses that fruit out too.
11. **Decision C, Say (he sees).** Abuelo lays his guineo and his manzana on the tray from the yard; he sees their backs. On our side one of them has black spots (drawn). NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, mira la fruta." Bubbles: "¡El guineo no huele bien!" / "¡La manzana no huele bien!" Abuelo tosses the one the frog names to the birds.
   - Right: a bird has it. Abuelo hands the other one in, and abuela lays it in the picnic basket. A star.
   - Wrong: the good one goes to the birds. Abuelo picks the other one up and turns it: black spots. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El guineo no huele bien." (Or: "La manzana…") The sign is on the right bubble; he says it; abuelo tosses the spotted one out.

**Count:** rolls: three (steps 2 and 3, and A). Plates of fruit: three (steps 2 and 3, and A). In the drawer: three guineos and three manzanas (steps 4 and 5, and B); it is shown full in step 1 and empty at the turn. Abuelo brings a guineo and a manzana three times (steps 6 and 7, and C). The good things of the opening are eaten (Carmen: a plate of fruit, a manzana, a guineo; Luis: a roll; abuelo: a manzana, a guineo). Three lots in the game, one for each hollow: the three old things always go to the birds. Into the picnic basket: three, less one for each miss. A hollow cannot be filled from anywhere else: every shelf, the roll basket and the drawer are empty at the turn.

**The ending.** Abuela shuts the picnic basket's lid half; what is in it shows. Luis carries it. NARR: "Luis y Carmen van al parque." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, abuela!"
- Nothing wrong (three stars): three things in the basket. Stars and a heart.
- Otherwise: a hollow is empty for each miss; the birds still peck at the good roll or fruit on the wall; Luis looks into the basket.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** pan 7 (step 1; step 2 five times; step 3), fruta 9 (step 1; step 2; step 3 four times; steps 6 and 7; step 8 comes too), guineo 7 (steps 1, 4 twice, 5, 6 twice, 7), manzana 7 (steps 1, 4, 5 twice, 6, 7 twice).
**A miss is filed under:** A and B: the thing abuela named. C: the thing with the spots.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two each. "El" and "la" go with the noun (el pan, el guineo; la fruta, la manzana): a second cue in A and B, for somebody who already knows which is which. "Pan" was heard before this scene (stories 18, 20, 30), so a learner who caught it then can do A; B and C need the two new words.

## Story 32: Jugo de china

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** on the counter a net of chinas, a big glass jug of juice with four marks on its side, and four metal cups: a green one, a yellow one, a red one, a blue one. Nobody can see into a metal cup from the side. On top of the fridge, two clear glasses, kept for abuela and the frog. Papá and abuelo sit in the yard. Give everybody juice.
- **At stake:** the last jug: four cups' worth. Juice poured into a cup that is already full runs over, and the jug is a mark lower. A full cup tipped into the sink is juice in the dishwater.
- **New words:** china, mantequilla, lleno, vacío.
- **Back again:** vaso, jugo, hace calor, hace, pone, pan, toma, mi, quiero, más, por favor, ahora, familia, verde, amarillo, rojo, azul, gracias, no hay.
- **Heard along the way:** ponle, le pone.

**Start:** the fridge is shut; two clear glasses stand on top of it. On the counter: the net of chinas, the jug, and the four metal cups; the green one and the red one are full, the yellow one and the blue one empty. The sink is full of dishwater. On the sill: a plate of bread and the butter dish. Abuela at the counter; Luis and Carmen beside her; mamá at the window. Papá and abuelo in the yard.

The jug is as big as the frog. When he pours he stands behind it on the counter and tips it: he cannot see into the cup. When a cup is spoken of in the opening, a round picture over it shows it from above: orange to the brim, or empty. In the game there is no such picture, except in a correction.

1. NARR: "Hace calor." Abuela squeezes a china into the jug: it is full to its fourth mark; during it: NARR: "Abuela hace jugo de china." The net twinkles: NARR: "Aquí hay chinas." The butter dish twinkles: NARR: "Y aquí hay mantequilla." Abuela butters a slice; during it: NARR: "Abuela le pone mantequilla al pan." NARR: "Luis come pan con mantequilla."

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **Pour, two lines, taught.** The green cup and the yellow cup stand in front. NARR: "El vaso verde está lleno." NARR: "El vaso amarillo está vacío." NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, un vaso está vacío. Ponle jugo de china." Sign on the yellow cup. He touches it; the frog pours: three marks. NARR: "Ahora el vaso amarillo está lleno."
3. **Pour, the children say it, taught.** Luis takes the green cup and Carmen the yellow one; Carmen drinks hers up. They set the cups on the counter and take bread in both hands. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mi vaso está vacío." NARR: "Luis dice: Mi vaso está lleno." Sign on the yellow cup. He touches it (a touch on Carmen counts as her cup); the frog pours: two marks. NARR: "Carmen toma jugo de china."
4. **Pour, one line, taught.** Mamá takes the red cup and the blue cup to the sill and looks down into them. NARR: "Mamá dice: El vaso rojo está lleno." She turns away to the yard. Sign on the blue cup. He touches it; the frog pours: one mark. Mamá carries the two cups out to papá and abuelo.
5. **Pour, one line, taught.** Luis drinks his cup up; the two cups stand on the counter. Abuela looks into both and turns to the bread. NARR: "Abuela dice: El vaso verde está vacío." Sign on the green cup. He touches it; the frog pours the last mark: the jug is empty. NARR: "Luis toma jugo de china."
6. **To the sink, one line, taught.** Mamá brings the red cup and the blue cup back to the sill; papá has drunk his up, abuelo has not. She looks down into them. NARR: "Mamá dice: El vaso rojo está vacío." She turns away. Sign on the red cup. He touches it; the frog carries it to the sink and tips it over the dishwater: nothing runs out. It goes in to be washed. Mamá takes the blue cup back to abuelo.
7. **To the sink, one line, taught.** Carmen has drunk hers up; Luis has not. The two cups stand on the counter; the children are at the bread. Abuela looks into both and goes out to the yard with a plate of bread. NARR: "Abuela dice: El vaso verde está lleno." Sign on the yellow cup. He touches it; the frog carries it to the sink and tips it: nothing runs out. NARR: "Carmen dice: Quiero pan con mantequilla y jugo de china."

**The turn**

8. Abuela comes back and squeezes the last chinas: she fills the green cup and the yellow cup, and then the jug to its fourth mark; the net is empty. NARR: "Abuela hace más jugo de china." Luis and Carmen take their cups and their bread out of the door. NARR: "Carmen come pan con mantequilla." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Más pan con mantequilla, por favor!" NARR: "La familia toma jugo de china." The card shows. The signs go.

**The game**

9. **Decision A, Pour (two lines).** Luis and Carmen come in for more bread, carrying their cups the same way; one has drunk his up, one has not (drawn). They set the cups on the counter and take bread in both hands, their backs to the cups. NARR: "Luis dice: Mi vaso está vacío." NARR: "Carmen dice: Mi vaso está lleno." (Who speaks first, drawn.) He touches a cup, or its child; the frog pours into that cup.
   - Right: the jug is one mark down. A star.

## For the owner, one line each

- "Busca" decides nothing in story 30, nor "china" and "mantequilla" in story 32; each is heard six times or more and tested the next day by its pair.
- "Nevera" and "fo" (what abuela says at a bad smell) are heard as helper words. They never decide. Only you can hear whether the voice says "fo" clearly; if not, it comes out.
- "No hay" is taught against "sí hay": a bare "Hay leche." sounds like "¡Ay, leche!". Only you can hear whether "Abuela dice: Sí hay leche." keeps its pause; without it, it turns into "says whether there is milk".
- "Leche", like "huevo", is never said with mi, tu or su. "Guineo" after "come" always has its article, except of the baby.
- Old bread and fruit "no huele bien": the stories do not say "viejo" of food.
- "Pan" has been heard since story 18, so story 31's first round can be done by a learner who caught it then.
- The three fruit pairs use each other's fruit. The other picture of "leche", "pan" and "busca" has an old name (jugo, arroz, come), as in La estufa.
- In stories 30 and 32 it is the frog who goes without first; in 30 it is his own money.

## What the first round of reviews changed (draft 1 to draft 2)

Beginner (all three "not ready") and Puerto Rican Spanish (thirteen lines).
- 30: papá could hear abuela, and abuela could send a wrong buy back. Now papá stands at the piragua cart, abuela at the sink with the tap running; a wrong buy is finished at once by Luis and Carmen. A right round emptied a dent as well, so the loss did not read: abuela's tray pays the first errand of each round, the frog's own jar the second. Every "busca" is a real errand. "¿Qué no hay?" became "¿Hay leche? ¿Hay queso?"; "Hay leche." became "Sí hay leche."; "¡Papá, busca la leche!" became "¡Papá, busca leche!". Papá's "¿Leche o queso?" is gone.
- 31: abuela had the bad thing at her nose and laid it back for the frog to throw: now she smells bent over the tray with her hands full, or over the drawer and then washes her hands. The spare good things of the opening are eaten. Round C is spoken: the frog sees the spots and says which. The bags became one picnic basket, so the ending is not story 30's. The children do not run in at the turn; abuelo comes with his hat on. "¡Ay!" became "¡Fo!".
- 32: nobody stands by while a full cup is filled: the children turn to the bread, mamá turns to the yard, abuela has her hands in the dishwater. The cups are named by colour, so nobody has to remember whose is whose. Rounds B and C are one line, so each of the two words is needed. The jug's marks are counted through the opening; the last two glasses stand in the first frame. "pon … en el vaso vacío" became "un vaso está vacío. Ponle jugo de china."; "pone mantequilla en el pan" became "le pone mantequilla al pan".
- Pairs: "Mira mi leche." (vulgar) became "Carmen toma leche."; "una china" became "tu china"; "en la mesa" is gone; "busca" shows an errand; bread is no longer the other picture three times.

## What the second round of reviews changed (draft 2 to draft 3)

Beginner (all three "not ready" again; every count held, every decision one in two) and Puerto Rican Spanish (ten lines).
- 30: abuela at the sink still faced the window. Now she lays one coin on the sill and leaves the kitchen with her plate, and comes back after the errand. One rule for every buy: Luis carries it to the fridge, which has one holder and one dish; a thing whose place is taken stays on the counter. The frog pays from his jar only when the sill is bare and abuela is out. Round C is answered with the picture cards: with speaking bubbles, "sí", "no" and the sound of the noun were enough. The tray of nine coins and "Espera, Coquí." are gone.
- 31: in the spoken round the frog threw the good fruit out against his own eyes. Now abuelo stands in the yard, sees only the backs, and throws what the frog names. Round B is a touch on the child who holds the fruit, not picture bubbles at a shut drawer (that was story 30's round). The drawer is shown full at the start and empty at the turn, and the three last lots are laid out at the turn, one for each hollow. "Hay guineos y manzanas." became "Aquí hay…" (a line that opens on "Hay" sounds like "¡Ay!"); a fruit after "come" has its article; "Vamos al parque." has a speaker.

The children have both hands on their bread. Juice that runs over pools on the counter and the floor.

## What the final Spanish check changed

All 112 lines to be recorded were read one by one. Two changed: "El guineo es para el pájaro." and "La manzana es para el pájaro." are now "…para los pájaros." (the singular "el pájaro" is island slang, and it sat beside "guineo"). A guineo with brown spots is ripe and good to a Puerto Rican, so the fruit that "no huele bien" has black spots.

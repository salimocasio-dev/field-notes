# La nevera: three stories (draft 1)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 3 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa (stories 1 to 11), El parque (12 to 23) and two scenes of La cocina: La mesa (24 to 26) and La estufa (27 to 29).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, otra, su, luna, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.
Words he has met (place 3, La mesa): mesa, plato, taza, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, cocina, pone, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo. Heard there: chocolate, lista, a comer, tiene hambre, tiene sed, tienes, esa.
Words he has met (place 3, La estufa): arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace, pollo, huevo, sal, huele, caliente, frío, cuidado, ya (as "ya está"). Heard there: listas, huele a, no hay más, ponle.

Scene words for La nevera (from the 40-scene list): leche, queso, pan, mantequilla, fruta, guineo, manzana, china, no hay, lleno, vacío, busca.

The picture: abuela's fridge stands at the left. Its door has two states: open towards us (we see in: three shelves and a drawer), or pulled half shut with its white front to us while abuela stands at its side with her head inside (only she sees in). To its right a counter with a sink in it. Over the counter a wide window to the yard, with a sill; whoever stands in the yard is seen in it from the waist up, and the top of the yard wall shows behind. The frog stands on a stool between the fridge and the counter. People in the kitchen stand on the floor in front of the counter, lower than its top, so nobody hides what is on it.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La mesa and La estufa. In short: a wrong move costs something in the picture that stays; then "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true at that moment; then the big sign shows the right one and he does it, with no star. Nobody points at or looks toward the answer in a decision. The sides of answer bubbles are drawn fresh every time.
A poke names people, the fridge and the kitchen only. It never names milk, cheese, bread, fruit, a cup or a coin.

The three games open differently: 30 with something he says, 31 with a touch on a thing, 32 with a touch on a person. The three losses differ: in 30 money is spent on what was already there, in 31 good food is thrown to the birds, in 32 juice runs over or goes down the sink.

## Story 30: No hay leche

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** on the window sill a jar with a piragua drawn on it and, beside it, six coins in a tray with six dents. The fridge holds milk and cheese; the family eats bread with cheese and drinks milk. Keep the fridge stocked, and keep the coins for piraguas.
- **At stake:** the six coins. After the turn, everything papá fetches from the colmado costs one coin. Three coins buy what runs out; the three left buy a piragua each for Luis, Carmen and the frog. A coin spent on a thing that was already there is gone.
- **New words:** leche, queso, no hay, busca.
- **Back again:** hay, aquí, ahora, mira, come, toma, bebé, pan (heard before), con, qué, o, espera, piragua, gracias.
- **Heard along the way:** nevera.

**Start:** the fridge is shut. In the yard, at the window, papá has come back from the colmado: two bottles of milk and two wedges of cheese stand on the sill in front of him. On the sill at the other end: the piragua jar and the tray of six coins. At the right of the kitchen: Luis, Carmen, and mamá with the baby and its bottle. Abuela stands at the fridge. On the counter: a loaf.

1. Abuela opens the fridge; during it: NARR: "Abuela abre la nevera." Inside: one bottle of milk, one wedge of cheese. NARR: "Aquí hay leche." (the bottle twinkles) NARR: "Y aquí hay queso." (the cheese twinkles) Mamá fills the baby's bottle from the milk, and abuela lays the cheese on a slice of bread for Luis; during it: NARR: "El bebé toma leche." NARR: "Luis come pan con queso." Mamá puts the empty bottle back in the fridge; half the cheese is left on its dish.

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **He says it, taught.** Abuela is at the counter, cutting bread, her back to the fridge. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira en la nevera. ¿Qué no hay?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡No hay leche!" / "¡No hay queso!" Sign on the first. COQUÍ: "¡No hay leche!" Papá hands a bottle in from the sill, and abuela stands it in the fridge; during it: NARR: "Papá busca la leche." NARR: "Ahora hay leche."
3. Abuela lays the last cheese on a slice for Carmen. NARR: "Carmen come pan con queso." The cheese dish in the fridge is empty. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿qué no hay?" Sign on "¡No hay queso!" COQUÍ: "¡No hay queso!" Papá hands a wedge in. NARR: "Papá busca el queso." NARR: "Ahora hay queso."
4. **Picture bubbles, taught.** The family goes on eating and drinking. Abuela goes to the fridge, pulls the door half shut and looks in; we cannot see in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay queso. No hay leche." Two picture bubbles: a bottle of milk, a wedge of cheese. Sign on the milk. He touches it. Papá hands in the second bottle. NARR: "Papá busca la leche."
5. Later: NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay queso. Hay leche." Sign on the cheese. He touches it. Papá hands in the second wedge. NARR: "Papá busca el queso." The sill in front of papá is empty.
6. **One line only, taught.** NARR: "Papá dice: ¿Leche o queso?" Abuela looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay leche." Two speaking bubbles: "¡Papá, busca la leche!" / "¡Papá, busca el queso!" Sign on the second. COQUÍ: "¡Papá, busca el queso!" Abuela takes a coin from her apron pocket and gives it to papá; he goes off along the yard wall and is back with a wedge of cheese.
7. NARR: "Papá dice: ¿Leche o queso?" NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay leche." Sign on "¡Papá, busca la leche!" COQUÍ: "¡Papá, busca la leche!" A coin from the apron; papá goes and is back with a bottle.

**The turn**

8. A bell. The piragua man's cart stops at the yard wall, small in the window. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Una piragua!" Abuela turns her apron pocket out: empty. She points at the six coins on the sill. NARR: "Abuela dice: Espera, Luis." The family goes on eating bread with cheese and drinking milk. The card shows. The signs go.

**The game.** In every round one of the two has run out (drawn fresh). The family eats both all the time, so nothing in the picture outside the fridge says which.

9. **Decision A, Say (he sees in).** The fridge is open to us. The bottle is empty, or the cheese dish is (drawn). NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿qué no hay?" Bubbles: "¡No hay leche!" / "¡No hay queso!"
   - Right: the frog tosses papá a coin from the tray; papá goes and is back with it; abuela puts it in the fridge. During it: NARR: "Papá busca la leche." (Or: "…el queso.") A star.
   - Wrong: the frog tosses papá a coin; papá is back with what the frog called. Abuela holds it up beside the one in the fridge: two cheeses, and an empty bottle. A dent in the tray is empty. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay leche." (Or: "No hay queso.") The sign is on the right bubble; he says it; a second coin; papá goes again. The spare goes on the counter, and the family eats it.
10. **Decision B, Picture bubbles (he hears both).** The door is half shut; abuela looks in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay leche. No hay queso." (Which one has run out, and which half she says first, drawn.) Picture bubbles: milk, cheese.
   - Right and wrong as in A. After a wrong one abuela opens the door to us: NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay queso." (Or: "No hay leche.")
11. **Decision C, Say (one line).** NARR: "Papá dice: ¿Leche o queso?" The door is half shut; abuela looks in. She says one line of four (drawn): "Abuela dice: Hay leche." / "No hay leche." / "Hay queso." / "No hay queso." Bubbles: "¡Papá, busca la leche!" / "¡Papá, busca el queso!"
   - Right and wrong as in A. After a wrong one abuela opens the door to us: "No, Coquí. Mira. No hay queso." (Or: "No hay leche.")

**Count:** six coins. A right round takes one, a wrong round two. Three to six are spent; three to none are left. A redo always has its coin. Milk and cheese at the colmado do not run out.

**The ending.** NARR: "Ahora hay leche y hay queso." Papá takes the coins that are left to the piragua man and comes back with one piragua for each: the first is Luis's, the second Carmen's, the third the frog's. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, papá!"
- Nothing wrong (three stars): three piraguas. Stars and a heart.
- Otherwise: empty dents in the tray; the frog has no piragua (one miss), nor Carmen (two), nor Luis (three), and whoever has none looks at the cart.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost** (a bubble's own voice and the frog's line each count): leche 12 (steps 1 to 7), queso 12, no hay 8 (steps 2 to 5 and 7), busca 8 (steps 2 to 7). "Hay" against "no hay" is heard side by side four times (steps 4 to 7).
**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that had run out. B and C: no hay.
**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two: saying back the word abuela said is right for "No hay…" and wrong for "Hay…".

## Story 31: Pan para los pájaros

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** three paper bags on the counter (Luis's green one, Carmen's pink one, a small one with a frog on it). Bread rolls in a basket; guineos and manzanas in the fridge drawer. Birds sit on the yard wall. Pack something good for the park in every bag.
- **At stake:** what goes to the park. After the turn there are three lots of two things. In each lot one thing is old and one is good, and from our side they look the same; abuela smells them and says which. The old one is for the birds. A good thing pushed out of the window is eaten by the birds at once, and its bag stays empty.
- **New words:** fruta, guineo, manzana, pan.
- **Back again:** huele, bien, tira, busca, pájaro, come, hay, aquí, vamos, parque, para, comida, este, esta, adiós.
- **Heard along the way:** van.

**Start:** the fridge is open: guineos and manzanas in its drawer. On top of the fridge, a basket of bread rolls. On the counter under the window: a tray, and beside it the three bags. Two birds on the yard wall, seen through the window. Abuela at the counter. The frog on his stool.

1. NARR: "Vamos al parque." NARR: "Abuela busca comida para el parque." The basket twinkles: NARR: "Aquí hay pan." The drawer twinkles: NARR: "Y aquí hay fruta." NARR: "Hay guineos." NARR: "Y hay manzanas."

**The opening** (big sign)

2. **Touch a thing, taught.** Abuela sets a roll and a plate of fruit (a guineo and a manzana) on the tray. She smells the roll; during it: NARR: "Abuela huele el pan." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Este pan no huele bien." NARR: "Abuela dice: Este pan es para los pájaros." Sign on the roll. He touches it; the frog pushes it out of the window onto the yard wall; during it: NARR: "Coquí tira el pan." A bird lands on it. NARR: "El pájaro come pan."
3. Abuela puts the plate of fruit aside, takes a new plate of fruit and a new roll to the tray, and smells the plate. NARR: "Abuela huele la fruta." NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta fruta no huele bien." Sign on the plate. He touches it. NARR: "Coquí tira la fruta." NARR: "El pájaro come fruta."
4. **Picture bubbles, taught.** Abuela goes to the fridge and bends over the drawer; the door is half shut, and we cannot see in. NARR: "Abuela dice: Este guineo no huele bien." Two picture bubbles: a guineo, a manzana. Sign on the guineo. He touches it; the frog hops to the drawer, comes out with a guineo and pushes it out of the window. NARR: "Coquí tira el guineo." NARR: "El pájaro come guineo."
5. NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta manzana no huele bien." Sign on the manzana. NARR: "Coquí tira la manzana." NARR: "El pájaro come manzana."

**The turn**

6. Luis and Carmen run in with caps on. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos al parque!" Three more birds land on the wall. Abuela takes down the last rolls and the last fruit. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hay pan y hay fruta para el parque." The card shows. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela smells both things of a lot, with the same face for each, then turns to the bags and speaks over her shoulder.

7. **Decision A, Touch (bread or fruit).** On the tray: a roll and a plate of fruit. NARR: "Abuela dice: El pan no huele bien." (Or: "La fruta no huele bien." Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it out; a bird has it. Abuela puts the other one by the bags. A star.
   - Wrong: the good one goes out, and the birds are on it at once. Abuela throws a hand up and turns over the one that is left: green spots underneath. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pan no huele bien." (Or: "La fruta no huele bien.") The sign is on it; he touches it; the frog pushes it out too. Nothing from this lot goes by the bags.
8. **Decision B, Picture bubbles (in the drawer).** Abuela bends over the drawer; the door is half shut. NARR: "Abuela dice: La manzana no huele bien." (Or: "El guineo no huele bien." Drawn.) Picture bubbles: a guineo, a manzana. The frog fetches the one he chose and pushes it out.
   - Right: abuela brings the other one to the bags. A star.
   - Wrong: the birds are on the good one at once. Abuela holds the other one up: brown spots. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. La manzana no huele bien." (Or: "El guineo…") The sign is on its bubble; he touches it; the frog pushes it out too.
9. **Decision C, Touch (a roll or one fruit).** On the tray: a roll and one fruit (a guineo or a manzana, drawn). Abuela names the old one (drawn): "El pan no huele bien." / "El guineo no huele bien." / "La manzana no huele bien."
   - As in A.

**Count:** three lots, six things. The three old ones always go out. Good things by the bags: three, less one for each miss. The birds get three to six things.

**The ending.** Abuela packs what is by the bags: the first into Luis's bag, the second into Carmen's, the third into the frog's. NARR: "Luis y Carmen van al parque." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, abuela!"
- Nothing wrong (three stars): each bag has something in it. Stars and a heart.
- Otherwise: the birds still peck at the good roll or fruit on the wall; the frog's bag is empty (one miss), and Carmen's (two), and Luis's (three), and its owner looks into it.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** pan 7 (step 1; step 2 five times; step 6), fruta 6 (step 1; step 3 four times; step 6), guineo 4 (steps 1 and 4), manzana 4 (steps 1 and 5).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing abuela named.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two each. "El" and "la" go with the noun (el pan, el guineo; la fruta, la manzana): a second cue, for somebody who already knows which is which.

## Story 32: Jugo de china

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** on the counter a net of chinas, a squeezer, a glass jug with four marks on its side, and a tray of four clear glasses. Papá, mamá and abuelo sit in the yard. Give everybody juice.
- **At stake:** the last jug: four cups' worth. Juice poured into a cup that is already full runs over onto the floor, and the jug is a mark lower. A full cup tipped into the sink is gone.
- **New words:** china, mantequilla, lleno, vacío.
- **Back again:** vaso, jugo, hace calor, hace, pone, pan (story 31), toma, mi, quiero, más, por favor, ahora, familia, gracias.
- **Heard along the way:** pon.

**Start:** the fridge is shut. On the counter: the net of chinas, the squeezer, the jug (empty), the tray of four clear glasses, two metal cups (a red one and a blue one), a loaf and the butter dish. Abuela at the counter; Luis and Carmen beside her. Papá, mamá and abuelo in the yard, seen through the window.

1. NARR: "Hace calor." Abuela squeezes chinas into the jug; during it: NARR: "Abuela hace jugo de china." The net twinkles: NARR: "Aquí hay chinas." The butter dish twinkles: NARR: "Y aquí hay mantequilla." Abuela butters a slice; during it: NARR: "Abuela pone mantequilla en el pan." NARR: "Luis come pan con mantequilla."

**The opening** (big sign; the glasses are clear, so the picture teaches the two words)

2. **Touch a thing, taught.** Two clear glasses stand on the counter, one full of juice, one empty. The full one twinkles: NARR: "Este vaso está lleno." The empty one twinkles: NARR: "Y este vaso está vacío." NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, pon jugo de china en el vaso vacío." Sign on the empty glass. He touches it; the frog pours from the jug. NARR: "Ahora el vaso está lleno."
3. **Touch a person, taught.** Papá and mamá come to the window, each with a clear glass. NARR: "Papá dice: Mi vaso está vacío." NARR: "Mamá dice: Mi vaso está lleno." Sign on papá. He touches him; the frog pours. NARR: "Papá toma jugo de china."
4. **One line, taught.** Abuelo's and abuela's clear glasses stand on the sill; abuelo's is full, abuela's is empty. NARR: "Mamá dice: El vaso de abuelo está lleno." Sign on abuela's glass. He touches it; the frog pours. NARR: "Ahora el vaso de abuela está lleno."
5. **To the sink, taught.** Papá's and mamá's glasses stand on the counter: papá's empty, mamá's full. NARR: "Abuela dice: El vaso de papá está vacío. El vaso de mamá está lleno." Sign on papá's glass. He touches it; the frog carries it to the sink and tips it over: nothing comes out. Abuela washes it. NARR: "Carmen dice: Quiero pan con mantequilla y jugo de china."

**The turn**

6. Papá carries the tray of clear glasses out to the yard; during it: NARR: "La familia toma jugo de china." Abuela squeezes the last chinas: the jug is full to its fourth mark, and the net is empty. Only the two metal cups are left: nobody can see into them from the side. Luis and Carmen take one each, and bread and butter, and go out to the yard. NARR: "Carmen come pan con mantequilla." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Más pan con mantequilla, por favor!" The card shows. The signs go.

**The game**

7. **Decision A, Touch a person (pour).** Luis and Carmen come in, each with his metal cup at his chest. NARR: "Luis dice: Mi vaso está vacío." NARR: "Carmen dice: Mi vaso está lleno." (Whose is empty, and who speaks first, drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pours; the jug is one mark down. A star.
   - Wrong: juice runs over that child's hand onto the floor: a puddle with drops. The jug is one mark down. A cross. The other child turns his cup over: nothing comes out. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El vaso de Luis está vacío." (Or: "…de Carmen…") The sign is on him; he touches him; the frog pours; a second mark down.
8. **Decision B, Touch a thing (one line).** Mamá, at the window, sets abuelo's and papá's metal cups on the sill (a grey one and a yellow one, each with its owner behind it); she looks down into them; the frog cannot. NARR: "Mamá dice: El vaso de abuelo está lleno." (Or: "…está vacío."; or of papá's. One of the two is full and one is empty. Drawn.) He touches a cup; the frog pours into it.
   - Right, wrong and the marks as in A; the puddle is on the counter under the sill and runs down the front of the cabinet. After a wrong one mamá tilts the empty cup towards us. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El vaso de papá está vacío." (The empty one, by its owner.)
9. **Decision C, Touch a thing (to the sink).** Luis's and Carmen's metal cups stand on the counter; the two are in the yard. Abuela looks into both and goes to the sink. NARR: "Abuela dice: El vaso de Carmen está vacío. El vaso de Luis está lleno." (Whose is empty, and which half she says first, drawn.) He touches a cup; the frog carries it to the sink and tips it over.
   - Right: nothing comes out. Abuela washes it. A star.
   - Wrong: the juice runs into the sink. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El vaso de Carmen está vacío." (The empty one.) The sign is on it; he touches it; the frog takes that one to the sink. The child whose juice is gone comes to the window and looks into the sink.

**Count:** four marks. A takes one or two; B takes one or two. Two, one or none are left. What is left goes, in this order: to the child whose cup was tipped out (only after a miss in C), to abuela, to the frog. A redo always has its mark: A and B together take four at most.

**The ending.** Abuela pours what is left. NARR: "Abuela come pan con mantequilla." COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!"
- Nothing wrong (three stars): abuela and the frog each have a glass of juice. Stars and a heart.
- Otherwise: the puddles stay, and the sink is orange; the frog has an empty glass (one miss), and abuela (two), and the child (three).

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lleno 6 (steps 2 to 5), vacío 5 (steps 2, 3 and 5). Not deciding, heard before the game: china 6 (steps 1 to 6), mantequilla 6 (steps 1, 5 and 6); mantequilla once more in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** A and C: vacío. B: the word mamá said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two each. In B, touching the cup of the man who was named is right for "vacío" and wrong for "lleno".

## For the owner, one line each

- "Busca" decides nothing in story 30, nor "china" and "mantequilla" in story 32; each is heard six times or more and tested the next day by its pair.
- "Nevera" is heard in story 30 as a helper word. It never decides.
- "No hay" is taught against "hay". Abuela said "No hay más." once in story 28.
- Old bread and fruit "no huele bien": the stories do not say "viejo" of food.
- The pairs for "manzana" and "china" use each other's fruit.

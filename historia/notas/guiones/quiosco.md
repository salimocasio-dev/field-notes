# El quiosco: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `quiosco`). Stories 66 to 68. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 65.

Scene words (page 02): trabajo, trabaja, maestra, doctor, cocinero, policía, estudiante, vendedor, ¿qué haces?, ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años.

The picture: the quiosco of the square, close. One view. A small eight-sided stand with a roof, open at the front: a counter with a coffee pot, cups, a glass dome for pastries and a tray; the vendedor stands behind it. In front of the counter four high stools in a row; at the left a small round table with two chairs (abuela's place); at the counter's right end a box (story 67). Paving; the square's trees and the clock tower far off.

People: the vendedor (an apron, a paper hat; he was heard in El reloj). New, in work clothes (story 66): the maestra (glasses, a pile of exercise books with an apple on top), the doctor (a white coat, a stethoscope round his neck), the cocinero (a tall white hat, a wooden spoon in his pocket), the policía (a blue uniform, a cap, a whistle). In story 67 nobody wears work clothes: the people are the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer and the joven of El parque. Story 68: Beto, Ana, Ana's mamá, and the niño and the niña of El patio.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 66: Un café para la maestra

- **Goal he can see:** under the glass dome on the counter, three quesitos, golden and shiny. The vendedor taps the dome and winks at the frog, Luis and Carmen: for the helpers, after the work.
- **At stake:** the quesitos (3). A cup taken to the wrong person: the one who is left waiting gets a quesito from the vendedor, to say sorry.
- **New words:** maestra, doctor, cocinero, policía.
- **Back again:** café, jugo, china, leche, vaso, taza, quiere, para, toma, aquí tiene, buenos días, buenas tardes, gracias, de nada, por favor, escuela, hospital, hace, comida, calle, cruza, espera, ¿algo más?, caliente, cuidado.

**Start:** midday. The vendedor behind his counter; the frog on the counter beside the tray; Luis and Carmen at abuela's table. The four stools are empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es mediodía. La gente viene al quiosco." **(mediodía: heard)** The maestra walks up with her books and sits on the first stool. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Buenas tardes, maestra!" Carmen jumps up and waves. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mira! Es mi maestra. La maestra viene de la escuela." Ask (signed, the maestra): "¿Dónde está la maestra?" He touches her: she lifts the apple.
2. The doctor walks up, his stethoscope swinging, and sits on the second stool. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Buenas tardes, doctor!" NARR: "Luis dice: El doctor viene del hospital." Ask (signed, the doctor): "¿Y el doctor? ¿Dónde está el doctor?" He touches him: the doctor listens to his own chest and smiles.
3. The cocinero and the policía come together; they take the last two stools. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: un cocinero. El cocinero hace comida." The cocinero twirls his spoon. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y un policía. El policía mira la calle." The policía touches his cap. Ask (signed, the cocinero): "¿Dónde está el cocinero?" He touches him. Ask (signed, the policía): "¿Y el policía?" He touches him: one short blow on the whistle.
4. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Coquí, ¿me ayudas?" **(me ayudas: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" The vendedor fills a cup and sets it on the frog's tray. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Un café para el policía." Ask (signed, the policía): "El café es para el policía. Toca al policía." He touches him: the frog slides along the counter with the tray. NARR: "Coquí dice: Aquí tiene." NARR: "El policía dice: Gracias." The vendedor fills a glass. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Y un jugo de china para el cocinero." Ask (signed, the cocinero): "¿Y el jugo? Toca al cocinero." He touches him: the glass goes to the cocinero.

**The turn**

5. The vendedor puts two more cups under the pot; the maestra and the doctor look at their watches. He taps the glass dome with the three quesitos and winks. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The vendedor fills a cup or a glass and says who it is for: one of the four, drawn; in round B another of the four. What is in it is drawn too (café, jugo de china, leche).

6. **Decision A, touch a person (the four on the stools; tests the word for the one named).** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Un café para la maestra." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog slides the tray to her. NARR: "Coquí dice: Aquí tiene." She takes the cup and nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog slides it to another. That one lifts the cup, stops and looks along the row; the one who was named holds out empty hands. NARR: "El vendedor dice: No, Coquí. Para la maestra." The vendedor lifts the dome and gives her a quesito, with a small bow. A cross. The sign is on the right one; he touches her: the cup goes to her.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game; another of the four).** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Y una leche para el doctor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The four finish, pay and get up. NARR: "La maestra dice: Gracias. Buenas tardes." The policía blows his whistle once for the frog and they go. The vendedor lifts the dome.
   - Three quesitos left: one for Luis, one for Carmen, one for the frog, which is as big as he is. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: Luis and Carmen take one each and each break off a piece for the frog.
   - One left: the vendedor cuts it in three.
9. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Quieres un quesito, Coquí?" **(quesito: heard; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: por favor (por favor)]: "¡Sí, por favor!" NARR: "La maestra va a la escuela. El doctor va al hospital. Y Coquí come."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** maestra 4, doctor 4, cocinero 4, policía 5 (steps 1 to 4; the ask's words count).
**A miss is filed under:** the word for the one who was named.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own. The clothes show who is who; only the word says who the cup is for.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): sí; por favor. ("Aquí tiene" is said by the frog's own voice, not by the learner: three turns would crowd the opening. To settle in the reviews.)

## Story 67: ¿Qué haces?

- **Goal he can see:** on the end of the counter, in the sun, three limbers in their little cups, frosty: the vendedor has taken them out of his cooler for the frog, Luis and Carmen, for when the box is empty.
- **At stake:** the limbers (3). Each time a thing goes to the wrong person and has to come back, one limber melts into its cup.
- **New words:** trabajo, trabaja, ¿qué haces?, vendedor, estudiante.
- **Back again:** maestra, doctor, cocinero, policía, escuela, hospital, tienda, farmacia, calle, soy, es, vende, caja, esto, aquí tiene, gracias, de nada, juega, joven, señor, señora, hombre, mujer, abuela, mucho, también.

**Start:** an afternoon with no work. The vendedor behind his counter with a box: over its edge hang a stethoscope and a strap; a tall white hat stands in it. Abuela at her table with a coffee; the frog on the table; Luis and Carmen beside her. Nobody wears work clothes.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira al vendedor. El vendedor trabaja en el quiosco." NARR: "El vendedor dice: Yo trabajo aquí. Soy vendedor. ¡Y tengo mucho trabajo!" He lifts the box onto the counter and shakes his head at it: the things people forgot. Ask (signed, the box): "¿Dónde está la caja?" He touches it: the box rattles.
2. A young woman sits down on a stool. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y tú, ¿qué haces?" NARR: "La joven dice: Soy estudiante. Voy a la escuela." The vendedor takes a thick book out of the box and holds it up. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Esto es de una estudiante." Ask (signed, the young woman): "¿Quién es estudiante?" He touches her: the frog carries the book over on his head. NARR: "La joven dice: ¡Gracias!"
3. The señor sits down beside her. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Qué haces?" NARR: "El señor dice: Soy cocinero. Trabajo en la escuela." The vendedor holds up the tall white hat. Ask (signed, the señor): "El señor es cocinero. Trabaja en la escuela. Toca al cocinero." He touches him: the frog brings the hat; the señor puts it on, and everybody claps.
4. Luis bends down to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: Y tú, Coquí, ¿qué haces? ¿Trabajas?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí no trabaja. ¡Coquí juega!"

**The turn**

5. The young woman and the señor go. Two more people walk up to the counter, and two more behind them. The vendedor looks into the box, and at the three limbers at the counter's end, in the sun. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Coquí, ¿me ayudas otra vez?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The vendedor holds up one thing from the box. Two people stand at the counter; abuela asks each what he does, and each says it. One of them is the thing's owner. Who the two are, what each does, and who speaks first are drawn, each round on its own.
Round A: the thing is the stethoscope, the pile of exercise books with the apple, or the cap with the whistle; the two are the señora and the hombre. The señora is a maestra or a policía; the hombre a doctor or a policía (never two of the same).
Round B: the thing is a school bag full of books, or a shop's apron; the two are the joven and the mujer. One is an estudiante, the other a vendedor (vendedora).

6. **Decision A, touch a person (two people; tests the words of story 66 in what a person says of himself).** The vendedor holds up the stethoscope. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Qué haces?" NARR: "La señora dice: Soy maestra. Trabajo en la escuela." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú?" NARR: "El hombre dice: Soy doctor. Trabajo en el hospital." (Drawn. The policía says: "Soy policía. Trabajo en la calle.")
   - Right: the frog carries the thing to its owner. NARR: "El hombre dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to the other, who turns it over, and gives it back. NARR: "La señora dice: No, no. Yo soy maestra." At the counter's end a limber sinks into its cup. A cross. The sign is on the right one; he touches him: the thing goes to its owner.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (two others; tests estudiante and vendedor).** The vendedor holds up the school bag. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Qué haces?" NARR: "El joven dice: Soy vendedor. Trabajo en la tienda." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú?" NARR: "La mujer dice: Soy estudiante. Voy a la escuela." (Drawn. The mujer as the seller says: "Soy vendedora. Trabajo en la tienda.")
   - Right and wrong as in A. The other one's line after a wrong move: "No, no. Yo soy vendedor." (or: "Yo soy estudiante.")

**The ending**

8. The box is empty; the vendedor turns it over and knocks on it. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¡Buen trabajo!" He pushes the limbers across.
   - Three limbers left: one each for the frog, Luis and Carmen; the frog's sticks to his tongue. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: Luis and Carmen have one each, and the frog licks both.
   - One left: the three of them take turns; the two melted cups are for Canelo.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Coquí trabaja también. ¡Trabaja en el quiosco!" The vendedor sets his paper hat on the frog's head. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Quieres un trabajo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡No, gracias!" **(limber: never said; the picture carries it)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** estudiante 3 (step 2), vendedor 3 in this story (step 1) and many times as the speaker's name in stories 63 and 66. Not deciding: trabaja 4, trabajo 4, ¿qué haces? 3 (steps 2, 3, 4) before the game, and twice more in each round.
**Forms:** trabajas counts with trabaja; vendedora with vendedor; "trabajo" is one clip for "I work" and "a job".
**A miss is filed under:** round A, the word for the owner (maestra, doctor or policía); round B, estudiante or vendedor, whichever the owner said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: nothing about the two shows what they do.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 4, 5, 9): no; sí; gracias.

## Story 68: ¿Cuántos años tienes?

- **Goal he can see:** the vendedor's tray: a row of small bizcochos, each with white icing, and beside it three more on a plate, set apart. A box of candles shaped like numbers. Beto, in a paper crown, cannot keep still.
- **At stake:** the three bizcochos on the plate. A candle with the wrong number pushed into a bizcocho: the child pulls it out, the bizcocho breaks, and the vendedor gives him a whole one from the plate.
- **New words:** ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años (años).
- **Back again:** uno to doce, the tens, niño, niña, amigo, hermano, mamá, abuela, tiene, también, ¿cuántos?, otro, toma, espera, quieres, sí, bizcocho (heard since story 2), pequeño, grande.

**Start:** afternoon. Paper chains on the quiosco. Beto on the middle stool in a paper crown; Ana and Carmen on the stools beside him; Luis standing; Ana's mamá and abuela at the table. The frog on the counter beside the tray and the box of number candles. The vendedor behind.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es el cumpleaños de Beto." **(cumpleaños: heard; the crown and the candles carry it)** NARR: "Ana dice: Beto, ¿cuántos años tienes?" Beto holds up one hand, all five fingers. NARR: "Beto dice: ¡Cinco! Tengo cinco años." The candle shaped like a 5 twinkles in the box. Ask (signed, the 5): "Beto tiene cinco años. Toca el cinco." He touches it: the frog pushes the 5 into a bizcocho and the vendedor sets it before Beto.
2. NARR: "Beto dice: ¿Y tú, Ana? ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "Ana dice: Tengo ocho años." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Yo también! Tengo ocho años." Ask (signed, the 8): "Ana tiene ocho años. ¿Dónde está el ocho?" He touches it: an 8 for Ana's bizcocho, and another 8 for Carmen's.
3. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo tengo diez años." He takes the 1 and the 0 himself. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, ¿cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Uy! Tengo setenta años." She holds a 7 and a 0 over one small bizcocho; it is too small for both, and everybody laughs. NARR: "La mamá de Ana dice: Y yo tengo treinta y ocho años."
4. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Cuántos años tienes?" COQUÍ [TURN: tres (tres)]: "¡Tres!" The frog holds up three fingers; he pushes a 3 into the smallest bizcocho.

**The turn**

5. The children of the square have seen the tray: a niño and a niña run up, and more behind them. The vendedor puts the plate of three whole bizcochos to one side and points at it, then at the frog, Luis and abuela. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Un bizcocho para cada niño. ¡Con sus años!" **(cada, sus: heard)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Ages are drawn from four to twelve, each round on its own (never the pairs dos and doce, seis and siete in one round).

6. **Decision A, touch a candle (three number candles on the tray; tests the number in "tengo … años").** The niño stands at the counter. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "El niño dice: Tengo nueve años." (Drawn.) Three candles lie on the tray: his number and two others, drawn.
   - Right: the frog pushes the candle into a bizcocho; the vendedor hands it over; the niño holds it up. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes in another number. The niño looks at it and shakes his head. NARR: "El niño dice: ¡No! Tengo nueve años." He pulls the candle out; the bizcocho breaks in two. Canelo eats the pieces. The vendedor takes a whole one from the plate. A cross. The sign is on the right candle; he touches it: the right number, and the niño has his bizcocho.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (one candle, two children; tests the same words the other way round).** One candle is left on the tray: a 7 (drawn). The niña and Ana's friend, another niño, stand at the counter. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "La niña dice: Tengo siete años." NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Y tú?" NARR: "El niño dice: Tengo once años." (Drawn; who speaks first is drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pushes the candle into a bizcocho and slides it to that child. A star.
   - Wrong: it goes to the other child, who shakes his head. NARR: "El niño dice: ¡No! Tengo once años." He pulls the candle out; the bizcocho breaks; Canelo. A whole one from the plate. A cross. The sign is on the right child; he touches her: the candle and the bizcocho go to her.

**The ending**

8. Everybody has a bizcocho. The vendedor lights Beto's 5 with a match; Beto blows it out with all his might, and his paper crown flies off.
   - Three on the plate: one for Luis, one for abuela, one for the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two on the plate: Luis and abuela; abuela breaks hers in two for the frog.
   - One on the plate: abuela cuts it in three with the vendedor's knife.
9. NARR: "Beto dice: Coquí, ¿quieres más bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" NARR: "Beto tiene cinco años. Y Coquí tiene tres."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tengo … años 6 (steps 1, 2, 3), ¿cuántos años tienes? 4 (steps 1 to 4).
**Forms:** "tiene … años" counts with "tengo … años".
**A miss is filed under:** tengo … años.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three in round A, one in two in round B. The numbers are El colmado's words; what is new is hearing them as an age.
**New facts about people (to keep from now on):** Beto is five today; Ana and Carmen are eight; Luis is ten; abuela is seventy; Ana's mamá is thirty-eight; the frog is three.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): tres; sí.

# El quiosco: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `quiosco`). Stories 66 to 68. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 65.

Scene words (page 02, as changed on Nov 21): trabajo, trabaja, maestra, doctor, cocinero, policía, estudiante, vendedor, ¿en qué trabajas?, ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años.
The plan had "¿qué haces?" for the question about work. Said bare, it is heard as "what are you doing?"; what people ask is "¿En qué trabajas?". The everyday one wins (page 16).

The picture: the quiosco of the square, close. One view. A small eight-sided stand with a roof, open at the front: a counter with a coffee pot, cups, a glass dome for pastries and a tray; the vendedor stands behind it. In front of the counter four high stools in a row; at the left a small round table with two chairs (abuela's place); at the counter's right end a box with a lid (story 67). Paving; the square's trees and the clock tower far off.

People: the vendedor (an apron, a paper hat; he was heard in El reloj). New, in work clothes (story 66): the maestra (glasses, a pile of exercise books with an apple on top), the doctor (a white coat, a stethoscope round his neck), the cocinero (a tall white hat, a wooden spoon in his pocket), the policía (a blue uniform, a cap, a whistle). In story 67 nobody wears work clothes: the people are the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer and the joven of El parque, and one new one, a young woman (la joven). Story 68: Beto, Ana, Ana's mamá, and the niño and the niña of El patio.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 66: Un café para la maestra

- **Goal he can see:** under the glass dome on the counter, three quesitos, golden and shiny. The vendedor taps the dome and winks at the frog, Luis and Carmen: for the helpers, after the work.
- **At stake:** the quesitos (3). A cup taken to the wrong person is pushed back and spills; the vendedor takes a quesito from under the dome and sets it on a saucer for the one who was left waiting.
- **New words:** maestra, doctor, cocinero, policía.
- **Back again:** café, jugo, china, leche, vaso, quiere, quiero, para, aquí tiene, buenas tardes, hola, gracias, escuela, hospital, hace, comida, carro, viene, va, mira, ¿quién?, sí, come, ahora, tú.

**Start:** midday. The vendedor behind his counter; the frog on the counter beside the tray; Luis and Carmen at the round table. The four stools are empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es mediodía. La gente viene al quiosco." **(mediodía: heard)** The maestra walks up with her books and sits on the first stool. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Buenas tardes, maestra!" Carmen jumps up and waves. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mira! Es mi maestra. La maestra viene de la escuela." Ask (signed, the maestra): "¿Dónde está la maestra?" He touches her: she lifts the apple.
2. The doctor walks up, his stethoscope swinging, and sits on the second stool. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Buenas tardes, doctor!" NARR: "Luis dice: El doctor viene del hospital." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Hola, doctor!" The doctor listens to his own chest and smiles.
3. The cocinero and the policía come together; they take the last two stools. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira, un cocinero! El cocinero hace la comida." The cocinero twirls his spoon. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y un policía. El policía mira los carros." The policía touches his cap. Ask (signed, the cocinero): "¿Quién es el cocinero?" He touches him: the spoon again.
4. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Coquí, ¿me ayudas?" **(me ayudas: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" The vendedor fills a cup and sets it on the frog's tray. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Un café para el policía." Ask (signed, the policía): "¿Quién es el policía?" He touches him: the frog slides along the counter with the tray. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" NARR: "El policía dice: Gracias." He drinks it in one go, blows his whistle once, and hands the cup back.

**The turn**

5. All four sit with empty hands. The vendedor sets out a row of cups, taps the glass dome with the three quesitos, and winks. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The vendedor fills a cup or a glass and says who it is for: one of the four, drawn, each round on its own (round B's can be the same one again). What is in it is drawn too (un café, un jugo de china, un vaso de leche). The one who is served drinks and hands the cup back, so nobody is left holding one.

6. **Decision A, touch a person (the four on the stools; tests the word for the one named).** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Un café para la maestra." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog slides the tray to her. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She takes the cup and nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog slides it to another, who pushes the tray back: the cup tips over and the coffee runs along the counter. The one who was named holds out empty hands. NARR: "El vendedor dice: No, Coquí. El café es para la maestra." He wipes the counter, lifts the dome, and sets a quesito on a saucer for her: two are left under the glass. A cross. He fills another cup. The sign is on the right one; he touches her: the cup goes to her.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game).** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Y un vaso de leche para el doctor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The four pay and get up. NARR: "La maestra dice: Gracias. Buenas tardes." The policía blows his whistle once for the frog and they go. The vendedor lifts the dome.
   - Three quesitos left: one for Luis, one for Carmen, one for the frog, which is as big as he is. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: Luis and Carmen take one each and each break off a piece for the frog.
   - One left: the vendedor cuts it in three.
9. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Quieres un quesito, Coquí?" **(quesito: heard; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "La maestra va a la escuela. El doctor va al hospital. Y Coquí come."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** maestra 4 (step 1), doctor 3 (step 2), cocinero 3 (step 3), policía 4 (steps 3, 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the word for the one who was named.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own. The clothes show who is who; only the word says who the cup is for. Nobody holds a cup when a round starts.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): sí; quiero. ("¡Aquí tiene!" is said in the frog's own voice.)

## Story 67: ¿En qué trabajas?

- **Goal he can see:** on the end of the counter, in the sun, three limbers in their little cups, frosty: the vendedor has taken them out of his cooler for when the box is empty.
- **At stake:** the limbers (3). Each time a thing goes to the wrong person and has to come back, one limber melts into its cup.
- **New words:** trabajo, trabaja, ¿en qué trabajas?, vendedor, estudiante.
- **Back again:** maestra, doctor, cocinero, policía, escuela, hospital, tienda, plaza, soy, es, caja, hay, eso, aquí, gracias, toma, quieres, juega, joven, señor, señora, hombre, mujer, abuela, mucho, también, voy.

**Start:** an afternoon with no work. The vendedor behind his counter with a box, its lid on. Abuela at her table with a coffee; the frog on the table; Luis and Carmen beside her. Nobody wears work clothes.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira al vendedor. El vendedor trabaja en el quiosco." NARR: "El vendedor dice: Yo trabajo aquí. Soy vendedor. ¡Y tengo mucho trabajo!" He lifts the box onto the counter and takes off its lid, shaking his head: the things people forgot. Ask (signed, the box): "¿Qué hay en la caja?" He touches it: the frog hops over, looks in, and lifts out a thick book.
2. A young man sits down on a stool. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y tú, ¿en qué trabajas?" NARR: "El joven dice: No trabajo. Soy estudiante. Voy a la universidad." **(universidad: heard)** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Un estudiante! Muy bien." The frog looks at the book, and at him. Ask (signed, the young man): "¿Quién es el estudiante?" He touches him: the frog carries the book over on his head. COQUÍ [TURN: toma (toma)]: "¡Toma!" NARR: "El joven dice: ¡Mi libro! ¡Gracias, Coquí!" **(libro: heard)**
3. The señor sits down beside him. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y usted? ¿En qué trabaja?" NARR: "El señor dice: Soy cocinero. Trabajo en la escuela." The frog lifts a tall white hat out of the box. Ask (signed, the señor): "¿Quién trabaja en la escuela?" He touches him: the frog brings the hat; the señor puts it on, and everybody claps.
4. Luis bends down to the frog. NARR: "Luis dice: Y tú, Coquí, ¿en qué trabajas?" The frog shakes his head. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí no trabaja. ¡Coquí juega!"

**The turn**

5. The young man and the señor go. Four people walk up to the counter: the señora and the hombre, and behind them the mujer and a young woman. The vendedor looks at the three limbers at the counter's end, in the sun, and at the box. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The frog lifts one thing out of the box (it shows over his head; the people at the counter are talking to abuela and do not see it). Abuela asks the two at the counter what they do, and each says it. One of them is the thing's owner. Who is what, and who speaks first, is drawn, each round on its own.
Round A: the thing is a stethoscope, a pile of exercise books with an apple, or a cap with a whistle. The two are the señora and the hombre. Each can be any of the three (maestra or maestro, doctora or doctor, policía), never both the same; one of them is what the thing needs.
Round B: the thing is a school bag full of books, or a shop's apron. The two are the mujer and the young woman. One is an estudiante, the other a vendedora.

6. **Decision A, touch a person (two people; tests the words of story 66 in what a person says of himself).** The frog holds up the stethoscope. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y usted? ¿En qué trabaja?" NARR: "La señora dice: Soy doctora. Trabajo en el hospital." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y usted?" NARR: "El hombre dice: Soy maestro. Trabajo en la escuela." (Drawn. The policía says: "Soy policía. Trabajo aquí, en la plaza.")
   - Right: the frog carries the thing to its owner. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" A star.
   - Wrong: the frog carries it to the other, who turns it over, and gives it back. NARR: "El hombre dice: No, eso no es mío. Yo soy maestro." **(mío: heard)** At the counter's end a limber sinks into its cup. A cross. The sign is on the right one; he touches her: the thing goes to its owner.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (two others; tests estudiante and vendedor).** The frog holds up the school bag. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y tú, ¿en qué trabajas?" NARR: "La joven dice: No trabajo. Soy estudiante." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y usted?" NARR: "La mujer dice: Soy vendedora. Trabajo en una tienda." (Drawn. To the mujer abuela says "¿Y usted? ¿En qué trabaja?", to the young woman "Y tú, ¿en qué trabajas?"; the second one asked hears only "¿Y usted?" or "¿Y tú?".)
   - Right and wrong as in A. The other one's line after a wrong move: "No, eso no es mío. Yo soy vendedora." (or: "Yo soy estudiante.")

**The ending**

8. The box is empty; the vendedor turns it over and knocks on it. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¡Buen trabajo!" He sets his paper hat on the frog's head and pushes the limbers across.
   - Three limbers left: a red one, a yellow one and a white one in a row before the frog; his tongue sticks to the first. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: two, and a cup of juice.
   - One left: one; Canelo laps at the two melted cups.
9. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Quieres un limber, Coquí?" **(limber: heard; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, sí gracias)]: "¡Sí, gracias!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Mira! Ahora Coquí también trabaja."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** estudiante 3 (step 2), vendedor 3 in this story (step 1) and many times as the speaker's name in stories 63 and 66. Not deciding: trabaja 4, trabajo 4, ¿en qué trabajas? 3 with "¿en qué trabaja?" (steps 2, 3, 4) before the game.
**Forms:** trabajas counts with trabaja; "¿en qué trabaja?" with "¿en qué trabajas?"; vendedora with vendedor; maestro with maestra; doctora with doctor; "trabajo" is one clip for "I work" and "a job".
**A miss is filed under:** round A, the word for the owner (maestra, doctor or policía); round B, estudiante or vendedor, whichever the owner said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: nothing about the two shows what they do, and either of them can be anything.
**What round A tests:** story 66's words, said by people about themselves, with the place they work in beside them. The words new in this story decide in round B only.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): toma; gracias.

## Story 68: ¿Cuántos años tienes?

- **Goal he can see:** on a cake stand on the counter, three small bizcochos with white icing, one on top of another: Beto's tower. Beside it a tray of more bizcochos, and a box of candles shaped like numbers. Beto, in a paper crown, cannot keep still.
- **At stake:** the bizcochos of Beto's tower (3). A candle with the wrong number pushed into a child's bizcocho: the child pulls it out, the bizcocho breaks, and the vendedor has to give him a whole one from the tower.
- **New words:** ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años (años).
- **Back again:** uno to doce, the tens, niño, niña, hermano, hermana, mamá, abuela, tiene, tengo, también, ¿dónde está?, quieres, un poco, sí, no, bizcocho (heard since story 2).

**Start:** afternoon. Paper chains on the quiosco. Beto on the middle stool in a paper crown; Ana and Carmen on the stools beside him; Luis standing; Ana's mamá and Luis's abuela at the table. The frog on the counter beside the tray and the box of number candles. The vendedor behind.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es el cumpleaños de Beto." **(cumpleaños: heard; the crown and the candles carry it)** NARR: "El vendedor dice: Beto, ¿cuántos años tienes?" Beto holds up one hand, all five fingers. NARR: "Beto dice: ¡Cinco! Tengo cinco años." The candle shaped like a 5 twinkles in the box. Ask (signed, the 5): "¿Dónde está el cinco?" He touches it: the frog pushes the 5 into the top of Beto's tower.
2. NARR: "Beto dice: ¿Y tú, Ana? ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "Ana dice: Tengo ocho años." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Yo también! Tengo ocho años." Ask (signed, the 8): "¿Y dónde está el ocho?" He touches it: an 8 for Ana's bizcocho, and another 8 for Carmen's.
3. NARR: "Luis dice: Yo tengo diez años. Y tengo una hermana." He points at Carmen, and takes the 1 and the 0 himself. NARR: "Luis dice: Abuela, ¿cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Tengo setenta años." She holds a 7 and a 0 over one small bizcocho; it is too small for both, and everybody laughs. NARR: "La mamá de Ana dice: Y yo tengo treinta y ocho años."
4. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Cuántos años tienes?" The frog holds up three fingers. COQUÍ [TURN: tres (tres)]: "¡Tres!" He pushes a 3 into the smallest bizcocho.

**The turn**

5. The children of the square have seen the tray: a niño and a niña run up, and more behind them. The vendedor points at the tray, and at Beto's tower: that one is Beto's. NARR: "El vendedor dice: Un bizcocho para cada niño. ¡Con su número!" **(cada, número: heard)** Beto puts both arms round his tower. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child at the counter says how old he is, and how many brothers or sisters he has (which is said first is drawn). Three number candles lie on the tray: his years, his brothers or sisters, and one more number. Years are drawn from five to twelve; brothers or sisters from two to four; the third candle is another number from one to twelve; never the pairs seis and siete, once and doce on one tray. The tray is cleared after each round. Each answer is ONE clip (both sentences): when he waits, the whole answer is said again, not its second half.

6. **Decision A, touch a candle (three candles; tests años).** The niño stands at the counter. NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "El niño dice: Tengo nueve años. Y tengo dos hermanos." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pushes the candle into a bizcocho from the tray; the vendedor hands it over; the niño holds it up. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes in another number. The niño looks at it and shakes his head. NARR: "El niño dice: ¡No! Tengo nueve años." He pulls the candle out; the bizcocho breaks in two, and Canelo eats the pieces. The vendedor sighs and takes a whole one from Beto's tower. A cross. The sign is on the right candle; he touches it: the right number, and the niño has his bizcocho.
7. **Decision B, touch a candle (the same game; the niña).** NARR: "El vendedor dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Cuántos años tienes?" NARR: "La niña dice: Tengo tres hermanas. Y tengo siete años." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Every child has a bizcocho. The vendedor lights the 5 on Beto's tower with a match; Beto blows it out with all his might, and his paper crown flies off.
   - Three in the tower: it stands as high as Beto's nose. Stars and a heart.
   - Two in the tower.
   - One: a single bizcocho with its 5.
9. NARR: "Beto dice: Coquí, ¿quieres bizcocho?" COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco)]: "¡Un poco!" Beto breaks off a piece as big as the frog. NARR: "Beto tiene cinco años. Y Coquí tiene tres."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tengo … años 6 and "tiene … años" (steps 1, 2, 3), ¿cuántos años tienes? 4 (steps 1 to 4).
**Forms:** "tiene … años" counts with "tengo … años".
**A miss is filed under:** tengo … años.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own. Two numbers are said in every answer; only "años" says which one is his age.
**New facts about people (to keep from now on):** Beto is five today; Ana and Carmen are eight; Luis is ten; abuela is seventy; Ana's mamá is thirty-eight; the frog is three.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): tres; un poco.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 66: the picture marked who was still to be served (two held drinks, two looked at their watches), so round A was one in two and round B was certain: now nobody holds a cup when a round starts, the watches are gone, and each round is drawn from all four. A quesito given "to say sorry" looked like a present: the cup is pushed back and spills, and the dome is seen to go from three to two. "Una leche" is not an order: "un vaso de leche". "Para la maestra." was a fragment. "¿Y el doctor? ¿Dónde está el doctor?" asked twice to make up a count: Carmen greets him. Fewer touch-the-person asks in a row.
- 67: "¿Qué haces?" said bare is "what are you doing?": the question is "¿En qué trabajas?", and to a grown stranger abuela says "¿Y usted? ¿En qué trabaja?". Round A gave itself away (the señora was never the doctor, the hombre never the maestra): either can be anything. Why would the owner not just say "that is mine"? Now the frog lifts the thing out of the box where the two at the counter do not see it, and they are only talking to abuela. The box showed a stethoscope from the start and was "empty" with things still in it: it has a lid. A grown-up who is a student does not go to "la escuela": "Voy a la universidad", and the student answers "No trabajo. Soy estudiante." "Trabajo en la calle" is also the street life: "Trabajo aquí, en la plaza". Two turns stood almost back to back, and the turn "No, gracias" to a job muddled the ending: the turns are "toma" and "gracias" ("de nada" and "por favor" have never been a story's new word, so they cannot be a turn's word; the builder's check found it).
- 68: only the number decided, and numbers are old words: now each child says two numbers ("Tengo nueve años. Y tengo dos hermanos."), and only "años" says which is his age. Round B's tray still held round A's candles: it is cleared. "¡Con sus años!" is what is said of old people: "¡Con su número!". Ana would not ask her own brother his age on his birthday: the vendedor asks. Nothing set up the frog's "tres": he holds up three fingers as the question lands. The three stories all ended with food shared three ways: 68 ends on Beto's tower and 67 on the frog's own limbers.

Left, and why:
- The island often says "¿En qué tú trabajas?", "¿Cuántos años tú tienes?". Both ways are said; the plain form is kept, as in El banco de la plaza.
- 67's round A is decided by story 66's words and by escuela, hospital and plaza. That is on purpose: the new frame ("Soy ... Trabajo en ...") is heard in every line, and round B is decided by the new words.
- "Ahora Coquí también trabaja" after "Coquí no trabaja": he has just worked, and he has the vendedor's hat on.
- Length: 67 has the most lines. The second question of each round is two words; the real number comes from machine play.

# El baño: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `bano`). Stories 99 to 101. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 98.

Scene words (page 02): baño, ducha, jabón, cepillo, dientes, espejo, peine, se baña, se lava, limpio, sucio, después (después is his already: La calle).
How they are shared out: 99 sucio, limpio, se lava, jabón; 100 baño, ducha, se baña, espejo; 101 cepillo, dientes, peine.

The picture: the bathroom, in daylight. One view. The back wall, tiled, faces us: at the left the shower (an open tiled corner with a shower head, a tap and a curtain drawn back); in the middle the sink under a mirror, with a shelf beside it; on the wall a small water heater with a gauge of three red bars; at the right the door. A stool; a basket for the washing; a towel rail. Nothing over anyone's head.

People: Luis, Carmen, abuela, abuelo, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 99: Limpio y sucio

- **Goal he can see:** on the sink, a big new bar of soap. Abuela taps it, and then the frog's muddy feet: afterwards, a bath for him.
- **At stake:** the soap (3 sizes). A dirty towel puts the mud back on washed hands: they are washed again, and the bar gets smaller.
- **New words:** sucio, limpio, se lava, jabón.
- **Back again:** mano, cara, pie, toalla, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, agua, caliente, frío, un poco, mucho, mira, ahora, ya, mi, con, por favor, no, sí.

**Start:** Luis and Carmen come in at the door from the patio with Canelo: mud on Luis's hands, on Carmen's face, on Canelo, on the frog's feet. Abuela at the sink with two folded towels over her arm.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Luis está sucio. Carmen está sucia. ¡Y Canelo!" Luis holds up his hands. Ask (signed, Luis's hands): "¿Dónde están las manos sucias de Luis?" He touches them: mud drips.
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, el jabón, por favor." Ask (signed, the soap): "¿Dónde está el jabón?" He touches it: the frog pushes it to Luis; bubbles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se lava las manos con jabón." The hands come out bright. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira! Mis manos están limpias."
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen se lava la cara." Bubbles over her face; then it shines. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ya! Mi cara está limpia." Ask (signed, Carmen's face): "¿Dónde está la cara limpia?" He touches it: a sparkle.
4. Abuela holds out her two towels, a green one and a yellow one; she smells the green one and wrinkles her nose. NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla verde está sucia. La toalla amarilla está limpia." Ask (signed, the yellow towel): "¿Cuál toalla está limpia?" He touches it: the frog carries it to Carmen; she dries her face. The green one goes into the basket. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Estás sucio?" The frog looks at his feet. COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco, sí un poco, un poquito)]: "¡Un poco!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Después, Coquí se lava los pies. Con mucho jabón."

**The turn**

5. Abuela takes Canelo into the shower and washes him, her back to the room. The children run out to the patio, and come in again with mud on their hands. Two folded towels lie on the stool. The big bar of soap shines on the sink. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child (drawn) washes his hands at the sink and holds them out, wet. On the stool lie two folded towels of two colours (drawn each round from rojo, azul, verde, amarillo and blanco; round B's are not round A's). They look the same: folded, no marks. Abuela, from the shower, says in one breath which is clean and which is dirty (which is said first is drawn). The frog carries one over.

6. **Decision A, touch a towel (two; tests limpia against sucia).** NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla azul está limpia. La toalla roja está sucia." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the child dries his hands: clean. A star.
   - Wrong: the child dries his hands: mud on them again, and on his nose. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! La toalla roja está sucia." He washes again; the bar of soap gets smaller. A cross. The sign is on the right towel; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a towel (the same game; the other child, two other colours).** NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla blanca está sucia. La toalla verde está limpia." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog's turn: he sits in the sink, with what is left of the soap.
   - The big bar: a mountain of foam, and a hat of foam on the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Half a bar: foam to his chin.
   - A sliver: a few bubbles.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Agua fría o agua caliente, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: caliente (caliente, agua caliente)]: "¡Caliente, por favor!" NARR: "Coquí se lava con jabón. Ahora está limpio."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** sucio 3 with sucia and sucias (steps 1, 4), limpio 5 with limpia and limpias (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: se lava 3 (steps 2, 3, 4), jabón 4 (steps 2, 4).
**Forms:** sucia, sucias count with sucio; limpia, limpias with limpio.
**A miss is filed under:** sucio (he took the towel that was called dirty).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the towels differ only in colour, and both colours are named.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): un poco; caliente.

## Story 100: Se baña

- **Goal he can see:** on the wall, the water heater's gauge: three red bars. Abuelo, in his robe with a towel over his shoulder, taps it and points at himself: hot water for his shower, afterwards.
- **At stake:** the hot water (3 bars). The wrong water, run with nobody under it, wastes a bar.
- **New words:** baño, ducha, se baña, espejo.
- **Back again:** se lava, sucio, limpio, mano, cara, agua, caliente, frío, ocupado, después, para mí, yo, mira, hola, ahora, por favor, no, sí.

**Start:** morning. Abuela and the frog in the bathroom; the frog on the shelf between the shower's tap and the sink's. Luis in the door, mud all over him. Carmen behind him, paint on her hands.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: el baño. Aquí está la ducha." Ask (signed, the shower): "¿Dónde está la ducha del baño?" He touches it: water sprays for a moment; the frog jumps.
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis está sucio. Luis se baña en la ducha." Ask (signed, the shower): "Luis se baña. ¿Dónde pones el agua?" He touches it: the shower runs; Luis steps in behind the curtain; steam.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen no se baña. Carmen se lava las manos." Ask (signed, the sink): "Carmen se lava las manos. ¿Dónde pones el agua?" He touches it: the sink's tap runs; she washes. She looks up. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira el espejo, Coquí. ¿Quién es?" **(quién: heard)** The frog sees a frog in the mirror. COQUÍ [TURN: yo (yo, soy yo)]: "¡Yo!"
4. Abuelo comes to the door in his robe. NARR: "Abuela dice: El baño está ocupado, abuelo." He taps the heater's gauge: three red bars. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo me baño después. Agua caliente para mí, por favor." **(me baño: a form of se baña)**

**The turn**

5. All morning the children run in and out with Canelo. Abuelo waits on a chair outside the door with his towel. The gauge shows three bars. The frog sits between the two taps. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child (drawn) comes in wrapped in a big towel from neck to feet, so that nothing shows of how dirty he is, and stands in the middle, as far from the shower as from the sink. Abuela, in the door with the washing in her arms, says what the child does (drawn, each round on its own): he has a shower, or he washes his hands. The frog runs the shower, or the sink's tap.

6. **Decision A, touch a tap (the shower, the sink; tests se baña against se lava).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se baña." (Or, drawn: "Luis se lava las manos.")
   - Right: the water runs, and the child goes to it. A star.
   - Wrong: the wrong water runs, hot, with nobody under it; the child stands and waits. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Luis se baña." A red bar on the gauge goes out. A cross. The sign is on the right tap; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a tap (the same game; the other child).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen se lava las manos." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuelo's turn, behind the curtain.
   - Three bars: steam, and he sings. Stars and a heart.
   - Two bars: a little steam; he hums.
   - One bar: no steam; a yelp, and then he laughs.
9. Abuelo comes out, combed, and stands before the mirror. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Mira el espejo, Coquí. ¿Estoy limpio?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" NARR: "Abuelo se baña en la ducha del baño. Ahora está limpio."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se baña 4 (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: baño 3 (steps 1, 4), ducha 3 (steps 1, 2), espejo 1 before the game (step 3) and 1 in the ending (to fix in the reviews: a third).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se baña or se lava).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): yo; sí.

## Story 101: Los dientes

- **Goal he can see:** on the shelf, a tube of toothpaste, nearly empty: three squeezes are left in it (three marks). Beside it, the frog's own tiny toothbrush. Carmen taps both: afterwards.
- **At stake:** the toothpaste (3 squeezes). A wrong thing, used with eyes shut, spoils the brush: it is washed, and a new squeeze goes on it.
- **New words:** cepillo, dientes, peine.
- **Back again:** espejo, se lava, jabón, toalla, pelo, boca, ojo, aquí, para, con, tiene, mi, ahora, por favor, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** morning. Carmen stands on the stool at the sink, before the mirror. On the shelf, in a row: a toothbrush, a comb, the soap, a small towel; and the tube, and the frog's tiny brush. The frog on the shelf's end. Abuela in the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen se lava los dientes." Carmen shows her teeth to the mirror. Ask (signed, Carmen): "¿Dónde están los dientes de Carmen?" He touches her: she grins; a sparkle.
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, el cepillo, por favor." Ask (signed, the toothbrush): "¿Dónde está el cepillo de dientes?" He touches it: the frog pushes it over; she squeezes the tube, and brushes: foam. NARR: "Carmen dice: Con el cepillo me lavo los dientes." **(me lavo: a form of se lava)**
3. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora, el peine." Ask (signed, the comb): "¿Dónde está el peine?" He touches it: the frog pushes it over; she combs her hair. NARR: "Abuela dice: El cepillo es para los dientes. El peine es para el pelo."
4. Luis comes in and washes his face; he gets soap in his eyes, shuts them tight, and feels about with one hand. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ay! ¡Jabón en los ojos! ¿Dónde está mi peine?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aquí está)]: "¡Aquí!" The frog pushes the comb into his hand.

**The turn**

5. Both children wash their faces at the sink, their eyes shut tight against the soap, a hand held out. Carmen has tapped the tube and the frog's tiny brush: afterwards. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** On the shelf lie four things: the toothbrush, the comb, the soap, the small towel (their order is drawn each round). A child (drawn), with his eyes shut, asks for the toothbrush or the comb (drawn; round B's is not round A's). The frog pushes one thing into his hand.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (four on the shelf; tests cepillo against peine).** NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, el peine, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Coquí, el cepillo, por favor.")
   - Right: he uses it: his hair lies flat (or his teeth shine). A star.
   - Wrong: he uses what he gets, without looking: the toothbrush goes through his hair, the comb (or the soap, or the towel) into his mouth. He opens one eye. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡El peine!" The brush has to be washed, and a new squeeze of the tube goes on it: one mark less. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the other child, the other thing).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, el cepillo, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog's turn: Carmen squeezes what is left onto his tiny brush.
   - Three squeezes: a fat stripe; a beard of foam. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: a stripe; foam on his lips.
   - One: a dot.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tú tienes dientes?" The frog opens his mouth wide: none. COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" They laugh. NARR: "Coquí no tiene dientes. Se lava la boca con su cepillo."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cepillo 4 (steps 2, 3), peine 4 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: dientes 5 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four; one in two for a learner who has jabón and toalla.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): aquí; no.

# El baño: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `bano`). Stories 99 to 101. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 98.

Scene words (page 02): baño, ducha, jabón, cepillo, dientes, espejo, peinilla, se baña, se lava, limpio, sucio (the plan had peine and después: see the reviews; después is his already).
How they are shared out: 99 sucio, limpio, se lava, jabón; 100 baño, ducha, se baña, espejo; 101 cepillo, dientes, peinilla.

The picture: the bathroom, in daylight. One view. The back wall, tiled, faces us: at the left the shower (an open tiled corner with a low wall in front, a shower head and a tap: whoever stands in it is seen from the shoulders up); in the middle the sink under a mirror, with a shelf beside it; on the wall a small water heater with a gauge of three red bars; at the right the door. A stool; a basket for the washing; a towel rail. Nothing over anyone's head.

People: Luis, Carmen, abuela, abuelo, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask: the sign shows what to touch, and the voice does not ask again.

## Story 99: Limpio y sucio

- **Goal he can see:** on the sink, a big new bar of soap. Abuela taps it, and then the frog's muddy feet: afterwards, a bath for him.
- **At stake:** the soap (3 sizes). A dirty towel puts the mud back on washed hands: they are washed again, and the bar gets smaller.
- **New words:** sucio, limpio, se lava, jabón.
- **Back again:** mano, cara, pie, toalla, rojo, azul, blanco, verde, amarillo, agua, caliente, frío, un poco, mucho, tengo, mira, ahora, ya, con, después, por favor, no, sí.

**Start:** Luis and Carmen come in at the door from the patio with Canelo: mud on Luis's hands, on Carmen's face, on Canelo, on the frog's feet. Abuela at the sink with two folded towels over her arm.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Luis está sucio. Carmen está sucia. ¡Y Canelo!" Luis holds up his hands. Ask (signed, Luis's hands): "¿Dónde están las manos sucias?" He touches them: mud drips.
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, el jabón, por favor." (signed, the soap.) He touches it: the frog pushes it to Luis; bubbles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se lava las manos con jabón." The hands come out bright. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira! Tengo las manos limpias."
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen se lava la cara." Bubbles over her face; then it shines. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Ya! Tengo la cara limpia."
4. Abuela holds out her two towels, a green one and a yellow one; she smells the green one and wrinkles her nose. NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla verde está sucia. La toalla amarilla está limpia." Ask (signed, the yellow towel): "¿Cuál toalla está limpia?" He touches it: the frog carries it to Carmen; she dries her face. The green one goes into the basket.
5. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Estás sucio?" The frog looks at his feet. COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco, sí un poco, un poquito)]: "¡Un poco!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Después te lavas los pies. Con mucho jabón." **(te lavas: a form of se lava)**

**The turn**

6. Abuela takes Canelo into the shower and washes him, her back to the room. The children run out to the patio, and come in again with mud on their hands. Two folded towels lie on the stool. The big bar of soap shines on the sink. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child (drawn) washes his hands at the sink and holds them out, wet. On the stool lie two folded towels of two colours (two of rojo, azul and blanco, drawn each round; round B's pair is not round A's). They look the same: folded, no marks. Abuela, from the shower, says in one breath which is clean and which is dirty (which is said first is drawn). The frog carries one over.

7. **Decision A, touch a towel (two; tests limpia against sucia).** NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla azul está limpia. La toalla roja está sucia." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the child dries his hands: clean. A star.
   - Wrong: the child dries his hands: mud on them again, and on his nose. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! La toalla roja está sucia." He washes again; the bar of soap gets smaller. A cross. The sign is on the right towel; he touches it.
8. **Decision B, touch a towel (the same game; the other child, another pair of colours).** NARR: "Abuela dice: La toalla blanca está sucia. La toalla azul está limpia." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

9. The frog's turn: he sits in the sink, with what is left of the soap.
   - The big bar: a mountain of foam, and a hat of foam on the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Half a bar: foam to his chin.
   - A sliver: a few bubbles.
10. Abuela has a hand on each tap. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Agua fría o agua caliente, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: caliente (caliente, agua caliente)]: "¡Caliente, por favor!" Steam rises from the sink; the frog sighs. NARR: "Coquí se lava con jabón. Ahora está limpio."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** sucio 4 with sucia and sucias (steps 1, 4, 5), limpio 4 with limpia and limpias (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: se lava 3 with te lavas (steps 2, 3, 5), jabón 3 (steps 2, 5).
**Forms:** sucia, sucias count with sucio; limpia, limpias with limpio; te lavas with se lava.
**A miss is filed under:** sucio (he took the towel that was called dirty).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the towels differ only in colour, and both colours are named.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): un poco; caliente.

## Story 100: Se baña

- **Goal he can see:** on the wall, the water heater's gauge: three red bars. Abuelo, in his robe with a towel over his shoulder, taps it and points at himself: hot water for his shower, afterwards.
- **At stake:** the hot water (3 bars). The wrong water, run with nobody under it, wastes a bar.
- **New words:** baño, ducha, se baña, espejo.
- **Back again:** se lava, sucio, limpio, mano, agua, caliente, ocupado, después, para mí, yo, mira, abre (abres), ahora, por favor, no, sí.

**Start:** morning. Abuela and the frog in the bathroom; the frog on the shelf between the shower's tap and the sink's. Luis in the door, mud all over him. Carmen behind him, paint on her hands.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: el baño. Aquí está la ducha." Ask (signed, the shower): "¿Dónde está la ducha?" He touches it: water sprays for a moment; the frog jumps.
2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis está sucio. Luis se baña en la ducha." Ask (signed, the shower's tap): "¿Dónde abres el agua?" **(abres: a form of abre)** He touches it: the shower runs; Luis steps in; steam; his head shows over the low wall.
3. Carmen holds up her painted hands. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen no se baña. Carmen se lava las manos." (signed, the sink's tap.) He touches it: the sink's tap runs; she washes. She looks up. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira en el espejo, Coquí. ¿Quién es?" **(quién: heard)** The frog sees a frog in the mirror. COQUÍ [TURN: yo (yo, soy yo)]: "¡Yo!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Sí! Coquí está en el espejo."
4. Abuelo comes to the door in his robe. NARR: "Abuela dice: El baño está ocupado, abuelo." He taps the heater's gauge: three red bars. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo me baño después. Agua caliente para mí, por favor." **(me baño: a form of se baña)**

**The turn**

5. All morning the children run in and out with Canelo. Abuelo waits on a chair outside the door with his towel. The gauge shows three bars. The frog sits between the two taps. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A child (drawn) comes in from the rain in a big raincoat with its hood up, so that nothing shows of how dirty he is, and stands in the middle, as far from the shower as from the sink. Abuela, in the door with the washing in her arms, says what the child does (drawn, each round on its own: the two rounds may be the same): he has a shower, or he washes his hands. The frog runs the shower, or the sink's tap.

6. **Decision A, touch a tap (the shower's, the sink's; tests se baña against se lava).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis se baña." (Or, drawn: "Luis se lava las manos.")
   - Right: the water runs, and the child goes to it. A star.
   - Wrong: the wrong water runs, hot, with nobody under it; the child stands and waits. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Luis se baña." A red bar on the gauge goes out. A cross. The sign is on the right tap; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a tap (the same game; the other child).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen se lava las manos." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuelo's turn in the shower; his head shows over the low wall.
   - Three bars: steam, and he sings. Stars and a heart.
   - Two bars: a little steam; he hums.
   - One bar: no steam; a yelp, and then he laughs.
9. Abuelo comes out, combed, and stands before the mirror. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Me miro en el espejo. Coquí, ¿estoy limpio?" **(me miro: a form of mira)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" NARR: "Abuelo se baña en la ducha. Ahora el baño no está ocupado."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se baña 4 with me baño (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: baño 3 (steps 1, 4, 9), ducha 3 (steps 1, 2), espejo 2 before the game (step 3) and 1 in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se baña or se lava).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): yo; sí.

## Story 101: Los dientes

- **Goal he can see:** on the sink's edge, a tube of toothpaste, nearly empty, with three marks on it, and beside it the frog's own tiny toothbrush. Carmen taps both: afterwards.
- **At stake:** the toothpaste (3 squeezes). A wrong thing, used without looking, wastes the squeeze that was on the brush: it is washed, and a new squeeze goes on.
- **New words:** cepillo, dientes, peinilla.
- **Back again:** espejo, limpio, toalla, pelo, boca, aquí, para, mi, mira, ahora, por favor, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** morning. Carmen stands on the stool at the sink, before the mirror. On the shelf: a toothbrush and a comb. On the sink's edge: the tube (four marks) and the frog's tiny brush. The frog on the shelf's end. Abuela in the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen shows her teeth to the mirror. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen se cepilla los dientes." **(se cepilla: heard; the cepillo is in it)** Ask (signed, Carmen): "¿Dónde están los dientes de Carmen?" He touches her: she grins; a sparkle.
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, el cepillo, por favor." (signed, the toothbrush.) He touches it: the frog pushes it over; she squeezes the tube (three marks are left), and brushes: foam. NARR: "Carmen dice: Me cepillo los dientes."
3. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora, la peinilla." (signed, the comb.) He touches it: the frog pushes it over; she combs her hair. NARR: "Abuela dice: El cepillo es para los dientes. La peinilla es para el pelo."
4. Luis comes in from the shower with a towel over his head, rubbing his hair; he cannot see. He holds out a hand. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está mi peinilla?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aquí está)]: "¡Aquí!" The frog pushes the comb into his hand.

**The turn**

5. Carmen puts a squeeze of paste on the toothbrush and lays it on the shelf, beside the comb; she taps the tube (three marks) and the frog's tiny brush: afterwards. Then both children stand at the sink with towels over their heads, rubbing their hair, a hand held out. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** On the shelf lie the toothbrush, with its squeeze of paste, and the comb (which is at the left is drawn each round). A child (drawn), under his towel, asks for one of them (drawn, each round on its own: the two rounds may be the same). The frog pushes one into his hand.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two on the shelf; tests cepillo against peinilla).** NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, la peinilla, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Coquí, el cepillo, por favor.")
   - Right: he uses it under the towel: his hair comes out flat (or his teeth shine). A star.
   - Wrong: he uses what he gets, without looking: the toothbrush goes through his hair, paste and all (or the comb goes into his mouth, and he squeezes paste onto it). He pulls the towel off. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡La peinilla!" The brush is washed, and a new squeeze goes on it: one mark less on the tube. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the other child).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, el cepillo, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog's turn: Carmen squeezes what is left onto his tiny brush.
   - Three squeezes: a fat stripe; a beard of foam. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: a stripe; foam on his lips.
   - One: a dot.
9. The frog brushes, and rinses. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira en el espejo, Coquí." He opens his mouth wide at the mirror: it sparkles. COQUÍ [TURN: limpio (limpio, estoy limpio, limpia)]: "¡Limpio!" NARR: "El cepillo es para los dientes. Y Coquí tiene su cepillo."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cepillo 3 (steps 2, 3), peinilla 3 (steps 3, 4). Not deciding: dientes 4 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the thing that was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): aquí; limpio.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- Words. A comb on the island is "la peinilla" (the plan's peine is understood, and less used): the scene's word, and page 02's list, follow. Teeth are brushed, not washed: "se cepilla los dientes", "me cepillo los dientes". "¿Dónde pones el agua?" asked where to put water: "¿Dónde abres el agua?". "Mira el espejo" is to look at the object: "Mira en el espejo". Of one's own hands and face: "Tengo las manos limpias". Abuela said "Coquí se lava los pies" to the frog's face: "te lavas". Nobody says "la ducha del baño".
- All three: an ask that repeated the request just made ("Coquí, el jabón, por favor" and then "¿Dónde está el jabón?"): the request carries the sign, and nothing is asked twice.
- 99: the opening's green (dirty) and yellow (clean) could be carried into the game: the game's towels are red, blue and white. The answer "caliente" now changes the picture (steam). The count of "sucio" was wrong.
- 100: a child wrapped in a towel reads as just bathed: he comes in from the rain in a raincoat with its hood up. The shower's curtain hid whoever was "behind" it and the set said nothing hides him: a low wall, and his head shows over it. "Espejo" had two hearings: three. The last step repeated step 3's line.
- 101: only the toothbrush in the hair spoilt the brush, so the other wrong moves cost a squeeze for no reason: the shelf has two things, the brush carries its squeeze, and either wrong move wastes it. The tube lost a squeeze in the opening and still had three at the end: it starts with four marks. Soap in the eyes and a wish for a comb made no sense together, and the picture pointed at the towel: the children are drying their hair under towels. The frog's tiny brush and the tube stood among the choices: they are on the sink's edge. The last line said the frog has no teeth, which made the goal pointless: he brushes, and his mouth sparkles in the mirror.

Left, and why:
- 100: "se baña" against "se lava las manos" can be settled by the old words "se lava" (story 99) and "manos". Any pair of two is open to that. The rounds are drawn on their own, so round B is not the other one.
- "Espejo" and "dientes" decide nothing; each is heard three times or more on a clean run (espejo: twice before the game, once in 101's ending and in 100's step 3).
- "¿Cuál toalla...?" stays: it is the island's way.

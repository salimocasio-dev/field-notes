# La estufa: three stories (final, after two rounds of reviews, a last gate and a last Spanish check)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 2 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa (stories 1 to 11), El parque (12 to 23) and the first scene of La cocina, La mesa (24 to 26).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (place 2): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, otra, su, luna, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Words he has met (place 3, La mesa): mesa, plato, taza, cuchara, tenedor, cuchillo, comida, cocina, pone, tengo hambre, tengo sed, listo. Heard there: chocolate, lista, a comer, tiene hambre, tiene sed, tienes, esa.

Scene words for La estufa (from the 40-scene list): arroz, habichuelas, pollo, huevo, olla, sal, caliente, frío, hace, huele, cuidado, ya.

The picture: abuela's stove, seen from the front and a little from above. It stands in the middle and has two burners; a flame shows under a pot or pan that is on, and steam rises only from a pot whose flame is on. Pots and pans are open at the top, so what is in them can be seen. On the left, a counter; on the right, a counter. Over the right counter is the window to the yard: the family can look in at it. The door is past the right edge. A waste bucket stands on the floor at the left. The frog stands on a stool in front of the stove. Everybody stands lower than the pots, so nobody hides them.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La mesa (historia/notas/guiones/mesa.md). In short: a wrong move costs something in the picture that stays; then "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true at that moment; then the big sign shows the right one and he does it, with no star. Nobody points at or looks toward the answer in a decision. The sides of answer bubbles are drawn fresh every time.
A poke names people, the stove and the kitchen only. It never names the cups of rice or of beans, what is in a pot, the pot of chocolate or the jug.

The three games open differently: 27 with a touch on a thing, 28 with something he says, 29 with something the frog does himself. The three losses differ: in 27 food is thrown away or boils over, in 28 a piece burns, is too salty or lands on the floor, in 29 a fritter is lost or a cup is used up.

## Story 27: Arroz con habichuelas

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** two pots of water on the stove; on the left counter three cups of rice and three cups of red beans; on the right counter six plates and one little plate. Help abuela and Luis cook the rice and the beans.
- **At stake:** lunch. One cup makes one plate's worth, and there are three cups of each for three plates. A cup that goes into the wrong pot has to come out again: the cook skims it off into the waste bucket, and one plate goes without that food. A pot that stays on its flame after it is ready boils over: foam runs down the stove, and a cup's worth is gone. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace.
- **Back again:** pone, dame, por favor, más, para, hay, aquí, otra, listo, mira, tiene hambre, tengo hambre, familia, come, comida.
- **Heard along the way:** listas.

**Start:** two pots of water steam on the stove, a flame under each. A sack of rice and a jar of beans stand on the shelf over the left counter. Abuela stands at the right of the stove, Luis at the left, each with a wooden spoon. Carmen stands at the left counter by the cups: three of rice, three of beans. The frog is on his stool.

1. NARR: "Abuela hace la comida." NARR: "Aquí hay una olla." (The left pot twinkles.) NARR: "Y aquí hay otra olla." (The right pot.) NARR: "Aquí hay arroz." (The cups of rice.) NARR: "Y aquí hay habichuelas." (The cups of beans.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Dame arroz, por favor." The big sign is on the cups of rice. He touches them. The frog carries one to abuela's pot and tips it in; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone el arroz en la olla." Right signal. The water turns white with rice. While abuela stirs: NARR: "Abuela hace arroz."
3. NARR: "Luis dice: Dame habichuelas, por favor." The sign is on the cups of beans. He touches them. The frog tips one into Luis's pot. Right signal. The water turns red. While Luis stirs: NARR: "Luis hace habichuelas."
4. **He calls for more, taught.** Abuela holds an empty cup up and looks at Carmen. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Arroz!" and "¡Habichuelas!" The big sign is on "¡Arroz!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Arroz!" Carmen tips a cup of rice into abuela's pot; while she does: NARR: "Más arroz para abuela." Right signal.
5. Luis holds an empty cup up. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Habichuelas!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Habichuelas!" Carmen tips a cup of beans into Luis's pot; while she does: NARR: "Más habichuelas para Luis." Right signal. Abuela and Luis tip in the last cup each.
6. **Which one is ready, taught.** Abuela, Luis and Carmen go to the right counter and set three plates out; only the frog is at the stove. Both pots are full and steam, both flames are on. NARR: "Abuela dice: El arroz está listo." The big sign is on the pot of rice. He touches it. The frog turns its knob: the flame goes out and the steam stops. Right signal.
7. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las habichuelas están listas." The sign is on the pot of beans. He touches it. The flame goes out. Right signal.

**The turn**

8. Abuelo, mamá and papá look in at the window. NARR: "La familia tiene hambre." Abuela fills the three plates, rice and beans on each, and Carmen hands them out of the window. While the three eat at the window: NARR: "La familia come arroz con habichuelas." The pots are empty. Luis looks into his. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" Carmen takes the sack of rice and the jar of beans down from the shelf, fills three cups from each and stands them on the counter; the sack and the jar are empty. One pot of water goes back on the stove. Three plates are left, for Luis, Carmen and abuela, and the little plate. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Who cooks what is not fixed: it follows from what the first cook asks for.

9. **Decision A, Touch (which one).** One pot of water stands on the stove, and one of the two cooks (drawn fresh) stands at it with a hand out. NARR: "Abuela dice: Dame habichuelas, por favor." (Or: "Dame arroz, por favor." Or Luis says it. Drawn fresh.) He touches the cups of rice or the cups of beans.
   - Right: the frog tips a cup of it into the pot. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog tips a cup of the other thing into the pot. The cook shakes his head, takes the skimmer and lifts it out into the waste bucket. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Aquí hay habichuelas." (The cups of beans twinkle. Or: "Aquí hay arroz.") The sign is on them; he touches them; the frog tips a cup in.
   Then the other cook puts the second pot of water on, and Carmen tips a cup of the other thing into it.
10. **Decision B, Say (what it needs more of).** One pot is white with rice, the other red with beans. One of the two cooks holds an empty cup up (drawn fresh; but after a miss in round A it is the cook of the food that was thrown away, so that the same food cannot be lost again here) and looks at Carmen, who stands between the cups of rice and the cups of beans. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Arroz!" and "¡Habichuelas!"
   - Right: Carmen tips a cup of it into that pot. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Carmen tips a cup of what the frog called into that pot: beans on the rice (or rice on the beans). The cook skims it off into the waste bucket. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Luis hace arroz." (The true one: that cook and what is in his pot.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Carmen tips it in.
   Then Carmen tips a cup of the other thing into the other pot, and each cook tips in the last cup of his own thing, as far as cups are left on the counter.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (which one is ready).** Abuela, Luis and Carmen go to the right counter and set the three plates out; only the frog is at the stove. Both pots steam, both flames are on; they look alike but for what is in them. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las habichuelas están listas." (Or: "El arroz está listo." Drawn fresh. Abuela says it whoever cooked it.) He touches one of the two pots.
   - Right: the frog turns its flame off. Right signal. A star. A moment later abuela calls the other one, and the frog turns it off too.
   - Wrong: the frog turns the flame off under the other pot. The pot that was ready boils over: foam runs down its far side and over the top of the stove; the frog leans back. A cup's worth is gone. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira las habichuelas." (Or: "Mira el arroz." The pot that boiled over twinkles.) The sign is on it; he touches it; the frog turns its knob, and its flame goes out. The foam stays. Abuela lights the other pot again, and turns it off when it is ready.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Abuela fills the three plates, one cup's worth of rice and of beans on each as far as the pots go. At least one cup of each food and three cups in all are always left, and she serves so that no plate is empty: rice from Luis's plate on, beans from her own plate on. NARR: "La comida está lista." Luis, Carmen and abuela take a plate each and eat; while they do: NARR: "Luis come. Carmen come. Abuela come." By what went wrong:
   - Nothing (three stars): three full plates, an empty waste bucket, a clean stove. Abuela spoons some from her plate onto the little plate for the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise, whichever happened: the bucket holds what was skimmed off; foam lies on the stove; and one plate or more has rice only, or beans only. Whoever has a short plate looks into it.
13. COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** arroz 10 (step 1; step 2 three times; step 4: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 5, the bubble; step 6; the turn), habichuelas 9 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 4, the bubble; step 5: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 7; the turn). Not deciding, but heard: olla 3 (steps 1 and 2), hace 3 (steps 1, 2 and 3), and again in a correction.

**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that was asked. B: the thing that pot needed. C: the thing that was ready.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two. In B the same cook as in A may hold the cup up; to use A's line he must still tell the two bubbles apart by ear.

## Story 28: El pollo y el huevo

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a drumstick and an egg fry side by side in abuela's wide pan; a tray on the counter holds three more drumsticks and three more eggs; plates stand on the right, and the frog's little plate beside his stool. Get every piece onto a plate golden and tasting right.
- **At stake:** the six pieces on the tray: after them there is no more. They are fried two at a time, a drumstick and an egg, and each pair is for somebody. A piece that goes on smoking turns black. A piece that gets salt twice is too salty: a white heap lies on it, and whoever it is for throws a hand up and pushes it to the rim of the plate. What the baby does not want, it throws on the floor. Each stays where it is to the end. The last pair is for the baby and the frog. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** pollo, huevo, sal, huele.
- **Back again:** hace, más, por favor, para, quiere, tiene, ahora, mira, negro, bebé, hay, aquí, come, gracias, toma.
- **Heard along the way:** ponle, huele a, no hay más.

Salt is seen while it falls from the shaker, and then no more; salt twice on one piece is a white heap. In the pan and on a plate the egg is a fried egg, with its yolk; turned, it is golden at the edge.
The shaker stands on the edge of the stove; whoever salts takes it from there.
The frog's own moves, in doing bubbles: he shakes salt over the drumstick; or over the egg.

**Start:** abuela stands at the left of the stove; the wide pan is on the left burner, the drumstick on its left side, the egg on its right. On the left counter: the tray. On the right counter: two plates; a third plate stands on the ledge of the stove. The frog is on his stool; his little plate lies beside it.

1. NARR: "Abuela hace pollo y un huevo." NARR: "Aquí hay pollo." (The drumstick in the pan twinkles.) NARR: "Y aquí hay un huevo." (The egg.) NARR: "¡Mmm! ¡Huele bien!"
2. Abuela hands him the shaker; while she does: NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma la sal, Coquí."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. **What he does himself, taught.** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ponle sal al pollo." Two doing bubbles: the shaker over a drumstick; the shaker over an egg. The big sign is on the first. He touches it. Right signal. The frog shakes salt over the drumstick; while it falls: NARR: "Ahora el pollo tiene sal."
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y ponle sal al huevo." The two doing bubbles; the sign is on the egg. He touches it. Right signal. He salts it; while the salt falls: NARR: "Ahora el huevo tiene sal." He puts the shaker back on the edge of the stove.
5. **He calls it out, taught.** Mamá comes in with the baby and stands at the far left, away from the stove. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Mmm! ¡Huele bien!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el pollo y el huevo." Abuela turns left to the baby; her back is to the pan. Smoke rises from the drumstick. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Abuela, el pollo!" and "¡Abuela, el huevo!" The big sign is on "¡Abuela, el pollo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Abuela, el pollo!" Abuela turns round; by now the smoke hangs over the whole pan. She turns the drumstick over: golden, and the smoke clears. Right signal. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!"
6. She turns to the baby again. Smoke rises from the egg. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Abuela, el huevo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Abuela, el huevo!" She turns round into the smoke and turns the egg over: golden. Right signal.
7. **For the baby, taught.** Abuela holds both out to the frog on a plate; the baby waves both arms at the plate. NARR: "Mamá dice: El bebé quiere el pollo." The big sign is on the drumstick on the plate. He touches it. The frog takes the drumstick over. Right signal. While the baby eats: NARR: "El bebé come el pollo."
8. NARR: "Mamá dice: Ahora el bebé quiere el huevo." The sign is on the egg on the plate. He touches it. Right signal. While the baby eats: NARR: "El bebé come el huevo."

**The turn**

9. Luis and Carmen run in, noses up, and stand at the right counter by the plates. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mmm! ¡Huele a pollo!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Y huele a huevo!" Abuela points at the tray: three drumsticks, three eggs. NARR: "Abuela dice: Aquí hay más." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Each round has its own pair from the tray.

10. **Decision A, Say (the smoke).** Abuela puts a drumstick and an egg in the pan and shakes salt over the pan. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el pollo y el huevo." She turns to the baby. Smoke rises from one of the two (drawn fresh). Two speaking bubbles: "¡Abuela, el pollo!" and "¡Abuela, el huevo!"
   - Right: abuela turns round into the smoke, which hangs over the whole pan by now, and turns over the one he called, in time: golden; the smoke clears. Right signal. A star. She turns the other one too, puts both on Luis's plate, and Luis eats them.
   - Wrong: abuela turns round into the smoke and turns over the one he called, which was fine; then the other, too late: it is black. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pollo está negro." (Or: "El huevo está negro.") The sign shows the right bubble; he says it. Both go on Luis's plate; he throws a hand up at the black one, eats the good one, and the black one stays.
11. **Decision B, Do (more salt).** Abuela fries the second pair and shakes salt over the pan once; one of the two got none, and nobody can see which. She sets the plate in front of Carmen. Carmen takes one bite of each (which first is drawn fresh; her face is the same for both). NARR: "Carmen dice: Más sal para el huevo, por favor." (Or: "para el pollo". Drawn fresh.) The frog takes the shaker. Two doing bubbles: the shaker over a drumstick; over an egg.
   - Right: the frog salts it. Right signal. A star. Carmen eats both.
   - Wrong: the frog salts the other one, which had its salt: a white heap on it. Carmen looks at the heap, throws a hand up, and pushes the piece to the rim of her plate. A cross. A thought bubble over Carmen shows the right piece with the shaker over it. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen quiere sal para el huevo." The sign is on the right bubble; he touches it; he salts it; Carmen eats it. The piece with the white heap stays on her plate.
12. **Decision C, Touch, last (for the baby).** Abuela puts the last pair in the pan and tips the tray towards us: it is empty. NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay más." She fries the pair herself and holds both out to the frog on a plate; the baby waves both arms at the plate. NARR: "Mamá dice: El bebé quiere el huevo." (Or: "el pollo". Drawn fresh.) He touches the drumstick or the egg on the plate.
   - Right: the frog takes it over; the baby eats. Right signal. A star. Abuela puts the other piece on the frog's little plate; while she does: NARR: "Abuela dice: Y esto es para Coquí."
   - Wrong: the frog takes over the one he touched. The baby throws it on the floor. A cross. A thought bubble over the baby shows the right one. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El bebé quiere el huevo." The sign is on the right piece; he touches it; the frog takes it over; the baby eats. The piece on the floor stays there; the frog's little plate stays empty.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. While the baby eats: NARR: "El bebé come." By what went wrong:
   - Nothing (three stars): clean plates; the frog eats his piece from his little plate. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise, whichever happened: the black piece on Luis's plate; the piece with the white heap on Carmen's; the piece on the floor, and the frog's empty little plate.
14. COQUÍ: "¡Huele bien, abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** pollo 10 (step 1 twice; step 3 twice; step 5: abuela's line, the bubble, the frog; step 6, the bubble; step 7 twice), huevo 10 (step 1 twice; step 4 twice; step 5: abuela's line, the bubble; step 6: the bubble, the frog; step 8 twice). Not deciding, but heard: sal 5 (steps 2, 3 and 4), huele 4 (steps 1 and 5, and twice at the turn).

**A miss is filed under:** A: the piece that smoked. B: the piece that was asked. C: the piece that was asked.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two.

## Story 29: ¡Cuidado!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** on the left, a pan of hot oil, a board with four balls of batter and an empty plate; on the right, a pot of hot chocolate, a jug of cold chocolate and nine clean cups in rows. Fry the fritters, and bring everybody the chocolate he wants.
- **At stake:** the merienda. After the turn there are three balls of batter, for three fritters: one for Carmen, one for abuela, one for the frog. A fritter that comes out too soon is soft and falls on the floor; one that stays in too long burns black. And there are five clean cups: a cup of the wrong chocolate is not wanted, Luis drinks it himself, and that cup lies empty on its side. The frog and abuela have theirs last, from what is left. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** caliente, frío, cuidado, ya.
- **Back again:** olla, chocolate, quiero, quiere, ¿quién?, bebe, mira, espera, hay, aquí, está, listo, negro.
- **Heard along the way:** ya está.

Hot chocolate steams. Cold chocolate has blue ice and a straw in it. A thought bubble shows one or the other.
The frog's own moves, in doing bubbles: both hands up, he waits; or he takes the fritter out with the tongs.
A fritter in the oil is under the bubbles: nobody can see how far it is until it comes out. Abuela looks into the pan before she calls.

**Start:** abuela stands at the left of the stove, at the pan; the board of batter and the plate are on the left counter. The pot of chocolate steams on the right burner. Luis stands at the right counter by the jug of cold chocolate and the cups. The frog is on his stool, with the tongs.

1. NARR: "La olla está caliente." (The pot twinkles.) NARR: "Aquí hay chocolate caliente." NARR: "Y aquí hay chocolate frío." (The jug twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. Papá comes in at the door. NARR: "Papá dice: Quiero chocolate caliente." Luis gives the frog a clean cup. The big sign is on the pot. He touches it. The frog fills the cup at the pot, with the ladle, and carries it to papá; while he does: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Está caliente." Right signal. While papá drinks: NARR: "Papá bebe chocolate caliente." He goes out with his cup.
3. Carmen comes in. NARR: "Carmen dice: Quiero chocolate frío." Luis gives the frog a clean cup. The sign is on the jug. He touches it. The frog fills the cup at the jug and carries it to Carmen. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Carmen bebe chocolate frío." She goes out with her cup.
4. **What he does himself, taught.** Abuela drops a ball of batter into the pan; the oil bubbles over it. She looks into the pan. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado, Coquí!" Two doing bubbles: both hands up; the tongs. The big sign is on the hands up. He touches it. The frog leans back and waits; while he does: NARR: "Coquí espera." Right signal.
5. Abuela looks into the pan again. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" The two doing bubbles; the sign is on the tongs. He touches it. The frog lifts the fritter out, golden, onto the plate. Right signal.
6. **Who wants it, taught.** Abuelo and mamá come in together; a thought bubble over abuelo shows a steaming cup, one over mamá a cup with ice. Luis holds a clean cup. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quién quiere chocolate caliente?" The big sign is on abuelo. He touches him. Luis fills the cup at the pot. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ya está!" He hands it over. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Está caliente." Right signal. While abuelo drinks: NARR: "Abuelo bebe chocolate caliente."
7. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quién quiere chocolate frío?" The sign is on mamá. He touches her. Luis fills a cup at the jug. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ya está!" He hands it over. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Mamá bebe chocolate frío." The two go out with their cups.

**The turn**

8. Luis crosses to the plate, takes the fritter and eats it. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, Luis!" He goes back to the cups. Three balls of batter are left on the board; five clean cups stand in a row of three and a row of two. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Do (wait, or take it out).** Abuela drops a ball of batter into the pan; the oil bubbles over it, and it fries a moment, the same either time. She looks into the pan, turns away to wipe the plate, and calls over her shoulder. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" (Or: "¡Cuidado, Coquí!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: both hands up; the tongs.
   - Right, "¡Ya está!": the frog lifts it out, golden, onto the plate. Right signal. A star.
   - Right, "¡Cuidado!": the frog waits. Abuela turns, looks into the pan, and says: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" He lifts it out, golden. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong, he takes it out at "¡Cuidado!": it is pale and soft; it slips from the tongs and lands on the floor beside the stove, away from the frog. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. No está listo." (The pale fritter twinkles.) Abuela drops the next ball in, looks, and says again: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado, Coquí!" The sign is on the hands up; he touches it; he waits; she looks and says "¡Ya está, Coquí!", and it comes out golden.
   - Wrong, he waits at "¡Ya está!": smoke rises from the pan. Abuela turns round at the smoke and lifts out a black fritter onto the edge of the stove. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Está negro." Abuela drops the next ball in, looks, and says again: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" The sign is on the tongs; he touches it; he lifts it out, golden.
10. **Decision B, Touch (which chocolate).** One of the four who went out (drawn fresh) comes in again, with empty hands. NARR: "Mamá dice: Quiero chocolate caliente." (Or: "chocolate frío". Drawn fresh; what she had before does not matter.) Luis gives the frog a clean cup. He touches the pot or the jug.
   - Right: the frog fills the cup there and carries it over; she drinks, and goes out with it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog fills the cup at the one he touched and carries it over. Mamá looks at it and shakes her head; a thought bubble over her shows the chocolate she asked for. Luis takes the cup, drinks it himself, and lays it on its side on the counter. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá quiere chocolate caliente." Luis gives the frog another clean cup. The sign is on the right one; he touches it; the frog fills it; she drinks.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (who wants it).** Two of the others (drawn fresh) come in together; a thought bubble over one shows a steaming cup, over the other a cup with ice (who has which is drawn fresh). Luis holds a clean cup, between the pot and the jug. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quién quiere chocolate frío?" (Or: "chocolate caliente". Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: Luis fills the cup and hands it to that one. Right signal. A star. Then he fills a clean cup with the other chocolate for the other one. The two go out with their cups.
   - Wrong: Luis fills the cup and hands it to the one he touched, who shakes his head: his thought bubble shows the other chocolate. Luis drinks it himself and lays the cup on its side. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Abuelo quiere chocolate frío." (The true one; his bubble twinkles.) The sign is on him; he touches him; Luis fills a clean cup for him. Then one for the other. The two go out.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Abuela fries the batter that is left; the fritters on the plate steam. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está!" Carmen comes in. Carmen and abuela take a fritter each; if a third is on the plate, the frog has it. If a clean cup is left, Luis fills it for abuela; if one more is left, for the frog. As Carmen bites: COQUÍ: "¡Carmen, cuidado! Está caliente." By what went wrong:
   - Nothing (three stars): the frog has a fritter and a cup, abuela has a cup. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise, whichever happened: the fallen or the black fritter lies where it is, and the frog has no fritter; the cups Luis emptied lie on their sides, and the frog, or the frog and abuela, have no cup.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cuidado 3 (steps 2, 4 and 6; step 4 is the call at the pan), ya 3 (steps 5, 6 and 7, always as "ya está"; step 5 is the call at the pan), caliente 8 (step 1 twice; step 2 three times; step 6 three times), frío 5 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A: cuidado or ya (the one abuela said). B and C: caliente or frío (the one that was asked).

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two.

## For the owner, one line each

- "Hace" and "olla" decide nothing in story 27, nor "sal" and "huele" in story 28; they are heard and then tested in the picture pairs.
- "Ya" is taught inside "ya está" (it is ready). Called at a child with his name, a bare "¡Ya, Coquí!" means "enough, stop it" in Puerto Rico.
- Abuela says "Ponle sal al pollo." A bare "Sal para el pollo." starts like the order "sal" (get out). "Ponle" is new; "pone" is yours.
- "Huevo" is only ever one egg, as food. With "mi", "mis" or "tiene" it turns vulgar in Puerto Rico, so no line says that.
- "Caliente" is only said of a thing; of a person it is vulgar.
- Cold chocolate is drawn in the same cup as hot, with ice and a straw, so that the cups can be counted. By the rule of La mesa it would be in a glass ("vaso").
- The fritter is never named aloud. If it should be: "buñuelo" (sweet, goes with chocolate) or "fritura" (any fried thing).
- In story 29 the frog does not speak the new words in a round; he acts on them.
- Abuela says "No hay más." once in story 28, over the empty tray; "no hay" is a word of the next scene, heard early here.
- The pairs for "arroz" and "habichuelas" are the same contrast twice. The pair for "frío" says "fría" (el agua); the pair for "ya" says "¡Ya está!".

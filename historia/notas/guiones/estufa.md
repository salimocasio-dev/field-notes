# La estufa: three stories (draft 2 of stories 27 and 29, after the first two reviews; draft 1 of story 28 and of the pairs)

Place 3, La cocina. Scene 2 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa (stories 1 to 11), El parque (12 to 23) and the first scene of La cocina, La mesa (24 to 26).

Scene words for La estufa (from the 40-scene list): arroz, habichuelas, pollo, huevo, olla, sal, caliente, frío, hace, huele, cuidado, ya.

The picture: abuela's stove, seen from the front and a little from above. It stands in the middle and has two burners; a flame shows under a pot or pan that is on, and steam rises only from a pot whose flame is on. Pots and pans are open at the top, so what is in them can be seen. On the left, a counter for what goes into the pots; a helper stands there. On the right, a counter for plates, and a waste bowl. Over the right counter is the window to the yard: the family can look in at it. At the right edge is the door. The cooks stand at the two sides of the stove. The frog stands on a stool in front. Everybody stands lower than the pots, so nobody hides them.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La mesa (historia/notas/guiones/mesa.md). In short: a wrong move costs something in the picture that stays; then "No, Coquí. Mira." and a whole sentence, true at that moment; then the big sign shows the right one and he does it, with no star. Nobody points at or looks toward the answer in a decision. The sides of answer bubbles are drawn fresh every time.
A poke names people, the stove and the kitchen only. It never names the cups of rice or of beans, what is in a pot, the pot of chocolate or the jug.

The three games open differently: 27 with a touch on a thing, 28 with something he says, 29 with something the frog does himself.

## Story 27: Arroz con habichuelas

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** two pots of water on the stove; on the left counter three cups of rice and three cups of red beans; on the right counter six plates and one little plate. Help abuela and Luis cook the rice and the beans.
- **At stake:** lunch. One cup makes one plate's worth, and there are three cups of each for three plates. A cup that goes into the wrong pot has to come out again: the cook skims it off into the waste bowl, and one plate goes without that food. A pot that stays on its flame after it is ready burns: smoke, a black pot, and black food on the plates. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** arroz, habichuelas, olla, hace.
- **Back again:** pone, dame, por favor, más, para, hay, aquí, otra, listo, mira, tiene hambre, tengo hambre, familia, come, comida.
- **Heard along the way:** listas, a comer.

**Start:** two pots of water steam on the stove, a flame under each. Abuela stands at the right of the stove, Luis at the left, each with a wooden spoon. Carmen stands at the left counter by the cups: three of rice, three of beans. The frog is on his stool.

1. NARR: "Abuela hace la comida." NARR: "Aquí hay una olla." (The left pot twinkles.) NARR: "Y aquí hay otra olla." (The right pot.) NARR: "Aquí hay arroz." (The cups of rice.) NARR: "Y aquí hay habichuelas." (The cups of beans.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "Abuela dice: Dame arroz, por favor." The big sign is on the cups of rice. He touches them. The frog carries one to abuela's pot and tips it in; while he does: NARR: "Coquí pone el arroz en la olla." Right signal. The water turns white with rice. While abuela stirs: NARR: "Abuela hace arroz."
3. NARR: "Luis dice: Dame habichuelas, por favor." The sign is on the cups of beans. He touches them. The frog tips one into Luis's pot. Right signal. The water turns red. While Luis stirs: NARR: "Luis hace habichuelas."
4. **He calls for more, taught.** Abuela holds an empty cup up and looks at Carmen. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Arroz!" and "¡Habichuelas!" The big sign is on "¡Arroz!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Arroz!" Carmen tips a cup of rice into abuela's pot; while she does: NARR: "Más arroz para abuela." Right signal.
5. Luis holds an empty cup up. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Habichuelas!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Habichuelas!" Carmen tips a cup of beans into Luis's pot; while she does: NARR: "Más habichuelas para Luis." Right signal. Abuela and Luis tip in the last cup each.
6. **Which one is ready, taught.** Abuela goes to the right counter and sets three plates out; her back is to the stove. Both pots are full and steam, both flames are on. NARR: "Abuela dice: El arroz está listo." The big sign is on the pot of rice. He touches it. The frog turns its knob: the flame goes out and the steam stops. Right signal.
7. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las habichuelas están listas." The sign is on the pot of beans. He touches it. The flame goes out. Right signal.

**The turn**

8. Abuelo, mamá and papá look in at the window. NARR: "La familia tiene hambre." Abuela fills the three plates, rice and beans on each, and Carmen hands them out of the window. While the three eat at the window: NARR: "La familia come arroz con habichuelas." The pots are empty. Luis looks into his. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" Abuela and Luis fill the pots with water and light the flames again; Carmen stands three cups of rice and three cups of beans on the counter. Three plates are left, for Luis, Carmen and abuela, and the little plate. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Who cooks what is not fixed: it follows from what the first cook asks for.

9. **Decision A, Touch (which one).** Both pots hold water only. One of the two cooks (drawn fresh) holds a hand out. NARR: "Abuela dice: Dame habichuelas, por favor." (Or: "Dame arroz, por favor." Or Luis says it. Drawn fresh.) He touches the cups of rice or the cups of beans.
   - Right: the frog tips a cup of it into that cook's pot. Right signal. A star. Carmen tips a cup of the other thing into the other pot.
   - Wrong: the frog tips a cup of the other thing into that cook's pot. The cook shakes his head, takes the skimmer and lifts it out into the waste bowl. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Aquí hay habichuelas." (The cups of beans twinkle. Or: "Aquí hay arroz.") The sign is on them; he touches them; the frog tips a cup in. Carmen tips a cup of the other thing into the other pot.
10. **Decision B, Say (what it needs more of).** One pot is white with rice, the other red with beans. One of the two cooks (drawn fresh) holds an empty cup up and looks at Carmen, who stands between the cups of rice and the cups of beans. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Arroz!" and "¡Habichuelas!"
   - Right: Carmen tips a cup of it into that pot. Right signal. A star. She tips a cup of the other thing into the other pot.
   - Wrong: Carmen tips a cup of what the frog called into that pot: beans on the rice (or rice on the beans). The cook skims it off into the waste bowl. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Luis hace arroz." (The true one: that cook and what is in his pot.) The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; Carmen tips it in. She tips a cup of the other thing into the other pot.
   Then each cook tips in the last cup of his own thing, if one is left on the counter.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (which one is ready).** Abuela goes to the right counter and sets the three plates out; her back is to the stove. Both pots steam, both flames are on; they look alike but for what is in them. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las habichuelas están listas." (Or: "El arroz está listo." Drawn fresh. Abuela says it whoever cooked it.) He touches one of the two pots.
   - Right: the frog turns its flame off. Right signal. A star. A moment later abuela turns round and turns the other one off.
   - Wrong: the frog turns the flame off under the other pot. The pot that was ready stays on its flame: smoke rises from it and it turns black. Abuela turns round at the smoke. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira las habichuelas." (Or: "Mira el arroz." The smoking pot twinkles.) The sign is on it; he touches it; its flame goes out. It stays black. Abuela lights the other pot again, and turns it off when it is ready.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Abuela fills the three plates, one cup's worth of rice and of beans on each as far as the pots go; what comes from a burnt pot is black. NARR: "Aquí hay arroz con habichuelas." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A comer!" Then Luis, Carmen and abuela take a plate each and eat. By what went wrong:
   - Nothing (three stars): three full plates, an empty waste bowl. Abuela fills the little plate for the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise: the waste bowl holds what was skimmed off, and a plate has rice only, or beans only; or a pot stands black on the stove, and its food lies black on the plates.
13. COQUÍ: "¡Arroz con habichuelas!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** arroz 10 (step 1; step 2 three times; step 4: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 5, the bubble; step 6; the turn), habichuelas 9 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 4, the bubble; step 5: the bubble, the frog, the line after; step 7; the turn). Not deciding, but heard: olla 3 (steps 1 and 2), hace 3 (steps 1, 2 and 3), and again in a correction.

**A miss is filed under:** A: the thing that was asked. B: the thing that pot needed. C: the thing that was ready.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two.

## Story 28: El pollo y el huevo

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a drumstick and an egg fry side by side in abuela's wide pan; a tray on the counter holds three more drumsticks and three more eggs; four plates stand on the right, and the frog's little mat lies on the floor. Get every piece onto a plate golden and tasting right.
- **At stake:** the six pieces on the tray: after them there is no more. They are fried two at a time, a drumstick and an egg, and each pair is for somebody. A piece that goes on smoking turns black. A piece that is salted twice is too salty: a white heap lies on it, and whoever bites it pulls a face and leaves it. What the baby does not want, it throws on the floor. Each stays where it is to the end. The last pair is for the baby and the frog. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** pollo, huevo, sal, huele.
- **Back again:** hace, comida, más, por favor, para, quiere, tiene, ahora, mira, negro, bebé, hay, tengo hambre, come, gracias, toma.
- **Heard along the way:** huele a, no hay más.

Salt shows as white specks on a piece; a second helping of salt is a white heap.

**Start:** abuela stands at the right of the stove; the wide pan is on the right burner, the drumstick on its left side, the egg on its right. On the left counter: the tray, and the salt shaker. On the right counter: four plates. The frog is on his stool.

1. NARR: "Abuela hace la comida." NARR: "Aquí hay pollo." (The drumstick in the pan twinkles.) NARR: "Y aquí hay un huevo." (The egg.) NARR: "¡Mmm! ¡Huele bien!"
2. Abuela hands him the shaker; while she does: NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma la sal, Coquí."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sal para el pollo." The big sign is on the drumstick. He touches it. The frog shakes salt over it: white specks. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el pollo tiene sal."
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y sal para el huevo." The sign is on the egg. He touches it. He salts it. Right signal. NARR: "Ahora el huevo tiene sal."
5. **He calls it out, taught.** Mamá comes in with the baby. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Mmm! ¡Huele bien!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el pollo y el huevo." She turns to the baby, who takes hold of her finger; her back is to the pan. Smoke rises from the drumstick. Two speaking bubbles: "¡Abuela, el pollo!" and "¡Abuela, el huevo!" The big sign is on "¡Abuela, el pollo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Abuela, el pollo!" She turns round and turns the drumstick over: golden. Right signal. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!"
6. She turns to the baby again. Smoke rises from the egg. The two speaking bubbles; the sign is on "¡Abuela, el huevo!" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Abuela, el huevo!" She turns the egg over: golden. Right signal.
7. **For the baby, taught.** Abuela holds both out on a plate; the baby waves both arms at the plate. NARR: "Mamá dice: El bebé quiere pollo." Two picture bubbles: a drumstick, an egg. The big sign is on the drumstick. He touches it. The frog takes the drumstick over. Right signal. While the baby eats: NARR: "El bebé come pollo."
8. NARR: "Mamá dice: Ahora el bebé quiere huevo." The two picture bubbles; the sign is on the egg. He touches it. Right signal. While the baby eats: NARR: "El bebé come huevo."

**The turn**

9. Luis and Carmen run in, noses up. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mmm! ¡Huele a pollo!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Tengo hambre!" Abuela shows them the tray: three drumsticks, three eggs. NARR: "Abuela dice: No hay más." The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Each round has its own pair from the tray.

10. **Decision A, Say (the smoke).** Abuela puts a drumstick and an egg in the pan and salts them. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira el pollo y el huevo." She turns to the baby, who takes hold of her finger. Smoke rises from one of the two (drawn fresh). Two speaking bubbles: "¡Abuela, el pollo!" and "¡Abuela, el huevo!"
   - Right: without letting go of the baby she reaches back and turns that one over, in time: golden. Right signal. A star. She turns the other one too, puts both on a plate, and Luis eats them.
   - Wrong: she reaches back and turns over the one he called, which was fine. The other smokes on and turns black. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El pollo está negro." (Or: "El huevo está negro.") The sign shows the right bubble; he says it; she turns round and turns it over: black. Both go on Luis's plate; he eats the good one, and the black one stays.
11. **Decision B, Touch (more salt).** Abuela fries the second pair, salts each once, and sets the plate in front of Carmen. Carmen takes one bite of each (which first is drawn fresh; her face is the same for both). NARR: "Carmen dice: Más sal para el huevo, por favor." (Or: "para el pollo". Drawn fresh.) The frog has the shaker. He touches the drumstick or the egg on her plate.
   - Right: the frog salts it. Right signal. A star. Carmen eats both.
   - Wrong: the frog salts the other one: a white heap on it. Carmen bites it, pulls a face, and puts it back. A cross. A thought bubble over Carmen shows the right piece under the shaker. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen quiere sal para el huevo." The sign is on it; he touches it; he salts it; Carmen eats it. The piece with the white heap stays on her plate.
12. **Decision C, Touch a picture, last (for the baby).** Abuela tips the tray: it is empty. She fries the last pair herself and holds the plate out; the baby waves both arms at the plate. NARR: "Mamá dice: El bebé quiere huevo." (Or: "pollo". Drawn fresh.) Two picture bubbles: a drumstick, an egg.
   - Right: the frog takes it over; the baby eats. Right signal. A star. Abuela puts the other piece on the frog's mat; while she does: NARR: "Abuela dice: Y esto es para Coquí."
   - Wrong: the frog takes over the one he touched. The baby throws it on the floor. A cross. A thought bubble over the baby shows the right one. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. El bebé quiere huevo." The sign shows the right picture; he touches it; the frog takes it over; the baby eats. The piece on the floor stays there; the frog's mat stays empty.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

13. NARR: "La familia come pollo y huevo." By what went wrong:
   - Nothing (three stars): clean plates; the frog eats his piece on his mat. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise: the black piece on Luis's plate, the piece with the white heap on Carmen's, the piece on the floor, and the frog's empty mat.
14. COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Huele bien, abuela!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** pollo 9 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 5: abuela's line, the bubble, the frog; step 6, the bubble; step 7 twice; the turn), huevo 9 (step 1; step 4 twice; step 5: abuela's line, the bubble; step 6: the bubble, the frog; step 8 twice). Not deciding, but heard: sal 5 (steps 2, 3 and 4), huele 3 (steps 1 and 5, the turn).

**A miss is filed under:** A: the piece that smoked. B: the piece that was asked. C: the piece that was asked.

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two.

## Story 29: ¡Cuidado!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a pot of hot chocolate and a pan of hot oil on the stove; a jug of cold chocolate and two stacks of clean cups on the left counter; a board with four balls of batter and an empty plate on the right counter. Fry the fritters, and bring everybody the chocolate he wants.
- **At stake:** the merienda. After the turn there are three balls of batter, for three fritters: one for Carmen, one for abuela, one for the frog. A fritter that comes out too soon is soft and falls on the floor; one that stays in too long burns black. And there are five clean cups: a cup of the wrong chocolate is not wanted, Luis drinks it himself, and that cup is gone. The frog and abuela have theirs last, from what is left. The card shows a cross for that round.
- **New words:** caliente, frío, cuidado, ya.
- **Back again:** olla, chocolate, quiero, quiere, ¿quién?, bebe, toma, come, mira, espera, hay, aquí, está.
- **Heard along the way:** ya está, a comer.

Hot chocolate steams. Cold chocolate has a straw in it. A thought bubble shows one or the other.
The frog's own moves, in doing bubbles: he leans back with both hands up, and waits; or he takes the fritter out with the tongs.
A fritter in the oil is under the bubbles: nobody can see how far it is until it comes out. Abuela looks into the pan before she calls.

**Start:** abuela stands at the pan, on the right of the stove, the board of batter beside her. The pot of chocolate steams on the left burner. Luis stands at the left counter by the jug of cold chocolate and the cups. The frog is on his stool, with the tongs.

1. NARR: "La olla está caliente." (The pot twinkles.) NARR: "Aquí hay chocolate caliente." NARR: "Y aquí hay chocolate frío." (The jug twinkles.)

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. Papá comes in at the door. NARR: "Papá dice: Quiero chocolate caliente." The big sign is on the pot. He touches it. Luis fills a cup from the pot. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ya está!" The frog carries the cup to papá; while he does: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Está caliente." Right signal. While papá drinks: NARR: "Papá bebe chocolate caliente." He goes out with his cup.
3. Carmen comes in. NARR: "Carmen dice: Quiero chocolate frío." The sign is on the jug. He touches it. Luis fills a cup from the jug. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ya está!" The frog carries it to Carmen. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Carmen bebe chocolate frío." She goes out with her cup.
4. **What he does himself, taught.** Abuela drops a ball of batter into the pan; the oil bubbles over it. She looks into the pan. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado, Coquí!" Two doing bubbles: the frog with both hands up; the tongs. The big sign is on the hands up. He touches it. The frog leans back and waits; while he does: NARR: "Coquí espera." Right signal.
5. Abuela looks into the pan again. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" The two doing bubbles; the sign is on the tongs. He touches it. The frog lifts the fritter out, golden, onto the plate. Right signal.
6. **Who wants it, taught.** Abuelo and mamá come in together; a thought bubble over abuelo shows a steaming cup, one over mamá a cup with a straw. Luis holds a clean cup. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quién quiere chocolate caliente?" The big sign is on abuelo. He touches him. Luis fills the cup from the pot and hands it over. NARR: "Luis dice: Toma." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Cuidado! Está caliente." Right signal. While abuelo drinks: NARR: "Abuelo bebe chocolate caliente."
7. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quién quiere chocolate frío?" The sign is on mamá. He touches her. Luis fills a cup from the jug and hands it over. Right signal. While she drinks: NARR: "Mamá bebe chocolate frío." The two go out with their cups.

**The turn**

8. Luis goes to the plate, takes the fritter and eats it. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay, Luis!" Three balls of batter are left on the board; five clean cups stand on the counter. The card with three empty places shows in the corner. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Do (wait, or take it out).** Abuela drops a ball of batter into the pan; the oil bubbles over it, the same either time. She looks into the pan. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" (Or: "¡Cuidado, Coquí!" Drawn fresh.) Then she turns to the board and shapes the next ball, her back to the pan. Two doing bubbles: both hands up; the tongs.
   - Right, "¡Ya está!": the frog lifts it out, golden, onto the plate. Right signal. A star.
   - Right, "¡Cuidado!": the frog waits. Abuela turns, looks into the pan, and says: NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está, Coquí!" He lifts it out, golden. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong, he takes it out at "¡Cuidado!": it is pale and soft; it slips from the tongs and lands on the floor beside the stove, away from the frog. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Está caliente." The sign is on the hands up; abuela drops the next ball in; he waits; she says "¡Ya está, Coquí!", and it comes out golden.
   - Wrong, he waits at "¡Ya está!": smoke rises from the pan. Abuela turns round at the smoke and lifts out a black fritter onto the edge of the stove. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. ¡Ya está!" The sign is on the tongs; abuela drops the next ball in; he lifts it out, golden.
   Abuela fries what batter is left. On the plate: three golden fritters, or two.
10. **Decision B, Touch (which chocolate).** One of the four who went out (drawn fresh) comes in again, with empty hands. NARR: "Mamá dice: Quiero chocolate caliente." (Or: "chocolate frío". Drawn fresh; what she had before does not matter.) He touches the pot or the jug.
   - Right: Luis fills a clean cup from it; the frog carries it over; she drinks, and goes out with it. Right signal. A star.
   - Wrong: Luis fills a clean cup from the one he touched; the frog carries it over. Mamá looks at it and shakes her head; a thought bubble over her shows the chocolate she asked for. Luis takes the cup back and drinks it himself; the empty cup stands beside him. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Mamá quiere chocolate caliente." The sign is on the right one; he touches it; Luis fills another clean cup; she drinks.
11. **Decision C, Touch, last (who wants it).** Two of the others (drawn fresh) come in together; a thought bubble over one shows a steaming cup, over the other a cup with a straw (who has which is drawn fresh). Luis holds a clean cup, away from the pot and the jug. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quién quiere chocolate frío?" (Or: "chocolate caliente". Drawn fresh.) He touches one of the two.
   - Right: Luis fills the cup and hands it to that one. Right signal. A star. Then he fills a clean cup with the other chocolate for the other one.
   - Wrong: Luis fills the cup and hands it to the one he touched, who shakes his head: his thought bubble shows the other chocolate. Luis drinks it himself; the empty cup stands beside him. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Abuelo quiere chocolate frío." (The true one; his bubble twinkles.) The sign is on him; he touches him; Luis fills a clean cup for him. Then one for the other.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. Carmen comes in. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ya está! ¡A comer!" Carmen and abuela take a fritter each; if a third is on the plate, the frog has it. If a clean cup is left, Luis fills it for the frog; if one more is left, for abuela. As Carmen bites: COQUÍ: "¡Cuidado, Carmen! Está caliente." By what went wrong:
   - Nothing (three stars): the frog has a fritter and a cup, abuela has a cup. Stars and a heart.
   - Otherwise: the fallen or the black fritter lies where it is, the cups Luis emptied stand beside him, and the frog, or the frog and abuela, go without.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cuidado 3 (steps 2, 4 and 6), ya 3 (steps 2, 3 and 5, always as "ya está"), caliente 9 (step 1 twice; step 2 three times; step 6 four times), frío 5 (step 1; step 3 twice; step 7 twice).

**A miss is filed under:** A: cuidado or ya (the one abuela said). B and C: caliente or frío (the one that was asked).

**Chance without the Spanish:** A one in two. B one in two. C one in two.

## For the owner, one line each

- "Hace" and "olla" decide nothing in story 27; they are tested the next day in the picture pairs.
- "Ya" is taught inside "ya está" (it is ready). Called at a child with his name, a bare "¡Ya, Coquí!" means "enough, stop it" in Puerto Rico.
- Cold chocolate is drawn in the same cup as hot, with a straw, so that the cups can be counted. By the rule of La mesa it would be in a glass ("vaso").
- The fritter is never named aloud. If it should be: "buñuelo" (sweet, goes with chocolate) or "fritura" (any fried thing).
- In story 29 the frog does not speak the new words in a round; he acts on them.
- "Sal" and "huele" decide nothing in story 28; they are heard and then tested in the picture pairs.
- Abuela says "No hay más." in story 28; "no hay" is a word of the next scene, heard early here.
- The pair for "frío" says "fría" (el agua). The pair for "ya" says "¡Ya está!".

## What the first two reviews changed (draft 1 to draft 2, stories 27 and 29)

Beginner review: both "not ready"; every decision of 27 held up, and 29's B and C. Puerto Rican Spanish review: two lines in each.
- 27: three cups of each for three plates, so a cup thrown away leaves a plate short. Abuela has her back to the stove, setting out plates, when a pot is ready: that is why one can burn. The family eats at the window, and the line is said while they eat. The six plates and the frog's little plate are there from the first frame. "La familia tiene hambre." The last lines are true in every ending.
- 29: the fritter fries under the bubbles, and abuela looks in before she calls, so nothing about it can be seen or guessed. "¡Ya está, Coquí!" "Cuidado" is heard three times before the game. The batter and the cups are counted: four balls, nine cups. Nobody sips a wrong cup: Luis drinks it. Abuela has her back turned, shaping the next ball, when one burns. The turn is Luis eating the first fritter, not "everything is eaten, again". The last round is "who wants it", on two people, not the frog's call. The corrections are sentences true to the eye.

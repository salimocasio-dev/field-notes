# La noche: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `noche`). Stories 105 to 107. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 104.

Scene words (page 02): cena, televisión, ve, prende, apaga, se acuesta, descansa, la noche, antes, siempre, nunca, todos los días (la noche is his already: La playa).
How they are shared out: 105 televisión, ve, prende, apaga; 106 cena, antes, siempre, nunca; 107 se acuesta, descansa, todos los días.

The picture: the living room at night. One view. The back wall faces us: a window with the moon, a wall clock, and three lamps along it (a standing lamp at the left, a lamp on a shelf in the middle, a lamp on a small table at the right). In the middle, a sofa that faces us, with a rug in front of it; beside it, at the left, an armchair. At the left front, the television on a low stand, turned so that we see its screen at an angle. At the right, the dinner table with a sideboard behind it (story 106); and at the far right, through a wide arch, a corner of the bedroom with a bed (story 107). Nothing over anyone's head.

People: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask: the sign shows what to touch, and the voice does not ask again. After a wrong move the mistake stays in the picture until he touches the sign.

## Story 105: Prende y apaga

- **Goal he can see:** abuela knits a small scarf: three stripes are done. She holds it against the frog's neck: for you.
- **At stake:** the scarf (3 stripes). A lamp put out when light was wanted: abuela drops her stitches in the dark. A lamp lit when dark was wanted: abuelo starts at the glare and knocks her wool. Either way a stripe comes undone.
- **New words:** televisión, ve, prende, apaga.
- **Back again:** luz, noche, quiero, también, gracias, por favor, mira, ahora, yo, tú, no, sí.

**Start:** night; the moon in the window. Of the three lamps, the middle one is lit. Abuelo on the sofa. Abuela in the armchair with her knitting. The frog on the sofa's arm. The television is dark.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, la televisión, por favor." (signed, the television.) He touches it: its screen lights up; music. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias, Coquí." NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí prende la televisión. Abuelo ve televisión."
2. Abuela holds her knitting close to her eyes. NARR: "Abuela dice: No veo. Coquí, prende una luz, por favor." **(veo: a form of ve)** Ask (signed, the dark lamp at the right): "¿Qué luz prendes?" He touches it: the lamp comes on. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Coquí prende la luz. Ahora veo."
3. Abuelo squints at the screen: the middle lamp's light shines on it. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Ay! No veo la televisión. Coquí, apaga una luz." Ask (signed, the middle lamp): "¿Qué luz apagas?" He touches it: the lamp goes out. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias. Ahora veo la televisión." NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí apaga la luz."
4. Luis and Carmen come in, in their pyjamas, and sit down on the rug. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Yo quiero ver televisión! ¿Y tú, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (yo también, también)]: "¡Yo también!" Abuela holds the small scarf against the frog's neck: three stripes.

**The turn**

5. Abuela knits; abuelo and the children watch the screen, all four with their eyes on it. Of the three lamps one is lit and two are dark. The ball of wool sits in abuela's lap. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Luis or Carmen (drawn), on the rug with eyes on the screen, asks for a lamp to be lit, or to be put out (drawn, each round on its own). The frog touches a lamp: a dark one to light it, a lit one to put it out. At the start of round A one lamp is lit and two are dark; the lamps stay as he leaves them, so in round B at least one is lit and one dark.

6. **Decision A, touch a lamp (three: tests prende against apaga).** NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, apaga una luz, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Coquí, prende una luz, por favor." The speaker is drawn too.)
   - Right: the lamp changes; a nod. A star.
   - Wrong: the lamp changes. A lamp put out when one was to be lit: the room is dimmer, and abuela's needles slip. A lamp lit when one was to be put out: the screen glares, abuelo starts, and his elbow knocks the wool off abuela's lap. A stripe of the scarf comes undone, in plain sight. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Apaga una luz!" (or "¡Prende una luz!") A cross. The sign is on a right lamp (after it, the lamp he changed wrongly goes back); he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a lamp (the same game; the lamps as they are now).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, prende una luz, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela bites off the wool, and puts the scarf on the frog.
   - Three stripes: twice round his neck. Stars and a heart.
   - Two stripes: once round.
   - One stripe: a collar.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Para ti, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, muchas gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" NARR: "Por la noche abuelo ve televisión. Coquí prende y apaga la luz."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** prende 3 (steps 1, 2) and prendes once; apaga 2 (step 3) and apagas once (to weigh: one more exact). Not deciding: televisión 6 (steps 1, 3, 4), ve 2 and veo four times, ver once (steps 1 to 4).
**Forms:** prendes with prende; apagas with apaga; veo, ver with ve.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (prende or apaga).
**Chance without the Spanish:** by kind, one in two: lamps of both kinds are in the picture, and only the word says which kind to touch (by touch it is two in three or one in three, as the lamps stand).
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): también; gracias.

## Story 106: Antes y después

- **Goal he can see:** on the dinner table, among the covered dishes, a tall candle. Abuela lights it, and smiles: dinner by candlelight.
- **At stake:** the candle (3 lengths). Each mix-up takes time to put right: the candle burns down as we watch.
- **New words:** cena, antes, siempre, nunca.
- **Back again:** después, limpio, mano, mesa, perro, come, jugo, queso, fruta, flan, caliente, rico, te lavas (se lava), va, pone (pones), mira, tú, no, sí.

**Start:** night. The dinner table is laid: covered dishes, plates. Abuela beside it; abuelo already in his chair. Luis and Carmen come to the table; Canelo behind them. The frog on the sideboard, with a tray beside him: a jug of juice, a flan, a plate of cheese, a bowl of fruit.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela lifts a cover: steam. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira: la cena. Está caliente." Ask (signed, the dinner table): "¿Dónde está la cena?" He touches it: the covers shine.
2. Luis reaches for a dish; his hands are grey with dirt. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Luis! Antes de la cena te lavas las manos." Luis runs out, and comes back with his hands bright. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las manos limpias, siempre. ¿Y tú, Coquí?" The frog holds his up. COQUÍ [TURN: limpio (sí limpias, limpias, están limpias, limpio)]: "¡Sí, limpias!"
3. Canelo puts his front paws on the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Canelo! El perro nunca come en la mesa." (signed, Canelo.) He touches him: the frog shoos him to his bowl in the corner. Canelo comes back, and puts his paws up again. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Nunca, Canelo! Tú comes antes de la cena." **(comes: a form of come)** He goes back to his bowl.
4. Abuela lights the tall candle. NARR: "Abuela dice: El jugo va antes de la cena." (signed, the dinner table.) He touches it: the frog carries the jug to the table; abuela pours. NARR: "Abuela dice: El flan va después de la cena." (signed, the sideboard's shelf.) He touches it: the flan stays on the sideboard, on its shelf.

**The turn**

5. Abuela turns to her bowl of salad at the sideboard's end, her back to the room. Luis and Carmen wait on the sofa; abuelo reads his newspaper in his chair. On the frog's tray: the cheese, the fruit. The candle is tall. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, her back to the room, names a thing on the frog's tray (round A the cheese, round B the fruit) and says whether it goes before dinner or after it (drawn, each round on its own: the two rounds may be the same). The frog carries it to the table, or puts it on the sideboard's shelf.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the table, the sideboard's shelf; tests antes against después).** NARR: "Abuela dice: El queso va antes de la cena." (Or, drawn: "El queso va después de la cena.")
   - Right: the thing goes where it should. A star.
   - Wrong: the thing goes to the other place, and stays there. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Antes de la cena." (or "Después de la cena.") She turns round to look; while she looks, the candle burns down. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog carries the thing across.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the fruit).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Las frutas van después de la cena." (Drawn.) **(van: a form of va)** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. They all sit down, and eat.
   - A tall candle: its light on all the faces. Stars and a heart.
   - Half a candle.
   - A stub: abuelo brings a lamp to the table as well.
9. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Cómo está la cena, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: rico (rica, está rica, muy rica, rico)]: "¡Rica!" Canelo's nose comes up at the table's edge; everybody looks at him; it goes down again. NARR: "Abuela dice: Las manos limpias, siempre. El perro en la mesa, nunca."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** antes 3 (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: cena 6 (steps 1 to 4), siempre 1 before the game and 1 in the last line (to weigh: a third), nunca 2 before the game (step 3) and 1 in the last line.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (antes or después).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: cheese and fruit may come before a dinner or after it.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): limpio; rico.

## Story 107: Se acuesta

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's storybook, open on her lap: three pages with pictures (a boat, a star, a frog). She shows them to the children, and looks at the clock.
- **At stake:** the three pages. Each mix-up takes time: abuela looks at the clock, and turns one page down.
- **New words:** se acuesta, descansa, todos los días.
- **Back again:** tiene sueño, cama, libro, noche, son las, ocho, a las (a las siete), después, quiero, buenas noches, hasta mañana, un momento, yo, tú, no, sí.

**Start:** night; the wall clock shows eight. Abuela in the armchair with her storybook open on her lap: three pages. Carmen and Luis stand on the rug in their pyjamas, yawning. Abuelo stands by the window with his newspaper. The frog on the sofa's arm. Through the arch, the bed.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Son las ocho de la noche. A las ocho, Carmen se acuesta." (signed, the bed.) He touches it: the frog turns the blanket back; Carmen gets in. NARR: "Carmen dice: Todos los días, a las ocho... Tengo sueño."
2. Abuelo folds his newspaper. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo no tengo sueño. Yo descanso un momento." **(descanso: a form of descansa)** (signed, the sofa.) He touches it: the frog pats the cushion; abuelo sits down and puts his feet up. NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo descansa todos los días. Carmen se acuesta."
3. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y yo?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo descansa. Tú te acuestas. Todos los días, a las ocho." **(te acuestas: a form of se acuesta)** Luis gets into the bed, beside Carmen.
4. Abuela holds up her open book. NARR: "Abuela dice: Después, un libro. ¿Quieres, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!"

**The turn**

5. But the children are up again: Carmen wants water, Luis has lost a sock; they stand on the rug. Abuelo sleeps on the sofa's end, his newspaper over his face; there is room beside him. Abuela looks at the clock, and at her three pages. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, her eyes on her book, says what one of the children does now (the child is drawn; what he does is drawn on its own: either may go to bed, either may rest a moment on the sofa). The frog leads that child to the bed, or to the sofa.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the bed, the sofa; tests se acuesta against descansa).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis descansa un momento." (Or, drawn: "Luis se acuesta." The child is drawn too.)
   - Right: he goes there: under the blanket, or onto the sofa with his feet up. A star.
   - Wrong: he goes there, and stays there. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Luis descansa." (or "Luis se acuesta.") Abuela looks at the clock, and turns one page down. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the frog leads the child across.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the other child).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen se acuesta." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela claps softly: both children get into the bed. She reads; the pictures of her pages float up, one after another.
   - Three pages: a boat, a star, a frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two pages: a boat, a star.
   - One page: a boat.
9. The lamps go out. The frog hops onto the window's sill, under the moon. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hasta mañana, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: buenas noches (buenas noches)]: "¡Buenas noches!" He sings: "¡Coquí! ¡Coquí!" NARR: "Todos los días, por la noche, la familia descansa. Y Coquí canta." **(canta: heard)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se acuesta 2 (steps 1, 2) and te acuestas once (step 3); descansa 2 (steps 2, 3) and descanso once (to weigh: one more exact each). Not deciding: todos los días 3 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**Forms:** te acuestas with se acuesta; descanso with descansa.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se acuesta or descansa).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: who is named does not say where he goes.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): quiero; buenas noches.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- Words. To watch television is "ver televisión", with no article; "la televisión" is the set ("prende la televisión", "no veo la televisión"). "¿Dónde prendes una luz?" is not a question anyone asks: "¿Qué luz prendes?". Abuelo thanked the frog and described him in one breath: he thanks, abuela describes (and each verb gets an exact hearing more).
- 105: in the game either grandparent could ask for either thing, while the picture showed abuela wanting light and abuelo wanting dark: the children ask, with their eyes on the screen. The lost stripe had nothing to do with a lamp: in the dark abuela's needles slip, in the glare abuelo knocks her wool, and the stripe comes undone in plain sight. Round B drew the lamps afresh and undid his first move: the lamps stay as he leaves them. The chance was called one in two per touch, which it is not: it is by kind. The lamp to put out in the opening could be the one he had just lit for abuela: it is the middle one. The frog's "¡Yo también!" echoed Luis's "¡Y yo!": Carmen asks him.
- 106: step 2 carried seven lines and three new things in one clipped sentence, with "¿Siempre?" "Siempre." only for the count: two plain sentences. The asks repeated the line before: the lines carry the signs. The game's lines had no verb: "El queso va antes de la cena." The last line had no speaker. The children sat at the table, which is one of the two places: they wait on the sofa. Bread after dinner was odd: cheese and fruit, and in the opening juice before and flan after. A wrong move was put right by abuela before the sign was touched: it stays wrong until he touches the sign (in 107 too). Abuelo appeared from nowhere in the ending: he is at the table from the start. "Nunca" is shown twice (Canelo is shooed, comes back, and is told again), and once more in the ending.
- 107: abuelo yawned while saying he was not sleepy: he folds his newspaper. Everyone went to bed and then everyone was up again: only the children get up, and abuelo sleeps on the sofa's end. The book was shut at the start and open in the goal: open. Abuela sat on the sofa, one of the two places: she is in the armchair.

Left, and why:
- 106: "después" is his already, so the rounds can be played on that word; "antes" is taught here, as "primero" is in 98.
- "Cena" stays: it is today's name for the evening meal on the island ("la comida" is older, and means food to him). The verb is "come", never "cena".
- "Todos los días" with bedtime: a native would more often say "todas las noches"; it is the scene's phrase and it is right.
- "Siempre", "nunca" and "todos los días" decide nothing. A game on siempre against nunca was tried on paper (a sticker on a rule); it had no loss that follows from the move.
- Exact forms: "apaga", "se acuesta" and "descansa" are heard twice in that form before the game and once more in another (apagas, te acuestas, descanso).

# La noche: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `noche`). Stories 105 to 107. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 104.

Scene words (page 02): cena, televisión, ve, prende, apaga, se acuesta, descansa, la noche, antes, siempre, nunca, todos los días (la noche is his already: La playa).
How they are shared out: 105 televisión, ve, prende, apaga; 106 cena, antes, siempre, nunca; 107 se acuesta, descansa, todos los días.

The picture: the living room at night. One view. The back wall faces us: a window with the moon, a wall clock, and three lamps along it (a standing lamp at the left, a lamp on a shelf in the middle, a lamp on a small table at the right). In the middle, a sofa that faces us. At the left front, the television on a low stand, turned so that we see its screen at an angle. At the right, the dinner table with a sideboard behind it (story 106); and at the far right, through a wide arch, a corner of the bedroom with a bed (story 107). Nothing over anyone's head.

People: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 105: Prende y apaga

- **Goal he can see:** abuela knits a small scarf: three stripes are done. She holds it against the frog's neck: for you.
- **At stake:** the scarf (3 stripes). In a muddle of lamps her wool rolls away, and a stripe comes undone.
- **New words:** televisión, ve, prende, apaga.
- **Back again:** luz, noche, quiero, también, gracias, por favor, mira, ahora, yo, no, sí, una.

**Start:** night; the moon in the window. Of the three lamps, the middle one is lit. Abuelo on the sofa. Abuela beside him with her knitting. The frog on the sofa's arm. The television is dark.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, la televisión, por favor." Ask (signed, the television): "¿Dónde está la televisión?" He touches it: its screen lights up; music. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí prende la televisión. Gracias." NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo ve la televisión."
2. Abuela holds her knitting close to her eyes. NARR: "Abuela dice: No veo. Coquí, prende una luz, por favor." **(veo: a form of ve)** Ask (signed, a dark lamp): "¿Dónde prendes una luz?" He touches it: the lamp comes on. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Ahora veo."
3. Abuelo squints at the screen: the lamp's light shines on it. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Ay! No veo la televisión. Coquí, apaga una luz." Ask (signed, a lit lamp): "¿Dónde apagas una luz?" He touches it: the lamp goes out. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Gracias. Coquí apaga la luz, y yo veo la televisión."
4. Luis and Carmen come in, in their pyjamas, and sit down on the rug. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Yo quiero ver la televisión!" NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Y yo!" COQUÍ [TURN: también (yo también, también)]: "¡Yo también!" Abuela holds the small scarf against the frog's neck: three stripes.

**The turn**

5. Abuela knits; abuelo and the children watch the screen. The three lamps: one or two are lit. The ball of wool sits beside abuela. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Of the three lamps, one or two are lit and the others are dark (drawn each round). Abuela or abuelo (drawn), with eyes on the knitting or on the screen, asks for a lamp to be lit, or to be put out (drawn on its own: either of them may ask for either). The frog touches a lamp: a dark one to light it, a lit one to put it out.

6. **Decision A, touch a lamp (three: at least one lit and one dark; tests prende against apaga).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, apaga una luz, por favor." (Or, drawn: "Coquí, prende una luz, por favor." The speaker is drawn too.)
   - Right: the lamp changes; a nod. A star.
   - Wrong: the lamp changes. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Apaga una luz!" (or "¡Prende una luz!") Abuelo and abuela both reach for the lamps; the ball of wool rolls off the sofa, and a stripe of the scarf comes undone. A cross. The sign is on a right lamp; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a lamp (the same game; the lamps drawn again).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Coquí, prende una luz, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela bites off the wool, and puts the scarf on the frog.
   - Three stripes: twice round his neck. Stars and a heart.
   - Two stripes: once round.
   - One stripe: a collar.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Para ti, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, muchas gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" NARR: "Por la noche abuelo ve la televisión. Coquí prende y apaga la luz."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** prende 3 with prendes (steps 1, 2), apaga 3 with apagas (step 3). Not deciding: televisión 5 (steps 1, 3, 4), ve 5 with veo and ver (steps 1 to 4).
**Forms:** prendes with prende; apagas with apaga; veo, ver with ve.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (prende or apaga).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two: the lamps of each kind are in the picture, and only the word says which kind to touch.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): también; gracias.

## Story 106: Antes y después

- **Goal he can see:** on the dinner table, among the covered dishes, a tall candle. Abuela lights it, and smiles: dinner by candlelight.
- **At stake:** the candle (3 lengths). Each mix-up takes time to put right: the candle burns down.
- **New words:** cena, antes, siempre, nunca.
- **Back again:** después, limpio, mano, mesa, perro, come, jugo, pan, queso, guineo, fruta, caliente, rico, lista (listo), pone (pones), mira, no, sí.

**Start:** night. The dinner table is laid: covered dishes, plates. Abuela beside it. Luis and Carmen come to the table; Canelo behind them. The frog on the sideboard, with a tray beside him: a jug of juice, a basket of bread, a plate of cheese, a bowl of bananas.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela lifts a cover: steam. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira: la cena. La cena está caliente." Ask (signed, the dinner table): "¿Dónde está la cena?" He touches it: the covers shine.
2. Luis reaches for the bread; his hands are grey with dirt. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Luis! Antes de la cena, siempre: las manos limpias." NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Siempre?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Siempre." Luis runs out, and comes back with his hands bright. Ask (signed, Luis's hands): "¿Dónde están las manos limpias?" He touches them: a sparkle. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿y tus manos?" The frog holds his up. COQUÍ [TURN: limpio (limpias, están limpias, limpio)]: "¡Limpias!"
3. Canelo puts his front paws on the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Canelo! El perro nunca come en la mesa. ¡Nunca!" Ask (signed, Canelo): "¿Quién nunca come en la mesa?" **(quién: heard)** He touches him: the frog shoos him to his bowl in the corner. NARR: "Carmen dice: Canelo come antes de la cena. Nunca en la mesa."
4. Abuela lights the tall candle. NARR: "Abuela dice: Antes de la cena, el jugo." Ask (signed, the dinner table): "Antes de la cena. ¿Dónde pones el jugo?" He touches it: the frog carries the jug to the table; abuela pours. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y después de la cena, las frutas." Ask (signed, the sideboard's shelf): "Después de la cena. ¿Dónde pones las frutas?" He touches it: the bananas stay on the sideboard, on its shelf.

**The turn**

5. Abuela turns to her bowl of salad, her back to the room. Luis and Carmen sit at the table and wait, their hands in their laps. On the frog's tray: the bread, the cheese. The candle is tall. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, her back to the room, names a thing on the frog's tray (the bread or the cheese: round B's is the other) and says whether it comes before dinner or after it (drawn, each round on its own). The frog carries it to the table, or puts it on the sideboard's shelf.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the table, the sideboard's shelf; tests antes against después).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Antes de la cena, el pan." (Or, drawn: "Después de la cena, el pan.")
   - Right: the thing goes where it should. A star.
   - Wrong: the thing goes on the table when she said after: the children start on it at once. (Or onto the shelf when she said before: Luis gets up to look for it.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Antes de la cena." (or "Después de la cena.") She comes and puts it right; meanwhile the candle burns down. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the cheese).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Después de la cena, el queso." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. They eat.
   - A tall candle: its light on all the faces. Stars and a heart.
   - Half a candle.
   - A stub: abuelo brings a lamp to the table as well.
9. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Cómo está la cena, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: rico (rica, está rica, muy rica, rico)]: "¡Rica!" NARR: "Antes de la cena, las manos limpias. Siempre. Y el perro en la mesa: nunca."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** antes 4 (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: cena 7 (steps 1 to 4), siempre 3 (step 2), nunca 3 lines (step 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (antes or después).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: bread and cheese may come before a dinner or after it.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): limpio; rico.

## Story 107: Se acuesta

- **Goal he can see:** abuela's storybook, open on her lap: three pages with pictures (a boat, a star, a frog). She shows them to the children, and looks at the clock.
- **At stake:** the three pages. Each mix-up takes time: abuela looks at the clock, and turns one page down.
- **New words:** se acuesta, descansa, todos los días.
- **Back again:** tiene sueño, cama, libro, lee, noche, son las, ocho, a las (a las siete), después, quiero, buenas noches, hasta mañana, yo, tú, no, sí.

**Start:** night; the wall clock shows eight. Abuela on the sofa with a book shut on her lap. Luis, Carmen and abuelo stand about, yawning. The frog on the sofa's arm. Through the arch, the bed.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Son las ocho de la noche. A las ocho, Carmen se acuesta." Ask (signed, the bed): "Carmen se acuesta. ¿Dónde está la cama?" He touches it: the frog turns the blanket back; Carmen gets in. NARR: "Carmen dice: Todos los días, a las ocho... Tengo sueño."
2. Abuelo yawns. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Yo no tengo sueño. Yo descanso." **(descanso: a form of descansa)** Ask (signed, the sofa): "Abuelo descansa. ¿Dónde?" He touches it: the frog pats the cushion; abuelo sits down and puts his feet up. NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo descansa todos los días. Carmen se acuesta."
3. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y yo?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo descansa. Tú te acuestas. Todos los días, a las ocho." **(te acuestas: a form of se acuesta)** Luis gets into the bed, beside Carmen.
4. Abuela opens her book: three pages. NARR: "Abuela dice: Después, un libro. ¿Quieres, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!"

**The turn**

5. But they are all up again: Carmen wants water, Luis has lost a sock, abuelo wanders with his newspaper. Abuela looks at the clock, and at her three pages. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, her eyes on her book, says what one of them does now (the person is drawn from Luis, Carmen and abuelo; what he does is drawn on its own: any of them may go to bed, any of them may rest). The frog leads that one to the bed, or to the sofa.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the bed, the sofa; tests se acuesta against descansa).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis descansa." (Or, drawn: "Luis se acuesta." The person is drawn too.)
   - Right: he goes there: under the blanket, or onto the sofa with his feet up. A star.
   - Wrong: he goes there. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Luis descansa." (or "Luis se acuesta.") He gets up again and goes across. Abuela looks at the clock, and turns one page down. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; another of them).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo se acuesta." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. They are all settled. Abuela reads; the pictures of her pages float up, one after another.
   - Three pages: a boat, a star, a frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two pages: a boat, a star.
   - One page: a boat.
9. The lamps go out. The frog hops onto the window's sill, under the moon. NARR: "Abuela dice: Hasta mañana, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: buenas noches (buenas noches)]: "¡Buenas noches!" He sings: "¡Coquí! ¡Coquí!" NARR: "Todos los días, por la noche, la familia descansa. Y Coquí canta." **(canta: heard)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se acuesta 3 (steps 1, 2) and te acuestas once (step 3); descansa 3 (steps 2, 3) and descanso once. Not deciding: todos los días 3 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**Forms:** te acuestas with se acuesta; descanso with descansa.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (se acuesta or descansa).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: who is named does not say where he goes.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): quiero; buenas noches.

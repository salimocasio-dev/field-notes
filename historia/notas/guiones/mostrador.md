# El mostrador: three stories (draft 1)

Place 4, El colmado. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `mostrador`). Stories 36 to 38. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa, El parque and La cocina (stories 1 to 35).

Scene words (page 02): colmado, buenos días, buenas tardes, necesito, esto, eso, ¿tiene…?, aquí tiene, bolsa, compra, vende, ¿algo más?

The picture: don Pepe's corner shop. Two views. `calle`: the shop front from the street: its door, an awning, a wall at the right; the sky shows the hour (morning: a low yellow sun and a rooster on the wall; afternoon: a high orange sun, no rooster, long shadows). `dentro`: inside: the counter across the picture, don Pepe behind it, shelves of goods behind him (leche, pan, huevos, queso, café, arroz, azúcar, jugo, habichuelas: each drawn so it can be told from the others), a ladder, the back door at the right, the street door at the left (the sky shows through it), a dish for coins on the counter. The frog stands on the counter when he serves or packs; otherwise on the floor in front of it.

New people: don Pepe, the shopkeeper (a man with a moustache and an apron). The customers are the señora and the señor of El banco.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La cocina. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places.

## Story 36: El colmado

- **Goal he can see:** a tray of three warm quesitos on the counter, steam rising. Luis and the frog look at it; don Pepe leaves it with them.
- **At stake:** the quesitos (3). A customer who is handed the wrong thing gets one, for the trouble. What is left at the end is theirs.
- **New words:** colmado, buenos días, necesito, aquí tiene.
- **Back again:** leche, pan, huevos, queso, café, arroz, azúcar, jugo, habichuelas, tengo, señora, señor, por favor, gracias, espera, no hay, huele.

**Start:** `calle`, morning. The rooster on the wall. Luis and the frog on the pavement; Luis holds abuela's list, a paper with three pictures (milk, bread, eggs). The corner card shows the list.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The rooster crows. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van al colmado." The shop twinkles. NARR: "Mira. Este es el colmado de don Pepe."
2. Ask (signed, the shop): "¿Dónde está el colmado?" He touches it: the door opens, a bell rings, they go in. The view goes to `dentro`.
3. Don Pepe lifts a hand. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Buenos días!" COQUÍ: "¡Buenos días, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Cómo está abuela?" COQUÍ [TURN: abuela (abuela), bien (bien)]: "Abuela está bien."
4. The quesitos steam. A thought over the frog: a quesito. NARR: "¡Mmm! ¡Huele bien!" Luis taps the list; the milk lights on the card. NARR: "Luis dice: Necesito leche, por favor." Ask (signed, the milk on the shelf): "¿Dónde está la leche?" He touches it: don Pepe takes it down and sets it on the counter. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." Luis puts it in his bag. A tick on the card.
5. The bread lights. NARR: "Luis dice: Necesito pan." Ask (signed, the bread): "Toca el pan." He touches it: don Pepe sets it on the counter. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, don Pepe!" A tick.
6. The eggs light. NARR: "Luis dice: Y necesito huevos." Don Pepe looks at the shelf: the place for eggs is empty. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí no hay huevos. ¡Espera!"

**The turn**

7. Don Pepe pushes the tray of quesitos over to Luis and the frog (a hand on the tray, then open towards them: yours), and goes out at the back door. The frog hops onto the counter. The bell: the señora comes in with a shut basket; behind her the señor with a shut bag. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días!" COQUÍ: "¡Buenos días, señora!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The frog serves. Each customer names two things: one that is already in the shut basket, one that is needed. Two things from the shelf stand on the counter in front of the frog: those two. Which two, and which is said first, is drawn each round; round B takes a fresh pair and the other order.

8. **Decision A, touch a thing (two things; tests necesito).** NARR: "La señora dice: Tengo café. Necesito arroz." (Or, drawn: "La señora dice: Necesito arroz. Tengo café.")
   - Right: the frog pushes it over. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She puts a coin in the dish and goes. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the wrong thing over; she shakes her head and opens her basket: the same thing is in it. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Tengo café. Necesito arroz." Luis gives her a quesito from the tray; she smiles. One quesito gone. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She pays and goes.
9. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the señor).** NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Buenos días!" Then his line, as in A, with the other order. Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

10. Don Pepe comes back with a carton of eggs. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Huevos! Aquí tiene." The last tick. He looks into the dish (two coins) and at the tray.
    - Three quesitos left: Luis and the frog eat one each; the third goes into the bag for abuela. COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Qué rico!" Stars and a heart.
    - Two left: one each. COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Qué rico!"
    - One left: Luis breaks it in two; half each. The frog says nothing.
11. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Adiós!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós, don Pepe!" They go out at the street door. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** necesito 3 (steps 4, 5, 6). Not deciding: colmado 3 (steps 1, 1, 2); buenos días 4 before the game (step 3 twice, step 7 twice); aquí tiene 2 before the game, 3 or more by the end.
**A miss is filed under:** necesito.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two. The order is drawn, both things stand on the counter and the basket is shut. A learner who knows the old word "tengo" and not "necesito" is right every time: the owner is told.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 3, 5, 11): abuela and bien; gracias; adiós.

## Story 37: ¿Algo más?

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen's five coins in a row on the counter, and through the street door the piragua man's cart outside. Three coins are for abuela's three things; what is left is for piraguas.
- **At stake:** the coins (5). A wrong answer makes don Pepe open a malta, or hand over a paper bag: each costs a coin.
- **New words:** ¿tiene…?, ¿algo más?, bolsa, compra.
- **Back again:** buenos días, necesito, aquí tiene, colmado, queso, café, azúcar, pan, leche, sí, no, tengo, gracias, piragua, casa.

**Start:** `dentro`, morning. Don Pepe behind the counter. Carmen and the frog come in; Carmen has a cloth bag on her shoulder and her purse in her hand. Abuela's list on the card: cheese, coffee, sugar. Outside the door, the piragua cart.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Carmen y Coquí están en el colmado." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Buenos días, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Buenos días! ¿Dónde está Luis?" COQUÍ [TURN: casa (casa)]: "Luis está en casa."
2. Carmen lays her five coins on the counter. The piragua cart twinkles; a thought over the frog: a piragua. NARR: "Carmen compra comida para abuela." The cheese lights on the card. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Tiene queso?" Ask (signed, the cheese on the shelf): "¿Dónde está el queso?" He touches it: don Pepe takes it down. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Sí. Aquí tiene." A coin goes into the dish. A tick. NARR: "Carmen compra queso."
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" The coffee lights. NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. ¿Tiene café?" Ask (signed, the coffee): "Toca el café." He touches it. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." A coin; a tick. NARR: "Carmen compra café."
4. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" The sugar lights. NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. ¿Tiene azúcar?" Ask (signed, the sugar): "¿Y el azúcar?" He touches it. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." A coin; the last tick. Two coins are left.
5. Carmen takes the cloth bag off her shoulder and holds it open. NARR: "Carmen tiene una bolsa." Don Pepe puts the three things in, one by one. NARR: "El queso, el café y el azúcar van en la bolsa." Ask (signed, the bag): "¿Dónde está la bolsa?" He touches it: Carmen lifts it. COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, don Pepe!"

**The turn**

6. The bell: the señora comes in, with no bag. Don Pepe turns to her. NARR: "La señora compra pan." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" NARR: "La señora dice: Sí. Necesito leche." He sets the milk beside the bread. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Tiene bolsa?" She shows her empty hands. NARR: "La señora dice: No. No tengo bolsa." He takes a paper bag from under the counter (a coin is drawn on the pile of bags), packs it and takes one coin more from her. She goes. Beside the dish stands a row of maltas (a coin is drawn on their crate). The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Pepe turns back to Carmen and the frog. Carmen's list is all ticked, and her bag is in her hand. He asks one of his two questions; the frog answers with the sí and no bubbles. Which question comes in round A is drawn; round B is the other one.

7. **Decision A, say sí or no (tests the question asked).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" (right: no) or NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Tiene bolsa?" (right: sí).
   - Right, "¿Algo más?": COQUÍ: "No, gracias." Don Pepe nods. A star. Right, "¿Tiene bolsa?": COQUÍ: "Sí." Carmen lifts her bag. A star.
   - Wrong, "¿Algo más?" (he said sí): don Pepe opens a malta with a hiss and sets it down; he takes a coin. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen tiene el queso, el café y el azúcar." Carmen drinks the malta and shrugs. The sign is on the no bubble; he touches it. COQUÍ: "No, gracias."
   - Wrong, "¿Tiene bolsa?" (he said no): don Pepe hands over a paper bag and takes a coin. A cross. NARR: "No, Coquí. Mira. Carmen tiene una bolsa." Carmen holds up both bags. The sign is on the sí bubble; he touches it. COQUÍ: "Sí."
8. **Decision B, the other question.** As A.

**The ending**

9. Carmen picks up the coins that are left. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Adiós, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Adiós! ¡Gracias!" They go out to the piragua cart.
    - Two coins left: a piragua for Carmen and one for the frog. NARR: "Carmen compra dos piraguas." COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, Carmen!" Stars and a heart.
    - One coin: one piragua; Carmen holds it out and they share it. NARR: "Carmen compra una piragua."
    - None: Carmen shows her empty purse; the piragua man waves. NARR: "Carmen no compra piraguas."
10. NARR: "Carmen y Coquí van a casa con la bolsa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿algo más? 3 (steps 3, 4, 6); ¿tiene…? 3 as a question to don Pepe (steps 2, 3, 4) and ¿tiene bolsa? once (step 6); bolsa 5 (steps 5, 5, 6, 6 and the question). Not deciding: compra 5 or more.
**A miss is filed under:** ¿algo más? or bolsa, by the question asked.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two: the picture is the same for both questions (list ticked, bag in hand), and the right answers differ.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 5): casa; gracias. ("No, gracias." in a costed decision is not a turn.)

## Story 38: Esto y eso

- **Goal he can see:** it is hot; three children wait outside the street door, each with a coin in his hand, looking in at don Pepe's little freezer: five limbers stand in it, seen through its glass lid.
- **At stake:** the limbers (5). Two are for the two customers of the game; three for the children. A limber pushed across to somebody who did not ask for it cannot go back into the freezer: it melts on the counter.
- **New words:** esto, eso, vende, buenas tardes.
- **Back again:** quiero, por favor, hace calor, frío, colmado, compra, aquí tiene, dame, niño, niña, triste, contento.

**Start:** `dentro`, afternoon: orange light at the door, long shadows. Don Pepe behind the counter with a fan in his hand; the freezer on the counter at his end. The frog on the counter at the other end. Luis beside the counter. The three children outside the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Hace calor." Luis comes in. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Buenas tardes, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" The freezer twinkles; don Pepe lifts a limber out and holds it up. NARR: "Don Pepe vende limbers." **(new: limber; the picture carries it)** NARR: "¡Mmm! Está frío."
2. Don Pepe sets one limber down at his own end (white, coconut) and slides another along the counter to the frog's end (orange). He lays his hand on the one beside him. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Esto es para Luis." He looks at the one by the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y eso es para Coquí." Ask (signed, the orange limber by the frog): "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, toma eso." He touches it: the frog picks it up and licks it. COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!"
3. Luis pays a coin and takes the white one. NARR: "Luis compra un limber." A fan stands at Luis's end of the counter; the coin dish stands at don Pepe's end. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Luis, dame eso, por favor." Ask (signed, the fan, far from don Pepe): "¿Dónde está eso?" He touches it: Luis hands the fan over. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Gracias. Y esto es para ti." **(para ti: heard)** He gives Luis a coin back out of the dish beside him. Luis goes out with his limber.
4. Don Pepe sits down on his chair behind the counter and fans himself. NARR: "Don Pepe está cansado." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, ¿quieres vender?" **(vender: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si), quiero (quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "Ahora Coquí vende limbers."

**The turn**

5. The bell: the señora comes in and stands at the far end of the counter from the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" COQUÍ: "¡Buenas tardes, señora!" Don Pepe, from his chair, lifts two limbers out of the freezer: one he sets at the señora's end, the other at the frog's end. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two limbers of different colours on the counter: one beside the customer, one far from her, beside the frog. Her hands stay on her purse. Which colour is where, and which she asks for, is drawn; round B is drawn again, and at least one of the two rounds asks for the far one.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two limbers; tests esto or eso).** NARR: "La señora dice: Quiero esto, por favor." (the one beside her) or NARR: "La señora dice: Quiero eso, por favor." (the one by the frog).
   - Right: the frog pushes it to her (or she lifts the one beside her as he touches it). COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She pays a coin. Don Pepe puts the other limber back into the freezer. NARR: "Coquí vende un limber." A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the wrong one into her hands; she shakes her head and sets it down on the counter. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Eso no. Quiero esto." (Or: "Esto no. Quiero eso.") The limber on the counter melts into a puddle. One limber gone. The sign is on the right one; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She pays.
7. **Decision B, the same game: the señor.** NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" Two more limbers come out. His line, as in A. Right and wrong as in A. NARR (right): "Coquí vende otro limber." **(otro: heard)**

**The ending**

8. Don Pepe gets up and opens the freezer: what is left can be counted. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Niños!" The three children come in. NARR: "Los niños dicen: ¡Buenas tardes!" Each child who gets a limber pays his coin.
    - Three left: every child has one. NARR: "Los niños compran limbers. Están contentos." COQUÍ: "¡Hace calor!" Don Pepe gives the frog the coin dish to hold: full. Stars and a heart.
    - Two left: two children have one; the third looks into the empty freezer. NARR: "Un niño no tiene limber. Está triste."
    - One left: one child has one; two look into the empty freezer. NARR: "Dos niños no tienen limber. Están tristes."
9. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¡Hasta mañana!" COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** esto 3 (steps 2, 3 and the frog hears it of the thing beside the speaker each time); eso 3 (steps 2, 2, 3). Not deciding: buenas tardes 4 before the game; vende 3 (steps 1, 4, and "vender" once).
**A miss is filed under:** esto or eso, whichever was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two: the two limbers differ only in colour and place, and she points at nothing.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 2, 4, 9): gracias; sí and quiero; hasta mañana.

## Needs (for the scene's designer and the code lane)

The set `mostrador`:
- Views: `calle` (the shop front, zoom 1), `dentro` (inside, zoom 1).
- States: `hora` morning or afternoon (sky, light, rooster); `puerta` shut or open; `atras` (back door) shut or open.
- In the set: shop front with door, awning and wall; inside: counter, shelves, ladder, back door, street door with the sky and (story 37) the piragua cart or (story 38) the three children outside it.
- Things he can touch: colmado (in `calle`); on the shelf and on the counter: leche, pan, huevos, queso, cafe, arroz, azucar, jugo, habichuelas; bolsa (Carmen's cloth bag); limber_a, limber_b (the two on the counter); abanico (the fan); quesitos; nevera (the freezer); pepe, luis, carmen, senora, senor, coqui; the sí and no bubbles.
- Spots: acera (pavement, both), puerta, frente (floor in front of the counter), mostrador_izq, mostrador_der (on the counter), cola1, cola2 (customers waiting), silla (don Pepe's chair), atras.
- New people (sprites): don Pepe (standing, reaching up, sitting with a fan); three children at the door. The señora and the señor are El banco's, with a basket or a bag, shut or open.
- New speaker key: `pepe` (bubble over don Pepe); `ninos` (the three children together).

Acts (name: what it does on screen; story and step):
- `entra` {quien}: comes in at the street door, bell (36: 2, 7; 37: 6; 38: 1, 5, 8). `sale` {quien, por}: goes out by the street or the back door (36: 7, 8, 9, 11; 37; 38).
- `lista` {k, hecho}: a picture on the list card lights, or gets its tick (36: 4 to 6, 10; 37: 2 to 4).
- `baja` {k}: don Pepe takes a thing off the shelf and sets it on the counter (36: 4, 5; 37: 2 to 4). `nohay` {k}: he looks at an empty place on the shelf and shrugs (36: 6).
- `guarda` {quien, k}: the thing goes into Luis's bag, or into Carmen's cloth bag held open (36: 4, 5; 37: 5).
- `bandeja` {que}: `da`: don Pepe pushes the tray to Luis and the frog; `quita`: Luis hands a quesito to a customer; `comen` n: the ending for three, two or one left (36: 7, 8, 9, 10).
- `piensa` {quien, k}: a thought bubble: quesito, piragua (36: 4; 37: 2).
- `pon2` {a, b}: two things stand on the counter in front of the frog (36: 8, 9). `sirve` {k, a}: the frog pushes a thing across to a customer (36: 8, 9; 38: 6, 7).
- `cesta` {quien, k}: a customer opens the basket or bag: the thing in it shows (36: 8, 9).
- `paga` {quien, n}: coins go from a hand, or from the row on the counter, into the dish (36; 37: 2 to 4, 6 to 8; 38).
- `monedas` {n}: Carmen's row of coins (37: 2 on). `malta`: don Pepe opens one, sets it down; Carmen drinks it (37: 7, 8). `bolsapapel` {a}: he hands a paper bag over (37: 6, 7, 8).
- `piraguas` {n}: the ending at the cart: two, one or none (37: 9).
- `limber` {que}: `saca` (one out of the freezer, held up), `pon` {col_a, col_b, cerca} (two on the counter: one beside the customer, one beside the frog), `vuelve` (the untouched one back into the freezer), `derrite` (the one set down melts), `cuenta` (the freezer opens: what is left) (38).
- `abanico`: Luis hands the fan over; don Pepe sits and fans himself (38: 3, 4). `ninos` {n}: the children come in; n of them get a limber and pay, the others look into the freezer (38: 8).
- `final` {k}: stars and a heart when nothing was lost.

# El mostrador: three stories (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 4, El colmado. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `mostrador`). Stories 36 to 38. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa, El parque and La cocina (stories 1 to 35).

Scene words (page 02): colmado, buenos días, buenas tardes, necesito, esto, eso, ¿tiene…?, aquí tiene, bolsa, compra, vende, ¿algo más?

The picture: don Pepe's corner shop. Two views. `calle`: the shop front from the street: its door, an awning, a wall at the right; the sky shows the hour (morning: a low yellow sun and a rooster on the wall; afternoon: a high orange sun, no rooster, long shadows). `dentro`: inside (the picture changes from one to the other; it does not slide): the counter across the picture, don Pepe behind it, shelves of goods behind him (leche, pan, mantequilla, queso, café, arroz, azúcar, jugo, habichuelas: each drawn so it can be told from the others), a ladder, the back door at the right, the street door at the left (the sky shows through it), a dish for coins on the counter. The frog stands on the counter when he serves or packs; otherwise on the floor in front of it.

New people: don Pepe, the shopkeeper (a man with a moustache and an apron). The customers are the señora and the señor of El banco.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in La cocina. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places.

## Story 36: El colmado

- **Goal he can see:** a tray of three warm quesitos on the counter, steam rising. From the moment they come in, abuela and the frog look at it (a thought of a quesito over the frog); don Pepe leaves it with them.
- **At stake:** the quesitos (3). A customer who is handed the wrong thing gets one, for the trouble. What is left at the end is theirs.
- **New words:** colmado, buenos días, necesito, aquí tiene.
- **Back again:** leche, pan, mantequilla, queso, café, arroz, azúcar, jugo, habichuelas, tengo, señora, señor, por favor, gracias, espera, no hay, huele, me llamo, se sienta.

**Start:** `calle`, morning. The rooster on the wall. Abuela and the frog on the pavement; abuela holds her list, a paper with three pictures (milk, bread, butter). The corner card shows the list.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The rooster crows. NARR: "Abuela y Coquí van al colmado." The shop twinkles. NARR: "Mira. Este es el colmado de don Pepe."
2. Ask (signed, the shop): "¿Dónde está el colmado?" He touches it: the door opens, a bell rings, they go in. The picture changes to `dentro`.
3. Don Pepe lifts a hand. The quesitos steam; abuela and the frog look at them; a thought over the frog: a quesito. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Buenos días!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Buenos días, don Pepe!" Don Pepe bends to the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Quién eres tú?" COQUÍ [TURN: me llamo (me llamo)]: "Me llamo Coquí."
4. Abuela taps her list; the milk lights on the card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Necesito leche, por favor." Ask (signed, the milk on the shelf): "¿Dónde está la leche?" He touches it: don Pepe takes it down and sets it on the counter. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." Abuela puts it in her bag. A tick on the card.
5. The bread lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y necesito pan." Ask (signed, the bread): "Toca el pan." He touches it: don Pepe sets it on the counter. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene." A tick.
6. The butter lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y necesito mantequilla." Don Pepe looks at the shelf: its place is empty. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Ay, aquí no hay. ¡Espere!" **(espere: heard)**

**The turn**

7. Don Pepe pushes the tray of quesitos over to abuela and the frog (a hand on the tray, then open towards them: yours), and goes out at the back door. Abuela sits down on the chair by the counter and fans herself. NARR: "Abuela se sienta." The frog hops onto the counter. The bell: the señora comes in with a shut basket; behind her the señor with a shut bag. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días!" COQUÍ: "¡Buenos días, señora!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The frog serves. Each customer names two things: one that is already in the shut basket, one that is needed. Two things from the shelf stand on the counter in front of the frog: those two. Which two, and which is said first, is drawn afresh each round.

8. **Decision A, touch a thing (two things; tests necesito).** NARR: "La señora dice: Tengo café. Necesito arroz." (Or, drawn: "La señora dice: Necesito arroz. Tengo café.")
   - Right: the frog pushes it over. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She puts a coin in the dish and goes. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the wrong thing over; she shakes her head and opens her basket: the same thing is in it. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Tengo café. Necesito arroz." Abuela gives her a quesito from the tray; she smiles. One quesito gone. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She pays and goes.
9. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the señor).** NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Buenos días!" Then his line, as in A, drawn afresh. Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

10. Don Pepe comes back with the butter. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡La mantequilla! Aquí tiene." The last tick. He looks into the dish (two coins) and at the tray. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres un quesito, Coquí?" **(new: quesito; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, por favor!"
    - Three quesitos left: abuela and the frog eat one each; the third goes into the bag. COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Qué rico!" Stars and a heart.
    - Two left: one each. COQUÍ: "¡Mmm! ¡Qué rico!"
    - One left: abuela breaks it in two; half each.
11. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Gracias! ¡Hasta mañana!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós, don Pepe!" They go out at the street door. NARR: "Abuela y Coquí van para la casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** necesito 3 (steps 4, 5, 6). Not deciding: colmado 3 (steps 1, 1, 2); buenos días 4 before the game; aquí tiene 2 before the game, said to abuela, and 3 or more by the end.
**A miss is filed under:** necesito.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, in each round on its own. The order is drawn, both things stand on the counter and the basket is shut. A learner who knows the old word "tengo" and not "necesito" is right every time: the owner is told.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 3, 10, 11): me llamo; sí; adiós. ("por favor" and "de nada" are not tracked words of any story, so no turn is filed under them.)

## Story 37: ¿Algo más?

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen's five coins in a row on the counter, and through the street door the piragua man's cart outside. Three coins are for abuela's three things; what is left is for piraguas.
- **At stake:** the coins (5). A wrong answer makes don Pepe open a malta, or hand over a paper bag: each costs a coin.
- **New words:** ¿tiene…?, ¿algo más?, bolsa, compra.
- **Back again:** buenos días, necesito, aquí tiene, colmado, queso, café, azúcar, pan, sí, no, tengo, gracias, todo, piragua, casa, bien.

**Start:** `dentro`, morning. Don Pepe behind the counter. Carmen and the frog come in; Carmen has a cloth bag on her shoulder and her purse in her hand. Abuela's list on the card: cheese, coffee, sugar. Outside the door, the piragua cart.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Carmen y Coquí están en el colmado." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Buenos días, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Buenos días! ¿Dónde está Luis?" COQUÍ [TURN: casa (casa)]: "Luis está en casa."
2. Carmen lays her five coins on the counter. The piragua cart twinkles; a thought over the frog: a piragua. NARR: "Carmen compra tres cosas para abuela." The cheese lights on the card. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¿Tiene queso?" Ask (signed, the cheese on the shelf): "¿Dónde está el queso?" He touches it: don Pepe takes it down. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Sí. Aquí tienes." **(aquí tienes: what he says to a child)** A coin goes into the dish. A tick. NARR: "Carmen compra queso."
3. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" The coffee lights. NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. ¿Tiene café?" Ask (signed, the coffee): "Toca el café." He touches it. A coin; a tick.
4. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" The sugar lights. NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. ¿Tiene azúcar?" Ask (signed, the sugar): "Toca el azúcar." He touches it. A coin; the last tick. Two coins are left. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" Carmen looks at her list: all ticks. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, gracias."
5. Carmen takes the cloth bag off her shoulder and holds it open. NARR: "Carmen tiene una bolsa." Don Pepe puts the three things in, one by one. NARR: "El queso, el café y el azúcar van en la bolsa." Ask (signed, the bag): "¿Dónde está la bolsa?" He touches it: Carmen lifts it.

**The turn**

6. The bell: the señora comes in, with nothing in her hands. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien)]: "¡Bien, gracias!" NARR: "La señora dice: Necesito pan." Don Pepe sets it down. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Aquí tiene. ¿Tiene bolsa?" She shows her empty hands. NARR: "La señora dice: No. No tengo bolsa." He takes a paper bag from under the counter (a coin is drawn on the pile of bags), packs the bread and takes one coin more from her. She goes. Carmen walks to the door and looks out at the piragua cart, the bag on her arm. Beside the dish stands a row of maltas (a coin is drawn on their crate). The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Don Pepe, wiping the counter, turns to the frog. Carmen's list is all ticked, and her bag is on her arm. He asks one of his two questions; the frog answers with the sí and no bubbles. Which question he asks is drawn afresh each round.

7. **Decision A, say sí or no (tests the question asked).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Algo más?" (right: no) or NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¿Tienes bolsa?" (right: sí).
   - Right, "¿Algo más?": COQUÍ: "No, gracias." Don Pepe nods. A star. Right, "¿Tienes bolsa?": COQUÍ: "¡Sí!" Carmen lifts her bag. A star.
   - Wrong, "¿Algo más?" (he said sí): don Pepe opens a malta with a hiss and sets it down; he takes a coin. A cross. Carmen turns round. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Tengo todo." She drinks the malta and shrugs. The sign is on the no bubble; he touches it. COQUÍ: "No, gracias."
   - Wrong, "¿Tienes bolsa?" (he said no): don Pepe hands over a paper bag and takes a coin. A cross. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Tengo bolsa." She holds up both bags. The sign is on the sí bubble; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Sí!"
8. **Decision B, drawn again.** As A.

**The ending**

9. Carmen picks up the coins that are left. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Adiós, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Adiós! ¡Gracias!" They go out to the piragua cart.
    - Two coins left: a piragua for Carmen and one for the frog. NARR: "Carmen compra dos piraguas." COQUÍ: "¡Gracias, Carmen!" Stars and a heart.
    - One coin: one piragua; Carmen holds it out and they share it. NARR: "Carmen compra una piragua."
    - None: Carmen shows her empty purse; the piragua man waves. NARR: "Carmen no compra piraguas."
10. NARR: "Carmen y Coquí van para la casa con la bolsa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿algo más? 3 (steps 3, 4, 4), with both a sí and a no answer heard; bolsa 5 (steps 5, 5, 5, 6, 6); ¿tiene…? 3 as a question to don Pepe (steps 2, 3, 4) and "¿Tiene bolsa?" once (step 6). Not deciding: compra 3 or more.
**A miss is filed under:** ¿algo más? or bolsa, by the question asked.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, in each round on its own: the picture is the same for both questions (list ticked, bag on her arm), and the right answers differ.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 6): casa; bien.

## Story 38: Esto y eso

- **Goal he can see:** it is hot; three children wait outside the street door, each with a coin in his hand, looking in at don Pepe's little freezer: five límbers stand in it, seen through its glass lid.
- **At stake:** the límbers in the freezer (5). Two are for the two customers of the game; three for the children. A límber pushed across to somebody who did not ask for it cannot go back into the freezer: it melts on the counter.
- **New words:** esto, eso, vende, buenas tardes.
- **Back again:** quiero, por favor, hace calor, frío, colmado, compra, aquí tiene, toma, mira, niño, triste, contento, cansado, gracias.

**Start:** `dentro`, afternoon: orange light at the door, long shadows. Don Pepe behind the counter; the freezer on the counter at his end, five límbers in it; a small tray on the counter with two more límbers, one at don Pepe's hand (white) and one at the far end, by the frog (orange). The frog on the counter. Luis comes in. The three children outside the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Hace calor." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Buenas tardes, don Pepe!" NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" He lays his hand on the límber beside him. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Esto es un límber." **(new: límber; the picture carries it)** NARR: "Don Pepe vende límbers."
2. Hand still on it. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Esto es para Luis." He looks along the counter at the one by the frog. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Y eso es para Coquí." Ask (signed, the orange límber by the frog): "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, toma eso." He touches it: the frog picks it up and licks it. COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "Gracias."
3. Luis pays a coin and takes the white one. NARR: "Luis compra un límber." He licks it. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mmm! ¡Esto está frío!" He nods at the freezer at the far end and the children outside. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira eso!"
4. Don Pepe sits down on his chair behind the counter and fans himself. NARR: "Don Pepe está cansado." NARR: "Don Pepe dice: Coquí, ¿quieres vender?" **(vender: heard)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si), quiero (quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "Ahora Coquí vende límbers."

**The turn**

5. The bell: the señora comes in and stands at the far end of the counter from the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" COQUÍ: "¡Buenas tardes, señora!" Don Pepe, from his chair, lifts two límbers out of the freezer: one he sets at the señora's end, the other at the frog's end. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two límbers of different colours on the counter: one beside the customer, one far from her, beside the frog. Her hands stay on her purse. Which colour is where, and which she asks for, is drawn afresh each round.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two límbers; tests esto or eso).** NARR: "La señora dice: Quiero esto, por favor." (the one beside her) or NARR: "La señora dice: Quiero eso, por favor." (the one by the frog).
   - Right: the frog pushes it to her (or she lifts the one beside her as he touches it). COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She pays a coin. Don Pepe puts the other límber back into the freezer. NARR: "Coquí vende un límber." A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the wrong one into her hands; she shakes her head and sets it down on the counter. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Eso no. Quiero esto." (Or: "Esto no. Quiero eso.") The límber on the counter melts into a puddle. One límber gone. The sign is on the right one; he touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Aquí tiene!" She pays.
7. **Decision B, the same game, drawn again: the señor.** NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Buenas tardes!" Two more límbers come out. His line, as in A. Right and wrong as in A. NARR (right): "Coquí vende otro límber." **(otro: heard)**

**The ending**

8. Don Pepe gets up and opens the freezer: what is left can be counted. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Vengan, niños!" **(vengan: heard)** The three children come in. NARR: "Los niños dicen: ¡Buenas tardes!" Each child who gets a límber pays his coin.
    - Three left: every child has one. NARR: "Los niños compran límbers. Están contentos." Stars and a heart.
    - Two left: two children have one; the third looks into the empty freezer. NARR: "Un niño no tiene límber. Está triste."
    - One left: one child has one; two look into the empty freezer. NARR: "Dos niños no tienen límber. Están tristes."
9. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" The frog waves.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** esto 3 (steps 1, 2, 3), each of the thing beside the speaker; eso 3 (steps 2, 2, 3), each of a thing far from the speaker. Not deciding: buenas tardes 4 before the game; vende 3 (steps 1, 4, and "vender" once).
**A miss is filed under:** esto or eso, whichever was asked for.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, in each round on its own: the two límbers differ only in colour and place, and she points at nothing.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 4): gracias; sí and quiero.

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers saw draft 1 with only the owner's rules: one as a beginner, one for Puerto Rican Spanish.

Fixed after the beginner: every second round is drawn on its own (in draft 1 it was "the other one", so it could be worked out from the first); 36 shows the quesitos and who looks at them from the moment they come in; three turns that echoed the line before them are real replies now ("Me llamo Coquí.", "¡Sí, por favor!", "¡Bien, gracias!"); 37 hears a "No, gracias." to "¿Algo más?" before the game, loses two lines that the ticks already said, has a short turn, and Carmen herself says what was wrong after a miss; 38's límbers are counted right (the two of the opening are on a tray, the five in the freezer are the ones to keep), the fan beat and the coin back are gone, and "esto" is heard three times before it can cost.

Fixed after the Spanish: don Pepe says tú to the children ("Aquí tienes.", "¿Tienes bolsa?") and usted to the grown-ups, so in 36 it is abuela who shops and "Aquí tiene." is said to her; butter is on the list in place of eggs ("no hay huevos" and "¡Huevos!" both have a rude second meaning); "van para la casa"; "límber" is written with its accent so the voice stresses it right; "¡Vengan, niños!"; a pointless "¿Dónde está eso?" is gone.

Left as written: "bolsa" is the island's word for a shopping bag (weighed against "funda"); "Quiero esto / eso" with no noun passes when it is said of a thing in view.

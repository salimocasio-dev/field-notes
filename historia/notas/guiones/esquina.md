# La esquina: three stories (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `esquina`). Stories 48 to 50. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa, El parque, La cocina and El colmado (stories 1 to 47).

Scene words (page 02): calle, esquina, carro, guagua, bicicleta, gente, cruza, pasa, viene, va, ahora, luego.

The picture (as the set `esquina-set.html` draws it): the street corner outside don Pepe's colmado. One view. Across the top, the colmado's front: its shop window under an awning, its door with the basket sign, then the building's corner at the right, where a narrow side street goes off behind it. In front of the colmado its pavement; then the street, two lanes, across the picture; then the near pavement at the bottom left, with the park's low wall and grass behind it. A striped crossing goes over the street beside the corner, with the traffic light's pole and its button box at the colmado's end of it. Whatever drives comes from the right and goes left, so its lit side faces us; a guagua's door is on that side. The frog's corner (story 49) is the colmado's pavement at the pole; "the far pavement" where mamá and the bebé wait is the near pavement at the bottom; going "round the corner" is walking off along the colmado's side wall. In story 48 abuela, the bebé and the frog stand on the near pavement, the car pulls up in the lane beside them and the guagua in the lane behind it. In story 50 the far lane is dug up beside the crossing (no stripes then), the near lane is the one lane left, and the two squares of cement are in the near pavement; the guagua always waits at the right end. Things that drive are a red car and a blue car (small cars, plainly not vans: in Puerto Rico a van or a pickup is a guagua too; dark windows), a guagua (a bus, big, dark windows, a door on its side), a bicycle with a rider.

New person: an obrero (a road worker: yellow helmet, a vest), story 50 only. The other people are the family, the señora and the señor of El banco, and don Pepe at his door.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 48: La calle

- **Goal he can see:** the bebé, on abuela's arm, holds three flowers up high: one for mamá, one for papá, one for Luis, who are all on their way home. The card shows the three flowers, each under a small face.
- **At stake:** the flowers (3). The frog stops the wrong car or guagua: a stranger looks out, the bebé holds a flower out, the stranger takes it and drives off.
- **New words:** calle, carro, guagua, bicicleta.
- **Back again:** abuela, bebé, mamá, papá, flor, tres, grande, rojo, señor, señora, tiene, aquí, bien, sí, gracias, casa, vamos, ¿cómo estás?

**Start:** afternoon. Abuela with the bebé on her arm and the frog stand on the near pavement. The bebé holds three flowers. The street is empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela, el bebé y Coquí están en la calle." The street twinkles. NARR: "Mira la calle. Es larga." **(larga: heard)** Far off, a car turns in.
2. The red car comes along. NARR: "Por la calle viene un carro. El carro es rojo." Ask (signed, the car): "Toca el carro." He touches it: it honks twice and drives on.
3. The guagua comes one way while the blue car goes by the other way. NARR: "Ahora viene una guagua. La guagua es grande." Ask (signed, the guagua; both are in the picture): "¿Dónde está la guagua?" He touches it: its door hisses open and shut; it drives on.
4. The señor comes on a bicycle. NARR: "Y ahora viene una bicicleta. El señor viene en bicicleta." Ask (signed, the bicycle): "Toca la bicicleta." He touches it: the bell rings, the señor stops and puts a foot down. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien)]: "Bien, gracias." The señor rides on.

**The turn**

5. The bebé waves the three flowers; on the card each flower stands under a face. NARR: "El bebé tiene tres flores: una para mamá, una para papá y una para Luis." Abuela looks along the street. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ya vienen mamá y papá." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A car and a guagua come along and pull up at the kerb, one behind the other, windows dark. Which stands in front is drawn. Who is in which is drawn, each round on its own (the car of round B is the blue one).

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the car, the guagua; tests the one named).** While the two are still coming along the street: NARR: "Abuela dice: Mamá viene en la guagua." (Or, drawn: "Abuela dice: Mamá viene en el carro.") They pull up.
   - Right: the frog hops to it and waves both arms. The door opens: mamá gets out. A star. The other one drives on.
   - Wrong: the frog hops to the other one and knocks. A window comes down: the señora looks out. The bebé holds a flower out to her; she takes it. NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Para mí? ¡Gracias!" She drives off with it. A cross: one flower gone. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Mamá viene en la guagua." The sign is on the right one; he touches it: mamá gets out.
   - Then, both ways: NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Hola! ¿Y mi bebé?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Aquí está!" The bebé reaches for her.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; papá).** A car and a guagua come along again. NARR: "Abuela dice: Papá viene en el carro." (Or the guagua.) Right and wrong as in A; the stranger at the window is don Pepe. Papá gets out, takes the bebé from abuela and swings him up high; the bebé laughs.

**The ending**

8. A bell: Luis rides up on his bicycle and stops beside them. NARR: "Y Luis viene en bicicleta." The bebé gives the flowers.
   - Three flowers left: one to mamá, one to papá, one to Luis, who puts it behind his ear. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: one to mamá, one to papá; Luis holds out his hand and gets the bebé's hand in it.
   - One left: to mamá; papá gets the bebé's hand on his nose, and laughs; Luis shrugs and grins.
9. NARR: "Papá dice: ¿Vamos a casa?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, papá!" They walk off along the pavement; Luis pushes his bicycle. NARR: "La familia va a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** carro 3 (step 2, twice; the ask), guagua 3 (step 3). Not deciding: calle 3 before the game (steps 1 and 2); bicicleta 3 (step 4) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the vehicle that was named (carro or guagua).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both stand at the kerb with dark windows; the order at the kerb is drawn.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 4, 6, 9): bien; aquí; sí.

## Story 49: La esquina

- **Goal he can see:** three balloons on strings in the frog's hand from the first moment, for the bebé, who waits with mamá on the far pavement and reaches for them.
- **At stake:** the balloons (3). The frog stops the traffic for somebody who is not crossing: a horn, the frog jumps, a balloon gets away.
- **New words:** esquina, gente, cruza, pasa.
- **Back again:** calle, carro, guagua, bicicleta, colmado, espera, hay, mucho, señora, señor, abuela, bebé, mamá, tres, ¿cuántos?, toma, tiene, aquí, no, ahora, casa.

**Start:** afternoon. The frog stands at the corner beside the traffic light's pole. Don Pepe's door is behind him. Mamá and the bebé are small on the far pavement.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The shop bell: abuela comes out of the colmado with three balloons and puts the strings in the frog's hand. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Los globos son para el bebé." **(globos: heard; the picture carries it)** Far off, the bebé lifts both arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: Espera aquí, en la esquina." She goes back into the colmado; the door shuts behind her. The corner twinkles. NARR: "Coquí está en la esquina." Ask (signed, the corner): "¿Dónde está la esquina?" He touches it: people walk up and stop at the kerb (the señora, the señor, Carmen, Luis).
2. NARR: "Mira. Hay mucha gente en la esquina. La gente espera." Ask (signed, the people): "¿Dónde está la gente?" He touches them: they look round and wave. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Dónde está tu abuela?" COQUÍ [TURN: colmado (colmado)]: "Está en el colmado."
3. The red car goes by, then the guagua. NARR: "Pasa un carro y pasa la guagua." A bicycle comes. NARR: "Ahora pasa una bicicleta." Ask (signed, the bicycle): "Toca la bicicleta." He touches it: its bell rings; it rides on. Luis looks at the balloons. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Globos! ¿Cuántos tienes?" COQUÍ [TURN: tres (tres)]: "¡Tres!"
4. The button on the pole twinkles. Ask (signed, the button): "Toca el botón, Coquí." **(botón: heard; the sign carries it)** He touches it: the frog presses it, the light turns red, the blue car and a guagua stop at the stripes. NARR: "Ahora la gente cruza la calle." The señora and Carmen cross. NARR: "La señora cruza. Carmen cruza." The señor and Luis do not: they walk on round the corner, past the colmado. NARR: "El señor y Luis pasan por la esquina." The light turns green; the traffic drives on.

**The turn**

5. Don Pepe comes to his door with a broom and leans on it, looking at the corner. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: ¡Muy bien, Coquí! Ahora viene más gente." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two people walk up and stop at the corner, side by side, drawn from the señora, the señor, Luis and Carmen; who stands on which side is drawn too. One of them is going to cross the street; the other is only going past, round the corner. Don Pepe, who knows everybody, says so from his door. Who crosses, and who is named first, is drawn, each round on its own. Both stand still, facing the street, until the frog has pressed the button.

6. **Decision A, touch a person (two people; tests cruza).** NARR: "Don Pepe dice: La señora cruza. El señor pasa por la esquina." (Or, drawn: "Don Pepe dice: El señor pasa por la esquina. La señora cruza.")
   - Right: the frog presses the button. The light turns red, the traffic stops. The one he touched crosses and waves from the far side; the other walks on round the corner. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog presses the button; the traffic stops; the one he touched walks on round the corner, and nobody crosses. The guagua honks. The frog jumps: one balloon gets away and floats up out of the picture. A cross. NARR: "Don Pepe dice: No, Coquí. El señor pasa por la esquina. La señora cruza." The sign is on the one who crosses; he touches her: the button again, she crosses.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game; two others).** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela comes out with her bag. The frog presses the button; the traffic stops. NARR: "Ahora abuela y Coquí cruzan la calle." They cross to mamá and the bebé. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Globos! ¿Son para mí?" COQUÍ [TURN: bebé (bebé, bebe)]: "¡No! Son para el bebé."
   - Three balloons left: the bebé takes the strings and is lifted onto his toes; mamá holds him down, laughing. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the bebé holds one in each hand.
   - One left: the bebé hugs it with both arms.
9. NARR: "Abuela, mamá, el bebé y Coquí van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cruza 3 (step 4), pasa 3 (step 3) and pasan once (step 4). Not deciding: esquina 5 before the game; gente 4 (steps 2, 4, 5).
**A miss is filed under:** cruza.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both stand at the kerb facing the street; sides and who is named first are drawn. No question is asked: the button is for the one who crosses, as the opening showed.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 2, 3, 8): colmado; tres; bebé.

## Story 50: Ahora y luego

- **Goal he can see:** two squares in the pavement at the corner. An old dry one with a hand print and a paw print in it; beside it a fresh, smooth, wet one with three cones round it. The frog looks at the old prints, at the wet square and at his own foot.
- **At stake:** the smooth cement (3 parts). The frog sends the wrong one first: it has to back up, knocks a cone over and leaves a wheel track through one part.
- **New words:** viene, va, ahora, luego.
- **Back again:** carro, guagua, bicicleta, calle, esquina, cuidado, listo, ¿qué es esto?, ¿cuál?, me gusta, mira, tú, tengo sed, agua, quiero, toma, gracias, casa.

**Start:** morning. Half the street at the corner is dug up: a hole, a fence of cones, one lane left. The obrero stands at the lane with a paddle (red on one side, green on the other). Beside him, in the pavement, the square of fresh cement. The frog and Luis stand looking.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Coquí y Luis están en la esquina. Un obrero arregla la calle." **(obrero, arregla: heard; the picture carries them)** The frog lifts a foot over the wet square. NARR: "El obrero dice: ¡Cuidado, Coquí! Mira." He points at the wet square with his trowel. COQUÍ [TURN: ¿qué es esto? (¿qué es esto?, que es esto)]: "¿Qué es esto?" NARR: "El obrero dice: Esto es cemento. No está listo." The frog pulls his foot back and looks at the old prints.
2. The red car comes to one end of the lane and the guagua to the other, at the same moment; both stop. NARR: "Viene un carro y viene la guagua." NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora va el carro. Luego va la guagua." Ask (signed, the car): "¿Cuál va ahora?" He touches it: the obrero turns the green side to the car; it drives through the lane and away. NARR: "Y luego va la guagua." The guagua drives through.
3. A bicycle and the blue car come up, one to each end. NARR: "Ahora viene una bicicleta." NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora va la bicicleta. Luego va el carro." Ask (signed, the bicycle): "¿Cuál va ahora?" He touches it: the bicycle rides through, bell ringing. NARR: "Y luego va el carro." The blue car drives through.

**The turn**

4. The obrero wipes his face, sits down on an upturned bucket with his water bottle, and holds two small flags out to the frog, a green one and a red one. NARR: "El obrero dice: Tengo sed. Quiero agua. Toma las banderas, Coquí." **(banderas: heard; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" The frog takes the flags and hops onto the fence. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two things that drive come up at the same moment, one to each end of the lane, drawn from the car, the guagua and the bicycle. The obrero, from his bucket, says which goes now and which goes after. Which is which, and which half of his line comes first, is drawn, each round on its own. No question is asked: his line is the cue.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two that wait; tests ahora).** NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora va la guagua. Luego va el carro." (Or, drawn: "El obrero dice: El carro va luego. Ahora va la guagua.")
   - Right: the frog points the green flag at it and the red one at the other. It drives through; its driver lifts a hand. Then the other. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog points the green flag at the one he touched; it starts into the lane. The obrero whistles. NARR: "El obrero dice: ¡Espera, Coquí!" It backs up, knocks a cone over and rolls a wheel through the wet square: a track across one part. A cross. NARR: "El obrero dice: No. Ahora va la guagua. Luego va el carro." The sign is on the right one; he touches it: it drives through, then the other.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; two others).** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The obrero gets up, takes his paddle back, presses a thumb on the wet square and nods. NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora está listo. Ahora tú, Coquí." The frog hops onto the smooth part and off again.
   - Three parts smooth: two frog prints side by side; then NARR: "El obrero dice: Y luego tú, Luis." Luis presses his hand in beside them, and the obrero draws a heart round all of it with his finger. Stars and a heart.
   - Two parts smooth: two frog prints, and Luis's hand beside them.
   - One part smooth: one small frog print between the wheel tracks.
8. NARR: "El obrero dice: Mira. ¿Te gusta?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" A car comes up to the lane; the obrero lifts his paddle. NARR: "Viene un carro. Coquí y Luis van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ahora 5 (steps 2 and 3), luego 4 (steps 2 and 3). Not deciding: viene 3 before the game (steps 2 and 3) and once at the end; va 8 before the game. Ahora, viene and va were heard in stories 48 and 49 too; luego is the one word nobody has met.
**A miss is filed under:** ahora.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both wait at the lane's ends; which half of the line comes first is drawn.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 4, 8): ¿qué es esto?; gracias; me gusta.

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 48: the third flower had no owner until the ending, so the first miss cost nothing anyone knew about. Now each flower is for a named person from step 5, on the card. The stranger at the window is no longer the señor who has just ridden off on his bicycle. "Es grande" of a street is "Es larga". "Y ahora, una bicicleta." was a label. "Vienen ahora" means "in a while" on the island: "Ya vienen". The decision line is said while the two are still moving. Papá gets his own beat. The last turn no longer says the question's verb back.
- 49: the frog has the balloons from the first moment and sees abuela go into the colmado, so "¿Dónde está tu abuela?" can be answered. The narrator's question after the answer is gone: don Pepe says who crosses and who goes past, from his door, and the frog acts. "La calle" no longer stands in one half only (it gave the crosser away). The three "Pasa..." lines in a row are one line and a touch. The opening shows two who cross and two who do not. "A man" is Luis. Mamá's "¿Para quién son?" is "¿Son para mí?", answered with a real no.
- 50: the first turn had nothing before it: now the obrero stops the frog and points. "Y ahora, ¿quién va?" made "ahora" mean the one that was "luego": gone. No question in the game. "¿Quién va?" of things is "¿Cuál va?". "¿Estás listo?" answered "estoy listo" was an echo with a word nobody has: now he hands over the flags and the frog says thank you. The old square with prints shows what the wet one is for. "The other one starts" said the wrong thing. The two that wait arrive together.

Left, and why:
- "luego": both Spanish readers say Puerto Ricans say "después" in speech. Luego is the word on the plan's list for this scene (page 02) and in "hasta luego"; it stays until Salim says otherwise.
- "pasa por la esquina" is not a clean opposite of "cruza" (one who crosses also passes the corner). Cruza is the tested word; a miss is filed under it.
- Length: the beginner reader guesses 95 to 105 s for each story at real speed. One ask and several lines are cut; the real number comes from machine play.
- The cones are never named ("cono" is one sound from a swear word).

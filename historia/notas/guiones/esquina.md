# La esquina: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `esquina`). Stories 48 to 50. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished La casa, El parque, La cocina and El colmado (stories 1 to 47).

Scene words (page 02): calle, esquina, carro, guagua, bicicleta, gente, cruza, pasa, viene, va, ahora, luego.

The picture: the street corner outside don Pepe's colmado. One view. The street runs across the picture; a second street leaves it at the left, behind the colmado's corner, so what comes along that street is hidden by the building until it turns in. Near side: the pavement where the frog stands, a traffic light on a pole with a button box at frog height, a striped crossing over the street. Far side: a pavement, a low wall, the trees of the park. Things that drive are a red car and a blue car (dark windows), a guagua (a bus, big, dark windows, a door at the front), a bicycle with a rider.

New person: an obrero (a road worker: yellow helmet, a vest), story 50 only. The other people are the family, the señora and the señor of El banco, and don Pepe at his door.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 48: La calle

- **Goal he can see:** the bebé, on abuela's arm, holds three flowers up high. Mamá and papá are coming home; the flowers are for them.
- **At stake:** the flowers (3). The frog stops the wrong car or guagua: a stranger looks out, the bebé holds a flower out, the stranger takes it and drives off.
- **New words:** calle, carro, guagua, bicicleta.
- **Back again:** abuela, bebé, mamá, papá, flor, tres, grande, rojo, señor, señora, tiene, aquí, gracias, casa, vamos.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuela with the bebé on her arm and the frog stand on the near pavement. The bebé holds three flowers. The street is empty.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela, el bebé y Coquí están en la calle." The street twinkles. NARR: "Mira la calle. Es grande." Ask (signed, the street): "¿Dónde está la calle?" He touches it: far off, a car turns in.
2. The red car comes along. NARR: "Por la calle viene un carro. El carro es rojo." Ask (signed, the car): "Toca el carro." He touches it: it honks twice and drives on.
3. The guagua comes. NARR: "Ahora viene una guagua. La guagua es grande." Ask (signed, the guagua): "¿Dónde está la guagua?" He touches it: its door hisses open and shut; it drives on.
4. The señor comes on a bicycle. NARR: "Y ahora, una bicicleta. El señor tiene una bicicleta." Ask (signed, the bicycle): "Toca la bicicleta." He touches it: the bell rings, the señor stops and puts a foot down. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Cómo estás?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien)]: "Bien, gracias." The señor rides on.

**The turn**

5. The bebé waves the three flowers. NARR: "El bebé tiene tres flores. Son para mamá y papá." Abuela looks along the street. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mamá y papá vienen ahora." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A car and a guagua come along and pull up at the kerb, one behind the other, windows dark. Which stands in front is drawn. Who is in which is drawn, each round on its own (the car of round B is the blue one).

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the car, the guagua; tests the one named).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Mamá viene en la guagua." (Or, drawn: "Mamá viene en el carro.")
   - Right: the frog hops to it and waves both arms. The door opens: mamá gets out. A star. The other one drives on.
   - Wrong: the frog hops to the other one and knocks. Its window comes down: the señora looks out. The bebé holds a flower out to her; she takes it. NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Para mí? ¡Gracias!" She drives off with it. A cross: one flower gone. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Mamá viene en la guagua." The sign is on the right one; he touches it: mamá gets out.
   - Then, both ways: NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Hola! ¿Dónde está mi bebé?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui)]: "¡Aquí está!" The bebé reaches for her.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; papá).** A car and a guagua pull up again. NARR: "Abuela dice: Papá viene en el carro." (Or the guagua.) Right and wrong as in A; the stranger is the señor. Papá gets out.

**The ending**

8. A bell: Luis rides up on his bicycle and stops beside them. NARR: "Y Luis viene en bicicleta." The bebé gives the flowers.
   - Three flowers left: one to mamá, one to papá, one to Luis, who puts it behind his ear. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: one to mamá, one to papá.
   - One left: to mamá; papá gets the bebé's hand on his nose, and laughs.
9. NARR: "Papá dice: ¿Vamos a casa?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, vamos!" They walk off along the pavement; Luis pushes his bicycle. NARR: "La familia va a casa por la calle."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** carro 3 (step 2, twice; the ask), guagua 3 (step 3). Not deciding: calle 3 before the game (step 1) and 2 after; bicicleta 3 (step 4) and once in the ending.
**A miss is filed under:** the vehicle that was named (carro or guagua).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both stand at the kerb with dark windows; the order at the kerb is drawn.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 4, 6, 9): bien; aquí; sí.

## Story 49: La esquina

- **Goal he can see:** three balloons on strings in the frog's hand, for the bebé, who waits with mamá on the far pavement.
- **At stake:** the balloons (3). The frog stops the traffic for somebody who is not crossing: a horn, the frog jumps, a balloon gets away.
- **New words:** esquina, gente, cruza, pasa.
- **Back again:** calle, carro, guagua, bicicleta, colmado, espera, hay, mucho, señora, señor, abuela, bebé, tres, ¿cuántos?, toma, aquí, gracias, para.

**Start:** afternoon. The frog stands at the corner beside the traffic light's pole. Don Pepe's door is behind him. Mamá and the bebé are small on the far pavement.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Coquí está en la esquina." The corner twinkles. NARR: "El colmado de don Pepe está en la esquina." Ask (signed, the corner): "¿Dónde está la esquina?" He touches it: people walk up and stop at the kerb (the señora, the señor, Carmen, a man).
2. NARR: "Mira. Hay mucha gente en la esquina. La gente espera." Ask (signed, the people): "¿Dónde está la gente?" He touches them: they look round and wave. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Hola, Coquí! ¿Dónde está tu abuela?" COQUÍ [TURN: colmado (colmado)]: "Está en el colmado."
3. The red car goes by, then the guagua, then a bicycle. NARR: "Pasa un carro. Pasa la guagua. Pasa una bicicleta." NARR: "Los carros pasan y la gente espera."
4. The button on the pole twinkles. Ask (signed, the button): "Toca el botón, Coquí." **(botón: heard; the sign carries it)** He touches it: the frog presses it, the light turns red, the car and the guagua stop at the stripes. NARR: "Ahora la gente cruza la calle." The people cross. NARR: "La señora cruza. El señor cruza." On the far side they walk away. The light turns green; the traffic drives on.

**The turn**

5. The shop bell: abuela comes out of the colmado with three balloons and puts the strings in the frog's hand. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Son para el bebé." The bebé, far off, lifts both arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cuántos globos hay?" **(globos: heard; the picture carries it)** COQUÍ [TURN: tres (tres)]: "¡Tres!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Espera aquí." She goes back in. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two people walk up and stop at the corner, side by side, drawn from the señora, the señor, Luis and Carmen. One of them is going to cross the street; the other is only going past, round the corner. Who, and who is named first, is drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a person (two people; tests cruza).** NARR: "La señora cruza la calle. El señor pasa por la esquina." (Or, drawn: "El señor pasa por la esquina. La señora cruza la calle.") Ask: "¿Quién cruza la calle?"
   - Right: the frog presses the button. The light turns red, the traffic stops. The one he touched crosses and waves from the far side; the other walks on round the corner. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog presses the button; the traffic stops; the one he touched walks on round the corner, and nobody crosses. The guagua honks. The frog jumps: one balloon gets away and floats up out of the picture. A cross. NARR: "No. El señor pasa. La señora cruza la calle." The sign is on the one who crosses; he touches her: the button again, she crosses.
7. **Decision B, touch a person (the same game; two others).** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuela comes out with her bag. The frog presses the button; the traffic stops. NARR: "Ahora abuela y Coquí cruzan la calle." They cross to mamá and the bebé. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Globos! ¿Para quién son?" COQUÍ [TURN: bebé (bebé, bebe)]: "¡Para el bebé!"
   - Three balloons left: the bebé takes the strings and is lifted onto his toes; mamá holds him down, laughing. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the bebé holds one in each hand.
   - One left: the bebé hugs it with both arms.
9. NARR: "La gente pasa por la esquina. Y el bebé está contento."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cruza 3 (step 4), pasa 3 (step 3). Not deciding: esquina 3 (step 1) and then in every round; gente 5 (steps 2 to 4).
**A miss is filed under:** cruza.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both stand at the kerb facing the street; who is named first is drawn.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 2, 5, 8): colmado; tres; bebé.

## Story 50: Ahora y luego

- **Goal he can see:** a square of fresh, smooth cement in the pavement at the corner, with three cones round it. The frog looks at it and at his own foot.
- **At stake:** the smooth cement (3 parts). The frog sends the wrong one first: it has to back up, knocks a cone over and leaves a wheel track through one part.
- **New words:** viene, va, ahora, luego.
- **Back again:** carro, guagua, bicicleta, calle, esquina, cuidado, listo, espera, ¿qué es esto?, me gusta, mira, tú, tengo sed, agua.

**Start:** morning. Half the street at the corner is dug up: a hole, a fence of cones, one lane left. The obrero stands at the lane with a paddle (red on one side, green on the other). Beside him, in the pavement, the square of fresh cement. The frog and Luis stand looking.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Coquí y Luis están en la esquina. Un obrero arregla la calle." **(obrero, arregla: heard; the picture carries them)** The cement twinkles; the frog leans over it. COQUÍ [TURN: ¿qué es esto? (¿qué es esto?, que es esto)]: "¿Qué es esto?" NARR: "El obrero dice: Es cemento. ¡Cuidado! No está listo." The frog pulls his foot back.
2. The red car comes to one end of the lane and stops. NARR: "Viene un carro." The guagua comes to the other end and stops. NARR: "Viene la guagua." A bicycle stops behind the car. NARR: "Y viene una bicicleta."
3. NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora va el carro. Luego va la guagua." Ask (signed, the car): "¿Quién va ahora?" He touches it: the obrero turns the green side to the car; it drives through the lane and away. Ask (signed, the guagua): "Y ahora, ¿quién va?" He touches it: the guagua drives through.
4. The blue car stops at the far end. NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora va la bicicleta. Luego va el carro." Ask (signed, the bicycle): "¿Quién va ahora?" He touches it: the bicycle rides through, bell ringing. NARR: "Y luego va el carro." The blue car drives through.

**The turn**

5. The obrero wipes his face, sits down on an upturned bucket with his water bottle, and holds two small flags out to the frog, a green one and a red one. NARR: "El obrero dice: Tengo sed. ¿Estás listo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo)]: "¡Sí, estoy listo!" The frog takes the flags and hops onto the fence. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two things that drive come up, one to each end of the lane, drawn from the car, the guagua and the bicycle. The obrero, from his bucket, says which goes now and which goes after. Which is which, and which half of his line comes first, is drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two that wait; tests ahora).** NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora va la guagua. Luego va el carro." (Or, drawn: "El carro va luego. Ahora va la guagua.") Ask: "¿Quién va ahora?"
   - Right: the frog points the green flag at it and the red one at the other. It drives through; its driver lifts a hand. Then the other. A star.
   - Wrong: the other one starts into the lane. The obrero whistles. NARR: "El obrero dice: ¡No! ¡Espera!" It backs up, knocks a cone over and rolls a wheel through the cement: a track across one part. A cross. NARR: "El obrero dice: No, Coquí. Ahora va la guagua. Luego va el carro." The sign is on the right one; he touches it: it drives through, then the other.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; two others).** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The obrero gets up, takes his paddle back and looks at the cement. He waves the frog over. NARR: "El obrero dice: Ahora tú, Coquí." The frog hops onto the smooth part and off again.
   - Three parts smooth: two frog prints side by side; then NARR: "El obrero dice: Y luego, Luis." Luis presses his hand in beside them, and the obrero draws a heart round all of it with his finger. Stars and a heart.
   - Two parts smooth: two frog prints, and Luis's hand beside them.
   - One part smooth: one small frog print between the wheel tracks.
9. NARR: "El obrero dice: Mira. ¿Te gusta?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" A car comes up to the lane; the obrero lifts his paddle. NARR: "Viene un carro. Coquí y Luis van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ahora 4 (steps 3 and 4), luego 3 (steps 3 and 4). Not deciding: viene 3 (step 2) and once at the end; va 6 before the game.
**A miss is filed under:** ahora.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both wait at the lane's ends; which half of the line comes first is drawn.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 5, 9): ¿qué es esto?; listo; me gusta.

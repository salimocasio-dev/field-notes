# Escuchar for El parque: first set (final: draft 2 and the last gate, as built)

Place 2, El parque. The listening set that goes with its twelve stories (12 to 23), built like La casa's (historia/notas/guiones/escuchar-casa.md):

- **Otra vez**: each of the twelve map stories told straight through, nothing to touch. Already works; nothing to write.
- **Cuentos con dibujos**: five new picture stories in the four scenes already drawn. He only watches and hears.
- **Haz esto**: four do-this routines, one for each scene. He does what he is told. Nothing is lost.

Nothing in Escuchar is scored. It only adds minutes listened.

Words he has met by now (places 1 and 2): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana, perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, ahora es de noche, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

His rule for listening: "they hear a bunch of words and focus on the words their learning." So a picture story is not held to the two lists. It is built on them, and where it uses another word the picture carries it. Those words are marked **(new)**. The routines stay inside the lists, because there he has to act on what he hears.

A picture story must not retell a map story: each has its own small turn.

Voices as in the stories: the narrator; "X dice:" for what a person says; the frog in his own voice (COQUÍ).

## Cuentos con dibujos

Each runs a little over a minute. Stage directions are in English and are not heard. Every line is said while the thing it tells is in the picture.

### 1. ¡Corre! (El camino): a race

The path. Luis stands far off beside the big tree. Carmen, the frog and Canelo stand at the start, by the gate. A grey cat sits on the bench by the path, washing itself.

1. NARR: "Este es el parque."
2. Luis waves. NARR: "Luis está allá." The big tree twinkles. NARR: "¡Mira! El árbol grande."
3. NARR: "Carmen y Coquí están aquí. Y Canelo también."
4. Luis holds up three fingers, one after the other. NARR: "Luis dice: Uno. Dos. Tres. ¡A correr!"
5. All three run up the path. NARR: "Carmen corre. Coquí corre. Y Canelo corre rápido."
6. The cat twinkles. Canelo stops short. NARR: "¡Mira! Un gato." NARR: "Canelo mira al gato."
7. The cat jumps down and runs back toward the gate; Canelo runs after it, the wrong way. NARR: "El gato corre. ¡Y Canelo corre!"
8. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Canelo! ¡Ven aquí!" Luis shakes his head.
9. The frog has stopped to look back. NARR: "Coquí espera." Carmen runs on. NARR: "Carmen corre y corre."
10. Carmen reaches the big tree and slaps the trunk. NARR: "Carmen toca el árbol grande." Luis laughs. NARR: "¡Carmen gana!" **(new: gana)** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Muy bien, Carmen!"
11. The frog hops on up the path to the big tree. NARR: "Ahora Coquí corre también."
12. The cat shoots up into the first small tree; the tree twinkles; its face looks out of the leaves. Canelo stands under it, barking. NARR: "¿Y el gato? El gato está arriba, en el árbol." NARR: "Canelo está abajo."
13. Canelo lies down. NARR: "Canelo está cansado." NARR: "Y el gato está contento." A heart.

### 2. La bola de Canelo (El campo)

The field. Luis on the left, Carmen on the right, the frog between them. Canelo sits on his patch of earth under the tree. A basket of balls stands in front. Luis holds a ball.

1. NARR: "Luis tiene una bola."
2. NARR: "Luis tira la bola." Luis throws; Carmen catches. NARR: "Carmen coge la bola."
3. NARR: "Carmen tira la bola para arriba." She throws high; the frog jumps and catches it. NARR: "Coquí salta. ¡Coquí coge la bola!"
4. The frog throws to Luis. Canelo runs in, jumps, takes the ball from him and carries it to his patch. NARR: "¡Ay! Canelo tiene la bola de Luis."
5. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dame la bola, Canelo!" Canelo shakes his head. NARR: "Canelo no quiere."
6. The basket twinkles. NARR: "Hay otra bola en la canasta." **(new: otra, canasta)** A ball flies from the basket to Carmen. NARR: "Carmen tiene la otra bola."
7. She throws it to the frog. Canelo leaves the first ball on his patch and runs to the frog. NARR: "Ahora Canelo quiere la otra bola." He jumps at it, twice. NARR: "Canelo salta y salta."
8. Luis walks to the patch and takes his ball back. NARR: "Luis tiene su bola. La bola de Luis." **(new: su)**
9. NARR: "Coquí tira la bola." The frog throws to Carmen; Canelo runs after it; the children laugh. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Corre, Canelo!" A heart.

### 3. Pan para el pato pequeño (El lago)

The pond. Three ducks near the dock: a white one, a black one and a small yellow one. Carmen stands on the dock with her bag of bread; the frog beside her. Luis sits on the far bank.

1. NARR: "Este es el lago." NARR: "Hay tres patos."
2. Each duck twinkles as it is named. NARR: "Un pato blanco." NARR: "Un pato negro." NARR: "Y un pato pequeño." NARR: "¿De qué color es el pato pequeño? Es amarillo."
3. Carmen holds up a piece of bread. NARR: "Carmen tiene pan."
4. NARR: "Carmen dice: Pan para el pato pequeño." She throws it onto the water. The small duck paddles toward it; the white duck gets there first and eats it. NARR: "¡Ay! El pato blanco es rápido. El pato blanco come el pan."
5. NARR: "Carmen dice: Otra vez. Pan para el pato pequeño." She throws again. The black duck gets there first. NARR: "¡Ay, no! Ahora el pato negro come el pan."
6. The small duck bobs, alone. NARR: "El pato pequeño no tiene pan. Está triste."
7. NARR: "Carmen dice: Toma, Coquí." The frog has a piece of bread.
8. Carmen holds another piece high. The white duck and the black duck paddle over to her side of the dock. NARR: "Carmen tiene más pan. El pato blanco y el pato negro miran a Carmen."
9. The frog hops along the dock to its far end. NARR: "Coquí va despacio." The small duck paddles up to him.
10. COQUÍ: "Pan para el pato pequeño." He drops it; the small duck eats. NARR: "¡El pato pequeño come el pan!"
11. Luis waves from the bank. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Muy bien, Coquí!" The small duck bobs. NARR: "Ahora el pato pequeño está contento." A heart.

### 4. La piragua de Luis (El banco)

The benches. The piragua man at his cart. Luis stands at the cart with a red piragua in his hand; Carmen beside him with one too. A pigeon on the wire. The frog in front.

1. The piragua man twinkles. NARR: "Este es el piragüero." **(new: piragüero, heard once as a name)** NARR: "Luis tiene una piragua. Carmen también tiene una piragua."
2. Luis runs toward the benches. NARR: "Luis corre."
3. The ice slides out of its paper onto the ground. Luis stops and looks down. NARR: "¡Ay, no! La piragua se cae." **(new: se cae)**
4. His head goes down; tears. NARR: "Luis está triste."
5. Carmen walks over and holds hers out. NARR: "Carmen dice: Toma, Luis. Toma mi piragua."
6. Luis takes it and eats. NARR: "Ahora Luis está contento."
7. The frog hops to the cart. COQUÍ: "Dos piraguas, por favor." The piragua man hands him two; the frog carries one to Carmen. NARR: "Una piragua para Carmen."
8. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" She eats; the frog eats the other. NARR: "Y una piragua para Coquí. Carmen está contenta."
9. The pigeon flies down and pecks at the ice on the ground. NARR: "¿Y el pájaro? El pájaro come." NARR: "El pájaro también está contento." A heart.

### 5. Vamos a casa (El banco)

The benches in evening light. The old gentleman on the first bench; the woman on the second. Abuela, Luis and Carmen stand in front with the frog. Canelo lies flat at abuela's feet, tongue out. One pigeon on the wire. The gate stands open at the right.

1. The light goes down; the lamp comes on; a moon is in the sky. NARR: "Ahora es de noche." NARR: "¡Mira! La luna." **(new: luna)**
2. The old gentleman gets up and walks slowly to the gate. NARR: "El señor camina despacio." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señor!" He waves and goes.
3. The woman gets up and goes. COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señora!" She waves.
4. The pigeon on the wire tucks its head in. NARR: "El pájaro duerme."
5. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a casa." Luis and Carmen walk to the gate and turn round. Canelo does not move.
6. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ven, Canelo!" Canelo lifts his head and lets it fall. NARR: "Canelo no quiere. Canelo está cansado."
7. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos, Canelo!" Canelo shuts his eyes; two Z's. NARR: "Canelo duerme."
8. Abuela takes a ball out of her bag. NARR: "Abuela tiene una bola."
9. She bounces it on the paving by his nose, three times. The Z's pop; he lifts his head, jumps up, wags. NARR: "¡Mira! Ahora Canelo no está cansado."
10. The ball rolls out through the gate; Canelo runs after it, out of the picture. NARR: "Canelo corre rápido." The children run after him.
11. Abuela walks to the gate, slowly, and goes out. NARR: "Y abuela camina despacio."
12. The frog looks at the empty benches. COQUÍ: "Hasta mañana, parque." He hops out through the gate. The lamp glows. A heart.

## Haz esto

How a routine works (as in La casa): a line is said; he touches or does it; the thing happens. No praise after each line; one "¡Muy bien!" at the end. Nothing is lost on a wrong touch: "No, ese es el gato." (what he touched), then the big sign shows the right one. After a wrong doing bubble the line is just "No.", then the sign. If he waits, the line is said again. Where two touches are fair, both count. Each routine ends with a line in two parts, which can be done in either order. A touch on something that is not one of the answers is only a poke: it is named, and nothing else happens.

In each group of lines, not every answer is asked for, and one may be asked for twice: so the last line of a group is never the only one left.

### A. En el camino

The path: Luis beside the big tree, Carmen, Canelo and the frog at the start, the cat on the bench, the three small trees with two birds in the first, three in the second and one in the third.

1. "Toca al perro." (Canelo barks once.) The cat and Carmen are the other answers.
2. "¿Dónde está el gato?" (The cat purrs.) Canelo and Carmen are the other answers.
3. "Toca el árbol grande." (It twinkles.) The three small trees are the other answers.
4. "Toca el árbol con tres pájaros." (It twinkles.) The other two small trees are the other answers. A touch on one of the three birds counts too.
5. "Toca el árbol con un pájaro." (It twinkles.) The same. The tree with two birds is never the answer.
6. Doing bubbles (walk, run, wait, as in story 12): "Luis dice: ¡Corre, Coquí!" (The frog and Canelo run to the next stone.)
7. The same three bubbles: "Luis dice: ¡Espera!" (They stand still; Luis laughs.)
8. The same three bubbles: "Luis dice: ¡Corre!" (They run to the next stone.) Walking is never the answer.
9. Two parts: "Toca al niño y a la niña." (Each waves.) The cat is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

### B. En el campo

The field: Luis with his ball in his hands, Carmen with hers, the frog's ball at his feet, Canelo on his patch, the basket, the tree. A touch on a child counts as a touch on the ball in that child's hands, and the other way round, whichever the line is about.

1. "Luis dice: Toca mi bola." (Luis holds his ball up.) Carmen's ball and the frog's are the other answers.
2. "¿Dónde está Canelo?" (He barks.) Luis and Carmen are the other answers.
3. "Carmen dice: Toca tu bola." (The frog hops.) Luis's ball and Carmen's are the other answers. Carmen's ball is never the answer.
4. Doing bubbles (catch, jump, run): "Luis dice: ¡Coge la bola!" (Luis throws his ball; the frog catches it.)
5. Doing bubbles (throw, jump, run; the frog holds Luis's ball): "Carmen dice: ¡Salta!" (The frog jumps; Canelo jumps too.)
6. The same three bubbles: "Luis dice: ¡Tira la bola!" (The frog throws it back to Luis.) Running is never the answer.
7. Two parts: "Toca tu bola y toca a Carmen." (The frog hops; Carmen waves.) Canelo is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

### C. En el lago

The pond: the three ducks (white, black, yellow) near the dock; a green, a blue and a red boat in a row on the dock; Carmen on the dock with her bag; the frog beside her; Luis on the far bank.

1. "Toca el pato blanco." (It quacks.) The other two ducks are the other answers.
2. "¿Cuál es el pato amarillo?" (It quacks.) The same.
3. The frog has a piece of bread. "Carmen dice: Pan para el pato blanco." (He throws it; the white duck eats.) The same.
4. "¿Dónde está el bote rojo?" (It rocks.) The other two boats are the other answers.
5. "Toca el bote verde." (It rocks.) The same.
6. A bird flies round over the pond. A fish swims round under the water. "¿Quién vuela?" (The bird dips and flies on.) The fish and the three ducks are the other answers.
7. "¿Dónde está el pez?" (The fish jumps.) The bird and the three ducks are the other answers.
8. Two parts: "Toca el bote azul y el pato negro." (The boat rocks; the duck quacks.) The red boat is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

### D. En el banco

The benches: the woman on the first bench, eating a piragua; the young man on the second, slumped, worn out, his rucksack beside him; the old gentleman on the third, drinking water. The piragua man at his cart. The old dog asleep by the cart, two Z's over him; the young dog sitting beside him.

1. "Toca al señor." (The old gentleman waves.) The woman and the young man are the other answers. The piragua man is a señor too: a touch on him counts.
2. "¿Dónde está el joven?" (He waves.) The woman and the old gentleman are the other answers.
3. "¿Quién bebe agua?" (The old gentleman holds his bottle up.) The woman and the young man are the other answers.
4. "¿Quién está cansado?" (The young man droops.) The same. Eating is never asked.
5. "¿Quién duerme?" (The old dog lifts his head and lies down again.) The young dog and the young man are the other answers.
6. "¿Dónde está la señora?" (She waves.) The old gentleman and the young man are the other answers.
7. Two parts: "¿Dónde están el perro viejo y la señora?" (The dog lifts his head; she waves.) The young dog is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

## For the owner, one line each

- The picture stories use a few words that are not on your lists, each carried by its picture: "gana" (wins), "otra" (another), "canasta" (basket), "su" (his), "se cae" (falls), "piragüero", "luna" (moon).
- "Canelo mira al gato" has "al" before the animal, as people say it.
- In Puerto Rico any grown man is "un señor"; in the bench routine a touch on the piragua man counts for "Toca al señor." too.
- "Salta" is your word; people there say "brinca". "Joven" for a young man is a little formal; everyday speech says "el muchacho".

## What the first round of reviews changed (draft 1 to draft 2)

- Three of the five picture stories retold map stories (13, 18, and the endings of 22 and 23). They have new turns: a race that Canelo loses to a cat; a bird that takes the bread and drops it in front of the smallest duck; Canelo too tired to go home until he sees a ball.
- Draft 1 held the picture stories to the word lists; his rule for listening is the opposite. Each story is longer and uses a few new words that its picture carries.
- "El gato es pequeño. Canelo es grande." was false to the eye (they are the same size): cut. "Mira el sol." had no sun in the picture: cut.
- "El pájaro duerme." was said of five pigeons: there is one.
- The frog's lines are in his own voice ("Coquí dice:" is gone). "¡Dame la bola, Canelo!" has the name after, like every other command.
- Routine A: "¡Camina despacio!" had no bubble to match (now "¡Camina!"). "¿Dónde hay dos pájaros?" was a sentence he had never heard, and the counts ran one, two, three from left to right (now "Toca el árbol con tres pájaros", counts shuffled, and the birds count as a touch).
- Routine B: "¡Dame la bola!" could be done by reading the speech bubble at Luis; throw and catch were told apart by who held the ball. Now both hold a ball. "Tu bola" was the only ball left; it is asked second.
- Routine C: "¿Quién vuela?" with nothing flying, and ducks accepted both times, needed no Spanish. Now the bird flies, the fish swims, and the duck stands on the bank.
- Routine D: two men fitted both "señor" and "hombre". There is one grown man when it matters. The steps no longer run from left to right.

## What the last gate changed (draft 2 to what is built)

- Story 3 was still story 20 (a black bird takes Carmen's bread and it falls in the water). Its turn is now its own: the two big ducks beat the small one to the bread twice; Carmen keeps them busy; the frog goes to the end of the dock and feeds the small duck.
- Story 1: the frog reaches the big tree too. "Luis está allá, en el árbol grande" put Luis in the tree; it is now two lines. Story 2: its ending shows who has which ball. Story 4: the frog gets a piragua too. Story 5: Canelo's eyes were shut when abuela held the ball up; she now bounces it by his nose.
- The stories had only three small new words; his rule for listening is "a bunch of words". They now carry seven, each with its picture.
- Routines: in each group every answer was asked for exactly once, so the last line needed no Spanish. Now one answer in each group is never asked for, or one is asked for twice.
- Routine B: a child holding a ball asked where it was (the speech bubble gave the answer); now "Toca mi bola." / "Toca tu bola.". After the catch the frog held the only ball, so "throw" was the only thing that could happen; the bubbles are now catch, jump, run and then throw, jump, run. The last line no longer names the only two children.
- Routine C: by the last line only one duck and one boat were left. The boats now stay on the dock and the ducks stay near it. "¿Quién nada?" is gone: with ducks on the water four of five animals were right.
- Routine D: the piragua man is a señor too (a touch on him counts). The last line is about a dog and the woman, not the only two grown-ups left.
- Left as it is: the stories still lean on the dog taking a ball and a dropped piragua, which he has seen; the turns are new.

## What the second Spanish review and the wordless readers changed

- Spanish: "¡Corre!" said to three runners was a grammar error: now "¡A correr!" (the pattern of "a jugar"). "Mira el árbol grande" after "Luis está allá" was heard as "he looks at the big tree": now "¡Mira! El árbol grande." "Su bola" beside "tu bola": now "Luis tiene su bola. La bola de Luis." A dog is called with "ven": "¡Ven aquí!" Lines that are somebody's words have their speaker. The last line of the bench routine is "¿Dónde están el perro viejo y la señora?": "toca a la mujer" has a faint improper ring to an adult ear.
- Frames with no sound and no words: Luis's three counting fingers stayed up for the whole story and read as a box. The frog was hidden behind the children at the big tree. Luis sat on Carmen's head at the pond. In the night story a sun and a moon were both in the sky, and dusk was too faint. In the routines the answer bubbles hid Carmen and the cat (they now stand in the sky), the sleeping dog's sign was cut by the edge, and the fish swam under a duck.
- Left as it is: in a still frame a cat or a ball seems to jump from place to place; in the game they are seen moving. The "walk" and "run" bubbles of the path both read as "go" to a fresh eye; they are story 12's.

# Escuchar for El parque: first set (draft 2, after the first two reviews)

Place 2, El parque. The listening set that goes with its twelve stories (12 to 23), built like La casa's (historia/notas/guiones/escuchar-casa.md):

- **Otra vez**: each of the twelve map stories told straight through, nothing to touch. Already works; nothing to write.
- **Cuentos con dibujos**: five new picture stories in the four scenes already drawn. He only watches and hears.
- **Haz esto**: four do-this routines, one for each scene. He does what he is told. Nothing is lost.

Nothing in Escuchar is scored. It only adds minutes listened.

Words he has met by now (places 1 and 2): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana, perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres, bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene, pájaro, pato, pez, vuela, nada, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, negro, ¿de qué color?, ¿cuál?, hombre, mujer, señor, señora, joven, viejo, cansado, contento, triste, come, bebe, duerme.
Heard along the way so far: bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, quieres, entra, está, están, dónde, quién, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, ese, esa, a jugar, muy, va, van, por arriba, por abajo, no más, pan, lago, bote, pinta, piragua, banco, del, de la, dice, ay, pequeño, contenta, amigos, ahora es de noche, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

His rule for listening: "they hear a bunch of words and focus on the words their learning." So a picture story is not held to the two lists. It is built on them, and where it uses another word the picture carries it. Those words are marked **(new)**. The routines stay inside the lists, because there he has to act on what he hears.

A picture story must not retell a map story: each has its own small turn.

Voices as in the stories: the narrator; "X dice:" for what a person says; the frog in his own voice (COQUÍ).

## Cuentos con dibujos

Each runs a little over a minute. Stage directions are in English and are not heard. Every line is said while the thing it tells is in the picture.

### 1. ¡Corre! (El camino): a race

The path. Luis stands far off at the big tree. Carmen, the frog and Canelo stand at the start, by the gate.

1. NARR: "Este es el parque."
2. Luis waves. NARR: "Luis está allá, en el árbol grande."
3. NARR: "Carmen y Coquí están aquí. Y Canelo también."
4. Luis counts on his fingers. NARR: "Luis dice: Uno. Dos. Tres. ¡Corre!"
5. All three run up the path. NARR: "Carmen corre. Coquí corre. Y Canelo corre rápido."
6. A grey cat sits on the bench by the path, washing itself. Canelo stops short. NARR: "¡Mira! Un gato." NARR: "Canelo mira al gato."
7. The cat jumps down and runs back toward the gate; Canelo runs after it, the wrong way. NARR: "El gato corre. ¡Y Canelo corre!"
8. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Canelo! ¡Aquí!"
9. The frog has stopped to look back. NARR: "Coquí espera." Carmen runs on. NARR: "Carmen corre y corre."
10. Carmen reaches the big tree and slaps the trunk. NARR: "Carmen toca el árbol grande." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Muy bien, Carmen!"
11. The frog hops on up the path. NARR: "Coquí camina despacio."
12. The cat shoots up into a small tree; its face looks out of the leaves. Canelo sits under it, tongue out. NARR: "¿Y el gato? El gato está arriba, en el árbol." NARR: "Canelo está abajo."
13. NARR: "Canelo está cansado." NARR: "Y el gato está contento." A heart.

### 2. La bola de Canelo (El campo)

The field. Luis on the left, Carmen on the right, the frog between them. Canelo sits on his patch of earth under the tree. Luis holds a ball.

1. NARR: "Luis tiene una bola."
2. Luis throws; Carmen catches. NARR: "Luis tira la bola. Carmen coge la bola."
3. Carmen throws high. NARR: "Carmen tira la bola para arriba." The frog jumps and catches it. NARR: "Coquí salta. ¡Coquí coge la bola!"
4. The frog throws to Luis. Canelo runs in, jumps and takes the ball out of the air. NARR: "¡Ay! Canelo tiene la bola."
5. Canelo runs off with it, round the field. NARR: "Canelo corre rápido."
6. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dame la bola, Canelo!" Canelo lies down on his patch with the ball between his paws.
7. NARR: "Canelo no quiere."
8. Carmen takes another ball out of the basket. NARR: "Carmen tiene otra bola." **(new: otra, heard in "otra vez")**
9. She throws it to the frog; Canelo drops the first ball and runs for the new one. NARR: "Ahora Canelo quiere la otra bola."
10. Luis picks up the ball Canelo dropped. NARR: "Luis tiene su bola." **(new: su)** The children laugh. NARR: "Canelo quiere más. ¡Otra vez!" A heart.

### 3. Pan para el pato pequeño (El lago)

The pond. Three ducks near the dock: a white one, a black one and a small yellow one. Carmen stands on the dock with her bag of bread; the frog beside her. Luis sits on the far bank. A black bird sits on a post of the dock.

1. NARR: "Este es el lago."
2. Each duck twinkles as it is named. NARR: "Hay tres patos. Un pato blanco. Un pato negro. Y un pato amarillo."
3. NARR: "El pato amarillo es pequeño."
4. Carmen holds up a piece of bread. NARR: "Carmen tiene pan." She throws it to the white duck. NARR: "Pan para el pato blanco."
5. She throws one to the black duck. NARR: "Pan para el pato negro." Both eat. NARR: "El pato blanco come. El pato negro come."
6. The yellow duck paddles up and looks at her. NARR: "¿Y el pato amarillo? El pato amarillo también quiere pan."
7. Carmen takes out a piece. The black bird swoops down and takes it from her hand. NARR: "¡Ay, no! El pájaro negro tiene el pan."
8. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mi pan!" The bird flies out over the water with it.
9. The white duck quacks up at it. The bird drops the bread. It falls into the water in front of the yellow duck. NARR: "¡Mira! El pan está en el agua."
10. The yellow duck eats it. NARR: "¡El pato amarillo come el pan!"
11. The bird sits on its post again, empty-beaked. NARR: "El pájaro negro no tiene pan."
12. Carmen laughs and throws the bird a piece of its own. NARR: "Carmen dice: Toma. Pan para ti también." **(new: ti)**
13. NARR: "Ahora el pato pequeño está contento." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, patos!" **(new form: patos, heard in "tres patos")** A heart.

### 4. La piragua de Luis (El banco)

The benches. The piragua man at his cart. Luis stands at the cart with a red piragua in his hand; Carmen beside him with one too. A pigeon on the wire. The frog in front.

1. NARR: "Luis tiene una piragua. Carmen también tiene una piragua."
2. Luis runs to the benches. NARR: "Luis corre."
3. The ice slides out of its paper onto the ground. Luis stops and looks down. NARR: "¡Ay, no! La piragua de Luis."
4. His head goes down; tears. NARR: "Luis está triste."
5. Carmen walks over and holds hers out. NARR: "Carmen dice: Toma, Luis. Toma mi piragua."
6. Luis takes it and eats. NARR: "Ahora Luis está contento."
7. The frog hops to the cart. COQUÍ: "Una piragua para Carmen, por favor." The piragua man hands one over; the frog carries it to Carmen.
8. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí!" She eats. NARR: "Carmen está contenta."
9. The pigeon flies down and pecks at the ice on the ground. NARR: "¿Y el pájaro? El pájaro come." NARR: "El pájaro también está contento." A heart.

### 5. Vamos a casa (El banco)

The benches in evening light. The old gentleman on the first bench; the woman on the second. Abuela, Luis and Carmen stand in front with the frog. Canelo lies flat at abuela's feet, tongue out. One pigeon on the wire.

1. The light goes down; the lamp comes on. NARR: "Ahora es de noche."
2. The old gentleman gets up and walks slowly to the gate. NARR: "El señor camina despacio." COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señor!" He lifts his hat and goes.
3. The woman gets up and goes. COQUÍ: "¡Adiós, señora!" She waves.
4. The pigeon on the wire tucks its head in. NARR: "El pájaro duerme."
5. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a casa." Luis and Carmen walk to the gate. Canelo does not move.
6. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ven, Canelo!" Canelo lifts one ear and lets it fall. NARR: "Canelo no quiere. Canelo está cansado."
7. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos, Canelo!" Canelo shuts his eyes. NARR: "Canelo duerme."
8. Abuela takes a ball out of her bag and holds it up. NARR: "Abuela tiene una bola."
9. Canelo's eyes open. He jumps up, wagging. NARR: "¡Mira! Ahora Canelo no está cansado."
10. Abuela rolls the ball out through the gate; Canelo runs after it, out of the picture. NARR: "Canelo corre rápido." The children laugh and run after him.
11. Abuela walks to the gate, slowly. NARR: "Y abuela camina despacio."
12. The frog looks at the empty benches. COQUÍ: "Hasta mañana, parque." He hops out through the gate. The lamp glows. A heart.

## Haz esto

How a routine works (as in La casa): a line is said; he touches or does it; the thing happens. No praise after each line; one "¡Muy bien!" at the end. Nothing is lost on a wrong touch: "No, ese es el gato." (what he touched), then the big sign shows the right one. After a wrong doing bubble the line is just "No.", then the sign. If he waits, the line is said again. Where two touches are fair, both count. Each routine ends with a line in two parts, which can be done in either order. A touch on something that is not one of the answers is only a poke: it is named, and nothing else happens.

### A. En el camino

The path: Luis at the big tree, Carmen, Canelo and the frog at the start, the cat on the bench, the three small trees with two birds in the first, three in the second and one in the third.

1. "Toca al perro." (Canelo wags and barks once.) The cat and Carmen are the other answers.
2. "¿Dónde está el gato?" (The cat arches its back and purrs.) Canelo and Carmen are the other answers.
3. "Toca el árbol grande." (It shakes; a leaf falls.) The three small trees are the other answers.
4. "Toca el árbol con tres pájaros." (The three sing.) The other two small trees are the other answers. A touch on one of the three birds counts too.
5. "Toca el árbol con un pájaro." (It sings.) The same.
6. Doing bubbles (walk, run, wait, as in story 12): "Luis dice: ¡Corre, Coquí!" (The frog and Canelo run to the next stone.)
7. The same three bubbles: "Luis dice: ¡Espera!" (They stand still; Luis laughs.)
8. The same three bubbles: "Luis dice: ¡Camina!" (They walk on to the next stone.)
9. Two parts: "Toca al niño y a la niña." (Each waves.) The cat is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

### B. En el campo

The field: Luis with his ball, Carmen with hers, the frog with his, Canelo on his patch, the basket, the tree.

1. "¿Dónde está Canelo?" (He barks.) Luis and Carmen are the other answers.
2. "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está mi bola?" (Luis holds his ball up.) Carmen's ball and the frog's are the other answers.
3. "Luis dice: Toca tu bola." (The frog bounces his ball.) Luis's ball and Carmen's are the other answers.
4. "Carmen dice: ¿Dónde está mi bola?" (Carmen holds hers up.) The other two balls are the other answers.
5. Doing bubbles (throw, catch, as in story 15; Luis holds his ball and the frog holds his own, so either can happen): "Luis dice: ¡Coge la bola!" (Luis throws his; the frog catches it.)
6. The same two bubbles: "Luis dice: ¡Tira la bola!" (The frog throws it back to Luis.)
7. Doing bubbles (jump, run, as in story 16): "Carmen dice: ¡Salta!" (The frog jumps; Canelo jumps too.)
8. The same two bubbles: "Carmen dice: ¡Corre!" (The frog runs to her and back; Canelo runs with him.)
9. Two parts: "Toca a Luis y a Carmen." (Each waves.) Canelo is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

### C. En el lago

The pond: the three ducks (white, black, yellow) near the dock; a red, a blue and a green boat in a row on the dock; Carmen on the dock with her bag; the frog beside her.

1. "Toca el pato blanco." (It quacks.) The other two ducks are the other answers.
2. "¿Cuál es el pato negro?" (It quacks.) The same.
3. Carmen gives the frog a piece of bread. "Carmen dice: Pan para el pato amarillo." (He throws it; the yellow duck eats.) The other ducks are the other answers.
4. "¿Dónde está el bote rojo?" (The frog pushes it into the water; it sails out.) The other two boats are the other answers.
5. "Toca el bote verde." (It sails out too.) The blue boat and the red boat are the other answers.
6. The black and the yellow duck paddle away. The white duck climbs out onto the far bank and stands there. A bird flies round over the pond. A fish swims round under the water. "¿Quién vuela?" (The bird dips and flies on.) The fish is the other answer. A touch on the duck is only a poke.
7. "¿Quién nada en el agua?" (The fish jumps.) The bird is the other answer. A touch on the duck is only a poke.
8. Two parts: "Toca el bote azul y el pato blanco." (The boat rocks; the duck quacks.) The red boat is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

### D. En el banco

The benches: the woman on the first bench, eating a piragua; the young man on the second, slumped, worn out, his rucksack beside him; the old gentleman on the third, drinking water. The old dog asleep by the cart; the young dog sitting beside him.

1. "¿Dónde está la señora?" (She waves.) The old gentleman and the young man are the other answers.
2. "Toca al señor." (He lifts his hat.) The woman and the young man are the other answers.
3. "¿Dónde está el joven?" (He lifts a hand.) The woman and the old gentleman are the other answers. A touch on the young dog is only a poke.
4. "¿Quién come?" (The woman holds her piragua up.) The old gentleman and the young man are the other answers.
5. "¿Quién bebe agua?" (The old gentleman holds his bottle up.) The same.
6. "¿Quién está cansado?" (The young man wipes his brow.) The same.
7. "¿Quién duerme?" (The old dog opens one eye and shuts it again.) The young dog is the other answer.
8. "¿Dónde está el perro joven?" (He runs once round the cart.) The old dog is the other answer.
9. The young man gets up, waves and goes home through the gate. Two parts: "Toca al hombre y a la mujer." (The old gentleman lifts his hat; the woman waves.) The old dog is the other answer. "¡Muy bien!" A heart.

## For the owner, one line each

- The picture stories use a few words that are not on your lists, each carried by its picture: "otra" (another), "su" (his), "para ti" (for you).
- "Canelo mira al gato" has "al" before the animal, as people say it.
- In Puerto Rico any grown man is "un señor" and the señor is "un hombre"; the bench routine has only one grown man in it when it asks for either.
- "Salta" is your word; people there say "brinca". "Joven" for a young man is a little formal; everyday speech says "el muchacho".

## What the first round of reviews changed (draft 1 to draft 2)

- Three of the five picture stories retold map stories (13, 18, and the endings of 22 and 23). They have new turns: a race that Canelo loses to a cat; a bird that takes the bread and drops it in front of the smallest duck; Canelo too tired to go home until he sees a ball.
- Draft 1 held the picture stories to the word lists; his rule for listening is the opposite. Each story is longer and uses a few new words that its picture carries.
- "El gato es pequeño. Canelo es grande." was false to the eye (they are the same size): cut. "Mira el sol." had no sun in the picture: cut.
- "El pájaro duerme." was said of five pigeons: there is one.
- The frog's lines are in his own voice ("Coquí dice:" is gone). "¡Dame la bola, Canelo!" has the name after, like every other command.
- Routine A: "¡Camina despacio!" had no bubble to match (now "¡Camina!"). "¿Dónde hay dos pájaros?" was a sentence he had never heard, and the counts ran one, two, three from left to right (now "Toca el árbol con tres pájaros", counts shuffled, and the birds count as a touch).
- Routine B: "¡Dame la bola!" could be done by reading the speech bubble at Luis; throw and catch were told apart by who held the ball. Now both hold a ball. "Tu bola" was the only ball left; it is asked second.
- Routine C: "¿Quién vuela?" with nothing flying, and ducks accepted both times, needed no Spanish. Now the bird flies, the fish swims, and the duck stands on the bank.
- Routine D: two men fitted both "señor" and "hombre". There is one grown man when it matters. The steps no longer run from left to right.

# El banco de la plaza: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `plaza`; "banco" is taken by El parque's bench). Stories 60 to 62. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 59.

Scene words (page 02): ¿cómo te llamas?, nombre, mucho gusto, ¿de dónde eres?, soy de, vivo en, hablo, español, inglés, entiendo, no sé.

The picture: a bench in the town square. One view. A long seat with a back, facing us, under a big tree; paving all round; pigeons; behind, a low wall and flower beds; far off, the square's clock tower. At the left a notice board on two posts (story 61). At the right the piragüero's cart (story 62). Everybody stands or sits on the paving in front of the seat or on it.

New people: Ana, a girl of Carmen's age who has just come to live here (two plaits, a yellow dress); her mamá; her small brother Beto (a red cap); her abuela, who is on a visit (a sun hat). All four speak Spanish. In story 62, visitors who speak only English: a woman with a map and a camera (la turista), and three more in a line. The others: Luis, Carmen, Canelo, the piragüero, and the people of El parque (the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer, the joven).

Answer bubbles (story 60): two round bubbles over the frog, each with a small picture; touching one makes the frog say its line in his own voice. What a visitor says in English is never heard: a bubble with a small flag (the flag of the United States) and a small picture shows what she is asking for.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 60: Mucho gusto

- **Goal he can see:** three pigeons walk along the seat's back towards the frog, who holds a little bag of bread. He wants them to stay.
- **At stake:** the pigeons (3). An answer that does not fit makes everybody burst out laughing: a pigeon flies off.
- **New words:** ¿cómo te llamas?, nombre, mucho gusto.
- **Back again:** me llamo, me gusta, hola, amigo, mamá, abuela, niña, perro, tiene, pan, sí, toma, mira, ven, voy, hay, nuevo.

**Start:** morning. Luis and Carmen in front of the seat; Canelo under it. The frog on the seat with his bag of bread; three pigeons on the seat's back. Ana comes across the square with her mamá, her abuela and Beto, and stops, shy.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Mira. Hay una niña nueva en la plaza." Luis waves the frog over. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ven." COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops to the seat's end. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?" NARR: "Ana dice: Me llamo Ana." NARR: "Luis dice: Mucho gusto, Ana. Yo me llamo Luis." They shake hands. NARR: "Ana dice: Mucho gusto."
2. Carmen comes up to her. NARR: "Carmen dice: Hola, Ana. Mi nombre es Carmen. Mucho gusto." Ana kneels down to Canelo. NARR: "Ana dice: ¡Hola, perro! ¿Cómo te llamas?" NARR: "Carmen dice: Su nombre es Canelo." Ask (signed, Canelo): "¿Dónde está Canelo?" He touches him: Canelo wags and licks Ana's hand.
3. Ana looks up at the frog and the pigeons. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Y tú? ¿Cómo te llamas?" Two bubbles appear over the frog: one with a name badge, one with two hands shaking. Ask (signed, the badge bubble): "¿Qué dice Coquí?" He touches it. COQUÍ: "Me llamo Coquí." NARR: "Ana dice: ¡Mucho gusto, Coquí! Me gusta el nombre." She holds out her hand. Ask (signed, the bubble with the hands): "¿Y ahora?" He touches it. COQUÍ: "¡Mucho gusto, Ana!" They shake hands; the frog's whole arm goes up and down.

**The turn**

4. The frog holds out a crumb; the three pigeons come close. Ana's mamá and her abuela walk up. The two bubbles stay over the frog. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A grown-up meets the frog in one of two ways, drawn, each round on its own: she asks his name, or Ana tells her his name and she greets him. He answers with a bubble. The two bubbles change sides, drawn, each round.

5. **Decision A, touch a bubble (two bubbles; tests which of the two new lines was said).** NARR: "La mamá de Ana dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?" (Or, drawn: NARR: "Ana dice: Mamá, es Coquí." NARR: "La mamá de Ana dice: ¡Mucho gusto, Coquí!")
   - Right: the frog says the bubble's line. To her question, "Me llamo Coquí.", and she says: NARR: "La mamá de Ana dice: ¡Hola, Coquí!" To her greeting, "¡Mucho gusto!", and they shake hands. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other line. Luis, Carmen and Ana burst out laughing; one pigeon flies off. A cross. She says it again, smiling: NARR: "La mamá de Ana dice: No, no. ¿Cómo te llamas?" (Or: "La mamá de Ana dice: ¡Mucho gusto, Coquí!") The sign is on the right bubble; he touches it: the line, and her reply.
6. **Decision B, touch a bubble (the same game; Ana's abuela).** NARR: "Ana dice: Abuela, es Coquí." NARR: "La abuela de Ana dice: ¡Mucho gusto, Coquí!" (Or, drawn: "La abuela de Ana dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?") Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Ana sits down beside the frog; Beto climbs up on his other side. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Tienes pan para las palomas?" **(palomas: heard; the pigeons carry it)** COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si, sí toma)]: "¡Sí! Toma." He gives her a crumb.
   - Three pigeons left: one hops onto Ana's hand, one onto Beto's cap, one onto the frog's head. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: one on Ana's hand, one on the frog's head; Beto claps.
   - One left: it eats from the frog's hand; Ana and Beto watch, very still.
8. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Quieren jugar?" **(jugar: heard)** The children run off across the square; the frog hops after them. NARR: "Coquí tiene amigos nuevos."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** ¿cómo te llamas? 3 (steps 1, 2, 3), mucho gusto 5 (steps 1 twice, 2, 3 twice). Not deciding: nombre 3 (steps 2, 3).
**A miss is filed under:** the line that was said to him (¿cómo te llamas? or mucho gusto).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. Both lines are new; the bubbles change sides.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 7): voy; sí.

## Story 61: ¿De dónde eres?

- **Goal he can see:** the notice board, with a row of five empty hooks under a picture of the island and a row under a picture of tall buildings; and on the board's ledge, under a stone, three blank cards and a pencil: the frog's own, for later.
- **At stake:** the blank cards (3). A photo hung under the wrong picture has to come down again: the frog reaches across for it, knocks the stone, and the wind takes a card.
- **New words:** ¿de dónde eres?, soy de, vivo en.
- **Back again:** casa, abuela, señor, señora, aquí, cerca, lejos, ahora, grande, mira, yo, tú, tiene, amigo, foto (heard), es.

**Start:** afternoon. Ana and the frog at the notice board with a box of small photos (faces). On the board, two pictures: at the left the island (a green island, a palm, the sea), at the right a city of tall buildings. Luis and Carmen look on; Ana's abuela sits on the seat.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Ana y Coquí tienen fotos de los amigos." Ana points at the island. NARR: "Ana dice: Esto es Puerto Rico." At the buildings. NARR: "Ana dice: Y esto es Nueva York. Está lejos." **(Puerto Rico, Nueva York: names; the pictures carry them)**
2. NARR: "Luis dice: Ana, ¿de dónde eres?" NARR: "Ana dice: Soy de Nueva York. Ahora vivo en Puerto Rico." Ana holds up her own photo. Ask (signed, the hooks under the buildings): "¿De dónde es Ana?" He touches them: Ana hangs her photo there.
3. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Y tú, Luis? ¿De dónde eres?" NARR: "Luis dice: Soy de Puerto Rico. Vivo aquí, cerca." Ask (signed, the hooks under the island): "¿De dónde es Luis?" He touches them: Luis's photo hangs there.
4. Ana's abuela comes over from the seat. NARR: "Ana dice: Mi abuela vive en Nueva York. Y es de Puerto Rico." NARR: "La abuela de Ana dice: Sí. Soy de Puerto Rico. Vivo en Nueva York." Ask (signed, the hooks under the island): "¿De dónde es la abuela de Ana?" He touches them: her photo hangs beside Luis's. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿De dónde eres?" COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aqui, de aquí)]: "¡De aquí!" The frog points at the island; his photo goes up too.

**The turn**

5. The señora and the señor walk up to look at the board. The wind lifts the corner of the top card on the ledge; the stone holds it. NARR: "Ana dice: Mira, Coquí. ¡Más amigos!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A grown-up says where he is from and where he lives: one is the island, the other the city, drawn, each round on its own; which half is said first is drawn too. The photo goes under the place he is FROM.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the two rows of hooks; tests soy de against vivo en).** NARR: "Luis dice: Señora, ¿de dónde es usted?" **(es usted: heard)** NARR: "La señora dice: Soy de Puerto Rico. Vivo en Nueva York." (Or, drawn: "Vivo en Nueva York. Soy de Puerto Rico."; or the two places the other way round.) NARR: "¿De dónde es la señora?"
   - Right: the frog hangs her photo there. She nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it under the other picture. She shakes her head, smiling. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Soy de Puerto Rico." The frog reaches across the ledge to take the photo down; his foot knocks the stone, and the wind takes a card away over the wall. A cross. The sign is on the right row; he touches it: the photo hangs there.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the señor).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Señor, ¿de dónde es usted?" NARR: "El señor dice: Vivo en Puerto Rico. Soy de Nueva York." (Drawn, as in A.) NARR: "¿De dónde es el señor?" Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The board is full of faces. Ana gives the frog the pencil. NARR: "Ana dice: Ahora tú, Coquí. ¿Dónde vives?" COQUÍ [TURN: cerca (cerca)]: "¡Cerca!" He takes the cards from under the stone.
   - Three cards left: the frog draws three pictures and hangs them: abuela's house, abuela, the whole family. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the house, and abuela.
   - One left: the house.
9. NARR: "Ana dice: ¡Qué casa más linda!" **(linda: heard)** NARR: "Coquí es de Puerto Rico. Y Ana vive en Puerto Rico."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** soy de 3 (steps 2, 3, 4; "es de" four times more), vivo en 3 with vive en (steps 2, 4 twice). Not deciding: ¿de dónde eres? 3 (steps 2, 3, 4).
**Forms:** es de counts with soy de; vive en, vives count with vivo en; ¿de dónde es? with ¿de dónde eres?
**A miss is filed under:** soy de.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both places are named in every answer, and only soy de and vivo en say which is which. Nothing about the señora or the señor shows where they are from.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 8): aquí; cerca.

## Story 62: Hablo español

- **Goal he can see:** the piragüero's cart, with a jar for his coins and, painted on its side, a big piragua of three colours. The piragüero points at the painting and winks at the frog. Visitors with maps and cameras walk towards the cart.
- **At stake:** the three visitors at the back of the line. A helper who cannot help: the last one in the line looks at his watch and walks away.
- **New words:** hablo, español, inglés, entiendo, no sé.
- **Back again:** perdón, quiere, busca, guagua, parada, cerca, piragua, ¿quién?, señor, señora, hombre, mujer, joven, yo, tú, sí, no, por favor, gracias, ahora, come.

**Start:** midday. The piragüero at his cart, at the right. The frog on the cart's edge beside the jar. Luis, Carmen and Ana near the seat. A visitor walks up to the cart: a woman with a map and a camera.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The visitor speaks to the piragüero: over her a bubble with the flag of the United States and a small piragua. The piragüero scratches his head. NARR: "El piragüero dice: Perdón. No entiendo. Yo hablo español. No hablo inglés." **(nothing in English is ever heard)** Ask (signed, the visitor): "¿Quién habla inglés?" He touches her: her bubble shakes; she says it again, slowly, pointing.
2. Ana runs up. NARR: "Ana dice: ¿Entiendes, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "Ana dice: Yo entiendo. Yo hablo inglés y español." Over Ana a bubble with both flags. She listens to the visitor and turns to the piragüero. NARR: "Ana dice: La turista quiere una piragua." **(turista: heard)** The piragüero shaves the ice, pours the red syrup and hands it over; the visitor drops a coin into the jar.
3. The visitor asks one more thing: the flag, and a small guagua. NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¿Y ahora? No sé." NARR: "Luis dice: Yo no sé. Yo hablo español." NARR: "Ana dice: Busca la guagua." Carmen points across the square. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡La parada está cerca!" Ana tells the visitor, who waves and goes.

**The turn**

4. Ana's mamá calls from across the square; Ana runs off. Five more visitors come up to the cart, in a line: two in front, and three behind them. The first speaks: the flag, and a small piragua. The piragüero looks at the frog and spreads his hands. NARR: "El piragüero dice: No entiendo. No sé. ¡Y Ana no está!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** A visitor asks for something; the piragüero asks who speaks English. Two people come up, drawn each round from the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer and the joven (nobody twice); each says what he speaks; who can help, and who speaks first, is drawn. The frog touches the one who can help.

5. **Decision A, touch a person (two people; tests inglés).** NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¿Quién habla inglés?" NARR: "La mujer dice: Yo hablo inglés." NARR: "El hombre dice: Yo solo hablo español." **(solo: heard)** (Drawn.)
   - Right: the helper listens to the visitor, and tells the piragüero; over the helper a bubble with both flags. The piragüero makes the piragua; a coin drops into the jar. The visitor goes off with her piragua, and the two who came up walk away. A star.
   - Wrong: the one he touched listens to the visitor, and shrugs. NARR: "El hombre dice: Perdón. No entiendo." At the end of the line a visitor looks at his watch and walks away. A cross. The sign is on the right one; he touches her: she helps; the piragua, the coin; the visitor and the two go, as after a right move.
6. **Decision B, touch a person (two others; tests hablo against no hablo).** The next visitor steps up: the flag, and a small piragua. NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¿Y ahora? ¿Quién habla inglés?" NARR: "El joven dice: No hablo inglés." NARR: "La señora dice: Hablo inglés y español." (Drawn.)
   - Right and wrong as in A. The line of the one who cannot help: "Perdón. No entiendo."

**The ending**

7. Ana comes back, out of breath, and helps the visitors who are still at the back of the line, one after another: piraguas, coins. The piragüero counts the coins in his jar and turns to the frog. NARR: "El piragüero dice: ¿Quieres una piragua, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si, sí por favor)]: "¡Sí, por favor!"
   - Three were still waiting: the jar is full to the top. The piragüero makes the piragua of his painting, three colours, as big as the frog, and gives it to him. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: a piragua of two colours.
   - One: a small red one.
8. NARR: "Ana dice: Yo hablo inglés y español. ¡Y tú hablas español, Coquí!" NARR: "Coquí habla español. Y come piragua."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** inglés 4 (steps 1 twice, 2, and the piragüero's question), hablo 5 with habla (steps 1 to 3). Not deciding: español 3 (steps 1, 2, 3); entiendo 4 with entiendes (steps 1, 2 twice, 4); no sé 3 (steps 3 twice, 4).
**Forms:** habla, hablas count with hablo; entiendes with entiendo.
**A miss is filed under:** inglés in round A, hablo in round B.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: nothing about the two shows what they speak. In round B both say "inglés": only "no hablo" and "hablo" say who can help.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 7): no; sí.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 60: the game set the new question against "¿cómo estás?", an old line, so the new one was never needed: now both lines are new (his name is asked, or he is greeted by name), and the bubbles change sides. Carmen asked a name she had just heard twice: Ana asks the dog's name. "Toca aquí. Y aquí." was a drill with bare labels: the bubbles come in inside Ana's own meeting with the frog. A pigeon flying off because a grown-up said a line again was thin: everybody bursts out laughing. Lines with no verb or the wrong moment ("Una niña nueva en la plaza", "¡mucho gusto! ¿Juegan?") are whole sentences. Counts put right.
- 61: the game's people had things that gave the answer away (the piragüero at his cart lives here; the abuela with a camera is a visitor), and the draw could contradict story 62: now the señora and the señor, with nothing to go by. The round never asked the board's question: the narrator asks "¿De dónde es la señora?" after her answer. A child asks a grown-up with usted. The cards sat on the seat and the stone rolled by itself: they are on the board's ledge, and the frog knocks the stone as he reaches for the wrong photo. "Hacen fotos" is Spain's: "tienen fotos". "Vivo en" was heard twice: Ana's abuela is the opposite case of Ana (from the island, lives in the city), which also shows that where he is FROM decides. Two turns stood one line apart: one is cut. "En la casa de abuela" copied the new pattern and could mean Ana's abuela: "¡Cerca!".
- 62: rebuilt. Round B could not be settled by the Spanish (whoever says "Hablo inglés" in Spanish speaks both), and the story said Ana's abuela and Beto spoke no Spanish after both had spoken it. Now the ones who speak only English are visitors; the piragüero, who speaks only Spanish, asks "¿Quién habla inglés?", which is what people ask; round B sets "No hablo inglés" against "Hablo inglés y español". The stake had no link to the move: now a helper who cannot help costs a visitor from the line. Both turns used new words: they are "no" and "sí" ("por favor" has never been a story's new word, so it cannot be the turn's word; the builder's check found it). "Ana dice: Abuela dice: ..." had two speakers: Ana says what the visitor wants. "No sé" was heard once: three times. Luis could be drawn speaking English after saying he does not: the family is out of the draw. The joke about mantecado is cut.

Left, and why:
- The island often puts the pronoun in: "¿Cómo tú te llamas?", "¿De dónde tú eres?". Both ways are said there; the reviewer was not sure which is more usual. The plain form is kept: it is the one he will be asked on the call, and it is understood everywhere.
- "¡De aquí!" has "de" in it, which belongs to the new phrase. The word he says is "aquí"; "de" alone has been heard since story 3.
- 62's round A can be passed by catching the one word "inglés". That is the word under test.
- Flags for languages: Puerto Ricans speak Spanish and many speak both. The flags are only on what the visitors say and on who is translating.

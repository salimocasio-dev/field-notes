# Las tiendas: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `tiendas`). Stories 54 to 56. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 53.

Scene words (page 02): tienda, banco, farmacia, escuela, hospital, correo, edificio, abierto, cerrado, entra, nuevo, alto.

The picture: a street in town, seen from the pavement across it. One view. A row of four fronts, each with a door and a picture sign over it: the tienda (a toy shop: a kite and a top in its window), the banco (a coin on its sign), the farmacia (a green cross), the correo (a letter on its sign, a post box beside the door). In front of the tienda stands a little horse that a child can ride for a coin. Story 55 has its own rows (state `fila` altos, `par` a or b): each has two buildings with no sign yet, a tall old one and a low new one. Pair a: four floors, grey, a cracked wall, old shutters; and one floor, bright paint, a ladder and a paint pot still beside it. Pair b: a tall brick one with a clock that has stopped; and a low yellow one with a young tree tied to a stick. Every door looks the same shut: nothing in the picture says whether a place is open (no light in a window, no sign on a door).

New people: nobody. The family, the señora and the señor, don Pepe on his bicycle.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 54: Las tiendas

- **Goal he can see:** the little horse in front of the toy shop. The frog looks at it; the bebé in his stroller points at it. Abuela shows three coins in her hand and puts them in her pocket: after the errands.
- **At stake:** the coins (3). In a wrong place the bebé takes something from the counter (a lollipop, a pencil, a sticker) and will not let go: abuela pays a coin.
- **New words:** tienda, banco, farmacia, correo.
- **Back again:** calle, gente, entra (heard), abuela, bebé, necesito, compra, chavos, ¿cuántos?, dos, tres, quiere, para, ahora, luego, vamos, gracias.

**Start:** morning. Abuela pushes the bebé's stroller along the near pavement; the frog hops beside it. Abuela has a letter in her hand. The card shows her three errands as pictures: a letter, a coin, a small bottle.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela, el bebé y Coquí están en la calle de las tiendas." The toy shop twinkles. NARR: "Mira. Esta es una tienda. En la tienda hay un caballito." **(caballito: heard; the picture carries it)** Ask (signed, the toy shop): "¿Dónde está la tienda?" He touches it: the horse rocks once; the bebé claps. Abuela shows her three coins. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luego, Coquí. Ahora tengo tres cosas que hacer."
2. The letter lights on the card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Tengo una carta. Vamos al correo." **(carta: heard)** The correo twinkles. NARR: "Este es el correo." Ask (signed, the correo): "¿Dónde está el correo?" He touches it: they cross to it; abuela drops the letter in the post box. A tick.
3. The coin lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora necesito chavos. Vamos al banco." The banco twinkles. NARR: "Este es el banco." Ask (signed, the banco): "Toca el banco." He touches it: abuela goes in and comes out with her purse fat. A tick.
4. The bottle lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y el abuelo necesita algo de la farmacia." The farmacia twinkles. NARR: "Esta es la farmacia." Ask (signed, the farmacia): "¿Dónde está la farmacia?" He touches it: abuela goes in and comes out with a small paper bag. The last tick.

**The turn**

5. Abuela's telephone rings; she listens and nods. A new card: two blank places. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Mamá necesita dos cosas más." She looks at the three coins, then at the horse. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cuántos chavos tengo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: tres (tres)]: "¡Tres!" The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela names where they go next, one of the banco, the farmacia and the correo, drawn, each round on its own. The frog leads: he touches a front. (All four fronts are in the picture.)

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the fronts; tests the place named).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora vamos a la farmacia." (Drawn.)
   - Right: they go in at that door and come out; abuela has a new small bag. A star.
   - Wrong: they go in at the door he touched. They come out: the bebé holds a lollipop (or a pencil, or a sticker sheet) in his fist; abuela, shaking her head, drops a coin into a hand at the door. A cross: one coin gone. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Esta no es la farmacia." The sign is on the right front; he touches it: in, and out with the bag.
   - Touching the toy shop counts as a wrong place: the bebé comes out with a small top.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another place, drawn).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y ahora, al correo." Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. They stop at the horse. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora sí. ¿Quieres el caballito, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!"
   - Three coins left: the frog rides, then the bebé on abuela's hands, then the frog again, hat-waving. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the frog rides, then the bebé.
   - One left: the frog and the bebé ride together, the frog holding him.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Te gusta la tienda?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Me gusta mucho!" NARR: "La tienda, el banco, la farmacia y el correo. ¡Vamos a casa!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** banco 3 (step 3), farmacia 3 (step 4), correo 3 (step 2). Not deciding: tienda 4 (step 1).
**A miss is filed under:** the place that was named.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four fronts, one in three if he leaves the toy shop out. The signs over the doors are the pictures the opening taught; the word says which.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 5, 8, 9): tres; quiero; me gusta.

## Story 55: El edificio alto

- **Goal he can see:** a stack of three lunch tins tied with a cloth, steam rising in three wisps, in the basket of Luis's bicycle. Lunch for mamá and for papá, hot.
- **At stake:** the heat (3 wisps). Carrying it up the wrong building cools it: a wisp goes.
- **New words:** edificio, alto, nuevo, escuela, hospital.
- **Back again:** calle, bicicleta, va, viene, mamá, papá, comida, caliente, frío, hace, grande, cuidado, tengo hambre, gracias, rico, también.

**Start:** midday, state `altos`. Luis stands with his bicycle on the near pavement, the lunch tins in its basket, the frog on the handlebar. Across the street the two buildings with no sign: the tall old one and the low new one. Children's voices: a bell rings somewhere.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van por la calle en bicicleta. Abuela hace la comida para mamá y papá." The tins twinkle; the steam rises. NARR: "La comida está caliente."
2. The tall building twinkles. NARR: "Mira este edificio. Es un edificio alto." Ask (signed, the tall one): "¿Dónde está el edificio alto?" He touches it: a window opens at the top and a hand waves.
3. The low one twinkles. NARR: "Y este edificio es nuevo. Mira: un edificio nuevo." Ask (signed, the low one): "Toca el edificio nuevo." He touches it: the painter on the ladder lifts his brush.
4. Luis counts the floors of the tall one with a finger, to the top. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Qué alto!" Then he touches the low one's wet paint and looks at his finger. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Y qué nuevo!" The señor walks past. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Hola! ¿Para quién es la comida?" COQUÍ [TURN: mamá (mamá, mama)]: "Para mamá y papá."

**The turn**

5. Luis takes a paper from his pocket, turns it round twice and scratches his head. NARR: "Luis dice: Mamá está en el hospital. Papá está en la escuela. Pero... ¿dónde están?" **(hospital, escuela: said here for the first time; nothing costs yet)** Two small signs lean against the wall of each building, face down. NARR: "El señor dice: El hospital es un edificio. La escuela también." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One building is tall and old, the other low and new. The señor, who knows the street, says which one the hospital is, by one of the two words. Round B is in the next street, with another tall old building and another low new one: the señora says which one the escuela is. Which word is used is drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two buildings; tests the word said).** NARR: "El señor dice: El hospital es el edificio alto." (Or, drawn: "El hospital es el edificio nuevo.")
   - Right: Luis and the frog run in with one tin. The sign over that door is turned up: a red cross. Mamá, in her nurse's coat, comes to the door and takes the tin. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Mi comida! ¡Gracias!" A star.
   - Wrong: they run in at the other door and up; a head looks out of a window, shakes, points across. They come out puffing. One wisp of steam is gone. A cross. NARR: "El señor dice: No. El hospital es el edificio alto." The sign is on the right building; he touches it: mamá at the door, the tin.
7. Luis and the frog ride on. NARR: "Luego, la otra calle." The picture changes to the second pair of buildings (`par` b): again a tall old one (brick, a clock that has stopped) and a low new one (yellow, a young tree tied to a stick in front). The señora stands there with her bag. **Decision B, touch a thing (the two buildings of the second pair; the escuela).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¿La escuela? La escuela es el edificio nuevo." (Or, drawn on its own: "La escuela es el edificio alto.") Right: the sign is turned up: a bell; papá, with a book, takes his tin. Wrong as in A, with the señora's line.

**The ending**

8. One tin is left in the basket: Luis's and the frog's own. They sit on the kerb and open it.
   - Three wisps: the food steams; they eat with big eyes. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Está caliente! ¡Qué rico!" Stars and a heart.
   - Two wisps: a little steam; they eat.
   - One wisp: no steam; Luis blows on his fingers as a joke; they eat all the same. NARR: "Luis dice: Está un poco frío. Pero está rico."
9. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Tienes hambre, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí, tengo hambre!" NARR: "El hospital y la escuela. Un edificio alto y un edificio nuevo."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** alto 3 (steps 2 and 4), nuevo 3 (steps 3 and 4). Not deciding: edificio 6 before the game; hospital 2 and escuela 2 before the game, and 3 or more each by the end.
**Why two pairs of buildings:** with one pair, round B would be told by round A (the building that is left), or the hospital and the school would share a building. Each round has its own tall old building and low new one.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (alto or nuevo).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: neither building has a sign up.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): mamá; tengo hambre.

## Story 56: Abierto y cerrado

- **Goal he can see:** a dark cloud at the edge of the sky, and abuela looking up at it with her bag of bread held close. Three things to get before the rain.
- **At stake:** the dry time (3 steps: the cloud far, the cloud over the street, rain). Trying a door that is shut costs a step.
- **New words:** abierto, cerrado, entra.
- **Back again:** tienda, banco, farmacia, correo, calle, lluvia (heard), necesito, compra, pan, agua, ¿tiene…?, aquí tiene, gracias, rápido, espera, me gusta, casa.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuela and the frog on the near pavement; the four fronts across the street, every door shut and alike. A dark cloud at the left edge of the sky. Abuela's list on the card: a stamp, a bottle of water, a small bottle.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela y Coquí están en la calle de las tiendas." Thunder, far off; abuela looks up. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Rápido, Coquí! Viene la lluvia." **(lluvia: heard; the cloud carries it)**
2. The stamp lights. Abuela pushes the correo's door: it opens. NARR: "El correo está abierto. Abuela entra." She goes in and comes out with the stamp. A tick. Ask (signed, the correo's open door): "¿Dónde entra abuela?" He touches it: the door swings.
3. Abuela tries the banco's door on the way: it does not move. NARR: "El banco está cerrado." She knocks; nothing. NARR: "Cerrado. Abuela no entra." Ask (signed, the banco's door): "Toca la puerta del banco." He touches it: the frog pushes with both hands; it rattles.
4. The señora comes out of the tienda with a kite. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenas tardes! La tienda está abierta. Pero el banco está cerrado." NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Entras en la tienda?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "No, gracias."

**The turn**

5. The cloud has moved in a little. Abuela sits down on a bench with her bag and gives the frog her purse. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, entra tú. Yo espero aquí con el pan." The card shows two places. Every door is shut again. The signs go.

**The game.** Each thing can be had in two places. The señora, on her way past, says which of the two is open and which is shut; that, and which half of her line comes first, is drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two doors; tests the word said).** The bottle of water lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Necesito agua." NARR: "La señora dice: La farmacia está abierta. La tienda está cerrada." (Or, drawn: "La tienda está cerrada. La farmacia está abierta.") Ask: "¿Dónde entra Coquí?"
   - Right: the frog pushes the door: it opens; he goes in and comes out with the water. A star, a tick.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the other door: it rattles and stays shut; he pushes again. The cloud slides over the street. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. La tienda está cerrada. La farmacia está abierta." The sign is on the open one; he touches it: in, and out with the water.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (two other doors; the small bottle).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y necesito algo para el abuelo." NARR: "La señora dice: El correo está cerrado. El banco está abierto." (The pair of fronts and the line are drawn afresh.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog comes back with the last thing. Abuela stands up.
   - No step lost: the cloud is still at the edge. They walk off along the pavement under a bright sky; the first drop falls behind them. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bien! Vamos a casa." Stars and a heart.
   - One lost: a few drops; abuela holds her bag over her head, the frog hops ahead with his mouth open to the drops.
   - Two lost: rain. Abuela stands under the tienda's awning with the bread inside her coat; the frog dances in the rain in front of her, and she laughs.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Te gusta la lluvia, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Sí! ¡Me gusta!" NARR: "La tienda está abierta. Y la calle también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** abierto 3 with its form abierta (steps 2 and 4), cerrado 4 (steps 3 and 4). Not deciding: entra 4 before the game, with entras once.
**Forms:** abierta, cerrada (la farmacia, la tienda) count as abierto, cerrado; entras as entra.
**A miss is filed under:** abierto.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both doors are shut and alike.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): no; me gusta. (Story 54 also ends on "me gusta": to change in the reviews if it reads as a repeat.)

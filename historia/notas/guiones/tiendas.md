# Las tiendas: three stories (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `tiendas`). Stories 54 to 56. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 53.

Scene words (page 02): tienda, banco, farmacia, escuela, hospital, correo, edificio, abierto, cerrado, entra, nuevo, alto.

The picture (as the set `tiendas-set.html` draws it): a street of shops in town. One view. A row of four fronts across the picture, each with a door and a picture sign over it: the tienda (a kite on its sign, toys in its window, a little horse outside that a child can ride for a coin), the banco (a coin), the farmacia (a green cross), the correo (a letter; a post box beside the door). In front of the row a wide pavement, where everybody walks, with a seat by the kerb; then the street, at the bottom left. Nobody crosses the street: "they cross to it" in the steps below is walking along the pavement to that door. Story 55 has its own rows (state `fila` altos, `par` a or b, `lado` izq or der): each has two buildings with no sign, a tall old one and a low new one; which stands on which side is drawn. Pair a: three floors, grey, a cracked wall, old shutters; and one floor, bright paint, a ladder and a paint pot still beside it. Pair b: a tall brick one with a clock that has stopped; and a low yellow one with a young tree tied to a stick. Every door looks the same shut: nothing in the picture says whether a place is open (no light in a window, no sign on a door). The cloud of story 56 is in the sky behind the row.

New people: nobody. The family, the señora and the señor.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 54: Las tiendas

- **Goal he can see:** the little horse in front of the tienda. The frog looks at it; the bebé in his stroller points at it. Abuela shows three coins in her hand and puts them in her pocket: after the errands. The card shows the three coins and the horse.
- **At stake:** the coins (3). In a wrong place the bebé takes something from the counter (a lollipop, a pencil, a top) and will not let go: abuela pays a coin.
- **New words:** tienda, banco, farmacia, correo.
- **Back again:** calle, abuela, abuelo, bebé, mamá, necesito, chavos, me gusta, tengo, cosa, dos, tres, ahora, luego, vamos, aquí, gracias, casa, listo.

**Start:** morning. Abuela pushes the bebé's stroller along the near pavement; the frog hops beside it. Abuela has a letter in her hand. Beside the coins and the horse, the card shows her three errands as pictures: a letter, a coin purse, a small bottle.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela, el bebé y Coquí están en la calle de las tiendas." The tienda twinkles; the horse rocks once; the bebé claps and points. NARR: "Mira. Aquí, en la tienda, hay un caballito." **(caballito: heard; the picture carries it)** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Te gusta el caballito, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" Abuela shows her three coins and puts them away. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luego, Coquí. Ahora tengo tres cosas que hacer."
2. The letter lights on the card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Tengo una carta. ¿Dónde está el correo?" **(carta: heard)** The correo twinkles. Ask (signed, the correo): "¿Dónde está el correo?" He touches it: they cross to it; abuela drops the letter in the post box. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Aquí está el correo!" A tick.
3. The coin purse lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora necesito chavos. Vamos al banco." The banco twinkles. Ask (signed, the banco): "¿Dónde está el banco?" He touches it: abuela goes in and comes out with her purse fat. A tick.
4. The bottle lights. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y abuelo necesita algo de la farmacia." The farmacia twinkles. Ask (signed, the farmacia): "¿Dónde está la farmacia?" He touches it: abuela goes in and comes out with a small paper bag. The last tick. Abuela counts on her fingers, looking along the row. NARR: "Abuela dice: El correo, el banco, la farmacia... ¡Listo!"

**The turn**

5. Abuela's telephone rings; she listens and nods. A new card: two blank places. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Ay! Mamá necesita dos cosas más." She looks at the horse, then at the frog, and shrugs. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela names where they go next: one of the banco, the farmacia and the correo, drawn; round B draws one of the two that are left. The frog leads: he touches a front. All four fronts are in the picture and any of them can be touched.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the fronts; tests the place named).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora vamos a la farmacia." (Or, drawn: "Ahora vamos al banco." / "Ahora vamos al correo.")
   - Right: they go in at that door and come out with that place's thing (the farmacia: a box of plasters; the banco: an envelope; the correo: a parcel). A star.
   - Wrong: they go in at the door he touched. They come out: the bebé holds a lollipop (the farmacia), a pencil (the banco or the correo) or a top (the tienda) in his fist; abuela, shaking her head, drops a coin into the shopkeeper's hand at the door (a hand and a sleeve; no face). A cross: one coin gone. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Esta no es la farmacia." The sign is on the right front; he touches it: in, and out with the thing.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; another place, drawn from the two left).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y ahora, al correo." (Or: "Y ahora, al banco." / "Y ahora, a la farmacia.") Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. They stop at the horse. Abuela takes out her coins. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora sí. ¡Vamos al caballito de la tienda, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, abuela!"
   - Three coins left: the frog rides, then the bebé on abuela's hands, then the frog again, waving. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: the frog rides, then the bebé.
   - One left: the frog and the bebé ride together, the frog holding him.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué día, Coquí! Vamos a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** correo 4 (steps 2 and 4), banco 3 (steps 3 and 4), farmacia 3 (step 4). Not deciding: tienda 2 before the game (step 1) and 3 on a clean run (step 8).
**A miss is filed under:** the place that was named.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four fronts (one in three if he leaves the tienda out). The signs over the doors are the pictures the opening taught; the word says which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): me gusta; gracias.

## Story 55: El edificio alto

- **Goal he can see:** a stack of three lunch tins tied with a cloth in the basket of Luis's bicycle, a wisp of steam over each. Lunch for mamá, for papá, and one for Luis and the frog, hot.
- **At stake:** the heat (3 wisps). Carrying the tins up the wrong building cools them: a wisp goes.
- **New words:** edificio, alto, nuevo, escuela, hospital.
- **Back again:** calle, bicicleta, va, mamá, papá, comida, caliente, frío, arroz, rico, un poco, tengo hambre, gracias, también, no, para, este, ahora.

**Start:** midday, state `altos`, pair a. Luis stands with his bicycle on the pavement, the lunch tins in its basket, the frog beside him. Across the street the two buildings with no sign: the tall old one and the low new one.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van por la calle en bicicleta." The tins twinkle; the steam rises. NARR: "Esta es la comida de abuela. Es para mamá y papá. Está caliente."
2. The tall building twinkles. NARR: "Mira este edificio. Es un edificio alto." Ask (signed, the tall one): "¿Dónde está el edificio alto?" He touches it: a window opens at the top and a hand waves. Luis counts its floors with a finger, to the top. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Qué alto!"
3. The low one twinkles. NARR: "Y este edificio es nuevo." Ask (signed, the low one): "¿Dónde está el edificio nuevo?" He touches it: the painter on the ladder lifts his brush. Luis touches the wet paint and looks at his finger. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Este es nuevo!"
4. The señor walks past and looks at the tin the frog is holding. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Hola! ¿La comida es para ti, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: mamá (mamá, mama)]: "¡No! Para mamá y papá."

**The turn**

5. Luis takes a paper from his pocket: two small drawings on it, a red cross and a bell. He turns it round twice and scratches his head. NARR: "Luis dice: Mamá trabaja en el hospital. Papá trabaja en la escuela. Pero ¿dónde está el hospital?" **(trabaja: heard. hospital, escuela: said here for the first time; nothing costs on them)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One building is tall and old, the other low and new; which is on which side is drawn. The señor, who knows the street, says which one the hospital is, by one of the two words. Round B is in the next street, with another tall old building and another low new one: the señora says which one the escuela is. Which word is used is drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two buildings; tests the word said).** NARR: "El señor dice: El hospital es el edificio alto." (Or, drawn: "El señor dice: El hospital es el edificio nuevo.")
   - Right: Luis and the frog run in with one tin. Mamá, in her nurse's coat, comes to the door, hangs a sign beside it (a red cross) and takes the tin. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Mi comida! ¡Gracias!" A star.
   - Wrong: they run in at the other door and up; a head looks out of a window, shakes, points across. They come out puffing. One wisp of steam is gone from every tin. A cross. NARR: "El señor dice: No. El hospital es el edificio alto." The sign is on the right building; he touches it: mamá at the door, the sign, the tin.
7. Luis and the frog ride on. NARR: "Ahora van a la calle de la escuela." The picture changes to the second pair of buildings (`par` b). The señora stands there with her bag. **Decision B, touch a thing (the two buildings of the second pair; the escuela).** NARR: "La señora dice: ¿La escuela? La escuela es el edificio nuevo." (Or, drawn on its own: "La escuela es el edificio alto.") Right: papá, with a book, comes to the door, hangs a sign beside it (a bell) and takes his tin. NARR: "Papá dice: ¡Qué rico! ¡Gracias!" Wrong as in A, with the señora's line.

**The ending**

8. One tin is left in the basket: Luis's and the frog's own. They sit down on the kerb. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Tienes hambre, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí! Tengo hambre." Luis opens the tin.
   - Three wisps: the rice steams; they eat with big eyes. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡El arroz está caliente! ¡Qué rico!" Stars and a heart.
   - Two wisps: a little steam; they eat. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Qué rico!"
   - One wisp: no steam; Luis blows on his fingers as a joke; they eat all the same. NARR: "Luis dice: El arroz está un poco frío. Pero está rico."
9. They get back on the bicycle. NARR: "Luis y Coquí van a casa en bicicleta."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** alto 3 (step 2), nuevo 3 (step 3). Not deciding: edificio 4 before the game; hospital 2 and escuela 1 before the game, and 3 each on a clean run (the two rounds and step 7).
**Why two pairs of buildings:** with one pair, round B would be told by round A (the building that is left), or the hospital and the school would share a building.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (alto or nuevo).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: neither building has a sign until the right door is found; sides are drawn.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 8): mamá; tengo hambre.

## Story 56: Abierto y cerrado

- **Goal he can see:** a dark cloud at the edge of the sky, and abuela looking up at it with her bag of bread held close. Two things to get before the rain: a bag for the bread, and an umbrella.
- **At stake:** the dry time (3 steps: the cloud far, the cloud over the street, rain). Trying a door that is shut costs a step.
- **New words:** abierto, cerrado, entra.
- **Back again:** tienda, banco, farmacia, correo, calle, puerta, lluvia (heard), necesito, bolsa, pan, busca, todo, me gusta, rápido, espera, aquí, ahora, gracias, casa, viene, adiós.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuela and the frog on the near pavement; the four fronts across the street, every door shut and alike. A dark cloud at the left edge of the sky. Abuela has a stamp to buy; her bread is in an open paper bag.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela y Coquí están en la calle de las tiendas." Thunder, far off; abuela looks up and holds the bread close. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Rápido, Coquí! ¡Ahí viene la lluvia!" **(lluvia: heard; the cloud carries it)**
2. Abuela walks to the correo. NARR: "El correo está abierto." Ask (signed, the correo's door): "¿Por dónde entra abuela?" He touches it: she pushes the door, it opens, she goes in and comes out with the stamp.
3. Abuela tries the banco's door on the way back: it does not move. She knocks; nothing. NARR: "El banco está cerrado. Abuela no entra." Ask (signed, the banco's door): "Toca la puerta del banco." He touches it: the frog pushes with both hands; it rattles. NARR: "Está cerrado."
4. The señora comes out of the tienda with a kite in her hand. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenas tardes! La tienda está abierta. Pero el banco está cerrado. Mira, Coquí. ¿Te gusta?" She holds the kite up. COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!"

**The turn**

5. A bell rings twelve somewhere. Along the row every door is pulled shut again; a key turns in one of them, but nothing shows which. Abuela sits down on the bench with her bread and gives the frog her purse. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, entra tú. Busca una puerta abierta. Yo espero aquí con el pan." The card shows two places: a bag, an umbrella. The signs go.

**The game.** Any shop that is open can give a bag, or lend an umbrella. In each round two fronts are named, drawn from the tienda, the banco and the farmacia: the señora, on her way past, says which is open now and which is shut. That, and which half of her line comes first, is drawn, each round on its own. (The correo is not drawn: "correo" and "cerrado" sound too much alike in one line.)

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (two doors; tests the word said).** The bag lights on the card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Necesito una bolsa para el pan." NARR: "La señora dice: Ahora la farmacia está abierta. La tienda está cerrada." (Or, drawn: "La señora dice: Ahora la tienda está cerrada. La farmacia está abierta.") Ask: "¿Por dónde entra Coquí?"
   - Right: the frog pushes the door: it opens; he goes in and comes out with a big bag; abuela puts the bread in it. A star, a tick.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the other door: it rattles and stays shut; he pushes again. The cloud slides over the street. A cross. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. La tienda está cerrada. La farmacia está abierta." The sign is on the open one; he touches it: in, and out with the bag.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (two doors drawn afresh; the umbrella).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y necesito una sombrilla." **(sombrilla: heard; the card carries it)** NARR: "La señora dice: Ahora el banco está abierto. La farmacia está cerrada." (Drawn.) Ask: "¿Por dónde entra Coquí?" Right and wrong as in A; he comes out with an umbrella.

**The ending**

8. The frog comes back with the umbrella. Abuela stands up. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Gracias, Coquí! ¿Tienes todo?" COQUÍ [TURN: todo (todo)]: "¡Sí, todo!"
   - No step lost: the cloud is still at the edge. They walk off along the pavement under a bright sky, the umbrella shut under abuela's arm; the first drop falls behind them. Stars and a heart.
   - One lost: a few drops; abuela opens the umbrella; the frog hops ahead with his mouth open to the drops.
   - Two lost: rain. Abuela stands under the umbrella with the bread in its bag; the frog dances in the rain in front of her, and she laughs.
9. NARR: "Abuela y Coquí van a casa. ¡Adiós, lluvia!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** abierto 3 with its form abierta (steps 2, 4, 5), cerrado 3 (steps 3, twice; step 4). Not deciding: entra 4 before the game (steps 2, 3, 5).
**Forms:** abierta, cerrada (la farmacia, la tienda) count as abierto, cerrado.
**A miss is filed under:** abierto (the frog is looking for the open door).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both doors are shut and alike. A third front can be touched too: it is shut, costs nothing and rattles.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 8): me gusta; todo.

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 54: three beats in a row said "Este es X" and then asked where X is: now abuela asks, and the picture answers. Each errand ends differently, and the game's visits bring different things from the opening's. The two rounds cannot name the same place. "¿Quieres el caballito?" means "do you want to have it"; "tres chavos" sounds like three cents: the coins are no longer counted aloud. The closing list of nouns is gone. Two turns stood back to back, and "¿Te gusta la tienda?" was an odd question: two turns now, far apart. Words listed as back again that no line said are off the list.
- 55: "Mamá está en el hospital" means she is a patient: "trabaja en". The second round is in another street with its own pair, so it is not told by the first. Sides are drawn. "¡Y qué nuevo!" is not said. "Está un poco frío" of "la comida": the line names the rice. "¿Tienes hambre?" came after they had eaten: it is asked before the tin is opened. The bell at the start pointed at the school. The face-down signs hinted: the sign is hung up by mamá or papá when the right door is found. Every tin loses its wisp, so the loss shows at once.
- 56: abierto was heard twice before it decided: three now. The first ask came after abuela had already gone in. The señora said the tienda was open and then, in the game, that it was shut: a bell rings twelve, every door is pulled shut, and her lines say "ahora". Water from a toy shop and medicine from a bank made no sense: the frog looks for a bag and an umbrella, which any open shop can give. "¿Dónde entra?" is "where does it fit": "¿Por dónde entra?". "Entras en la tienda" is "entrar a", and "no, gracias" did not answer it: the señora shows her kite. "La calle también" meant nothing. The last turn is true in all three endings.

Left, and why:
- 54's game has three or four fronts to choose from, not two. The rule asks for two real options; more of them lowers the chance of a lucky touch, and every front does something.
- "Luego, Coquí": "después" is what is said; luego is story 50's word.
- Mamá and papá, not mami and papi: the course's words since La casa.
- 55's first turn and 56's last say words back from earlier lines ("Para mamá y papá", "todo"): they answer a real question the picture supports.
- "¿Tienes hambre?" answered "Tengo hambre": the verb turned round, as a child answers.
- Length: the beginner reader guesses all three are over 85 s. Lines are cut; the real number comes from machine play.

# El cielo: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `cielo`). Stories 81 to 83. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 80.

Scene words (page 02): cielo, nube, lluvia, llueve, viento, hace frío, luna, estrella, noche, bonito, feo, ¿qué tiempo hace?
How they are shared out: 81 cielo, nube, viento, ¿qué tiempo hace?; 82 lluvia, llueve, hace frío, bonito, feo; 83 luna, estrella, noche.

The picture: the porch of a little beach house, and a great deal of sky. One view. The sky fills the upper half: it changes with the story (blue; clouds coming; grey and rain; night with a moon and stars). Under it the sea's line and the sand. In front, at the left, the porch: two posts, a small roof with a roll-down awning, a table with a radio on it, abuela's rocking chair. In front of the porch, on the sand, a closed beach umbrella stuck in the sand and a blanket.

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. A voice: the man on the radio (he is only heard; a small bubble over the radio shows who is talking).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 81: ¿Qué tiempo hace?

- **Goal he can see:** on the blanket, a tray with three tall glasses of juice. Abuela sets it down and wags a finger: careful. Beside the blanket: the closed umbrella, and Luis's kite.
- **At stake:** the glasses of juice (3). The umbrella opened when the wind comes: it is blown over the blanket and knocks a glass down. The kite thrown up when there is no wind: it falls on the tray.
- **New words:** cielo, nube, viento, ¿qué tiempo hace?
- **Back again:** hace calor, sol, hay, no hay, azul, blanco, grande, pequeño, mira, vuela, viene, se va, ahora, jugo, vaso, quiero, un poco, cuidado, abuela, tres.

**Start:** afternoon. A blue sky with one small white cloud. Abuela in her rocking chair on the porch beside the radio. Luis with his kite, Carmen with the umbrella's handle, the frog on the blanket beside the tray.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela turns the radio on: a few notes of music. NARR: "La radio dice: ¿Qué tiempo hace? Hace sol. El cielo está azul." Ask (signed, the sky): "¿Dónde está el cielo?" He touches it: a gull crosses it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes calor?" COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco)]: "¡Un poco!"
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: Hace sol. ¡La sombrilla!" **(sombrilla: heard; the picture carries it)** Ask (signed, the umbrella): "Hace sol. ¿Qué necesita abuela?" He touches it: the frog pulls its cord and it opens over the blanket; the tray is in the shade. The small cloud drifts. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: una nube. Una nube pequeña en el cielo."
3. The music again. NARR: "La radio dice: ¿Qué tiempo hace ahora? Hace viento. Viene mucho viento." The palm's leaves stir; the umbrella shivers. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Viento! ¡Cuidado con la sombrilla!" Carmen shuts it. Luis holds up the kite. Ask (signed, the kite): "Hace viento. ¿Qué vuela?" He touches it: Luis throws the kite up, the wind takes it, and it climbs past the cloud. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vuela! ¡Hasta la nube!"

**The turn**

4. The wind drops; the kite comes down on the sand. The umbrella is shut, the kite lies ready; the tray stands between them. Abuela pats the radio. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis, Carmen: ¿qué tiempo hace? La radio dice." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The radio says what is coming: wind, or sun with no wind (drawn, each round on its own). The frog makes ready: the kite, or the umbrella. Nothing in the sky shows it yet. Before round B the umbrella is shut again and the kite lies on the sand.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the umbrella, the kite; tests viento against sol).** NARR: "La radio dice: ¿Qué tiempo hace? Viene viento." (Or, drawn: "La radio dice: ¿Qué tiempo hace? Viene sol.")
   - Right: the kite goes up as the wind arrives (or the umbrella opens as the sun comes out hot). A star.
   - Wrong: the umbrella opens, the wind arrives and takes it: it rolls across the blanket and knocks a glass down. (Or: Luis runs with the kite; no wind; it falls on the tray, and a glass goes over.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Viene viento." A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the kite flies.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; both words in every line).** NARR: "La radio dice: Ahora hace sol. No hay viento." (Or, drawn: "La radio dice: Ahora hace viento. No hay sol.") Right and wrong as in A; abuela's line after a wrong move: "¡No, Coquí! No hay viento." (or: "¡No, Coquí! Hace viento.")

**The ending**

7. The sun is low; the cloud has gone pink. Abuela comes down from the porch to the tray.
   - Three glasses: one for Luis, one for Carmen, one for abuela, who gives the frog her straw. Stars and a heart.
   - Two glasses: Luis and Carmen; abuela and the frog share a straw.
   - One glass: four straws in one glass.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres jugo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "El cielo está azul. Y hay una nube."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** viento 4 (step 3). Not deciding: cielo 4 (steps 1, 2); nube 3 (steps 2, 3); ¿qué tiempo hace? 3 (steps 1, 3, 4).
**A miss is filed under:** viento.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. In round B both words are in every line; only "hace" and "no hay" say which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): un poco; quiero.

## Story 82: Llueve

- **Goal he can see:** on the porch table, supper laid, and three candles burning in jars. Abuela lights the last one and shakes out the match. The sky behind is filling with grey cloud.
- **At stake:** the candles (3). Rain on a table with the awning up puts a candle out. Abuela, cold with no shawl, sneezes: a candle goes out.
- **New words:** lluvia, llueve, hace frío, bonito, feo.
- **Back again:** cielo, nube, viento, ¿qué tiempo hace?, hace calor, sol, viene, ahora, mañana, día, abuela, mesa, come, comida, caliente, frío, tengo, gracias, no, sí, mira, grande, negro.

**Start:** evening. A grey sky; one strip of orange at the sea's line. Abuela in her rocking chair with a shawl folded on the chair's arm (not on her). Luis and Carmen at the table; the frog on the porch rail, beside the awning's cord. The radio on the table.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen looks up. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira el cielo. Hay nubes grandes. ¡Qué feo!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. El cielo está feo." She turns the radio on. NARR: "La radio dice: ¿Qué tiempo hace? Viene lluvia. Mucha lluvia." A first drop lands on the table. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Llueve!" Ask (signed, the awning's cord): "Llueve. ¿Qué necesita la mesa?" He touches it: the frog pulls the cord and the awning rolls down over the table. The rain drums on it; the candles burn.
2. The rain stops as fast as it came; Luis rolls the awning up again. A cold gust: abuela rubs her arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Uy! Ahora hace frío. Tengo frío." Ask (signed, the shawl): "Hace frío. ¿Qué necesita abuela?" He touches it: the frog drags the shawl up over her shoulders. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias, Coquí."
3. The music. NARR: "La radio dice: Ahora llueve. Y hace frío. Un día feo." NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y mañana?" NARR: "La radio dice: Mañana, sol. ¡Un día bonito!" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Bonito! ¿Vamos a la playa mañana, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: vamos (vamos, sí vamos)]: "¡Sí, vamos!"

**The turn**

4. Abuela hangs her shawl back on the chair's arm to serve the supper. The awning is up; the candles burn; the sky is black at one end. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A comer! Coquí, mira el cielo." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The radio says what is coming: rain, or cold (drawn, each round on its own). The frog makes ready: the awning's cord, or the shawl. Nothing shows it yet. Before round B the awning is up again and the shawl is back on the chair's arm.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing (the cord, the shawl; tests llueve against hace frío).** NARR: "La radio dice: Ahora viene lluvia." (Or, drawn: "La radio dice: Ahora hace frío.")
   - Right: the awning comes down as the rain starts (or the shawl is on her shoulders as the cold gust comes). A star.
   - Wrong: the shawl goes on, the rain starts, and a drop hisses on a candle: out. (Or: the awning comes down, the cold gust comes, abuela sneezes: a candle goes out.) NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Llueve!" (or: "Abuela dice: ¡Achís! Hace frío.") A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing (both in every line).** NARR: "La radio dice: Hace frío. No llueve." (Or, drawn: "La radio dice: Llueve. No hace frío.") Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. The rain passes out to sea. They eat.
   - Three candles: the table glows; the strip of sky at the sea's line turns pink. Stars and a heart.
   - Two candles.
   - One candle: the four of them lean in round it.
8. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí. Ahora el cielo está bonito." COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta)]: "¡Me gusta!" NARR: "Hace frío. Pero la comida está caliente." **(pero: heard)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lluvia 2 and llueve 3 (steps 1, 3); hace frío 3 with "tengo frío" once more (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: feo 3 (steps 1, 3); bonito 2 before the game, and once in the ending (to fix: one more).
**Forms:** lluvia and llueve are two words of the list; a miss on either is filed under the one that was said.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (lluvia, llueve or hace frío).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): vamos; me gusta.

## Story 83: La luna

- **Goal he can see:** night. Luis has a telescope made of a cardboard tube, on a stand. Across the top of the sky, now and then, a small star falls with a tail of light: Carmen holds up three fingers. Three wishes.
- **At stake:** the three falling stars. Each falls close to something in the sky; if the telescope is turned to the wrong thing, the star is gone before it swings back.
- **New words:** luna, estrella, noche.
- **Back again:** cielo, nube, bonito, grande, pequeño, blanco, mira, ¿dónde está?, allá, arriba, hay, uno, dos, tres, quiero, duerme, cansado, hace frío, no, hasta mañana, abuela.

**Start:** night. A dark blue sky: a big moon at the left, one very bright star at the right, a long pale cloud in the middle, and small stars everywhere. Abuela in her rocking chair with her shawl. Luis at the telescope on the sand; Carmen beside him; the frog on the telescope's stand.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es de noche. El cielo está bonito." NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: la luna. Una luna grande y blanca." Ask (signed, the moon): "¿Dónde está la luna?" He touches it: the frog turns the telescope to it; a round picture opens beside the telescope: the moon, close, with its marks.
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y allá hay una estrella. ¡Una estrella grande!" Ask (signed, the bright star): "¿Dónde está la estrella grande?" He touches it: the telescope swings; in the round picture, the star with four points. NARR: "Luis dice: Hay una estrella, dos estrellas, tres estrellas... ¡Muchas estrellas!"
3. A falling star crosses beside the moon: Carmen gasps. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Una estrella! ¡Al lado de la luna!" Luis swings the telescope to the moon; in the round picture the falling star goes by, and Carmen shuts her eyes and wishes. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes frío?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Me gusta la noche. La luna, las estrellas..."

**The turn**

4. Luis gives the frog the telescope: the frog sits on it, to turn it. Carmen holds up two fingers: two more. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora tú, Coquí. ¡Rápido!" **(rápido is a word of El camino)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Somebody (drawn: abuela, Luis or Carmen) sees where the next falling star is coming and calls it: at the moon, at the big star, or at the cloud (drawn, each round on its own). The frog turns the telescope there. The falling star itself is not seen until the telescope is turned.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing in the sky (the moon, the big star, the cloud; tests the word).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, la luna!" (Drawn: "¡Coquí, la estrella grande!", "¡Coquí, la nube!")
   - Right: the telescope swings there; in the round picture the falling star goes by. Carmen wishes. A star.
   - Wrong: the telescope swings to another thing: nothing there. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No! ¡La luna!" By the time it swings back, the tail of light is fading. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the telescope turns there: only the moon.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing in the sky (the same game).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí, la estrella grande!" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Carmen opens her eyes.
   - Three wishes: from the dark all round, small voices answer the frog: co-quí, co-quí. The whole night sings. Stars and a heart.
   - Two wishes: one voice answers from the palm.
   - One wish: the frog sings alone, and abuela hums with him.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: Es tarde. A dormir, Coquí." **(a dormir: heard; "duerme" is a word of El banco)** COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!" NARR: "Es de noche. Hay luna y hay estrellas. Y Coquí duerme."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** luna 5 (steps 1, 3), estrella 7 with estrellas (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: noche 3 (steps 1, 3).
**Forms:** estrellas counts with estrella.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (luna or estrella; for the cloud, nube, a word of story 81).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): no; hasta mañana.

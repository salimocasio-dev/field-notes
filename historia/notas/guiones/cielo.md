# El cielo: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 7, La playa. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `cielo`). Stories 81 to 83. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 80.

Scene words (page 02, as changed on Nov 21): cielo, nube, lluvia, está lloviendo, viento, hace frío, luna, estrella, noche, bonito, feo, ¿cómo está el tiempo?
The plan had "¿qué tiempo hace?" and "llueve". On the island the question is "¿Cómo está el tiempo?", and of rain falling now people say "está lloviendo". The set on the table is "el radio". The everyday ones win (page 16).
How they are shared out: 81 cielo, nube, viento, ¿cómo está el tiempo?; 82 lluvia, está lloviendo, hace frío, bonito, feo; 83 luna, estrella, noche.

The picture: the porch of a little beach house, and a great deal of sky. One view. The sky fills the upper half: it changes with the story (blue; grey; night with a moon and stars). Under it the sea's line and the sand. In front, at the left, the porch: an open wooden deck with no roof over it (a roof would hide whoever sits there); two posts at its back carry a beam with a rolled-up blind, which comes down behind the table, on the sea's side, where the rain blows in from. On the deck a table with a radio on it and abuela's rocking chair. In front of the porch, on the sand, a blanket.

People: abuela, Luis, Carmen, Canelo. A voice: the man on the radio (he is only heard; a small bubble over the radio shows who is talking).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 81: ¿Cómo está el tiempo?

- **Goal he can see:** on the blanket, a tray with three tall glasses of juice. Abuela sets it down and wags a finger: careful. Round the blanket: a closed beach umbrella, Luis's kite, and a ball.
- **At stake:** the glasses of juice (3). Whatever is got ready for the wrong weather ends on the tray: the umbrella is blown over it by the wind, the kite falls on it when there is no wind, the ball is kicked into it by somebody who cannot see for the sun.
- **New words:** cielo, nube, viento, ¿cómo está el tiempo?
- **Back again:** hace calor, sol, hay, no hay, azul, grande, pequeño, mira, vuela, viene, sale, ahora, jugo, vaso, bola, juega, quiero, un poco, cuidado, abuela, tres, con, sombrilla (heard), chiringa (heard).

**Start:** afternoon. A blue sky with one small white cloud; the sun out. Abuela in her rocking chair on the porch beside the radio. Luis with his kite, Carmen with the ball, the frog on the blanket beside the tray and the closed umbrella.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela turns the radio on: a few notes of music. NARR: "El radio dice: ¿Cómo está el tiempo? Hay sol. El cielo está azul." Ask (signed, the sky): "¿Dónde está el cielo?" He touches it: a gull crosses it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes calor?" COQUÍ [TURN: un poco (un poco)]: "¡Un poco!"
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: Hay sol. ¡La sombrilla!" **(sombrilla: heard; the picture carries it)** Ask (signed, the umbrella): "Hay sol. ¿Dónde está la sombrilla?" He touches it: the frog pulls its cord and it opens over the blanket; the tray is in the shade.
3. The music again. NARR: "El radio dice: ¿Cómo está el tiempo ahora? Hay viento. Viene mucho viento." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Viento! ¡Cuidado con la sombrilla!" Carmen shuts it; only then the wind arrives and the palm's leaves stir. Luis holds up the kite. Ask (signed, the kite): "Hay viento. ¿Qué vuela con el viento?" He touches it: Luis throws the kite up, the wind takes it, and it climbs. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vuela! ¡Mira el cielo!"
4. The wind drops; the kite comes down. A big white cloud slides over the sun: the light goes flat and cool. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira: una nube. Una nube grande." NARR: "El radio dice: Ahora hay nubes. No hay sol." NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Nubes! No hace calor. ¡A jugar bola!" **(a jugar: heard)** Ask (signed, the ball): "Hay nubes. ¿Dónde está la bola?" He touches it: Carmen and Luis kick the ball to and fro on the sand.

**The turn**

5. Abuela holds up a hand. The umbrella is shut, the kite lies on the sand, the ball is still; the tray stands in the middle of them. The sky is half cloud, half blue: it could turn any way. Abuela pats the radio. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Cómo está el tiempo? Coquí, ¿qué dice el radio?" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The radio says what is coming: wind, sun, or clouds (drawn, each round on its own). The frog gets the right thing ready: the kite, the umbrella, or the ball. Nothing in the sky shows it yet; then it comes. Before round B the three things are back where they were.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the umbrella, the kite, the ball; tests the word).** NARR: "El radio dice: Viene viento." (Drawn: "El radio dice: Sale el sol.", "El radio dice: Vienen nubes.")
   - Right: the kite goes up as the wind arrives (the umbrella opens as the sun comes out hot; the ball game starts as the cloud slides over). A star.
   - Wrong: the weather that comes is not the one he got ready for. Luis and Carmen run for the right thing, trip over the thing he got ready, and it lands on the tray: a glass goes over. (The same for every pair of thing and weather: the open umbrella, the kite or the ball ends on the tray.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Hay viento." (or: "Hay sol.", "Hay nubes.") A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the kite flies.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game).** NARR: "El radio dice: Ahora vienen nubes." (Drawn: "Ahora sale el sol.", "Ahora viene viento.") Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The sun is low; the cloud has gone pink. Abuela comes down from the porch to the tray. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Quieres jugo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!"
   - Three glasses: one for Luis, one for Carmen, one for abuela, who gives the frog her straw. Stars and a heart.
   - Two glasses: Luis and Carmen; abuela and the frog share a straw.
   - One glass: four straws in one glass.
9. NARR: "Hay una nube en el cielo. Y Coquí toma jugo."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** viento 5 (step 3), nube 5 with nubes (step 4). Not deciding: cielo 4 (steps 1, 3); ¿cómo está el tiempo? 3 (steps 1, 3, 5).
**Forms:** nubes counts with nube.
**A miss is filed under:** the weather that was said (viento or nube; for the sun, sol, a word of El patio).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own. A learner who knows "sol" is still left with two new words and two things.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 8): un poco; quiero.

## Story 82: Está lloviendo

- **Goal he can see:** on the porch table, supper laid, and three candles burning in jars. Abuela lights the last one and shakes out the match. The sky behind is filling with grey cloud.
- **At stake:** the candles (3). Rain on a table with the blind up puts one out. Wind on a table with no glass cover over them blows one out. If abuela is cold and has no shawl, she takes a candle indoors with her to get warm.
- **New words:** lluvia, está lloviendo, hace frío, bonito, feo.
- **Back again:** cielo, nube, viento, ¿cómo está el tiempo?, hace calor, sol, viene, ahora, mañana, día, abuela, mesa, come, comida, caliente, frío, tengo, tengo hambre, gracias, no, sí, mira, grande, me gusta.

**Start:** evening. A grey sky; one strip of orange at the sea's line. Abuela in her rocking chair, a shawl folded on the chair's arm. Luis and Carmen at the table; the frog in the middle of the porch floor, as near the blind's cord as the shawl and the big glass cover that stands on the floor. The radio on the table.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Carmen looks up. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira el cielo. Hay nubes grandes. ¡Qué feo!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí. El cielo está feo." She turns the radio on. NARR: "El radio dice: ¿Cómo está el tiempo? Viene lluvia. Mucha lluvia." A first drop lands on the table. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Lluvia! ¡Está lloviendo!" Ask (signed, the blind's cord): "Está lloviendo. ¿Qué bajamos?" **(bajamos: a form of baja)** He touches it: the frog pulls the cord and the blind rolls down behind the table, between the rain and the candles. The rain drums on it; the candles burn.
2. The rain stops as fast as it came; Luis rolls the blind up again. NARR: "Luis dice: Ya no está lloviendo." A cold gust: abuela rubs her arms. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Uy! Ahora hace frío. Tengo frío." NARR: "Luis dice: Sí, hace frío." Ask (signed, the shawl): "Hace frío. ¿Qué necesita abuela?" He touches it: the frog drags the shawl up over her shoulders. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias, Coquí. Ya no tengo frío."
3. The candles' flames lean over: wind. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Viento!" Ask (signed, the glass cover): "Hay viento. ¿Qué ponemos en la mesa?" He touches it: the frog pushes the big glass cover up over the three candles; the flames stand straight. The music. NARR: "El radio dice: Hoy, lluvia, viento y frío. ¡Qué día feo! Mañana hay sol: un día bonito." A small sun shows in the radio's bubble. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Un día bonito! Me gusta."
4. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿tienes hambre?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre, sí tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí, tengo hambre!"

**The turn**

5. Abuela takes her shawl off to serve the supper, and hangs it on the chair's arm; Luis lifts the glass cover off, to light his plate; the blind is up. The candles burn; the sky is black at one end. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡A comer! Coquí, ¿qué dice el radio?" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The radio says what is coming: rain, cold, or wind (drawn, each round on its own). The frog makes ready: the blind's cord, the shawl, or the glass cover. Nothing shows it yet; then it comes. Before round B the three things are back as they were.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the cord, the shawl, the glass cover; tests the word).** NARR: "El radio dice: Viene lluvia." (Drawn: "El radio dice: Viene frío.", "El radio dice: Viene viento.")
   - Right: the blind is down as the rain starts (the shawl is on her as the cold comes; the cover is on as the wind comes). A star.
   - Wrong: what comes finds the table as it was. Rain: a drop hisses on a candle, and it is out. Wind: a flame is blown out. Cold: abuela shivers, takes a candle in its jar and goes indoors with it. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Está lloviendo!" (or: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Hace frío.", "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Hay viento.") A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game).** NARR: "El radio dice: Ahora viene frío." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The clouds pass out to sea, and the strip of sky at the sea's line turns pink. They eat.
   - Three candles: the table glows. Stars and a heart.
   - Two candles.
   - One candle: the four of them lean in round it.
9. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí. Ahora el cielo está bonito. ¿Te gusta?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" NARR: "La comida está caliente. Y mañana hay sol."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lluvia 4 (steps 1, 3), hace frío 3 (step 2), with "tengo frío" twice more, viento is a word of story 81 and is heard twice here. Not deciding: está lloviendo 3 (steps 1, 2); feo 3 (steps 1, 3); bonito 3 before the ending (step 3), and once in it.
**A miss is filed under:** the weather that was said (lluvia or hace frío; for the wind, viento).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own. "Frío" and "viento" are known words: a learner who knows both and not "lluvia" finds the rain by what is left. Said plainly: this story's game teaches lluvia by setting it among two he knows.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): tengo hambre; me gusta.

## Story 83: La luna

- **Goal he can see:** night. Luis has a telescope made of a cardboard tube, on a stand. Across the top of the sky, now and then, a small light falls with a tail: Carmen holds up three fingers. Three wishes.
- **At stake:** the three falling lights. Each falls close to the moon or to the big star; if the telescope is turned to the wrong one, the light is gone before it swings back.
- **New words:** luna, estrella, noche.
- **Back again:** cielo, nube, bonito, grande, blanco, mira, ¿dónde está?, allá, hay, uno, dos, tres, mucho, duerme, cansado, no, me gusta, hasta mañana, abuela, al lado de, tarde, luz (heard).

**Start:** night. A dark blue sky: a big white moon at the left; at the right one star far bigger and brighter than the rest, with four points; a long pale cloud between them; small stars everywhere. Abuela in her rocking chair with her shawl. Luis at the telescope on the sand; Carmen beside him; the frog on the telescope's stand.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es de noche. El cielo está bonito." NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: la luna. Una luna grande y blanca." Ask (signed, the moon): "¿Dónde está la luna?" He touches it: the frog turns the telescope to it; a round picture opens beside the telescope: the moon, close, with its marks.
2. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y allá hay una estrella. ¡Una estrella grande!" Ask (signed, the big star): "¿Dónde está la estrella grande?" He touches it: the telescope swings; in the round picture, the star with its four points. NARR: "Luis dice: Una estrella, dos estrellas, tres estrellas... ¡Muchas estrellas! ¡Qué noche bonita!"
3. A falling light crosses beside the moon: Carmen gasps. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Mira! ¡Una luz! ¡Al lado de la luna!" **(luz: heard)** Luis swings the telescope to the moon; in the round picture the light goes by, and Carmen shuts her eyes and wishes. The frog sings, once: co-quí. Far off, one small voice answers. NARR: "Abuela dice: Me gusta la noche. Coquí, ¿estás cansado?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!"

**The turn**

4. Luis gives the frog the telescope: the frog sits on it, to turn it. Carmen holds up two fingers: two more. Luis and Carmen and abuela watch the sky; nobody points. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora tú, Coquí. ¡Avanza!" **(avanza: heard)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Somebody (drawn: abuela, Luis or Carmen) sees where the next falling light is coming and calls it: at the moon, or at the big star (drawn, each round on its own). The frog turns the telescope there. Nobody points, and the light itself is not seen until the telescope is turned.

5. **Decision A, touch a thing in the sky (the moon, the big star; tests luna against estrella).** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, mira la luna!" (Or, drawn: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, mira la estrella grande!")
   - Right: the telescope swings there; in the round picture the falling light goes by. Carmen wishes. A star.
   - Wrong: the telescope swings to the other: nothing there. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡La luna!" By the time it swings back, the tail of light is fading. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it: the telescope turns there: only the moon.
6. **Decision B, touch a thing in the sky (the same game).** NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Coquí, mira la estrella grande!" (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

7. Carmen opens her eyes. The frog sings: co-quí.
   - Three wishes: from the dark all round, small voices answer him: co-quí, co-quí. The whole night sings. Stars and a heart.
   - Two wishes: two voices answer, from the palm and from the roof.
   - One wish: one voice answers, and abuela hums with them.
8. NARR: "Abuela dice: Es tarde. A dormir, Coquí." **(a dormir: heard; "duerme" is a word of El banco)** COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!" NARR: "Es de noche. Hay luna y hay estrellas. Y Coquí duerme."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** luna 4 (steps 1, 3), estrella 6 with estrellas (step 2). Not deciding: noche 3 (steps 1, 2, 3).
**Forms:** estrellas counts with estrella.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (luna or estrella).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own. Both words are new; the falling light is called "una luz", so "estrella" names only the stars.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): no; hasta mañana.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- All three: "¿Qué tiempo hace?" is the textbook's question: "¿Cómo está el tiempo?". "La radio" is the medium; the set is "el radio". "Hace sol", "hace viento" are "hay sol", "hay viento". Lines said by the radio of weather that had not come yet ("Ahora hace viento") were false when heard: it says what is coming ("Viene viento", "Sale el sol").
- 81: a new word set against one old word ("viento" or "sol") was settled by the old one: now three weathers and three things (wind and the kite, sun and the umbrella, clouds and the ball game), so knowing "sol" leaves two new words. "Viene sol" is not said, and the sun was already out: a cloud covers it before the game. The juice was handed out before abuela asked if he wanted any. "¿Qué necesita abuela?" for an umbrella that shades a tray; "La radio dice." as a sentence with nothing said: gone.
- 82: "¡Llueve!" for rain falling now is a book's word: "¡Está lloviendo!". "Lluvia" had been heard twice: four times. Rain and cold were one new word against one old one: wind is the third, with the glass cover. The sneeze that put a candle out was arbitrary: cold makes abuela take a candle indoors. She was cold, took her shawl off, and the last line still said "hace frío": the cold passes, and the last line is about tomorrow's sun. "Un día feo" was said after the rain had stopped, at night: the radio sums up the day. The pink sky came only with three candles while "el cielo está bonito" was said in every ending: it is pink in all three. The frog sat beside the cord: he starts in the middle. "¡Me gusta!" answered nothing: Carmen asks. Three radio lines with nothing moving: the flames lean, the bubble shows a sun.
- 83: three things to choose from, one of them an old word (nube): now the moon against the big star. "¡Una estrella!" for the falling light sent the telescope to the moon while "la estrella grande" sent it to the star: the falling one is "una luz". The calls were bare labels: "¡Coquí, mira la luna!". Nobody may point. "¿Tienes frío?" in the middle of a wish: "¿estás cansado?", which leads to the last line. "Noche" had been heard twice: three times. The frog's song came from nowhere in the ending: he sings once in the opening, and one voice answers.

Left, and why:
- 82's game is one new word among two he knows. The other new words of the story (está lloviendo, bonito, feo) are heard three times each and decide nothing. A game on bonito against feo was tried on paper and had no cost that follows from the move.
- "Hace frío" stays ("hace calor" is the course's phrase since La casa); the radio's "viene frío" is the forecast's way of saying it.
- "Una nube pequeña": the island's everyday word is "chiquita"; pequeño is the course's word.
- "¡Sí, quiero!" to "¿Quieres jugo?": "¡Sí, gracias!" is more usual from a child; "quiero" is the turn's word here, and "gracias" is the turn of other stories.

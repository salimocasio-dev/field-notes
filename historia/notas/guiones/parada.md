# La parada: three stories (draft 2, after the two reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `parada`). Stories 51 to 53. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 50.

Scene words (page 02): parada, voy, vas, se monta, se baja, llega, se va, cerca, lejos, temprano, tarde, ¿para dónde vas?

The picture (as the set `parada-set.html` draws it): a bus stop on a street of small houses. One view. Across the top, a row of house fronts and their pavement. Then the street, two lanes: whatever drives goes from right to left, so a guagua's lit side faces us. At the bottom, the stop's wide pavement: the stop's sign on a pole at a yellow kerb (a picture of a guagua), a seat with a back (no roof over it: it would hide the guagua's doors), a street lamp across the street, and at the right a post that shows a walking person, where a footpath leaves. A guagua that stops here stands side on: a door near its front with an arrow pointing in beside it, a door near its back with an arrow pointing out, a round button beside each door at frog height, a row of dark windows, and on its roof a plate with a picture of where it goes (a tree: the park; an awning: the colmado; a house: casa). Two guaguas can stand at the stop, one behind the other. The hour shows in the light and the shadows (and, early, a low sun in the corner of the sky); the sun of story 53 is on the card.

New person: the chofer (the driver: a cap, seen at his window and at the door). The others are the family, the señora and the señor, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 51: ¿Para dónde vas?

- **Goal he can see:** a strip of four guagua tickets, in abuela's hand from the first frame and on the card. Every ride tears one off.
- **At stake:** the tickets (4; two rides need two). Getting on the wrong guagua tears one off for nothing. What is left decides how they go home.
- **New words:** parada, ¿para dónde vas?, voy, vas.
- **Back again:** guagua, viene, va, parque, colmado, casa, yo, tú, ¿cuál?, señora, abuela, gracias, toma, no, cansado, bien, adiós, hasta mañana, después, ahora, vamos.

**Start:** morning. Abuela (the ticket strip in her hand), Luis (with his ball), Carmen (with a basket) and the frog walk up to the stop; the frog bounces as he goes. The señora sits on the bench.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela, Luis, Carmen y Coquí están en la parada." The stop's sign twinkles. Ask (signed, the sign and shelter): "¿Dónde está la parada?" He touches it: the frog bounces on the spot; the señora moves along the bench and pats the seat. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días! ¿Estás cansado, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "No. Estoy bien."
2. Two guaguas roll in and stop, one behind the other: one with the tree, one with the awning. NARR: "La guagua viene a la parada." NARR: "La señora dice: Luis, ¿para dónde vas?" Luis holds up his ball. NARR: "Luis dice: Voy al parque." Ask (signed, the guagua with the tree): "¿Cuál es la guagua de Luis?" He touches it: its door opens. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¿Para dónde vas?" NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Al parque!" The chofer tears a ticket off Luis's strip; Luis gets on.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen, tú vas al colmado." Carmen lifts her basket. NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. Yo voy al colmado." Ask (signed, the guagua with the awning): "¿Cuál es la guagua de Carmen?" He touches it: a ticket, she gets on. Both guaguas drive away.
4. The señora gets up; a guagua with the house stops for her. NARR: "La señora dice: Yo voy a casa. ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Para dónde vas?" The frog looks up at abuela. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, hoy tú vas en la guagua." The señora rides away.

**The turn**

5. Abuela puts the strip of four tickets in the frog's hand. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, abuela!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two guaguas come and stand at the stop, each with its picture, drawn from the tree, the awning and the house; which stands in front is drawn. Abuela is going to one place and sends the frog to the other. Which, and which half of her line comes first, is drawn, each round on its own. Neither chofer leans out; nobody asks anything: her line is the cue.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two guaguas; tests vas).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo voy al colmado. Tú vas al parque." (Or, drawn: "Abuela dice: Tú vas al parque. Yo voy al colmado.")
   - Right: the frog hops onto his guagua; its chofer tears off a ticket. Abuela gets onto the other. Both drive away. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hops on behind abuela; the chofer tears off a ticket. Abuela shakes her head and points at the other guagua. A cross: a ticket gone for nothing. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Yo voy al colmado. Tú vas al parque." The sign is on the right guagua; he touches it: he hops across, a ticket, both drive away.
   - Then, both ways: the street is empty for a moment; the sun is a little higher. NARR: "Y después..." The two guaguas come back; abuela gets off one with her bag, the frog off the other.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; two guaguas drawn afresh).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora yo voy a casa. Tú vas al colmado." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A. They come back as in A; the sun is low. The señora is on the bench again.

**The ending**

8. The guagua with the house comes. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora vamos a casa." The frog looks at his strip.
   - Two tickets left: the frog gives one to the chofer for abuela and one for himself; they sit at the front window and wave. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!" Stars and a heart.
   - One left: the frog gives it to the chofer; abuela gets on and the frog rides on her lap at the window. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!"
   - None left: the guagua drives off without them; abuela takes the frog's hand and they walk off along the pavement, the frog hopping. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!"
9. COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós!" NARR: "Abuela y Coquí van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** vas 5 (three times inside "¿para dónde vas?", twice as "tú vas", steps 2 to 4); voy 3, from three people in three sentences (steps 2, 3, 4). Not deciding: parada 3 (steps 1, 2); ¿para dónde vas? 3.
**A miss is filed under:** vas.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both places are named, both guaguas stand at the stop. A learner who knows yo and tú (story 8) and not voy and vas is right: the pair "yo voy, tú vas" is what the round drills.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 5, 9): no; gracias; adiós.

## Story 52: Se monta y se baja

- **Goal he can see:** abuela sits at the guagua's open front window with a basket of three warm rolls on the sill, for the picnic. Under the stop's bench Canelo lies with his nose up, sniffing.
- **At stake:** the rolls (3). A wrong door lets Canelo in: he comes out with a roll.
- **New words:** se monta, se baja, llega, se va.
- **Back again:** guagua, parada, gente, ahora, después, va, pan, huele, rico, tengo hambre, ¿qué quieres?, abuela, señor, sí, parque, gracias.

**Start:** morning. The stop. The frog, Luis and abuela stand at the kerb; abuela has her basket. Canelo under the bench.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. A guagua with the awning pulls in. NARR: "Mira. Llega una guagua." Carmen knocks on the glass of its back door from inside, her basket full; the button beside that door twinkles, and the arrow beside it points out. NARR: "Carmen se baja de la guagua." Ask (signed, the back door): "¿Por dónde se baja Carmen?" He touches it: the frog presses the button, the door opens, Carmen gets off. The guagua drives away. NARR: "La guagua se va."
2. The guagua with the tree pulls in. NARR: "Ahora llega otra guagua. Va al parque." The button by its front door twinkles; the arrow beside it points in. NARR: "Abuela se monta en la guagua." Ask (signed, the front door): "¿Por dónde se monta abuela?" He touches it: the door opens, abuela gets on, sits at the front window and stands her basket on the sill. Steam rises from the rolls. Canelo lifts his head. NARR: "El pan huele rico. Canelo tiene hambre."
3. Somebody knocks at the back door from inside: the señor. NARR: "El señor se baja de la guagua." Ask (signed, the back door): "¿Por dónde se baja el señor?" He touches it: the door opens, the señor gets off. The frog bounces, looking at the guagua. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias! ¿Vas al parque, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!"
4. NARR: "Ahora se monta Luis." Ask (signed, the front door): "¿Por dónde se monta Luis?" He touches it: Luis gets on.

**The turn**

5. The chofer leans out of his window and gives the frog a thumb up. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Muy bien, Coquí! Ahora tú." Behind, a guagua with the house pulls in and stops. NARR: "Llega otra guagua." People walk up to the kerb at abuela's guagua; inside it, people come to the back door's glass. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Both rounds are at abuela's guagua. People wait at its kerb to get on, and people wait inside at its back door to get off: both groups are there in every round. The chofer says who goes now. Drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two doors; tests the word said).** NARR: "El chofer dice: Ahora se baja la gente." (Or, drawn: "El chofer dice: Ahora se monta la gente.")
   - Right: the frog presses that door's button. The people inside get off (or the people at the kerb get on). A star. Then the other door, by itself.
   - Wrong: the frog presses the other button; that door opens. The chofer whistles. Canelo shoots in through the open door, along the aisle to abuela's window, and out again with a roll in his mouth; he eats it under the bench. A cross: one roll gone. NARR: "El chofer dice: No, Coquí. Ahora se baja la gente." (Or: "El chofer dice: No, Coquí. Ahora se monta la gente.") The sign is on the right door; he touches it: the people get off; then the other door, by itself.
   - Then, both ways: the guagua with the house drives away. NARR: "La guagua se va." New people walk up to the kerb; others come to the back door's glass.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game, drawn afresh).** Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Coquí! ¡Móntate!" The frog hops on at the front door and sits beside abuela and Luis at the window. Abuela lifts the cloth from the basket. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Qué quieres, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: pan (pan)]: "¡Pan, por favor!"
   - Three rolls left: one each for abuela, Luis and the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: Luis and the frog get one each; abuela smiles.
   - One left: abuela breaks it in two for Luis and the frog.
9. NARR: "La guagua se va de la parada." It drives away; Canelo runs after it, wagging. NARR: "Y después llega al parque."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** se monta 4 (steps 2 and 4), se baja 4 (steps 1 and 3). Not deciding: llega 3 before the game (steps 1, 2, 5) and 4 by the end; se va 1 before the game and 3 by the end.
**Forms:** móntate counts as se monta.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (se monta or se baja).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both groups wait, both doors are shut.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): sí; pan.

## Story 53: Cerca y lejos

- **Goal he can see:** the sun, low and yellow, on the card and in the sky; and Luis and Carmen's ball left on the stop's bench for the game after the errands.
- **At stake:** the day (3 steps of the sun: morning, late afternoon, evening). Walking to a place that is far, or waiting for a guagua to a place that is near, loses a step.
- **New words:** cerca, lejos, temprano, tarde.
- **Back again:** parada, guagua, llega, voy, vas, casa, colmado, parque, señor, caja, otro, toma, juega, cansado, bien, no, camina, vamos, espera, aquí, mañana.

**Start:** early morning: a low sun, long shadows. Abuela and the frog at the stop with two small boxes tied with string. The ball lies on the bench.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es temprano. Abuela y Coquí están en la parada." The sun twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos temprano. No quiero llegar tarde." She holds up the two boxes. NARR: "Abuela tiene dos cajas. Una es para don Pepe. La otra es para la señora."
2. Abuela points along the path. NARR: "Abuela dice: El colmado está cerca." Ask (signed, the walking post): "¿Por dónde camina Coquí?" He touches it: the frog hops off along the path and is back at once. NARR: "Está cerca. Coquí camina."
3. Abuela points far up the street. NARR: "Abuela dice: El parque está lejos." Ask (signed, the stop's sign): "¿Dónde está la parada?" He touches it: the frog hops to the sign; a guagua with the tree stops; he gets on and waves from a window; it drives off; another brings him back. NARR: "Está lejos. Coquí va en guagua."
4. The señor comes along the path, wiping his face, and sits down on the bench. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Ay! El colmado está cerca. Pero el parque está lejos. Y hoy la guagua llega tarde."

**The turn**

5. Abuela sits down on the bench beside the ball and holds the first box out to the frog. NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo espero aquí. Coquí, ¿vas tú?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "Sí. Yo voy." The card shows two places and the sun. The signs go.

**The game.** Each box goes to a house: don Pepe's, then the señora's. Neither of them is in the picture. Abuela says whether the house is near or far; that is drawn, each round on its own. The frog walks (the post) or rides (the stop's sign).

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the walking post, the stop's sign; tests the word said).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Esto es para don Pepe. Su casa está lejos." (Or, drawn: "Su casa está cerca.")
   - Right (far, the stop's sign): a guagua comes at once; the frog rides off and is soon back without the box. (Near, the post: he hops off along the path and is soon back without it.) A star.
   - Wrong (far, but he walks): the frog hops off along the path. The sun moves a step. He comes back with the box still in his hands, tongue out. A cross. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Su casa está lejos." The sign is on the stop's sign; he touches it: he rides off, and is back without the box.
   - Wrong (near, but he waits for the guagua): the frog stands at the kerb with the box. No guagua. His shadow grows; he droops. The sun moves a step. A cross. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Su casa está cerca." The sign is on the post; he touches it: he walks, and is back without the box.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the second box).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y esto es para la señora. Su casa está cerca." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog comes back with empty hands. Luis and Carmen run up.
   - No step lost: the sun is still low and yellow. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bien! Es temprano. No es tarde." Luis, Carmen and the frog play with the ball on the pavement; abuela claps. Stars and a heart.
   - One lost: late afternoon, the sun going down. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ya es tarde. Juega un poco, Coquí, y a casa." One throw to the frog, who catches it.
   - Two lost: evening, an orange sky, the street lamp lit. NARR: "Abuela dice: Es muy tarde. Mañana jugamos." Luis carries the ball under his arm; the frog yawns.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Estás cansado, Coquí?" With no step or one step lost: COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "No. Estoy bien." With two lost: COQUÍ [TURN: cansado (cansado)]: "Sí. Estoy cansado." NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a casa. Está cerca."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cerca 3 (step 2, twice; step 4), lejos 3 (step 3, twice; step 4). Not deciding: temprano 2 before the game and 3 on a clean run; tarde 2 before the game and 3 on a clean run.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (cerca or lejos).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: nobody has said where don Pepe or the señora lives, and neither is in the picture.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 9): voy; then no or cansado, by the ending. (voy is a word of story 51.)

## Reviews (Nov 20)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 51: "Voy al colmado. Vas al parque." with no yo and tú "sounds like a telegram": abuela now says "Yo voy... Tú vas...". "La guagua viene a la parada" had no guagua in the picture. Luis said the same sentence twice in three lines; voy now comes from three people. The tickets are in the picture from the first frame. "Y vas solo" is cut. In the ending with no ticket left the chofer said goodbye after driving off: the señora says it. The chofer no longer leans out of one guagua only.
- 52: the game moved to a second guagua while abuela and her rolls sat in the first: both rounds are at hers, with new people each time. "La guagua del parque llega" means it comes from the park: "Va al parque". "La señora baja" also means "the short lady", and another señora sat on the bench: Carmen gets off instead. "Toca la puerta" means knock: the asks are "¿Por dónde sube...?" and "¿Por dónde baja...?". Sale was heard twice: three now, each as a guagua moves off. Llega has its three before the game. The last turn no longer flips the question's verb.
- 53: the señor said his house was far and then the game could say it was near: the boxes go to don Pepe and the señora, who are not in the picture, and the señor only complains about the guagua. "Cerca: Coquí camina." was a label, and by ear "Cerca Coquí camina" means "walks nearby". The frog is now seen riding the guagua when the line says he does. The turn that used the new word cerca is gone. "Caja" and "casa" two seconds apart in the costed line: the line says "Esto". "Un poco de bola" is not said. "Ya es tarde" under a high sun: the sun is going down. The last turn answers what the picture shows (not tired after a clean run).

Left, and why:
- (Changed on Nov 21, on Salim's word "pick whatever is most used": the plan's sube, baja and sale are now se monta, se baja and se va; "¿adónde vas?" is "¿para dónde vas?"; "luego" is "después".)
- 51's round is decided by yo and tú as much as by voy and vas. Without the pronouns the Spanish is wrong; with a name in place of tú the name gives it away.
- "Sí. Yo voy." answers "¿vas tú?" with the verb turned round. That is how a child answers, and it makes him say the form.
- Length: the beginner reader guesses each story is over 85 s. The openings are shorter; the real number comes from machine play.

## Needs (for the scene's designer and the code lane)

The set `parada`:
- View: `wide` (zoom 1).
- States: `hora` (temprano: a low yellow sun and long shadows; manana; tarde: the sun going down; noche: an orange sky and the street lamp lit).
- In the set: the pavement; the shelter with its roof and bench; the pole with the stop's sign; the street; the path along the pavement with the walking-person post; a street lamp. The guaguas, the people, the dog and everything that moves are sprites.
- Things he can touch: parada (the sign and shelter; in story 53 the way to ride), guagua_a, guagua_b (the one in front, the one behind), puerta_sube, puerta_baja (the front and back door of the guagua at the stop, each with its button), poste (the walking post), sol, bola, each person (abuela, luis, carmen, senora, senor, chofer, coqui), perro.
- Spots: bordillo (the kerb: abuela, the frog), banco1 to banco3 (on the bench), bajo_banco (Canelo), cola (people at the kerb), parada1, parada2 (where the front and the second guagua stand), camino (where the path leaves the picture), ventana1 to ventana3 (the three front windows of the guagua at parada1: abuela, Luis, the frog).
- New sprites: the guagua side on, with its picture board (tree, awning, house), front door open or shut, back door open or shut, people at its windows and at the back door's glass, the frog waving at a window; the chofer (at his window, leaning out, whistling, thumb up); the strip of tickets (4 to 0), in abuela's hand and in the frog's, and one ticket torn off; the basket of rolls on the sill (3 to 0, steaming) with its cloth; Canelo (lying, head up, running in and along the aisle and out with a roll, eating, running after the guagua); two small tied boxes; the sun at three heights; the frog bouncing, drooping with a long shadow, yawning, tongue out.
- Speaker keys: `chofer`; the others exist.

Acts (name: what it does on screen; story and step):
- `guagua` {k, que, a}: a guagua with picture k comes and stops at parada1 or parada2 (`llega`), drives away (`sale`), or drives by (`pasa`) (51 to 53).
- `boleto` {quien}: the chofer tears a ticket off somebody's strip; `boletos` {n, quien}: the strip, in abuela's hand or the frog's (51).
- `sube` {quien, k}, `baja` {quien, k}: somebody gets on at the front door, or off at the back door, of a guagua (51, 52). `vuelve51`: the two guaguas come back; abuela and the frog get off (51: 6, 7).
- `final51` {n}: the ending for two, one or no tickets left (51: 8).
- `puerta` {cual, abre}: the frog presses a door's button; the door opens or shuts (52). `llama52` {quien}: somebody knocks on the back door's glass from inside (52: 1, 3). `gente52` {que}: `viene` (people walk up to the kerb and others come to the back door's glass), `sube` (the people at the kerb get on), `baja` (the people inside get off) (52: 5 to 7). `perro52`: Canelo runs in at the open door, along the aisle and out with a roll, and eats it under the bench (52: 6, 7). `panes` {n}: the rolls on the sill. `final52` {n} (52: 8).
- `camina53` {ok}, `monta53` {ok}: the frog walks off along the path, or rides off waving from a window, and comes back: soon and without the box, or late and with it (53: 2, 3, 6, 7). `espera53`: he waits at the kerb, no guagua comes, his shadow grows (53: 6, 7). `sol` {n}: the sun's step (53). `final53` {n} (53: 8).
- `gesto` {quien, que}: `saluda`, `no`, `senala` with `a`, `mira` with `a`, `sienta` (sits down on the bench), `pulgar` (the chofer's thumb up), `silba`, `bosteza` (the frog yawns), `lengua` (the frog, tongue out, tired), `salta` (the frog bounces on the spot).
- `brilla`: the engine's own twinkle (the stop's sign, the door buttons, the walking post, the sun).
- `ya`: waits for an act started with `suelto: 1`.
- Values: in 51 `a`, `b` the pictures of the two guaguas (arbol, toldo, casa), `frente` which stands in front, `mio` the frog's; `primero` which half of abuela's line is said first. In 52 `dice` sube or baja (which door; the words said are se monta, se baja). In 53 `dice` cerca or lejos, `para` pepe or senora.

# La parada: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 5, La calle. Scene 2 of 4 (scene id `parada`). Stories 51 to 53. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 50.

Scene words (page 02): parada, voy, vas, sube, baja, llega, sale, cerca, lejos, temprano, tarde, ¿adónde vas?

The picture: a bus stop on the street. One view. A shelter with a roof and a bench; beside it a pole with the stop's sign (a picture of a guagua). The street runs across in front. A guagua that stops here stands side on: a door at the front with an arrow pointing in painted beside it, a door at the back with an arrow pointing out, a round button beside each door at frog height, a row of windows, and on its front a board with a picture of where it goes (a tree: the park; an awning: the colmado; a house: casa). Two guaguas can stand at the stop, one behind the other. A path leaves along the pavement at the right, with a post that shows a walking person. The sky shows the hour.

New person: the chofer (the driver: a cap, seen at his window and at the door). The others are the family, the señora and the señor, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes, answer bubbles and the corner card are as in El colmado. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`.

## Story 51: ¿Adónde vas?

- **Goal he can see:** a strip of four guagua tickets in the frog's hand (and on the card). Every ride tears one off.
- **At stake:** the tickets (4; two rides need two). Getting on the wrong guagua tears one off for nothing. What is left decides how they go home.
- **New words:** parada, ¿adónde vas?, voy, vas.
- **Back again:** guagua, viene, va, parque, colmado, casa, bola, señora, abuela, gracias, toma, no, cansado, bien, adiós, hasta mañana, luego.

**Start:** morning. Abuela, Luis (with his ball), Carmen (with a basket) and the frog walk up to the stop. The señora sits on the bench.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela, Luis, Carmen y Coquí están en la parada." The stop's sign twinkles. NARR: "La guagua viene a la parada." Ask (signed, the sign and shelter): "¿Dónde está la parada?" He touches it: the señora moves along the bench and pats the seat. NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días! ¿Estás cansado, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "No. Estoy bien."
2. NARR: "La señora dice: Luis, ¿adónde vas?" Luis holds up his ball. NARR: "Luis dice: Voy al parque." The guagua with the tree comes and stops. Its door opens. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¿Adónde vas?" NARR: "Luis dice: Voy al parque." Ask (signed, that guagua): "Toca la guagua de Luis." He touches it: the chofer tears a ticket off Luis's strip, Luis gets on, the guagua drives away.
3. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen, vas al colmado." Carmen lifts her basket. NARR: "Carmen dice: Sí. Voy al colmado." The guagua with the awning comes. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¿Adónde vas?" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Al colmado!" Ask (signed, that guagua): "¿Dónde está la guagua de Carmen?" He touches it: a ticket, she gets on, it drives away.
4. The señora looks at the frog. NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Adónde vas?" The frog looks up at abuela. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, hoy vas en la guagua. Y vas solo." **(solo: heard; the next beat shows it)**

**The turn**

5. Abuela takes a strip of four tickets from her bag and puts it in the frog's hand; the card shows it. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias, abuela!" The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two guaguas come and stand at the stop, each with its picture, drawn from the tree, the awning and the house; which stands in front is drawn. Abuela is going to one place and sends the frog to the other. Which, and which half of her line comes first, is drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two guaguas; tests vas).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Voy al colmado. Vas al parque." (Or, drawn: "Vas al parque. Voy al colmado.") The chofer of the front guagua leans out. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¿Adónde vas?"
   - Right: the frog hops onto his guagua; its chofer tears off a ticket. Abuela gets onto the other. Both drive away. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hops on behind abuela; the chofer tears off a ticket. Abuela shakes her head and points at the other guagua. A cross: a ticket gone for nothing. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Yo voy al colmado. Tú vas al parque." The sign is on the right guagua; he touches it: he hops across, a ticket, both drive away.
   - Then, both ways: the street is empty for a moment; the sun is a little higher. NARR: "Luego..." The two guaguas come back; abuela gets off one with her bag, the frog off the other.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; two guaguas drawn afresh).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora voy a casa. Vas al colmado." (Drawn.) NARR: "El chofer dice: ¿Adónde vas?" Right and wrong as in A. They come back as in A; the sun is low.

**The ending**

8. The guagua with the house comes. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora vamos a casa." The frog looks at his strip.
   - Two tickets left: the frog gives one to the chofer for abuela and one for himself; they sit at the front window and wave. Stars and a heart.
   - One left: the frog gives it to the chofer; abuela gets on and the frog rides on her lap at the window.
   - None left: the guagua drives off without them; abuela takes the frog's hand and they walk home along the pavement, the frog hopping.
9. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: adiós (adiós, adios)]: "¡Adiós!" NARR: "Abuela y Coquí van a casa."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** vas 3 as a plain word (steps 3 and 4) and 5 inside "¿adónde vas?"; voy 4 (steps 2 and 3). Not deciding: parada 3 (step 1); ¿adónde vas? 5 before the game.
**A miss is filed under:** vas.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both places are named, both guaguas stand at the stop, and only voy and vas say which is whose. A learner who takes the first place named is right half the time.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 5, 9): no; gracias; adiós.

## Story 52: Sube y baja

- **Goal he can see:** abuela sits at the guagua's open front window with a basket of three warm rolls on the sill, for the picnic. Under the stop's bench Canelo lies with his nose up, sniffing.
- **At stake:** the rolls (3). A wrong door lets Canelo in: he comes out with a roll.
- **New words:** sube, baja, llega, sale.
- **Back again:** guagua, parada, gente, ahora, luego, espera, pan, caliente, huele, perro, abuela, señor, señora, tengo hambre, sí, parque.

**Start:** morning. The stop. People on the bench (the señora, Carmen, a man). The frog, Luis and abuela stand at the kerb; abuela has her basket. Canelo under the bench.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. A guagua with the awning comes and stops. NARR: "Mira. Llega una guagua." Its doors open and shut. NARR: "Y la guagua sale." It drives away. Then the guagua with the tree comes. NARR: "Ahora llega la guagua del parque."
2. The button by the front door twinkles; the arrow beside it points in. NARR: "Abuela sube a la guagua." Ask (signed, the front door): "Toca la puerta. Abuela sube." He touches it: the frog presses the button, the door opens, abuela gets on, sits at the front window and stands her basket on the sill. Steam rises from the rolls. Canelo lifts his head. NARR: "El pan huele rico. Canelo tiene hambre."
3. Somebody knocks at the back door from inside; the arrow beside it points out. NARR: "El señor baja de la guagua." Ask (signed, the back door): "Toca la puerta. El señor baja." He touches it: the back door opens, the señor gets off. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Gracias! ¿Vas al parque, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!"
4. NARR: "Luis sube." Ask (signed, the front door): "¿Dónde sube Luis?" He touches it: Luis gets on. NARR: "La señora baja." Ask (signed, the back door): "¿Dónde baja la señora?" He touches it: a señora with a bag gets off.

**The turn**

5. The chofer leans out of his window and gives the frog a thumb up. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Muy bien, Coquí! Ahora tú." People stand up from the bench and come to the kerb; inside, people stand at the back door's glass. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** People wait at the kerb to get on, and people wait inside at the back door to get off: both groups are there in every round. The chofer says who goes now. Drawn, each round on its own.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the two doors; tests the word said).** NARR: "El chofer dice: Ahora baja la gente." (Or, drawn: "Ahora sube la gente.")
   - Right: the frog presses that door's button. The people inside get off (or the people at the kerb get on). A star. Then the other door, by itself.
   - Wrong: the frog presses the other button; that door opens. The chofer whistles. Canelo shoots in through the open door, and out again with a roll in his mouth; he eats it under the bench. A cross: one roll gone. NARR: "El chofer dice: No, Coquí. Ahora baja la gente." The sign is on the right door; he touches it: the people get off; then the other door, by itself.
7. A second guagua with the tree pulls in behind (the first is full). **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game at the second guagua, drawn afresh).** Right and wrong as in A; Canelo takes his roll from abuela's sill by way of the door and the aisle.

**The ending**

8. NARR: "El chofer dice: ¡Coquí! ¡Sube!" The frog hops on at the front door and sits beside abuela and Luis at the window. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Tienes hambre, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí! Tengo hambre."
   - Three rolls left: one each for abuela, Luis and the frog. Stars and a heart.
   - Two left: Luis and the frog get one each; abuela smiles.
   - One left: abuela breaks it in two for Luis and the frog.
9. NARR: "La guagua sale de la parada." It drives away; Canelo runs after it, wagging. NARR: "Y luego llega al parque."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** sube 4 (steps 2 and 4), baja 4 (steps 3 and 4). Not deciding: llega 2 before the game and 3 by the end; sale 1 before the game and 2 by the end (a third in the game's own line, below).
**To fix in the reviews if they agree:** sale is heard only twice on a clean run. Each round ends with "La guagua sale." when the first guagua drives off, which makes four.
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (sube or baja).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: both groups wait, both doors are shut.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 8): sí; tengo hambre.

## Story 53: Cerca y lejos

- **Goal he can see:** the sun, low and yellow, on the card and in the sky; and Luis and Carmen's ball left on the stop's bench for the game after the errands.
- **At stake:** the day (3 steps of the sun: morning, afternoon, evening). Walking to a place that is far, or waiting for a guagua to a place that is near, loses a step.
- **New words:** cerca, lejos, temprano, tarde.
- **Back again:** parada, guagua, llega, sale, voy, vas, casa, señora, señor, caja, toma, gracias, bola, juega, cansado, sol, camina, vamos.

**Start:** early morning: a low sun, long shadows. Abuela and the frog at the stop with two small boxes tied with string. The post with the walking person twinkles once. The ball lies on the bench.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es temprano. Abuela y Coquí están en la parada." The sun twinkles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos temprano. No quiero llegar tarde." She holds up the two boxes. NARR: "Una caja es para la señora. Una caja es para el señor."
2. Abuela points along the path. NARR: "Abuela dice: El colmado está cerca." Ask (signed, the walking post): "El colmado está cerca. ¿Dónde camina Coquí?" He touches it: the frog hops off along the path and is back at once with a paper bag. NARR: "Cerca: Coquí camina."
3. Abuela points far up the street. NARR: "Abuela dice: El parque está lejos." Ask (signed, the guagua's sign): "El parque está lejos. ¿Dónde está la guagua?" He touches it: the guagua with the tree comes and goes by. NARR: "Lejos: Coquí va en la guagua."
4. The señor comes along the path, wiping his face. NARR: "El señor dice: ¡Uf! Mi casa está lejos. La guagua llega tarde." He sits down. NARR: "El señor dice: ¿Y tu casa, Coquí? ¿Está cerca?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "Sí. Está cerca."

**The turn**

5. Abuela sits down on the bench beside the ball and gives the frog the first box. NARR: "Abuela dice: Toma, Coquí. Tú vas. Yo espero aquí." COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Voy!" The card shows two places and the sun. The signs go.

**The game.** Each box goes to a house. Abuela says whose house and whether it is near or far; that is drawn, each round on its own. The frog walks (the post) or rides (the guagua's sign).

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the walking post, the guagua's sign; tests the word said).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Esta caja es para la señora. Su casa está lejos." (Or, drawn: "Su casa está cerca.")
   - Right (far, the guagua): a guagua comes at once; the frog rides off and is soon back without the box. (Near, the post: he hops off along the path and is soon back.) A star.
   - Wrong (far, but he walks): the frog hops off along the path. The sun climbs a step. He comes back with the box still in his hands, tongue out. A cross. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Su casa está lejos." The sign is on the guagua's sign; he touches it: he rides off, and is back without the box.
   - Wrong (near, but he waits for the guagua): the frog stands at the kerb. No guagua. The sun climbs a step. A cross. NARR: "Abuela dice: No, Coquí. Su casa está cerca." The sign is on the post; he touches it: he walks, and is back without the box.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the second box, for the señor).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Y esta caja es para el señor. Su casa está cerca." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The frog comes back with empty hands. Luis and Carmen run up.
   - No step lost: the sun is still low. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡Qué bien! Es temprano." Luis, Carmen and the frog play with the ball on the pavement; abuela claps. Stars and a heart.
   - One lost: afternoon, a high sun. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ya es tarde. Un poco de bola, y a casa." One throw to the frog, who catches it.
   - Two lost: evening, an orange sky, the street lamp lit. NARR: "Abuela dice: Es muy tarde. Mañana jugamos." Luis carries the ball under his arm; the frog yawns.
9. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Estás cansado, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: cansado (cansado)]: "Sí. Estoy cansado." NARR: "Abuela dice: Vamos a casa. Está cerca."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** cerca 4 (steps 2 and 4), lejos 4 (steps 3 and 4). Not deciding: temprano 2 before the game and 3 on a clean run; tarde 2 before the game.
**To fix in the reviews if they agree:** tarde is heard twice on a clean run (steps 1 and 4). The señora on the bench can say it once more in step 3 ("Hoy la guagua llega tarde.").
**A miss is filed under:** the word that was said (cerca or lejos).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: nobody knows where the señora or the señor lives.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 4, 5, 9): sí; voy; cansado. (voy is a word of story 51.)

## Needs (for the scene's designer and the code lane)

The set `parada`:
- View: `wide` (zoom 1).
- States: `hora` (temprano: a low yellow sun and long shadows; manana; tarde: a high sun; noche: an orange sky and the street lamp lit).
- In the set: the pavement; the shelter with its roof and bench; the pole with the stop's sign; the street; the path along the pavement with the walking-person post; a street lamp. The guaguas, the people, the dog and everything that moves are sprites.
- Things he can touch: parada (the sign and shelter), guagua_a, guagua_b (the one in front, the one behind), puerta_sube, puerta_baja (the front and back door of the guagua at the stop, each with its button), poste (the walking post), senal (the stop's sign, as the way to ride in story 53), sol, bola, each person (abuela, luis, carmen, senora, senor, chofer, coqui), perro.
- Spots: bordillo (the kerb: abuela, the frog), banco1 to banco3 (on the bench), bajo_banco (Canelo), cola (people at the kerb), parada1, parada2 (where the front and the second guagua stand), camino (where the path leaves the picture), ventana1 to ventana3 (the three front windows of the guagua at parada1: abuela, Luis, the frog).
- New sprites: the guagua side on, with its picture board (tree, awning, house), front door open or shut, back door open or shut, people at its windows and at the back door's glass; the chofer (at his window, leaning out, whistling, thumb up); the strip of tickets (4 to 0) and one ticket torn off; the basket of rolls on the sill (3 to 0, steaming); Canelo (lying, head up, running in and out with a roll, eating, running after the guagua); two small tied boxes; the sun at three heights.
- Speaker keys: `chofer`; the others exist.

Acts (name: what it does on screen; story and step):
- `guagua` {k, que, a}: a guagua with picture k comes and stops at parada1 or parada2 (`llega`), drives away (`sale`), or drives by (`pasa`) (51 to 53).
- `boleto` {quien}: the chofer tears a ticket off somebody's strip; `boletos` {n}: the frog's strip (51).
- `sube` {quien, k}, `baja` {quien, k}: somebody gets on at the front door, or off at the back door, of a guagua (51, 52). `vuelve51`: the two guaguas come back; abuela and the frog get off (51: 6, 7).
- `final51` {n}: the ending for two, one or no tickets left (51: 8).
- `puerta` {cual, abre}: the frog presses a door's button; the door opens or shuts (52). `gente52` {que}: the people at the kerb get on, or the people inside get off (52: 6, 7). `perro52`: Canelo runs in at the open door and out with a roll, and eats it under the bench (52: 6, 7). `panes` {n}: the rolls on the sill. `final52` {n} (52: 8).
- `camina53` {ok}, `monta53` {ok}: the frog walks off along the path, or rides off, and comes back: at once and without the box, or late and with it (53: 2, 3, 6, 7). `espera53`: he waits at the kerb and no guagua comes (53: 6, 7). `sol` {n}: the sun's step (53). `final53` {n} (53: 8).
- `gesto` {quien, que}: `saluda`, `no`, `senala` with `a`, `mira` with `a`, `sienta` (sits down on the bench), `pulgar` (the chofer's thumb up), `silba`, `bosteza` (the frog yawns), `lengua` (the frog, tongue out, tired).
- `brilla`: the engine's own twinkle (the stop's sign, the door buttons, the walking post, the sun).
- `ya`: waits for an act started with `suelto: 1`.
- Values: in 51 `a`, `b` the pictures of the two guaguas (arbol, toldo, casa), `frente` which stands in front, `mio` the frog's; `primero` which half of abuela's line is said first. In 52 `dice` sube or baja. In 53 `dice` cerca or lejos, `para` senora or senor.

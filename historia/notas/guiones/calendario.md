# El calendario: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `calendario`). Stories 69 to 71. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 68.

Scene words (page 02): lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo, hoy, mañana, día, semana, fin de semana.

The picture: the square's board of the week, close. One view. A long board on two posts under a little roof: seven columns side by side. Each column has a coloured square at its top (no writing: the first five green, the last two orange) and three hooks under it. Over the two orange columns, an arch of little paper flags with a hook at its middle. Along the board's foot a low step, so the frog can hop from column to column. At the right a seat for three, turned to the board. Behind: the bandstand's steps, trees. The week on this board starts with lunes, at the left.

What goes on the board: picture cards, one for each thing that happens in the square (a basket of fruit, a ball, the bingo cage, a bicycle, a guitar, a piragua, a cup of coffee, flowers, a loaf, a roast chicken, a glass of juice). A card hangs from a hook by a tack; a column can take three cards.

The square's week, the same in all three stories: lunes fruit, martes the children's ball game, miércoles bingo, jueves bicycles, viernes music, the fin de semana piraguas. The cards of the games (coffee, flowers, a loaf, a roast chicken, juice) have no day of their own.

People: the señora (the lady with the bingo cage in El reloj): the board is her work. The others: Luis, Carmen, Beto, abuela, don Tito, the señor, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh: only the cards its own start names are on the board. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 69: El lunes hay frutas

- **Goal he can see:** three balloons tied to the board's left post, bobbing. Beto stands under them, looking up.
- **At stake:** the balloons (3). A card hung under the wrong day has to come down: the señora pulls out its tack, the tack flies from her fingers, and a balloon bursts.
- **New words:** lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves.
- **Back again:** hay, fruta, bola, bicicleta, juega, corre, niños, voy, va, vienes, café, flor, pan, jugo, pone, buenos días, trabaja, ¿dónde está?, sí, ahora, más, tú.

**Start:** morning. The board is empty; a cloth hangs over its last three columns. The señora at its left end with a box of cards; the frog on the step. Three balloons on the post; Beto under them.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La señora trabaja en la plaza." NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenos días (buenos días, buenos dias)]: "¡Buenos días!" She points at the first four columns, one after another; each lights as she names it. NARR: "La señora dice: Lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves." Ask (signed, the first column): "¿Dónde está el lunes?" He touches it: it lights, and the frog hops under it.
2. She holds up the card with the basket of fruit. NARR: "La señora dice: El lunes hay frutas en la plaza." Ask (signed, the first column): "¿Dónde ponemos las frutas?" He touches it: the frog hangs the card there. She holds up the ball. NARR: "La señora dice: El martes los niños juegan bola. ¿Vienes el martes, Beto?" Beto nods hard. Ask (signed, the second column): "¿Y dónde ponemos la bola?" He touches it: the ball hangs under the second.
3. Abuela walks by with her bag. The señora holds up the bingo cage's card. NARR: "La señora dice: El miércoles hay bingo." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡El miércoles! Yo voy el miércoles." Ask (signed, the third column): "¿Dónde está el miércoles?" He touches it: the card hangs there. Luis rides up on his bicycle. NARR: "La señora dice: Y el jueves corren bicicleta." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡El jueves voy yo!" Ask (signed, the fourth column): "¿Dónde ponemos la bicicleta?" He touches it.
4. The señora steps back and looks at the four cards. NARR: "La señora dice: Lunes, martes, miércoles y jueves. ¡Muy bien!"

**The turn**

5. The señora tips the box: more cards (a cup of coffee, flowers, a loaf, a glass of juice). She hands the frog a tack. Beto pulls at the balloons' string; they bob. NARR: "La señora dice: Hay más. Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The señora holds up a card, drawn from the four in the box, and names its day: one of lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, drawn, each round on its own (round B's can be the same day again). The frog hangs it under a column (the first four; a column can take three cards).

6. **Decision A, touch a column (four columns; tests the day).** She holds up the cup of coffee. NARR: "La señora dice: El jueves hay café." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hangs it there; the column lights. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it under another day. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Hay café el jueves." She pulls out the tack; it flies from her fingers and a balloon bursts. Beto jumps. A cross. The sign is on the right column; he touches it: the card hangs there.
7. **Decision B, touch a column (the same game).** She holds up the flowers. NARR: "La señora dice: Y el martes hay flores." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The señora steps back and looks at the board. She unties the balloons.
   - Three balloons: one for Beto, one for Luis's bicycle, one for the frog, who goes up a little way with it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: one for Beto, one for the frog.
   - One: Beto has it, and the frog rides on Beto's cap, holding the string.
9. NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Vienes el lunes, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy, sí voy)]: "¡Sí, voy!" NARR: "El lunes hay frutas en la plaza. Y Coquí va."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lunes 4, martes 4, miércoles 6, jueves 4 (steps 1 to 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the day that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own. Nothing on the board says which column is which: only the order, lunes first.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): buenos días; voy.

## Story 70: El fin de semana

- **Goal he can see:** on the paving at the board's foot, a kite that Luis and Carmen have made, with a long tail of three paper bows. Canelo lies beside the tail with one eye open.
- **At stake:** the bows on the kite's tail (3). A wrong card, pulled off the board, flutters down onto the tail: Canelo jumps on it, and a bow comes off in his teeth.
- **New words:** viernes, sábado, domingo, fin de semana.
- **Back again:** lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, hay, no hay, me gusta, también, después, vamos, vienes, flor, pan, pollo, jugo, piragua, pone, vuela, mira, sí, no.

**Start:** afternoon. Under the first four columns hang their cards: fruit, the ball, the bingo cage, the bicycle. The last three columns are empty. The señora and the frog at the board. Luis and Carmen come from school with their school bags; their kite lies on the paving at the board's foot, Canelo beside it.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Carmen vienen de la escuela." The señora points along the board. NARR: "La señora dice: Lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves... y viernes." The fifth column lights. Carmen holds up her school bag. NARR: "Carmen dice: El viernes hay clases." **(clases: heard; the school bags carry it)** Ask (signed, the fifth column): "¿Dónde está el viernes?" He touches it: it lights.
2. The two orange columns light. NARR: "La señora dice: Y después, sábado y domingo." Luis throws his school bag into the air. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡El sábado no hay clases! ¡Y el domingo tampoco!" **(tampoco: heard)** Ask (signed, the sixth column): "¿Dónde está el sábado?" He touches it. Ask (signed, the seventh): "¿Y dónde está el domingo?" He touches it.
3. The señora sweeps her hand along the arch of flags over the two. NARR: "La señora dice: El sábado y el domingo son el fin de semana." The arch twinkles. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Me gusta el fin de semana!" NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y a ti, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (también, tambien, a mí también)]: "¡A mí también!"
4. The señora holds up the guitar. NARR: "La señora dice: El viernes hay música en la plaza." **(música: heard; the guitar carries it)** NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Música! Me gusta la música." Ask (signed, the fifth column): "¿Dónde ponemos la música?" He touches it: the frog hangs the card. She holds up the piragua. NARR: "La señora dice: Y el fin de semana hay piraguas." Ask (signed, the arch): "¿Dónde ponemos las piraguas?" He touches it: the frog hangs the card from the hook at the arch's middle, over both orange columns.

**The turn**

5. Luis lifts the kite and looks at the sky. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Mira la chiringa! El sábado la volamos." **(chiringa: heard; the picture carries it)** He lays it down again; Canelo puts a paw on the tail. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Canelo, no!" The señora tips out more cards. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The señora holds up a card, drawn (flowers, a loaf, a roast chicken, a glass of juice), and says when: viernes, sábado, domingo or el fin de semana, drawn, each round on its own. The frog hangs it under a column or on the arch. For "el fin de semana" the arch is right, and so are the sábado and the domingo columns (the card then slides up to the arch): all three are the fin de semana.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the seven columns and the arch; tests the day).** NARR: "La señora dice: El domingo hay flores." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hangs it there. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it somewhere else. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Hay flores el domingo." She pulls the card off; it flutters down onto the kite's tail. Canelo jumps on it, and a bow comes off in his teeth; he shakes it to bits. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Canelo, no!" A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the card hangs there.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game).** NARR: "La señora dice: Y el fin de semana hay pollo." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The light changes: it is the next morning, and the sixth column is lit. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Es sábado! ¡Vamos!" Luis runs with the kite; Carmen lets the tail go.
   - Three bows: the kite goes straight up over the board, its tail dancing; the frog rides up the string a little way and slides down. Stars and a heart.
   - Two bows: it flies, leaning.
   - One bow: it spins, and flies low over the seat; Canelo runs under it.
9. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¿vienes el domingo también?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" NARR: "El sábado y el domingo no hay clases. ¡Es el fin de semana!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** viernes 4, sábado 4, domingo 4, fin de semana 3 (steps 1 to 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the day that was said (or fin de semana).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in eight as the places go; one in four for a learner who sees that only the new places are in play (for el fin de semana, three in eight). The colours tell the fin de semana from the other days, not sábado from domingo, and not viernes from the four before it.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): también; sí.

## Story 71: Hoy y mañana

- **Goal he can see:** three people on the seat, turned to the board, each with an empty basket on his knees: abuela, don Tito and the señor. They have come for whatever the square has today.
- **At stake:** the people on the seat (3). A card under the wrong day: one of them reads it, gets up with his basket and looks round the square for a thing that is not there; he goes home with the basket empty.
- **New words:** hoy, mañana, día, semana.
- **Back again:** lunes to domingo, hay, no hay, otro, nuevo, todo, uno to siete, buenos días, adiós, hasta mañana, fruta, bola, juega, café, flor, pan, pollo, jugo, pone, tarde, ya, no.

**Start:** early morning. The board is empty: a new week. The señora at its left end with a peg shaped like a sun and the box of cards; the frog on the step. Abuela, don Tito and the señor on the seat, with their baskets.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es un nuevo día en la plaza. Y es una semana nueva." The señora hangs the sun on the first column's square. NARR: "La señora dice: Buenos días. Hoy es lunes." Ask (signed, the first column, with the sun): "¿Cuál es el día de hoy?" He touches it: the sun shines.
2. She points at the next column. NARR: "La señora dice: Y mañana es martes." Ask (signed, the second column): "¿Y cuál es el día de mañana?" He touches it: it lights.
3. She runs her finger along all seven; each lights in turn. NARR: "La señora dice: Un día, dos días, tres días, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete. Siete días son una semana." Ask (signed, the whole board): "Toca toda la semana." He touches it: all seven light at once.
4. She holds up the basket of fruit. NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy hay frutas." Ask (signed, the first column): "¿Dónde ponemos las frutas?" He touches it: the frog hangs the card under the sun. Abuela reads the board, gets up with her basket, and comes back with fruit in it. The señora holds up the ball. NARR: "La señora dice: Y mañana los niños juegan bola." Ask (signed, the second column): "¿Y dónde ponemos la bola?" He touches it: the ball hangs under the second column.
5. The picture goes dark and light again: a night. The señora moves the sun to the second column. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Qué día es hoy?" NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy es martes. Hoy juegan bola. Y mañana hay bingo." She hangs the bingo cage under the third. Abuela half gets up. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿hay bingo hoy?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy no. Mañana." Abuela sits down again.

**The turn**

6. The señora takes every card down: the board is empty again. She shakes the box. The three on the seat lean forward with their empty baskets. NARR: "La señora dice: Otra semana. Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The señora hangs the sun on a day and says which day it is: in round A a day from lunes to jueves, drawn; in round B the day after (one night passes). She holds up one card and says two things: what there is today and what there is tomorrow. The two cards are drawn from coffee, flowers, a loaf, a roast chicken and juice (none was on the board in the opening); which is said first is drawn; whether the card she holds is today's or tomorrow's is drawn. The frog hangs the card she holds. She hangs the other one herself afterwards.

7. **Decision A, touch a column (seven columns; tests hoy against mañana).** The sun goes on the third column. NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy es miércoles." She holds up the loaf. NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy hay flores. Mañana hay pan." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hangs it under the day after the sun (or, for today's card, under the sun). The three on the seat read it, and nod. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it under another day. One of the three reads the board, gets up with his basket, and walks round the square looking for the thing: nothing. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. Mañana hay pan." She takes the card down. The one who got up shakes his head and goes home, his basket empty. A cross. The sign is on the right column; he touches it: the card hangs there.
8. **Decision B, touch a column (the same game; the next day).** A night; the sun moves on one column. NARR: "La señora dice: Otro día. Hoy es jueves." She holds up the cup of coffee. NARR: "La señora dice: Mañana hay jugo. Hoy hay café." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A; her words after a wrong move: "No, Coquí. Hoy hay café." (the line of the card she held.)
   - Who goes home: the señor first, then don Tito. Abuela stays.

**The ending**

9. Evening: the lamps come on. The señora takes the sun down and puts it in her pocket.
   - Three on the seat: all three get up with something in their baskets, and each waves to the frog as he goes. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: abuela and don Tito wave.
   - One: abuela takes the frog on her shoulder.
10. NARR: "La señora dice: Ya es tarde, Coquí. Adiós." COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!" NARR: "Mañana es otro día en la plaza."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** hoy 9, mañana 6 (steps 1 to 5). Not deciding: día 7 with días; semana 3 (steps 1, 3), and it has been heard in "fin de semana".
**A miss is filed under:** the word that went with the card she held (hoy or mañana).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two for a learner who sees that the sun is today; one in seven as the columns go. Both things are named in every line; only hoy and mañana say which is which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): no; hasta mañana.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- All three: the asks were bare labels that said the line again ("Las frutas: el lunes. Toca el lunes."), and "toca el viernes" beside music is "it plays on Friday": the asks are real questions ("¿Dónde ponemos las frutas?"). "Hay bola" is not said: "los niños juegan bola". Bicycles are "corren bicicleta". Colon labels are sentences ("El sábado y el domingo son el fin de semana.", "Siete días son una semana."). Round B was "another day", which let him rule out round A's answer: each round is drawn by itself.
- 69: the recital "Lunes, frutas. Martes, bola..." was four bare labels and handed him the whole board before the game: cut. The turn "¡Listo!" echoed "¿Listo?": cut. A card with a dog was not among the cards: it is juice. Martes had been heard twice: three and more.
- 70: "El fin de semana vuela" is the saying "the weekend flies by": Luis names the kite, "¡Mira la chiringa! El sábado la volamos." "El fin de semana hay pollo" is true of the sábado column and the domingo column as well as the arch: all three are right. The arch had never held a card before it could cost: in the opening he hangs the piraguas on it. Canelo pulling a bow off had nothing to do with the wrong card: the card flutters down onto the tail and he jumps on it. "¡Canelo, cuidado!" warns the dog: "¡Canelo, no!". "Hay escuela" is "hay clases". Música is heard three times.
- 71: "¿Dónde está hoy?" is "where is he today?", and "Toca hoy" is "he plays today": the asks are "¿Cuál es el día de hoy?", "¿Y cuál es el día de mañana?", "Toca toda la semana." The game's cards were the opening's (fruit today, the ball tomorrow) and went against the week the other two stories teach: the game uses cards with no day of their own, never seen in the opening. "Las frutas son hoy" is not said: she says the "hay" line again. Three people on a seat showed no goal: each has an empty basket, abuela fills hers in the opening, and the one who is misled goes home with his empty. One night could jump two days: round B is the day after round A. The last turn echoed "¡Hasta mañana!": she says "Adiós." "¿Qué día es hoy?" is asked, by abuela. Counts put right.

Left, and why:
- The week on the board starts with lunes: the plan's order, and it keeps the two orange columns together under one arch. Most printed calendars in Puerto Rico start with domingo.
- "Mañana" is one new word here (tomorrow); "hasta mañana" has been a phrase of its own since El patio, so the last turn is an earlier phrase.
- "¡A mí también!": the word he says is "también"; "mi" has been a word since El campo.
- "¡Buenos días!" answers "¡Buenos días!": that is how a greeting is answered.
- "Lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves" is a count, like numbers, not a row of labels.
- The balloons are never named (the island's word is "bomba").
- Length: 71 is the longest; the real number comes from machine play.

- `globos` {n}; `revienta69`: the tack flies and a balloon bursts, Beto jumps (69: 6, 7). `final69` {n} (69: 8).
- `lazos` {n}: the bows on the kite's tail; `canelo70`: the card flutters down onto the tail, Canelo jumps on it and shakes a bow to bits (70: 6, 7). `final70` {n}: the next morning; the kite flies (70: 8).

- `gesto` {quien, que}: `saluda`, `no`, `si`, `rie`, `mira` with `a`, `senala` with `a`, `bulto` (holds up or throws a school bag), `levanta` (half gets up; sits again), `aplaude`.

- Values: in 69 `dia` (1 to 4) and `carta`, each round; in 70 `dia` (5, 6, 7 or `arco`) and `carta`, each round; in 71 `hoy` (round A 1 to 4; round B the day after), the two cards, which one she holds, and which is said first.

# El calendario: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 6, La plaza. Scene 4 of 4 (scene id `calendario`). Stories 69 to 71. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 68.

Scene words (page 02): lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo, hoy, mañana, día, semana, fin de semana.

The picture: the square's board of the week, close. One view. A long board on two posts under a little roof: seven columns side by side. Each column has a coloured square at its top (no writing: the first five green, the last two orange) and three hooks under it. Over the two orange columns, an arch of little paper flags. Along the board's foot a low step, so the frog can hop from column to column. At the right a seat for three, turned to the board. Behind: the bandstand's steps, trees. The week on this board starts with lunes, at the left.

What goes on the board: picture cards, one for each thing that happens in the square (a basket of fruit, a ball, the bingo cage, a bicycle, a guitar, a piragua, a family at a table, a cup of coffee, flowers, a loaf, a roast chicken, a glass of juice). A card hangs from a hook by a tack; a column can take three cards.

People: the señora (the lady with the bingo cage in El reloj): the board is her work. The others: Luis, Carmen, Beto, abuela, don Tito, the señor, Canelo.

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 69: El lunes hay frutas

- **Goal he can see:** three balloons tied to the board's left post, bobbing. Beto stands under them, looking up.
- **At stake:** the balloons (3). A card hung under the wrong day has to come down: the señora pulls out its tack, the tack flies from her fingers, and a balloon bursts.
- **New words:** lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves.
- **Back again:** hay, fruta, bola, bicicleta, juega, niños, voy, va, viene, café, flor, perro, pan, buenos días, listo, trabaja, toca, ¿dónde está?, uno to cuatro, sí, globo (heard in La calle).

**Start:** morning. The board is empty; a cloth hangs over its last three columns. The señora at its left end with a box of cards; the frog on the step. Three balloons on the post; Beto under them.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "La señora trabaja en la plaza." NARR: "La señora dice: ¡Buenos días, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenos días (buenos días, buenos dias)]: "¡Buenos días!" She points at the first four columns, one after another; each lights as she names it. NARR: "La señora dice: Lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves." Ask (signed, the first column): "¿Dónde está el lunes?" He touches it: it lights, and the frog hops under it.
2. She holds up the card with the basket of fruit. NARR: "La señora dice: El lunes hay frutas en la plaza." Ask (signed, the first column): "Las frutas: el lunes. Toca el lunes." He touches it: the frog hangs the card there. She holds up the ball. NARR: "La señora dice: El martes hay bola. Los niños juegan el martes." Ask (signed, the second column): "¿Y el martes?" He touches it: the ball hangs under the second.
3. Abuela walks by with her bag. The señora holds up the bingo cage's card. NARR: "La señora dice: El miércoles hay bingo." NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡El miércoles! Yo voy el miércoles." Ask (signed, the third column): "¿Dónde está el miércoles?" He touches it: the card hangs there. Luis rides up on his bicycle. NARR: "La señora dice: Y el jueves hay bicicletas." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡El jueves voy yo!" Ask (signed, the fourth column): "¿Y el jueves?" He touches it.
4. The señora runs her finger along the four cards. NARR: "La señora dice: Lunes, frutas. Martes, bola. Miércoles, bingo. Jueves, bicicletas. ¿Listo, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo)]: "¡Listo!"

**The turn**

5. The señora tips the box: more cards (a cup of coffee, flowers, a loaf, a dog). She hands the frog a tack. Beto pulls at the balloons' string; they bob. NARR: "La señora dice: Hay más. Ahora tú." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The señora holds up a card, drawn from the four in the box, and names its day: one of lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, drawn; in round B another day. The frog hangs it under a column (the first four; a column can take more than one card).

6. **Decision A, touch a column (four columns; tests the day).** She holds up the cup of coffee. NARR: "La señora dice: El jueves hay café." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hangs it there; the column lights. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it under another day. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. El jueves." She pulls out the tack; it flies from her fingers and a balloon bursts. Beto jumps. A cross. The sign is on the right column; he touches it: the card hangs there.
7. **Decision B, touch a column (the same game; another day).** She holds up the flowers. NARR: "La señora dice: Y el martes hay flores." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The señora steps back and looks at the four full columns. She unties the balloons.
   - Three balloons: one for Beto, one for Luis's bicycle, one for the frog, who goes up a little way with it. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: one for Beto, one for the frog.
   - One: Beto has it, and the frog rides on Beto's cap, holding the string.
9. NARR: "La señora dice: ¿Vienes el lunes, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy)]: "¡Sí, voy!" NARR: "El lunes hay frutas en la plaza. Y Coquí va."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lunes 6, martes 5, miércoles 6, jueves 5 (steps 1 to 4; the ask's words count).
**A miss is filed under:** the day that was said.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in four, each round on its own. Nothing on the board says which column is which: only the order, lunes first.
**Speaking turns:** three (steps 1, 4, 9): buenos días; listo; voy.

## Story 70: El fin de semana

- **Goal he can see:** on the paving, a kite that Luis and Carmen have made, with a long tail of three paper bows. Canelo lies beside the tail with one eye open.
- **At stake:** the bows on the kite's tail (3). While a wrong card is moved, Canelo pulls a bow off and shakes it to bits.
- **New words:** viernes, sábado, domingo, fin de semana.
- **Back again:** lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, hay, no hay, escuela, me gusta, también, después, vamos, viene, familia, come, flor, pan, pollo, jugo, piragua, perro, cuidado, arriba, vuela, mira.

**Start:** afternoon. The cloth is gone from the board. Under the first four columns hang their cards: fruit, the ball, the bingo cage, the bicycle. The last three columns are empty. The señora and the frog at the board. Luis and Carmen come from school with their school bags; their kite lies on the paving, Canelo beside it.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Luis y Carmen vienen de la escuela." The señora points along the board. NARR: "La señora dice: Lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves... y viernes." The fifth column lights. Carmen holds up her school bag. NARR: "Carmen dice: El viernes hay escuela." Ask (signed, the fifth column): "¿Dónde está el viernes?" He touches it: it lights.
2. The two orange columns light. NARR: "La señora dice: Y después, sábado y domingo." Luis throws his school bag into the air. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡El sábado no hay escuela! ¡Y el domingo no hay escuela!" Ask (signed, the sixth column): "¿Dónde está el sábado?" He touches it. Ask (signed, the seventh): "¿Y el domingo?" He touches it.
3. The señora sweeps her hand along the arch of flags over the two. NARR: "La señora dice: Sábado y domingo: el fin de semana." The arch twinkles. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Me gusta el fin de semana!" NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Y a ti, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: también (también, tambien, a mí también)]: "¡A mí también!" Ask (signed, the arch): "¿Dónde está el fin de semana?" He touches it: the arch and both columns light.
4. The señora holds up the guitar. NARR: "La señora dice: El viernes hay música en la plaza." **(música: heard; the guitar carries it)** Ask (signed, the fifth column): "La música: el viernes. Toca el viernes." He touches it: the frog hangs the card. She hangs two more herself, pointing at each. NARR: "La señora dice: El sábado hay piraguas. Y el domingo viene la familia a la plaza."

**The turn**

5. Luis lifts the kite and looks at the sky. NARR: "Luis dice: El fin de semana vuela. ¡Mira!" **(the kite is never named; the picture carries it)** Canelo puts a paw on the tail. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Canelo, cuidado!" The señora tips out more cards. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The señora holds up a card, drawn (flowers, a loaf, a roast chicken, a glass of juice), and says when. Round A: viernes, sábado or domingo, drawn. Round B: one of the two days that round A did not use, or el fin de semana, drawn. The frog hangs it under a column, or on the arch.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the seven columns and the arch; tests the day).** NARR: "La señora dice: El domingo hay flores." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hangs it there. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it somewhere else. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. El domingo." While she takes it down, Canelo pulls a bow off the kite's tail and shakes it to bits. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Canelo, no!" A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it: the card hangs there.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game).** NARR: "La señora dice: Y el fin de semana hay pollo." (Drawn; a card for the fin de semana hangs from the middle of the arch.) Right and wrong as in A; her words after a wrong move: "No, Coquí. El fin de semana."

**The ending**

8. The light changes: it is the next morning, and the sixth column is lit. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Es sábado! ¡Vamos!" Luis runs with the kite; Carmen lets the tail go.
   - Three bows: the kite goes straight up over the board, its tail dancing; the frog rides up the string a little way and slides down. Stars and a heart.
   - Two bows: it flies, leaning.
   - One bow: it spins, and flies low over the seat; Canelo runs under it.
9. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¿vienes el domingo también?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí!" NARR: "Sábado y domingo no hay escuela. ¡Es el fin de semana!"

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** viernes 6, sábado 5, domingo 5, fin de semana 4 (steps 1 to 5).
**A miss is filed under:** the day that was said (or fin de semana).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in eight as the places go; one in three for a learner who sees that only the new places are in play. The colours give the fin de semana away from the weekdays, not sábado from domingo.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 3, 9): también; sí.

## Story 71: Hoy y mañana

- **Goal he can see:** three people on the seat, turned to the board, waiting to see what the day brings: abuela, don Tito and the señor. And a peg shaped like a sun in the señora's hand.
- **At stake:** the people on the seat (3). A card under the wrong day: they read it, get ready for the wrong thing, and when it is put right one of them gets up and goes home.
- **New words:** hoy, mañana, día, semana.
- **Back again:** lunes to domingo, hay, no hay, otro, nuevo, uno to siete, buenos días, hasta mañana, gente, viene, bingo (heard), fruta, bola, bicicleta, espera, se va, tarde, ya.

**Start:** early morning. The board is empty: a new week. The señora at its left end with the sun peg and the box of cards; the frog on the step. Abuela, don Tito and the señor on the seat.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Es un día nuevo en la plaza. Y es una semana nueva." The señora hangs the sun peg on the first column's square. NARR: "La señora dice: Buenos días. Hoy es lunes." Ask (signed, the first column, with the sun): "Hoy es lunes. ¿Dónde está hoy?" He touches it: the sun shines.
2. She points at the next column. NARR: "La señora dice: Y mañana es martes." Ask (signed, the second column): "¿Dónde está mañana?" He touches it: it lights.
3. She runs her finger along all seven; each lights in turn. NARR: "La señora dice: Un día, dos días, tres días, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete. Siete días: una semana." Ask (signed, the whole board): "¿Dónde está la semana?" He touches it: all seven light at once.
4. She holds up the basket of fruit. NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy hay frutas." Ask (signed, the first column): "Hoy hay frutas. Toca hoy." He touches it: the frog hangs the card under the sun. The three on the seat look and nod. She holds up the ball. NARR: "La señora dice: Y mañana hay bola." Ask (signed, the second column): "¿Y mañana?" He touches it: the ball hangs under the second column.
5. The picture goes dark and light again: a night. The señora moves the sun to the second column. NARR: "La señora dice: Otro día. Hoy es martes. Hoy hay bola. Y mañana hay bingo." She hangs the bingo cage under the third. Abuela half gets up, with her bingo card. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿hay bingo hoy?" COQUÍ [TURN: no (no)]: "¡No!" NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy no. Mañana." Abuela sits down again.

**The turn**

6. The señora takes every card down: the board is empty again. She shakes the box. The three on the seat lean forward. NARR: "La señora dice: Otra semana. Ahora tú, Coquí." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The señora hangs the sun on a day, drawn (round A: lunes to jueves; round B: a later day, to sábado), and says which day it is. She holds up one card and says two things: what there is today and what there is tomorrow (which is said first is drawn; whether the card she holds is today's or tomorrow's is drawn). The frog hangs the card she holds. She hangs the other one herself afterwards.

7. **Decision A, touch a column (seven columns; tests hoy against mañana).** The sun goes on the third column. NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy es miércoles." She holds up the guitar. NARR: "La señora dice: Hoy hay bicicletas. Mañana hay música." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog hangs it under the day after the sun (or, for today's card, under the sun). The three on the seat look, and nod. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog hangs it under another day. The three on the seat read the board: one of them claps, gets up and looks about the square for the music. NARR: "La señora dice: No, Coquí. La música es mañana." She takes the card down. The one who got up shakes his head and goes home. A cross. The sign is on the right column; he touches it: the card hangs there.
8. **Decision B, touch a column (the same game; a later day).** A night; the sun moves on. NARR: "La señora dice: Otro día. Hoy es viernes." She holds up the fruit. NARR: "La señora dice: Mañana hay bola. Hoy hay frutas." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A; her words after a wrong move: "No, Coquí. Las frutas son hoy." (or: "... es mañana.", "... son mañana.")
   - Who goes home: the señor first, then don Tito. Abuela stays.

**The ending**

9. Evening: the lamps come on. The señora takes the sun down and puts it in her pocket.
   - Three on the seat: all three get up, and each waves to the frog as he goes. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: abuela and don Tito wave.
   - One: abuela takes the frog on her shoulder.
10. NARR: "La señora dice: Ya es tarde. ¡Hasta mañana, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!" NARR: "Mañana es otro día en la plaza."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** hoy 8, mañana 7 (steps 1 to 5). Not deciding: día 5 with días; semana 4 (steps 1, 3, 6).
**A miss is filed under:** the word that went with the card she held (hoy or mañana).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two for a learner who sees that the sun is today; one in seven as the columns go. Both things are named in every line; only hoy and mañana say which is which.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 5, 10): no; hasta mañana.

## Left for the reviews to settle

- The week on the board starts with lunes (the plan's order, and the fin de semana comes out as the last two). Most printed calendars in Puerto Rico start with domingo.
- "Mañana" is one new word here (tomorrow); "hasta mañana" has been a phrase of its own since El patio.

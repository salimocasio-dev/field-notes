# La sala, de día: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `sala_dia`). Stories 102 to 104. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 101.

Scene words (page 02): estudia, lee, escribe, libro, papel, teléfono, computadora, llama, escucha, habla, la tarde, a veces.
How they are shared out: 102 libro, papel, lee, escribe; 103 teléfono, llama, habla, escucha; 104 estudia, computadora, la tarde, a veces.

The picture: the living room, in the afternoon. One view. The back wall faces us: a window with the afternoon sun, a shelf of books, a wall clock. At the left, a desk with a computer and a chair behind it (whoever sits there faces us). In the middle, a low table; the children sit on cushions along its far side, facing us. At the right, an armchair, with a small table beside it for the telephone. Nothing over anyone's head.

People: Luis, Carmen, abuela, abuelo; mamá (on the telephone: her photo on its screen, her voice).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose. Where a step says "signed" after somebody's own line, that line is the ask: the sign shows what to touch, and the voice does not ask again.

## Story 102: Lee y escribe

- **Goal he can see:** on the low table, Carmen's pad of paper. In the corner of its top sheet she draws a very small frog; she counts the sheets that are left, one, two, three, and points at the frog: a big one, afterwards.
- **At stake:** the three sheets of paper. A sheet given to someone who should read becomes a paper plane and flies out of the window; a book given to someone who should write gets written in, and Carmen tears out a sheet to cover the page.
- **New words:** libro, papel, lee, escribe.
- **Back again:** me llamo, ¿cómo te llamas?, nombre, bonito, me gusta, mira, ahora, yo, mi, después, para, ventana, no, sí.

**Start:** afternoon. Luis and Carmen sit at the low table, with nothing in front of them. The frog on the table. Abuela by the shelf of books, with a pad of five sheets in her hand.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela takes a book off the shelf and lays it on the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira: un libro." (signed, the book.) He touches it: the frog pushes it to Luis, who opens it; small pictures float up from the page: a boat, a star. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis lee el libro."
2. Abuela lays the pad on the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y aquí hay papel." (signed, the pad.) He touches it: the frog pulls a sheet across to Carmen; she writes; letters appear. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen escribe en el papel." NARR: "Carmen dice: Escribo mi nombre. ¿Y tú? ¿Cómo te llamas?" **(escribo: a form of escribe)** COQUÍ [TURN: me llamo (me llamo coquí, me llamo coqui, coquí)]: "¡Me llamo Coquí!" She writes it under her own name.
3. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora yo escribo." The frog pulls a sheet across to him. NARR: "Carmen dice: Y yo leo." **(leo: a form of lee)** Ask (signed, the book): "Ahora Carmen lee. ¿Dónde está el libro?" He touches it: the frog pushes the book to Carmen. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis escribe. Carmen lee."
4. Carmen holds up the pad: three clean sheets are left; she counts them. In the corner of the top one she draws a very small frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: Después, el papel es para ti, Coquí."

**The turn**

5. Abuela goes to the kitchen's door; her hands are white with flour. The book and the pad lie in the middle of the low table, between the two children. The window is open. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, from the kitchen's door, looking at her hands, says what one of the children does now (the child and what he does are drawn, each round on its own: the two rounds may be the same). The frog pushes the book, or a sheet from the pad, to that child.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the book, the pad; tests lee against escribe).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Luis lee." (Or, drawn: "Ahora Luis escribe." The child is drawn too.)
   - Right: he reads (pictures float up), or he writes (letters appear, on a sheet of his own from before). A star.
   - Wrong: he gets a clean sheet when he should read: he folds it into a plane, and it flies out of the window. (Or he gets the book when he should write: he writes in the book; abuela gasps; Carmen tears a clean sheet out of the pad to cover the page.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Luis lee." A clean sheet is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; drawn again).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen escribe." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Carmen draws on the clean sheets that are left.
   - Three sheets, side by side: a frog as big as the table. Stars and a heart.
   - Two sheets: a frog as big as the frog.
   - One sheet: a small frog.
9. She holds it up. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para ti, Coquí. ¿Te gusta?" COQUÍ [TURN: bonito (qué bonito, bonito, muy bonito)]: "¡Qué bonito!" NARR: "Luis lee un libro. Carmen escribe en el papel: Coquí."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lee 3 and leo once (steps 1, 3), escribe 3 and escribo twice (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: libro 3 (steps 1, 3), papel 3 (steps 2, 4).
**Forms:** leo counts with lee; escribo with escribe.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (lee or escribe).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): me llamo; bonito.

## Story 103: El teléfono

- **Goal he can see:** the telephone's screen: mamá's photo, and in its corner a battery with three bars. The frog opens his mouth at it, and small rings go from him towards the screen: he wants to sing to her.
- **At stake:** the battery (3 bars). While the telephone is at the wrong end of abuelo's head, the call runs on and nobody hears anybody: a bar blinks, and goes.
- **New words:** teléfono, llama, habla, escucha.
- **Back again:** mamá, abuelo, oreja, boca, hola, bien, ¿cómo estás?, hablo, hasta luego, hasta mañana, pone (pones), ahora, yo, con, por favor, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuelo dozes in the armchair. The telephone lies on the small table beside him. Luis and Carmen at the low table. Abuela by the window. The frog on the armchair's arm.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The telephone rings; its screen lights up. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡El teléfono! Coquí, por favor." (signed, the telephone.) He touches it: the frog carries it to abuela. On the screen, mamá's photo. NARR: "Abuela dice: Llama mamá. ¡Hola!"
2. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?" Abuela holds the telephone to her ear, and then before her mouth. NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo escucho... y ahora hablo. Bien, gracias." **(escucho: a form of escucha)**
3. Abuelo wakes, and cups a hand behind his ear. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Quién llama?" **(quién: heard)** NARR: "Abuela dice: Llama mamá. Abuelo escucha." (signed, abuelo's ear.) He touches it: the frog holds the telephone to abuelo's ear; mamá's voice comes out as small pictures (a guagua, a beach); abuelo smiles. NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora abuelo habla." (signed, abuelo's mouth.) He touches it: the frog holds it before his mouth. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Hola! Yo estoy bien." NARR: "Abuela dice: Abuelo habla con mamá."
4. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¿Y Coquí? ¿Cómo estás, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien, muy bien, bien gracias)]: "¡Bien, gracias!" The frog opens his mouth to sing; abuela lays a finger on her lips: afterwards. The battery shows three bars.

**The turn**

5. Abuela goes off to the kitchen: something is on the stove. The frog stands on the armchair's arm with the telephone, beside abuelo's head. Mamá has a great deal to tell, and to ask. Abuelo sits still, his mouth shut, his hands on his knees. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen looks up from her paper and says what abuelo does now (drawn, each round on its own: the two rounds may be the same): he listens, or he talks. Abuelo's face is the same every time. The frog holds the telephone to abuelo's ear, or before his mouth.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (abuelo's ear, abuelo's mouth; tests escucha against habla).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora abuelo escucha." (Or, drawn: "Ahora abuelo habla.")
   - Right: for "escucha" small pictures come out of the telephone into his ear, and he nods; for "habla" his own small pictures (a fish, a hat) go into it. A star.
   - Wrong: the telephone is before his mouth while mamá talks: her pictures fall to the floor. (Or at his ear while he talks: his pictures go past it, and mamá's small voice says "¿Hola? ¿Hola?") NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Abuelo escucha." (or "Abuelo habla.") A bar of the battery blinks, and goes. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; drawn again).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora abuelo habla." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuelo holds the telephone out to the frog. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Hasta luego, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!" And the frog sings into it.
   - Three bars: he sings "¡Coquí! ¡Coquí!" twice over; mamá's laugh comes out as hearts. Stars and a heart.
   - Two bars: he sings it once; a heart.
   - One bar: "¡Co...!" and the screen goes dark; abuelo plugs the telephone in.
9. NARR: "Mamá llama por teléfono. Abuelo escucha, y habla. Y Coquí también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** escucha 2 and escucho once (steps 2, 3), habla 3 and hablo once (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: teléfono 1 before the game and 1 in the last line, and it rings and is carried (to weigh: a third); llama 3 (steps 1, 3).
**Forms:** escucho counts with escucha; hablo is his already (La plaza).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (escucha or habla).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 8): bien; hasta mañana.

## Story 104: Por la tarde

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen's album for school, "my day": three pages, one with a rising sun, one with a high orange sun, one with a moon. On its cover, a photo of the frog; and in her box, three gold stars: she holds them against the cover, round the frog.
- **At stake:** the three gold stars. A photo stuck on the wrong page has to be peeled off: its corner tears, and a star goes on the tear.
- **New words:** estudia, computadora, la tarde, a veces.
- **Back again:** la mañana, noche, son las, tres, lee, escribe, libro, juega, bola, día, me gusta, aquí, pone (pones), mira, con, mi, yo, no, sí.

**Start:** afternoon; the sun is in the window; the clock shows three. Luis sits at the desk with the computer and a notebook. Carmen at the low table with her album and a box of photos. The frog on the low table.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Son las tres de la tarde. Luis estudia." Ask (signed, the computer): "Luis estudia en la computadora. ¿Dónde está?" He touches it: its screen lights up; Luis looks from the screen to his notebook, and writes.
2. Carmen opens her album: three pages; each one glows as she names it. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí: mi día. La mañana, la tarde y la noche." Ask (signed, the page with the high orange sun): "¿Dónde está la tarde?" He touches it: it glows.
3. She holds up a photo: Luis at the computer. NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la tarde, Luis estudia." (signed, the afternoon's page.) He touches it: the frog sticks the photo on. She holds up another: abuela with a book. NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la noche, abuela lee." The frog sticks it on the page with the moon.
4. Over Luis, at the desk, two small pictures change places: the computer, his notebook, the computer. NARR: "Carmen dice: A veces Luis estudia en la computadora. A veces estudia con un libro." Luis shuts the notebook, picks up his ball, and looks at abuela. NARR: "Abuela dice: A veces, Luis. Ahora no." NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¿dónde están mis estrellas?" The box is under the frog's foot. COQUÍ [TURN: aquí (aquí, aquí están)]: "¡Aquí!" She holds the three gold stars against the album's cover, round the frog's photo, and puts them back in the box.

**The turn**

5. Carmen takes more photos out of her box: people of the house, indoors, with no window and no clock in them, doing things that are done at any hour. The album lies open at its three pages. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen, her eyes on the photo in her hand, says when the thing in it happens and who does what (who and what are drawn from: abuelo lee, abuela escribe, Carmen juega, Luis lee, abuela juega). One of the two rounds, drawn, is "por la tarde"; the other is "por la mañana" or "por la noche" (drawn). The photo shows the person and the thing, and nothing of the time of day. The frog sticks it on one of the three pages.

6. **Decision A, touch a page (three; tests la tarde against la mañana and la noche).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la tarde, abuelo lee." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the photo is stuck on. A star.
   - Wrong: the photo is stuck on. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. Por la tarde." She peels it off; its corner tears; a gold star goes on the tear. A cross. The sign is on the right page; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a page (the same game; another photo, another time of day).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la mañana, abuela escribe." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Carmen shuts the album, and sticks the stars that are left on its cover, round the frog's photo.
   - Three stars. Stars and a heart.
   - Two stars.
   - One star.
9. NARR: "Carmen dice: Este es mi día. ¿Te gusta, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" NARR: "Por la tarde Luis estudia. A veces en la computadora, a veces con un libro."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** la tarde 4 (steps 1, 2, 3). Not deciding: estudia 4 (steps 1, 3, 4), computadora 2 before the game (steps 1, 4) and once in the last line, a veces 2 lines before the game (step 4) and the last line.
**A miss is filed under:** the time of day that was said (la tarde, la mañana or noche).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): aquí; me gusta.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- Words. "Es la tarde" is a word-for-word translation: "Son las tres de la tarde". On the island you study "en la computadora", not "con". "¿Cómo está?" of a drawing sounds like a formal "how are you": "¿Te gusta?", and the frog's "¡Qué bonito!". A mother ends a call with "¡Hasta luego!", not "¡Adiós!". The news of who is calling puts the name last: "Llama mamá."
- All three: an ask that repeated the line just said: the line itself carries the sign.
- 102: the goal's three clean sheets were written on in the opening: the pad has five, two are used, and Carmen counts the three that are left. Abuela, at the kitchen's door with floury hands, tore a sheet out on the table: Carmen does. The two rounds read as "one of each", which gave the second away: they are drawn on their own and may be the same (in 103 too). "Carmen lee" was said before she had the book.
- 103: the frog's wish to sing could not be seen: rings go from his mouth towards the screen. "The call runs on" could not be seen either: the bar blinks, and the voices' pictures fall to the floor. Abuelo's face might show what he was doing: it is the same every time, and Carmen looks up to speak. "¿Cómo están?", "Estamos bien", "¿Qué?" were three more forms than a story of four new words needs. "Habla" had two exact hearings: three.
- 104: the afternoon could go untested if both rounds drew the morning or the night: one round is always the afternoon. The asleep photo taught "sleeping is night", and "abuelo duerme" and "Luis come" could then be placed from the photo alone: the photos are of reading, writing and playing. "A veces" was carried by no picture, and Luis's "¡Todos los días!" turned it into a quarrel in a phrase he does not have: two small pictures change places over Luis, and abuela's "A veces, Luis. Ahora no." gives it a use. The frog's hop to the desk and back did nothing: gone, and his turn is "¡Aquí!". Studying "con un libro" looked like reading: Luis copies into a notebook.

Left, and why:
- "Teléfono" is heard twice in lines (it also rings, and is the thing carried). "El celular" is as everyday for the thing; "¡El teléfono!" is what is said when it rings.
- "Computadora" and "a veces" decide nothing.
- 104's three pages: a third thing to choose from, two of them his already; the afternoon is forced into one round.
- 103's openings are the longest of the place: the length is measured when the story files exist.

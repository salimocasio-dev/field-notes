# La sala, de día: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 9, La casa, un día entero. Scene 3 of 4 (scene id `sala_dia`). Stories 102 to 104. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 101.

Scene words (page 02): estudia, lee, escribe, libro, papel, teléfono, computadora, llama, escucha, habla, la tarde, a veces.
How they are shared out: 102 libro, papel, lee, escribe; 103 teléfono, llama, habla, escucha; 104 estudia, computadora, la tarde, a veces.

The picture: the living room, in the afternoon. One view. The back wall faces us: a window with the afternoon sun, a shelf of books, a wall clock. At the left, a desk with a computer and a chair behind it (whoever sits there faces us). In the middle, a low table; the children sit on cushions along its far side, facing us. At the right, an armchair, with a small table beside it for the telephone. Nothing over anyone's head.

People: Luis, Carmen, abuela, abuelo; mamá (on the telephone: her photo on its screen, her voice).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 102: Lee y escribe

- **Goal he can see:** on the low table, Carmen's pad of paper: three clean sheets are left. In its corner she draws a very small frog, and points at the three sheets, and at the frog: a big one, afterwards.
- **At stake:** the three sheets of paper. A sheet given to someone who should read becomes a paper plane and flies out of the window; a book given to someone who should write gets written in, and abuela tears out a sheet to cover the page.
- **New words:** libro, papel, lee, escribe.
- **Back again:** me llamo, ¿cómo te llamas?, nombre, bonito, mira, quiere, ahora, yo, mi, esto, después, para, no, sí, ventana.

**Start:** afternoon. Luis and Carmen sit at the low table, with nothing in front of them. The frog on the table. Abuela by the shelf of books.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, mira: un libro." Ask (signed, a book on the shelf): "¿Dónde está el libro?" He touches it: the frog carries it to Luis, who opens it; small pictures float up from the page: a boat, a star. NARR: "Abuela dice: Luis lee el libro."
2. Abuela puts the pad on the table. NARR: "Abuela dice: Y esto es papel." Ask (signed, the pad): "¿Dónde está el papel?" He touches it: the frog pulls a sheet across to Carmen; she writes; letters appear. NARR: "Abuela dice: Carmen escribe en el papel." NARR: "Carmen dice: Escribo mi nombre. ¿Y tú? ¿Cómo te llamas?" **(escribo: a form of escribe)** COQUÍ [TURN: me llamo (me llamo coquí, me llamo coqui, coquí)]: "¡Me llamo Coquí!" She writes it under her own name.
3. NARR: "Luis dice: Ahora yo escribo." NARR: "Carmen dice: Y yo leo." **(leo: a form of lee)** Ask (signed, the book): "Carmen lee. ¿Qué quiere Carmen?" He touches it: the frog pushes the book to Carmen, and a sheet to Luis. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen lee y Luis escribe."
4. Carmen holds up the pad: three clean sheets. In its corner she draws a very small frog. NARR: "Carmen dice: Después, el papel es para ti, Coquí."

**The turn**

5. Abuela goes to the kitchen's door; her hands are white with flour. The book and the pad lie in the middle of the low table, between the two children. The window is open. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Abuela, from the kitchen's door, looking at her hands, says what one of the children does now (the child and what he does are drawn, each round on its own). The frog pushes the book, or a sheet from the pad, to that child.

6. **Decision A, touch a thing (the book, the pad; tests lee against escribe).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Luis lee." (Or, drawn: "Ahora Luis escribe." The child is drawn too.)
   - Right: he reads (pictures float up), or he writes (letters appear). A star.
   - Wrong: he gets a sheet when he should read: he folds it into a plane, and it flies out of the window. (Or he gets the book when he should write: he writes in the book; abuela gasps, and tears a sheet out of the pad to cover the page.) NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡No, Coquí! Luis lee." A sheet is gone. A cross. The sign is on the right thing; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a thing (the same game; the other child).** NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora Carmen escribe." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Carmen draws on what is left of the pad.
   - Three sheets, side by side: a frog as big as the table. Stars and a heart.
   - Two sheets: a frog as big as the frog.
   - One sheet: a small frog.
9. She holds it up. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para ti, Coquí. ¿Cómo está?" COQUÍ [TURN: bonito (bonito, qué bonito, muy bonito)]: "¡Bonito!" NARR: "Luis lee un libro. Carmen escribe en el papel: Coquí."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** lee 4 with leo (steps 1, 3), escribe 4 with escribo (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: libro 3 (step 1), papel 4 (steps 2, 4).
**Forms:** leo counts with lee; escribo with escribe.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (lee or escribe).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 2, 9): me llamo; bonito.

## Story 103: El teléfono

- **Goal he can see:** the telephone's screen: mamá's photo, and in its corner a battery with three bars. The frog taps his own chest: he wants to sing to her.
- **At stake:** the battery (3 bars). While the telephone is at the wrong end of abuelo's head, the call runs on and nobody hears anybody: a bar goes.
- **New words:** teléfono, llama, habla, escucha.
- **Back again:** mamá, abuelo, oreja, boca, hola, bien, ¿cómo estás?, hablo, adiós, hasta mañana, pone (pones), ahora, yo, con, por favor, gracias, no, sí.

**Start:** afternoon. Abuelo dozes in the armchair. The telephone lies on the small table beside him. Luis and Carmen at the low table. Abuela by the window. The frog on the armchair's arm.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The telephone rings; its screen lights up. NARR: "Abuela dice: ¡El teléfono! Coquí, el teléfono, por favor." Ask (signed, the telephone): "¿Dónde está el teléfono?" He touches it: the frog carries it to abuela. On the screen, mamá's photo. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mamá llama. ¡Hola!"
2. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Hola! ¿Cómo están?" **(están: heard)** Abuela holds the telephone to her ear, and then before her mouth. NARR: "Abuela dice: Yo escucho... y ahora hablo. Estamos bien, gracias." **(escucho: a form of escucha; estamos: heard)**
3. Abuelo wakes, and cups a hand behind his ear. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¿Qué? ¿Quién llama?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Mamá llama. Abuelo escucha." Ask (signed, abuelo's ear): "Abuelo escucha. ¿Dónde pones el teléfono?" He touches it: the frog holds the telephone to abuelo's ear; mamá's voice comes out as small pictures (a guagua, a beach); abuelo smiles. NARR: "Abuela dice: Ahora abuelo habla." Ask (signed, abuelo's mouth): "Abuelo habla. ¿Dónde pones el teléfono?" He touches it: the frog holds it before his mouth. NARR: "Abuelo dice: ¡Hola! Yo estoy bien."
4. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¿Y Coquí? ¿Cómo estás, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: bien (bien, muy bien, bien gracias)]: "¡Bien, gracias!"

**The turn**

5. Abuela goes off to the kitchen: something is on the stove. The frog stands on the armchair's arm with the telephone, beside abuelo's head. Mamá has a great deal to tell, and to ask. The battery shows three bars. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen, at the low table with her eyes on her paper, says what abuelo does now (drawn, each round on its own): he listens, or he talks. The frog holds the telephone to abuelo's ear, or before his mouth.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (abuelo's ear, abuelo's mouth; tests escucha against habla).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora abuelo escucha." (Or, drawn: "Ahora abuelo habla.")
   - Right: for "escucha" small pictures come out of the telephone into his ear, and he nods; for "habla" his own small pictures (a fish, a hat) go into it. A star.
   - Wrong: the telephone is before his mouth while mamá talks: nobody hears her. (Or at his ear while he talks: mamá's small voice says "¿Hola? ¿Hola?") NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡No, Coquí! Abuelo escucha." (or "Abuelo habla.") A bar of the battery goes. A cross. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Ahora abuelo habla." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Abuelo holds the telephone out to the frog. NARR: "Mamá dice: ¡Adiós, Coquí!" COQUÍ [TURN: hasta mañana (hasta mañana, hasta manana)]: "¡Hasta mañana!" And the frog sings into it.
   - Three bars: he sings "¡Coquí! ¡Coquí!" twice over; mamá's laugh comes out as hearts. Stars and a heart.
   - Two bars: he sings it once; a heart.
   - One bar: "¡Co...!" and the screen goes dark; abuelo plugs the telephone in.
9. NARR: "Mamá llama por teléfono. Abuelo escucha, y habla. Y Coquí también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** escucha 3 with escucho (steps 2, 3), habla 3 (step 3) and hablo once (step 2). Not deciding: teléfono 5 (steps 1, 3), llama 3 (steps 1, 3).
**Forms:** escucho counts with escucha; hablo is his already (La plaza).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (escucha or habla).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 8): bien; hasta mañana.

## Story 104: Por la tarde

- **Goal he can see:** Carmen's album for school, "my day": three pages, one with a rising sun, one with a high orange sun, one with a moon. On its cover, a photo of the frog; and in her box, three gold stars: she holds them against the cover, round the frog.
- **At stake:** the three gold stars. A photo stuck on the wrong page has to be peeled off: its corner tears, and a star goes on the tear.
- **New words:** estudia, computadora, la tarde, a veces.
- **Back again:** la mañana, noche, lee, escribe, libro, juega, bola, duerme, come, día, me gusta, voy, ven, pone (pones), mira, con, mi, yo, no, sí.

**Start:** afternoon; the sun is in the window. Luis sits at the desk with the computer and a book. Carmen at the low table with her album and a box of photos. The frog on the low table.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Es la tarde. Luis estudia." Ask (signed, the computer): "Luis estudia con la computadora. ¿Dónde está la computadora?" He touches it: its screen lights up. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ven acá. Mira la computadora." COQUÍ [TURN: voy (voy, ya voy)]: "¡Voy!" The frog hops to the desk, looks at the screen, and hops back.
2. Carmen opens her album: three pages; each one glows as she names it. NARR: "Carmen dice: Mira, Coquí: mi día. La mañana, la tarde y la noche." Ask (signed, the page with the high orange sun): "¿Dónde está la tarde?" He touches it: it glows.
3. She holds up a photo: Luis at the computer. NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la tarde, Luis estudia." Ask (signed, the afternoon's page): "Luis estudia por la tarde. ¿Dónde pones la foto?" He touches it: the frog sticks the photo on. She holds up another: Carmen asleep. NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la noche, yo duermo." **(duermo: a form of duerme)** The frog sticks it on the page with the moon.
4. A third photo: Luis with a ball. NARR: "Carmen dice: A veces, por la tarde, Luis juega con la bola." NARR: "Luis dice: ¿A veces? ¡Todos los días!" **(todos los días: heard)** NARR: "Carmen dice: A veces Luis estudia con la computadora, y a veces con un libro." She holds the three gold stars against the album's cover, round the frog's photo, and puts them back in the box.

**The turn**

5. Carmen takes more photos out of her box: people of the house, indoors, with no window and no clock in them. The album lies open at its three pages. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Carmen, her eyes on the photo in her hand, says when the thing in it happens (drawn: la mañana, la tarde or la noche; round B's is not round A's) and who does what (drawn from: abuelo lee, abuela escribe, Luis come, Carmen juega, abuelo duerme). The photo shows the person and the thing, and nothing of the time of day. The frog sticks it on one of the three pages.

6. **Decision A, touch a page (three; tests la tarde against la mañana and la noche).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la tarde, abuelo lee." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the photo is stuck on. A star.
   - Wrong: the photo is stuck on. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. Por la tarde." She peels it off; its corner tears; a gold star goes on the tear. A cross. The sign is on the right page; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a page (the same game; another photo, another time of day).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Por la mañana, Luis come." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Carmen shuts the album, and sticks the stars that are left on its cover, round the frog's photo.
   - Three stars. Stars and a heart.
   - Two stars.
   - One star.
9. NARR: "Carmen dice: Este es mi día. ¿Te gusta, Coquí?" COQUÍ [TURN: me gusta (me gusta, sí me gusta)]: "¡Sí, me gusta!" NARR: "Por la tarde Luis estudia. A veces con la computadora, a veces con un libro."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** la tarde 6 (steps 1 to 4). Not deciding: estudia 5 (steps 1, 3, 4), computadora 4 (steps 1, 4), a veces 3 lines (step 4).
**A miss is filed under:** the time of day that was said (la tarde, la mañana or noche).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in three, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): voy; me gusta.

# La entrada: three stories (draft 2, after the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `entrada`). Stories 84 to 86. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 83.

Scene words (page 02): fonda, mesero, mesera, menú, para dos, libre, ocupado, adelante, bienvenido, un momento, con, sin.
How they are shared out: 84 fonda, mesera, bienvenido, adelante, un momento; 85 mesero, libre, ocupado, para dos; 86 menú, con, sin.

The picture: the room of a small fonda, seen from inside, at dusk and at night. One view. In the back wall, at the left, the open door (the street's light in it), with a doormat inside it. To the right of the door, against the back wall, a bench to wait on. Then the mesera's stand, and from the stand a rope on two posts across the way into the room. Beyond the rope, three tables in a row, each with its own cloth; at the far right of the back wall the kitchen's swing door, a stool beside it, and the vellonera (the jukebox). Lamps.

New people: the mesera (an apron, a pencil behind her ear) and the mesero (a bow tie, a tray). The family: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen. Other guests: the people of El parque (the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer, the joven).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16). In a game nobody looks at, turns to or points at any of the things he can choose.

## Story 84: Bienvenidos

- **Goal he can see:** beyond the rope, the three tables, one behind another: the near one (mesa3) has flowers and a small card with a star on it, the middle one (mesa2) is plain, the far one (mesa1) is beside the kitchen's swing door. Abuela points at the one with the flowers. In the doorway behind the family, the señor and the señora look at the same table.
- **At stake:** the good tables (3, best first). The mesera says to come on, or to wait a moment. If the frog, who leads the family, does the other, the people behind them get the best table that is left.
- **New words:** fonda, mesera, bienvenido, adelante, un momento.
- **Back again:** buenas tardes, hola, tengo hambre, familia, gente, mucho, hay, espera, va, vamos, aquí, cuatro, por favor, sí, mira.

**Start:** dusk. The family stands in the open door: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen, and the frog in front of them on the doormat. Behind them, in the doorway, the señor and the señora. The mesera at her stand. The star card twinkles on the table with the flowers.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. Abuela points into the room, at the table with the flowers. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: la fonda. Aquí comemos." **(comemos: heard)** The mesera looks up and smiles. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Hola! ¡Bienvenidos!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenas tardes (buenas tardes)]: "¡Buenas tardes!" Ask (signed, the mesera): "¿Dónde está la mesera de la fonda?" He touches her: she bows to the frog.
2. A mesero crosses in front of the stand with a tall pile of plates. The mesera holds up a hand. NARR: "La mesera dice: Un momento, por favor." Ask (signed, the bench): "¿Dónde espera Coquí un momento?" He touches it: the frog sits on the bench; the plates go by. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Un momento, Coquí. Espera aquí."
3. The mesera waves them on. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Adelante!" NARR: "Abuela dice: Adelante, Coquí. Vamos." Ask (signed, the mesera): "¡Adelante! ¿A dónde va Coquí?" He touches her: the frog hops up to her stand, and the family after him. NARR: "La mesera dice: Bienvenido, Coquí. ¿Cuántos son?" **(son: heard)** NARR: "Abuela dice: Somos cuatro... y Coquí." **(somos: heard)**
4. The mesera looks at her list, and at the room: more people come in at the door. NARR: "La mesera dice: Un momento. Hay mucha gente." She walks the family back to the doormat, the frog in front.

**The turn**

5. The family waits on the doormat. Behind them the señor and the señora look over their shoulders at the table with the flowers. The mesera reads her list. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two stretches of the way in: from the doormat to the stand, and from the stand past the rope. At each the mesera, her eyes on her list, calls one of two things (drawn, each round on its own): to come on, or to wait a moment. The frog hops up to her, or sits on the bench. Both calls end with "por favor".

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the mesera at her stand, the bench; tests adelante against un momento).** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Adelante, por favor!" (Or, drawn: "La mesera dice: Un momento, por favor.")
   - Right: for "adelante" the frog hops up to the stand, and the family after him. For "un momento" he sits; a mesero goes by with a full tray; then she waves, and they all go up to the stand. A star.
   - Wrong: he sits when she said to come on: the señor and the señora walk past him, and she takes them to the best table that is free. (Or he hops on when she said to wait: he runs into the mesero's legs; the tray tips; while she helps, she waves the señor and the señora round them to the table.) NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Adelante!" (or: "¡No, Coquí! ¡Un momento!") A cross. The best table left is gone. The sign is on the right place; he touches it. The family ends at the stand.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the mesera, who now stands beyond the rope, and the bench; behind the family stand the señor and the señora if they are still waiting, and if they have their table, the hombre and the mujer, who have come in).** NARR: "La mesera dice: Un momento, por favor." (Or, drawn: "La mesera dice: ¡Adelante, por favor!") Right: for "adelante" she unhooks the rope and they go through; for "un momento" he sits, a mesero goes by, and then she unhooks the rope. Wrong: as in A, and the hombre and the mujer get the best table left. Either way the family ends beyond the rope.

**The ending**

8. The mesera leads the family to the best table that is left.
   - Three: the table with the flowers and the star. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: the table in the middle.
   - One: the table by the kitchen's door; the door swings, and abuelo holds his hat each time.
9. NARR: "La mesera dice: Bienvenidos. ¿Tienen hambre?" **(tienen: a form of tiene)** COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre, sí tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí, tengo hambre!" NARR: "La familia está en la fonda. Y Coquí tiene hambre."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** un momento 4 (steps 2, 4), adelante 3 (step 3). Not deciding: fonda 3 (steps 1, 9); mesera 1 and as the speaker's name; bienvenido 3 with bienvenidos (steps 1, 3, 9).
**Forms:** bienvenidos counts with bienvenido.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (adelante or un momento).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): buenas tardes; tengo hambre.

## Story 85: ¿Está libre?

- **Goal he can see:** the mesero has a bad foot (a bandage, a stick). On his stool beside him, a saucer with three wrapped sweets: he taps it, and points at the frog: for the helper.
- **At stake:** the three sweets. A couple shown to a table that is taken has to get up again when its people come back; the mesero says sorry, and gives them a sweet.
- **New words:** mesero, libre, ocupado, para dos.
- **Back again:** fonda, mesera, un momento, mesa, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, dos, señor, señora, hombre, mujer, joven, gente, espera, listo, perdón, toma, hay, no hay, gracias, por favor, pie, malo.

**Start:** evening; the lamps are lit. Three tables: the far one (mesa1), by the kitchen, is kept for a party: a card stands on it, and nobody sits there. The other two stand empty: the middle one (mesa2) with a red cloth, the near one (mesa3) with a blue cloth. Two people stand at the vellonera, their backs to the room, choosing a song. The mesero sits on his stool by the kitchen door with his foot up, the saucer of sweets on the stool's shelf; the frog beside him. The señor and the señora at the rope.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El mesero tiene un pie malo." **(malo: a word of Las frutas)** Ask (signed, the mesero): "¿Dónde está el mesero?" He touches him: the mesero lifts his stick, and takes off his bow tie. NARR: "El mesero dice: Coquí, ahora tú eres el mesero." He puts the bow tie on the frog.
2. NARR: "La señora dice: Buenas noches. Una mesa para dos, por favor." **(buenas noches: heard)** The mesero points with his stick at the two empty tables in turn. NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa roja está libre. La mesa azul está ocupada." Ask (signed, the red table): "Una mesa para dos. ¿Cuál mesa está libre?" He touches it: the frog hops ahead, and the couple sits down at the red table. The two at the vellonera come back and sit down at the blue one.
3. The joven comes in with a friend. NARR: "El joven dice: ¿Hay una mesa libre? Para dos." The mesero shakes his head. NARR: "El mesero dice: No. La mesa roja está ocupada. La mesa azul está ocupada." NARR: "El mesero dice: Un momento, por favor." The two sit down on the bench.
4. More people come in at the door. NARR: "El mesero dice: La gente espera, Coquí. ¿Vamos?" COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo)]: "¡Listo!"

**The turn**

5. Later: the window is darker. The people at the red table and the blue table pay and go; the mesera puts two new cloths on. The mesero taps the saucer of sweets, and points at the frog. Couples wait at the rope. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The middle table and the near one stand empty, with cloths of two colours (drawn each round from rojo, azul, verde, amarillo and blanco; round B's two are not round A's). Who asks is drawn from the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer and the joven (round B's is not round A's): each comes with a companion. One of them is taken: its two people are at the vellonera, their backs to the room. The mesero, from his stool, says in one breath which is free and which is taken (which is said first is drawn). Nothing on the tables shows it. The frog leads the couple at the rope to a table. Between the rounds it is later again: new cloths, new people at the vellonera.

6. **Decision A, touch a table (two tables; tests libre against ocupada).** NARR: "La mujer dice: Una mesa para dos, por favor." NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa verde está ocupada. La mesa amarilla está libre." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the couple sits down; the mesero nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the couple sits down; the two come back from the vellonera and stand over them. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¡No, Coquí! La mesa verde está ocupada." The couple gets up, red in the face. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¡Perdón! Toma." He holds out the saucer; they take a sweet, and sit down at the other table. A cross. The sign is on the right table; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a table (the same game; two new colours, another couple).** NARR: "El joven dice: Para dos, por favor." NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa blanca está libre. La mesa roja está ocupada." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. Later still. Every table is taken (the party has come to the far one); music from the vellonera. The mesero holds out the saucer to the frog.
   - Three sweets. Stars and a heart.
   - Two sweets.
   - One sweet; the frog unwraps it slowly.
9. NARR: "El mesero dice: Toma, Coquí. Son para ti." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias, muchas gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" NARR: "La fonda está llena. No hay mesas libres." **(llena: a form of lleno)**

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** libre 3 (steps 2, 3), ocupada 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: mesero 3 (step 1) and as the speaker's name; para dos 3 before the game (steps 2, 3) and once in each round.
**Forms:** ocupada counts with ocupado; libres with libre.
**A miss is filed under:** ocupado (he took them to the table that was said to be taken).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two tables differ only in colour, and both colours are named.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): listo; gracias.

## Story 86: Con y sin

- **Goal he can see:** on the family's table, three small candles in glass cups, lit: the table glows. Abuela holds her hands to them and smiles.
- **At stake:** the candles (3). A wrong glass or plate, taken back in a hurry, splashes: one candle goes out.
- **New words:** menú, con, sin.
- **Back again:** mesero, mesera, café, leche, agua, limón, pan, mantequilla, negro, quiero, quieres, para, tengo sed, toma, aquí tiene, por favor, gracias, sí, no, mira.

**Start:** evening. The family at the middle table: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; the frog on the table beside the three candles. The mesera with her pad; the mesero behind her with a tray.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The mesera gives abuela a big card with pictures on it. NARR: "La mesera dice: Aquí tiene el menú." Ask (signed, the card): "¿Dónde está el menú?" He touches it: abuela opens it; on it, pictures of glasses, cups and plates. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Mira el menú, Coquí."
2. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Qué quieren tomar?" **(quieren, tomar: forms of quiere and toma)** NARR: "Luis dice: Tengo sed. Agua con limón, por favor." The mesero's tray holds two glasses of water: one with a slice of green lemon in it, one with none. Ask (signed, the glass with lemon): "Luis quiere agua con limón. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the frog pushes it to Luis. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para mí, agua sin limón." Ask (signed, the glass with no lemon): "¿Cuál es el agua sin limón?" He touches it: it goes to Carmen.
3. Two cups: one pale with milk, one black. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Un café con leche, por favor." NARR: "Abuela dice: Y para mí, un café negro. Sin leche." The mesero sets the pale cup before abuelo and the black one before abuela.
4. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Quieres agua?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, por favor!" NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Con limón o sin limón?" The frog points at the lemon; a small glass comes, with a slice in it.

**The turn**

5. The glasses are empty. The mesero comes back with a full tray: two glasses of water, two small plates of bread; he looks from one to the other, and at the family, lost. The candles flicker. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¡Ay! ¿Con limón? ¿Sin limón? ¡Ayúdame, Coquí!" **(ayúdame: heard; his face carries it)** The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One of the family (drawn; round B's is not round A's) asks for something, with or without (drawn). The tray shows the two of it, side by side (which is at the left is drawn). The frog pushes one across. Round A is bread, with butter or without (a pat of butter on the slice, or none); round B is water, with lemon or without.

6. **Decision A, touch one of the two (tests con against sin).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Para mí, pan sin mantequilla." (Or, drawn: "pan con mantequilla"; the speaker is drawn too.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it across; she eats, and nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the other one across; she looks at it, and shakes her head. NARR: "Carmen dice: No, Coquí. Sin mantequilla." The mesero snatches it back; a glass tips and splashes: a candle goes out. A cross. The sign is on the right one; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch one of the two (another of the family; water).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Agua con limón, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The lamps of the room go low for the music. The mesera brings a basket of bread.
   - Three candles: the whole table glows; all four faces are lit. Stars and a heart.
   - Two candles: abuelo's end of the table is dim.
   - One candle: only abuela's face is lit; abuelo holds the menu close to the flame.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿quieres pan?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Con mantequilla o sin mantequilla?" The frog points at the butter. NARR: "Coquí quiere pan con mantequilla."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** con 5 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5), sin 5 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5). Not deciding: menú 3 (step 1).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (con or sin).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two differ in what is seen (butter or none, lemon or none); only "con" or "sin" says which is wanted.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): sí; quiero.

## Reviews (Nov 21)

Two fresh reviewers read draft 1 with his rules and nothing else: one as a beginner, one as a Puerto Rican speaker.

Fixed:
- 84: the story contradicted itself: a right first round seated the family, and the second round found them at the door again. Now the way in has two stretches (doormat to stand, stand past the rope), the same two places to choose in both, and the family sits only in the ending. The goal (the table with the flowers, the star card, the pair behind eyeing it) is in the first picture. After a right "un momento" the frog is waved on, so where he sits says nothing about the next call. "Adelante" had been heard in one doubled line and one ask: three lines now. "Un momento, por favor" against a bare "¡Adelante!" could be told apart by "por favor": both calls carry it. "Abuelo dice: ... La mesera dice: un momento" put two speakers in one line. The mesera's greeting was echoed by the frog's turn: she says "¡Hola! ¡Bienvenidos!" and he answers "¡Buenas tardes!". Nobody on the island welcomes you "a la fonda" at its own door, or asks "¿Una mesa para la familia?": she asks "¿Cuántos son?". "¿Dónde entra?" for a door is "¿por dónde?": the ask is now "¿A dónde va Coquí?".
- 85: three tables could not hold the family, three couples and two more parties. The family is not in this story; one table is taken throughout; the other two are cleared between rounds ("later": a darker window, new cloths). The stake was couples seated, which the room could not show: it is the helper's three sweets, and a couple made to get up is given one with "¡Perdón! Toma." The mesero made the frog "the mesero" and the next line asked where the mesero was: the ask comes first, the bow tie after. The hat-on-a-chair beat and the bare label "Ocupada. No está libre." are gone; "ocupada" is heard in three whole sentences. "No hay una mesa libre" is "No hay mesas libres". The title's "u" is gone: "¿Está libre?". The mesero no longer thanks the frog before the frog thanks him.
- 86: nobody orders "un café sin leche": abuela's is "un café negro. Sin leche.", and coffee is out of the game (bread and water, which are said exactly so). "¿Qué quieren?" from a mesera is blunt: "¿Qué quieren tomar?". "Con, sin, con, sin... Coquí, por favor" was a drill and sounded cross: "¿Con limón? ¿Sin limón? ¡Ayúdame, Coquí!". The soup nobody ordered is a basket of bread. The ending with two candles had no picture of its own. The frog no longer wants his bread both ways, which undid the lesson. One ask fewer in the opening. The lemon is the island's small green one.

Left, and why:
- "Listo" as a turn (85): the beginner's reviewer could not know it is an earlier story's word (La mesa). It is.
- 85's miss is filed under ocupado only: whichever table he picks wrongly is the one that was called taken.
- The speaker names "el joven" and "la mujer" stay (the island would say el muchacho, la muchacha): they are the course's names for these two since El parque.
- "Bienvenido" and "fonda" decide nothing; each is heard three times.

# La entrada: three stories (draft 1, before the reviews)

Place 8, La fonda. Scene 1 of 4 (scene id `entrada`). Stories 84 to 86. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished stories 1 to 83.

Scene words (page 02): fonda, mesero, mesera, menú, para dos, libre, ocupado, adelante, bienvenido, un momento, con, sin.
How they are shared out: 84 fonda, mesera, bienvenido, adelante, un momento; 85 mesero, libre, ocupado, para dos; 86 menú, con, sin.

The picture: the way in to a small fonda, at dusk. One view, from inside the room looking at the door. At the left, the open door with the street's last light in it and a bench just outside; beside the door a little stand where the mesera keeps her list. The room: three tables along the windows, each with a cloth of its own colour, and at the right the swing door of the kitchen. Lamps.

New people: the mesera (an apron, a pencil behind her ear) and the mesero (a bow tie, a tray). The family: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen. Other guests: the people of El parque (the señor, the señora, the hombre, the mujer, the joven).

Voices, the signal for a right move, what follows a wrong move, pokes and the corner card are as in La calle. Every story has two scored rounds; the card shows two places. Every story ends with the shared `fin`. Every story starts fresh. Words: the everyday Puerto Rican word wins over the plan's (page 16).

## Story 84: Bienvenidos

- **Goal he can see:** the three tables along the windows: one has flowers and the best view, one is in the middle, one is beside the kitchen's swing door. A small card with a star stands on the table with the flowers. Abuela points at it through the door: that one.
- **At stake:** the good tables (3, best first). The mesera says to come in, or to wait a moment. The frog, who leads the family, does the other: the table that was ready goes to the people behind them.
- **New words:** fonda, mesera, bienvenido, adelante, un momento.
- **Back again:** buenas tardes, hola, tengo hambre, familia, mesa, espera, entra, puerta, por favor, gracias, vamos, aquí, sí, no, cuatro, noche (heard: buenas noches).

**Start:** dusk. The family stands outside the open door: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen, the frog in front on the doormat. Behind them on the street, the señor and the señora. Inside, the mesera at her stand.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "Abuela dice: Mira, Coquí: la fonda. Aquí comemos." **(comemos: heard)** Ask (signed, the door): "¿Dónde está la puerta de la fonda?" He touches it: the frog hops onto the doormat. The mesera looks up and smiles. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Buenas tardes! ¡Bienvenidos a la fonda!" COQUÍ [TURN: buenas tardes (buenas tardes)]: "¡Buenas tardes!"
2. A mesero crosses the doorway with a tall pile of plates. The mesera holds up a hand. NARR: "La mesera dice: Un momento, por favor." Ask (signed, the bench outside): "Un momento. ¿Dónde espera Coquí?" He touches it: the frog sits on the bench; the plates go by. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Un momento, Coquí. La mesera dice: un momento."
3. The mesera waves them in. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Adelante, adelante!" Ask (signed, the door): "¡Adelante! ¿Dónde entra Coquí?" He touches it: the frog hops in, and the family after him. NARR: "La mesera dice: Bienvenido, Coquí. ¿Una mesa para la familia?" NARR: "Abuela dice: Sí, por favor. Somos cuatro... y Coquí." **(somos: heard)**
4. The mesera looks at her list, and at the room: the table with the flowers is being cleared. She walks them back to the doormat. NARR: "La mesera dice: Un momento. La mesa no está lista." **(lista: a form of listo)**

**The turn**

5. The family waits on the doormat, the frog in front. Behind them the señor and the señora look over their shoulders at the same table. The mesera goes to and fro between her list and the room. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** The mesera, from inside, calls one of two things, drawn, each round on its own: to come in, or to wait a moment. Nothing in the picture shows which (she is looking at her list). The frog goes in through the door, or sits on the bench.

6. **Decision A, touch a place (the door, the bench; tests adelante against un momento).** NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡Adelante!" (Or, drawn: "La mesera dice: Un momento, por favor.")
   - Right: the frog does it. For "adelante" the family walks in to the table with the flowers; for "un momento" a mesero goes by with a full tray, and then she waves them in. A star.
   - Wrong: he sits when she said to come in: the señor and the señora walk past him to the table that was ready. (Or he goes in when she said to wait: he runs into the mesero's legs; the mesera, with her hands full of the mess, waves the señor and the señora to the table.) NARR: "La mesera dice: ¡No, Coquí! ¡Adelante!" (or: "¡No, Coquí! ¡Un momento!") A cross. The best table left is gone. The sign is on the right place; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch a place (the same game; the hombre and the mujer are behind the family now).** NARR: "La mesera dice: Un momento, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The mesera leads the family to the best table that is left.
   - Three: the table with the flowers, by the window. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: the table in the middle.
   - One: the table by the kitchen's door; the door swings, and abuelo's hat lifts each time.
9. NARR: "La mesera dice: Bienvenidos. ¿Tienen hambre?" **(tienen: a form of tiene)** COQUÍ [TURN: tengo hambre (tengo hambre, sí tengo hambre)]: "¡Sí, tengo hambre!" NARR: "La familia está en la fonda. Y Coquí tiene hambre."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** un momento 5 (steps 2, 4), adelante 3 (step 3). Not deciding: fonda 4; mesera many times (as the speaker's name); bienvenido 3 with bienvenidos (steps 1, 3).
**Forms:** bienvenidos counts with bienvenido.
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (adelante or un momento).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 1, 9): buenas tardes; tengo hambre.

## Story 85: ¿Libre u ocupada?

- **Goal he can see:** the mesero has a bad foot (a bandage, a stick): he gives the frog his bow tie and points at the door, where people are waiting. On his stand, a saucer with three wrapped sweets: for the helper.
- **At stake:** the people waiting at the door (three couples). A couple shown to a table that is taken has to get up again when its people come back: they leave the fonda.
- **New words:** mesero, libre, ocupado, para dos.
- **Back again:** fonda, mesera, adelante, bienvenido, un momento, mesa, silla, rojo, azul, verde, amarillo, blanco, dos, señor, señora, hombre, mujer, joven, gracias, por favor, aquí, está, no, sí.

**Start:** evening; the lamps are lit. Three tables: at one the family eats; two more stand empty, with cloths of two colours (on one of them, nothing shows that it is taken). The mesero sits on a stool by the kitchen door with his foot up. The frog on the mesero's stand, the bow tie round his neck. A couple at the door.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. NARR: "El mesero tiene un pie malo." **(malo: a word of Las frutas)** NARR: "El mesero dice: Coquí, ahora tú eres el mesero." Ask (signed, the mesero): "¿Dónde está el mesero?" He touches him: the mesero taps the frog's bow tie with his stick.
2. The couple at the door. NARR: "La señora dice: Buenas noches. Una mesa para dos, por favor." **(buenas noches: heard)** The mesero points with his stick at the two empty tables in turn. NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa roja está libre." He pulls a chair out with his stick. NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa azul está ocupada." Two people come back from the vellonera (the jukebox at the back wall) and sit down at the blue one. Ask (signed, the red table): "Una mesa para dos. ¿Cuál mesa está libre?" He touches it: the frog hops ahead, and the couple sits down at the red table.
3. Another empty-looking table; a hat lies on its chair. NARR: "El mesero dice: Mira: una mesa para dos. ¿Está libre?" The frog hops towards it; the mesero shakes his head. NARR: "El mesero dice: No. Está ocupada. El señor viene ahora." The señor comes back with a glass and sits down by his hat. NARR: "El mesero dice: Ocupada. No está libre."
4. Another couple at the door. NARR: "El joven dice: ¿Hay una mesa para dos?" NARR: "El mesero dice: Un momento, por favor. ¡Vamos, mesero!" He points at the frog. COQUÍ [TURN: listo (listo)]: "¡Listo!"

**The turn**

5. The tables are cleared; two stand empty again, with new cloths. Three couples wait at the door. The mesero holds up the saucer of sweets, and puts it down. The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** Two empty tables with cloths of two colours, drawn each round from rojo, azul, verde, amarillo and blanco. The mesero, from his stool, says which is free and which is taken (which is said first is drawn). Nothing on the tables shows it. The frog leads the couple at the door to a table.

6. **Decision A, touch a table (two tables; tests libre against ocupada).** NARR: "La mujer dice: Una mesa para dos, por favor." NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa verde está ocupada. La mesa amarilla está libre." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the couple sits down; the mesero nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the couple sits down; two people come back from the vellonera and stand over them. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¡No, Coquí! La mesa verde está ocupada." The couple gets up, red in the face, and goes out of the door. A cross. The sign is on the right table; he touches it: the frog bows to it; it stays empty.
7. **Decision B, touch a table (the same game; two new colours).** NARR: "El joven dice: Para dos, por favor." NARR: "El mesero dice: La mesa blanca está libre. La mesa roja está ocupada." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The last couple at the door is shown in by the mesero himself, hopping on one foot.
   - Three couples seated: the fonda is full; music. The mesero gives the frog the saucer: three sweets. Stars and a heart.
   - Two: one table stands empty.
   - One: two tables stand empty; the mesera sits down at one and takes off her shoes.
9. NARR: "El mesero dice: Gracias, mesero. Toma." COQUÍ [TURN: gracias (gracias)]: "¡Gracias!" NARR: "La fonda está llena. No hay una mesa libre." **(llena: a form of lleno)** (With an empty table: "En la fonda hay una mesa libre.")

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** libre 4 (steps 2, 3), ocupada 3 (steps 2, 3). Not deciding: mesero 6; para dos 4 before the game (steps 2, 3, 4) and once in each round.
**Forms:** ocupada counts with ocupado.
**A miss is filed under:** libre.
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two tables differ only in colour, and both colours are named.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): listo; gracias.

## Story 86: Con y sin

- **Goal he can see:** on the family's table, three small candles in glass cups, lit: the table glows. Abuela holds her hands to them and smiles.
- **At stake:** the candles (3). A wrong glass or cup, taken back in a hurry, splashes: one candle goes out.
- **New words:** menú, con, sin.
- **Back again:** mesero, mesera, fonda, café, leche, agua, limón, pan, mantequilla, vaso, taza, quiero, quieres, para, por favor, gracias, sí, no, ¿qué quieres?, tengo sed, toma, aquí tiene.

**Start:** evening. The family at its table: abuela, abuelo, Luis, Carmen; the frog on the table beside the three candles. The mesera with her pad; the mesero behind her with a tray.

**The opening** (big sign)

1. The mesera gives abuela a big card with pictures on it. NARR: "La mesera dice: Aquí tiene el menú." Ask (signed, the card): "¿Dónde está el menú?" He touches it: abuela opens it; on it, pictures of glasses, cups and plates. NARR: "Abuela dice: Gracias. Mira, Coquí: el menú."
2. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Qué quieren?" **(quieren: a form of quiere)** NARR: "Luis dice: Tengo sed. Agua con limón, por favor." The mesero's tray holds two glasses of water: one with a slice of lemon in it, one with none. Ask (signed, the glass with lemon): "Agua con limón. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: the frog pushes it to Luis. NARR: "Carmen dice: Para mí, agua sin limón." Ask (signed, the glass with no lemon): "Sin limón. ¿Cuál es?" He touches it: it goes to Carmen.
3. Two cups: one black, one pale with milk. NARR: "Abuelo dice: Un café con leche, por favor." NARR: "Abuela dice: Y para mí, un café sin leche." Ask (signed, the pale cup): "¿Cuál es el café con leche?" He touches it: it goes to abuelo; the mesero sets the black one before abuela. NARR: "Abuela dice: Sin leche. Gracias."
4. NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Y tú, Coquí? ¿Quieres agua?" COQUÍ [TURN: sí (sí, si)]: "¡Sí, por favor!" NARR: "La mesera dice: ¿Con limón o sin limón?" The frog points at the lemon; a small glass comes, with a slice in it.

**The turn**

5. The glasses are empty. The mesera writes a second round on her pad; the mesero comes back with a full tray: pairs of glasses, of cups and of plates, and he looks from one to the other, lost. The candles flicker. NARR: "El mesero dice: ¡Ay! Con, sin, con, sin... Coquí, por favor." The card shows two places. The signs go.

**The game.** One of the family (drawn) asks for something, with or without (drawn): agua con limón or sin limón, café con leche or sin leche, pan con mantequilla or sin mantequilla. The tray shows the two of it, side by side (which is at the left is drawn). The frog pushes one across.

6. **Decision A, touch one of the two (tests con against sin).** NARR: "Abuelo dice: Para mí, un café sin leche." (Drawn.)
   - Right: the frog pushes it across; he drinks, and nods. A star.
   - Wrong: the frog pushes the other one across; he sips, and makes a face. NARR: "Abuelo dice: No, Coquí. Sin leche." The mesero snatches it back; it splashes: a candle goes out. A cross. The sign is on the right one; he touches it.
7. **Decision B, touch one of the two (another of the family, another thing).** NARR: "Carmen dice: Pan con mantequilla, por favor." (Drawn.) Right and wrong as in A.

**The ending**

8. The soup comes. The lamps of the room go low for the music.
   - Three candles: the table glows; the family's faces are lit; the people at the next table turn to look. Stars and a heart.
   - Two candles: lit well enough.
   - One candle: abuelo holds the menu close to it to read.
9. NARR: "Abuela dice: Coquí, ¿quieres pan?" COQUÍ [TURN: quiero (quiero, sí quiero)]: "¡Sí, quiero!" NARR: "Abuela dice: ¿Con mantequilla o sin mantequilla?" The frog holds up both hands. NARR: "Coquí quiere pan con mantequilla. Y pan sin mantequilla también."

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** con 5 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5), sin 6 (steps 2, 3, 4, 5). Not deciding: menú 3 (step 1).
**A miss is filed under:** the one that was said (con or sin).
**Chance without the Spanish:** one in two, each round on its own: the two differ in what is seen (lemon or none, milk or none, butter or none); only "con" or "sin" says which he wants.
**Speaking turns:** two (steps 4, 9): sí; quiero.

# El campo: three stories (draft 1, before reviews)

Place 2, El parque. Scene 2 of 4. The learner is the little frog (Coquí). He has finished place 1, La casa (eleven stories), and El camino (stories 12 to 14).

Words he has met (place 1): casa, puerta, ventana, abuela, hola, toca, abre, cierra, sí, no, grande, pequeña, adiós, silla, se sienta, vaso, jugo, agua, toma, quiero, por favor, gracias, de nada, ¿cómo estás?, bien, mamá, papá, abuelo, bebé, familia, hermano, hermana, él, ella, yo, tú, soy, eres, me llamo, niño, niña, juega, flor, sol, aquí, allá, amigo, ven, mira, vamos, ¿qué es esto?, hasta mañana.
Words he has met (El camino): perro, gato, árbol, parque, camina, corre, espera, rápido, despacio, hay, ¿dónde está?, uno, dos, tres.
Heard along the way so far: pájaro, bizcocho, lluvia, mesa, balcón, sala, patio, bola, portón, foto, hace calor, para, con, también, ahora, quiere, tiene, entra, come, está, están, dónde, quién, mi, es, un, una, y, de, en, se llama, este, esta, a jugar, muy, Luis, Carmen, Canelo.

Scene words for El campo (from the 40-scene list): bola, tira, coge, salta, dame, mi, tu, arriba, abajo, más, otra vez, tiene.

The picture: the open field of the park. Flat grass in the middle. On the left, one tree with a round crown and a low fork; under it, a worn patch of earth, which is Canelo's spot. On the right the ground slopes down to the edge of the pond: reeds, and a strip of water in the bottom right corner. Sky, hills. Luis (the boy, green shirt), Carmen (the girl, braids, pink dress) and Canelo (abuela's small tan dog, one ear down, red collar) are here.

The balls. Each one looks different, so nobody needs a colour word:
- Luis's ball: big, with stripes.
- Carmen's ball: middle-sized, with dots.
- The frog's balls: small, with a star. He has one at his feet; three more are in a little basket beside him (story 15).

Voices: the narrator speaks to the learner. A person's lines are spoken by the narrator and start with who says it ("Luis dice:"); the words show in a bubble at that person. The frog has his own voice (COQUÍ). The dog only makes his sounds.

The signal for a right move is the same as always: chime, stars, the frog hops, then "¡Sí! ¡Muy bien!".
After a wrong move, always: the cost; what he did stays done; a short "No." line that repeats the word; then the big sign shows the right one, he does it, and the right side plays.
Poking is free: anything he touches outside an answer is named ("Es el árbol." "Es la bola de Luis." "Es el agua." "Es el perro. Se llama Canelo.").
A decision's line is said once; a touch before it ends does not count; if he waits it plays again, free.
Nobody points with a hand or looks toward the answer in a decision. Nobody else moves until he has touched.

Two kinds of answer bubble over the frog, both known:
- **Doing bubbles**: pictures of what the frog can do; he touches one. The pictures move. Which ones show, and where, is drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.
- **Speaking bubbles**: no words in them; each says its line aloud in his voice, one after the other; he picks by ear. Which side each is on is drawn fresh every time, also under the big sign.

The doing pictures of this scene:
- throwing: the frog with one arm swung forward, a ball well away from him in the air, flying off, lines trailing behind it;
- catching: the frog with both arms up and open, a ball dropping into his hands from above;
- giving: the frog holding a ball out in both hands, toward an open hand at the edge of the picture;
- hands up (story 16): the frog stretched tall on his toes, both arms straight up, an arrow pointing up;
- hands down (story 16): the frog crouched low, both arms down to the grass, an arrow pointing down;
- jumping (story 16): the frog high in the air, legs out, his shadow on the ground far below;
- holding (story 17): the frog hugging the ball to his chest with both arms, standing still, the raised hand of "wait" beside him.

## Story 15: La bola

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** three friends in a field, each with a ball, and a dog who cannot take his eyes off them. Play catch. Keep your balls.
- **At stake:** the frog's small balls: the one in play and the ones in his basket (three in all once the game starts). Each wrong move, Canelo takes the ball in play to his spot under the tree and lies on it. It stays there.
- **New words:** bola, tiene, tira, coge, dame.
- **Back again:** grande, pequeña, mira, vamos, toma, gracias, perro, hay, tres, también, juega.
- **Heard along the way:** campo, ahora, no tira.

**Start:** Luis stands on the left with his big ball in his hands. Carmen stands on the right with hers. The frog stands between them, nearer us, a small ball at his feet and the little basket with three more beside him. Canelo sits under the tree, ears up, watching the balls.

1. NARR: "Este es el campo." NARR: "Mira. Luis tiene una bola." (Luis's ball twinkles.) NARR: "Carmen tiene una bola." (Hers twinkles.) NARR: "Y Coquí tiene una bola también." (His twinkles.) COQUÍ: "¡Mi bola!"

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "¿Quién tiene la bola grande?" The big sign is on Luis. He touches him. Right signal. NARR: "Luis tiene la bola grande."
3. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos a jugar! Coquí, ¡coge la bola!" Two doing bubbles: catching, throwing. The big sign is on catching. He touches it. Luis throws his big ball in a high arc; the frog catches it. NARR: "Luis tira la bola. Coquí coge la bola."
4. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ahora, tira la bola!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on throwing. He touches it. The frog throws the big ball back; Luis catches it. NARR: "Coquí tira la bola. Luis coge la bola."
5. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Coquí, coge la bola!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on catching. He touches it. Carmen throws her dotted ball; the frog catches it. NARR: "Carmen tira la bola. Coquí coge la bola."
6. Carmen comes two steps nearer and holds out both hands. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Dame la bola!" Two doing bubbles: giving, throwing. The sign is on giving. He touches it. The frog walks over and puts the ball in her hands. COQUÍ: "Toma, Carmen." NARR: "Carmen dice: Gracias." She goes back to her place.

**The turn**

7. Canelo cannot stand it any longer. He dashes in, takes the small ball from the frog's feet, runs back to his spot under the tree and lies down with it between his paws. NARR: "¡Ay! ¡Canelo tiene la bola!" COQUÍ: "¡Canelo! ¡Dame la bola!" Canelo wags his tail and does not move. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Canelo! ¡Dame la bola!" Canelo puts his chin on the ball.
8. Luis laughs and points at the little basket. NARR: "Luis dice: Mira. Hay tres bolas." (The basket twinkles; the three small balls show in the corner of the screen.) Carmen takes one out and puts it at the frog's feet. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Do (throw or catch).** Luis has his big ball in his hands; the frog has a small ball at his feet. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, tira la bola!" (Or: "¡Coquí, coge la bola!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: throwing, catching.
   - Right, "tira": the frog picks up his ball and throws it; Luis lets his own drop, catches the small one and rolls it back. Right signal.
   - Right, "coge": Luis throws his big ball; the frog catches it and throws it back. Right signal.
   - Wrong (he throws when he should catch): the frog throws his small ball just as Luis throws the big one. The big ball bumps the frog on the head and rolls back to Luis. The small ball sails past Luis; Canelo leaps, takes it out of the air and carries it to his spot. One ball fewer in the corner. NARR: "No. ¡Coge la bola!" Carmen puts a new ball at his feet. The sign; he does it.
   - Wrong (he gets ready to catch when he should throw): the frog stands with his arms up. Nothing comes. Canelo trots over, takes the ball from his feet and carries it to his spot. One ball fewer. NARR: "No. ¡Tira la bola!" A new ball. The sign; he does it.
10. **Decision B, Do (throw or give).** The frog picks up his small ball and holds it. Carmen has put hers down at her feet; her hands are empty and at her sides. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Coquí, dame la bola!" (Or: "¡Coquí, tira la bola!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: giving, throwing.
   - Right, "dame": the frog walks over and puts it in her hands; she says "Gracias" and gives it back. Right signal.
   - Right, "tira": he throws; she catches it and rolls it back. Right signal.
   - Wrong (he throws when she said "dame"): she has stepped forward with her hands out low; the ball sails over her head. Canelo takes it out of the air. One ball fewer. NARR: "No. ¡Dame la bola!" A new ball. The sign; he does it.
   - Wrong (he walks over when she said "tira"): she has stepped back with her arms up; on his way across, Canelo runs past and takes the ball out of his hands. One ball fewer. NARR: "No. ¡Tira la bola!" A new ball. The sign; he does it.
11. **Decision C, Touch (which ball).** Luis rolls his big ball over to the frog. Now two balls lie at the frog's feet, side by side: Luis's big one and his own small one. (Which is on the left is drawn fresh.) NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Dame la bola grande!" (Or: "¡Dame la bola pequeña!" Drawn fresh.) He touches a ball.
   - Right: the frog carries that ball to Luis. COQUÍ: "Toma, Luis." Right signal. (If it was the small one, Luis gives it back.)
   - Wrong: the frog carries the other ball to Luis; Luis shakes his head. Canelo takes the small ball (from the grass if the frog left it behind; from the frog's hands if he was carrying it). One ball fewer. NARR: "No. La bola grande." (Or "No. La bola pequeña.") The sign; he does it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. By the small balls the frog has left:
   - Three: the frog, Luis and Carmen throw all their balls high at once; stars and a heart; Canelo runs round them barking.
   - One or two: they throw what they have; fewer balls in the air.
   - None: the frog has no ball. He sits down in the grass by Canelo and scratches his ears, while Luis and Carmen throw theirs.
13. NARR: "Luis tira la bola. Carmen coge la bola." NARR: "¿Y Canelo? Canelo tiene una bola. ¡Y Canelo no tira la bola!" COQUÍ: "¡Ay, Canelo!" Canelo wags his tail on his ball.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** tira 5 (steps 3, 4 twice, 5; the bubbles' picture), coge 5 (steps 3 twice, 4, 5 twice), dame 3 (steps 6, 7 twice), grande 2 here and many in place 1, pequeña many in place 1.

**A miss is filed under:** A: the word that was called (tira or coge). B: the word that was called (dame or tira). C: grande or pequeña.

## Story 16: ¡Arriba! ¡Abajo!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** a ball stuck up in the tree, and everybody looking up at it. Then: play catch with high balls and low balls, by the pond.
- **At stake:** the balls in Luis's basket (three once the game starts). A ball the frog does not get goes on down the slope and into the pond: plop. It floats away. It stays there.
- **New words:** arriba, abajo, salta.
- **Back again:** bola, tira, coge, tiene, árbol, ¿dónde está?, hay, tres, agua, corre, mira.
- **Heard along the way:** va, en el agua, mi.

**Start:** Luis has just kicked his striped ball. It is in the fork of the tree. Luis, Carmen, Canelo and the frog stand under the tree, looking up. A basket with three balls stands in the grass behind Luis.

1. NARR: "¡Mira! La bola está arriba." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Ay, no! Mi bola está arriba, en el árbol."
2. Luis jumps; his hand is far below the ball. NARR: "Luis salta." Carmen jumps; no. NARR: "Carmen salta." Canelo jumps and barks. NARR: "Canelo salta."

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

3. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Coquí, salta!" Two doing bubbles: jumping, running. The big sign is on jumping. He touches it. The frog crouches and jumps, very high, higher than anybody, and knocks the ball out of the tree. Right signal. NARR: "¡Coquí salta arriba!"
4. The ball drops, bounces once on the grass and rolls down the slope. It stops in the reeds at the water's edge. NARR: "Ahora la bola está abajo." NARR: "¿Dónde está la bola?" The big sign is on the ball in the reeds. He touches it. The frog hops down, gets it and brings it up. Right signal. NARR: "Coquí tiene la bola."
5. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Vamos a jugar! ¡Coge la bola!" The frog stands with his back to the slope and the pond; Luis faces him with the ball held at his chest. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Arriba!" Two doing bubbles: hands up, hands down. The sign is on hands up. He touches it. Luis throws high; the frog jumps and catches it over his head. NARR: "La bola va arriba. Coquí salta."
6. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Abajo!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on hands down. He touches it. Luis rolls the ball along the grass; the frog crouches and takes it. NARR: "La bola va abajo."

**The turn**

7. Canelo wants a go. He stands where the frog stood. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Canelo! ¡Abajo!" Luis rolls the ball. Canelo jumps straight up in the air. The ball rolls under him, on down the slope and into the pond: plop. It floats off. NARR: "¡Ay, no! La bola está en el agua." Canelo looks at the water, ears down.
8. Luis fetches the basket. NARR: "Luis dice: Hay tres bolas." (The basket twinkles; three balls show in the corner of the screen.) The frog takes his place again, his back to the pond. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign). Decisions A and B come in an order drawn fresh; C is last.

9. **Decision A, Do (high or low).** Luis holds a ball at his chest. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Arriba!" (Or: "¡Abajo!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: hands up, hands down.
   - Right: Luis throws it the way he called; the frog gets it and tosses it back into the basket. Right signal.
   - Wrong: Luis throws it the way he called; the frog is doing the other thing. The ball goes over his head (or between his feet), down the slope and into the pond: plop. One ball fewer. NARR: "No. ¡Arriba!" (Or "No. ¡Abajo!") Luis takes another ball. The sign; he does it; he gets that one.
10. **Decision B, Touch (where to look).** Carmen has the ball. She kicks it hard, straight up; it flies out of the top of the picture. Everybody looks up. Nothing comes down that we can see. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está la bola?" NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Está arriba!" (Or: "¡Está abajo!" Drawn fresh.) He touches the crown of the tree or the reeds.
   - Right, "arriba": the frog jumps up into the tree; the ball comes out of the leaves. Right signal.
   - Right, "abajo": the frog hops down to the reeds; the ball is there. Right signal.
   - Wrong: the frog goes to the other place and finds nothing (a bird flies out of the tree; or a little fish jumps by the reeds). Meanwhile the ball comes loose where it really was, rolls down the slope and into the pond: plop. One ball fewer. NARR: "No. Está arriba." (Or "No. Está abajo.") Luis kicks another the same way. The sign shows the right place; he touches it; he gets that one.
11. **Decision C, Say, last (he sees it; Luis does not).** Luis sits down to tie his shoe, his head down. Carmen kicks the ball. This time we see where it lands, drawn fresh: in the fork of the tree, or down in the reeds. Luis looks up. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Dónde está la bola?" Two speaking bubbles: "¡Está arriba!" and "¡Está abajo!"
   - Right: the frog says where it is. Luis goes there and gets it (he shakes the tree; or he picks it out of the reeds). Right signal.
   - Wrong: the frog says the other. Luis goes to the wrong place and looks. Meanwhile the ball comes loose, rolls down and into the pond: plop. One ball fewer. NARR: "No. Está arriba." (Or "No. Está abajo.") Carmen kicks another to the same place. The sign shows the right bubble; he touches it; Luis gets that one.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. By the balls left in the basket:
   - Three: Luis tips the basket out; all three balls go up in the air at once and the frog jumps among them. Stars and a heart.
   - One or two: they throw what is left.
   - None: the basket is empty. They all stand at the edge and look at the balls floating in the pond.
13. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Arriba!" Everybody jumps, Canelo too. NARR: "Luis salta. Carmen salta. ¡Y Coquí salta más arriba!" Canelo barks at the pond.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** arriba 5 (steps 1 twice, 3, 5 twice), abajo 4 (steps 4, 6 twice, 7).

**A miss is filed under:** the word that was called or that was true (arriba or abajo).

## Story 17: ¡Otra vez!

- **Goal he can see, from the first frame:** Canelo, wet from the pond, drops a ball at the frog's feet and stares at him, tail going. Throw the ball for the dog. Stay dry.
- **At stake:** the frog, dry. When the game goes wrong, Canelo, who is wet, shakes himself right beside the frog. Each time the frog is wetter: a few drops; dripping; soaked, standing in a puddle. It stays. Three drops in the corner of the screen fill in, one a time.
- **New words:** mi, tu, más, otra vez.
- **Back again:** bola, tira, dame, coge, tiene, quiere, agua, perro, espera, no, gracias.
- **Heard along the way:** esta es, no más, quiere más.

**Start:** the three balls are in the reeds at the water's edge. Canelo is in the water up to his chest. Luis, Carmen and the frog stand in the field. Canelo comes up the slope three times, a ball in his mouth each time, and drops them in a row in front of the frog: the striped one, the dotted one, the small one with the star. He is dripping.

1. NARR: "Mira. Canelo tiene las bolas." Luis points at the striped one. NARR: "Luis dice: Esta es mi bola." (It twinkles.) Carmen points at the dotted one. NARR: "Carmen dice: Esta es mi bola." (It twinkles.) Luis points at the small one. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, esta es tu bola." (It twinkles.) COQUÍ: "¡Mi bola!"

**The opening** (big sign, nothing can go wrong)

2. NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¡dame mi bola!" The big sign is on the striped ball. He touches it. The frog carries it to Luis. COQUÍ: "Toma. Tu bola." Right signal. NARR: "Luis dice: Gracias." Luis puts it back in the row.
3. NARR: "Carmen dice: Coquí, ¡tira tu bola!" The sign is on the small ball. He touches it. The frog throws it down the slope; Canelo tears after it, splashes into the pond, brings it back and drops it at the frog's feet. Down by the water, he shakes himself. Right signal. NARR: "Coquí tira la bola. Canelo coge la bola."
4. Canelo pushes the ball at the frog with his nose and barks. NARR: "Canelo quiere más." NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Otra vez!" Two doing bubbles: throwing, holding. The sign is on throwing. He touches it. The frog throws; Canelo fetches. NARR: "Coquí tira la bola otra vez."
5. Canelo barks again. NARR: "Carmen dice: ¡Más! ¡Más!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on throwing. He touches it. Throw; fetch. NARR: "Canelo quiere más. ¡Otra vez!"
6. Canelo comes back slowly, his tongue out. NARR: "Luis dice: No más. Canelo quiere agua." The two doing bubbles; the sign on holding. He touches it. The frog hugs the ball and stands still. Canelo goes down and drinks from the pond. NARR: "No más."
7. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Otra vez!" The two doing bubbles; the sign on throwing. He touches it. Throw; fetch. Canelo is wetter than ever.

**The turn**

8. Carmen has picked up the ball. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Otra vez, Carmen!" But Carmen is watching a butterfly. Canelo waits. He looks at her. Then he shakes himself, from nose to tail, right beside her: water everywhere. Carmen stands dripping. NARR: "¡Ay, Carmen!" Luis laughs. Canelo walks over and sits down beside the frog, still very wet. Three empty drops show in the corner of the screen. The three balls are in their row in front of the frog. The signs go away.

**The game** (no sign)

9. **Decision A, Touch (whose ball).** NARR: "Luis dice: Coquí, ¡tira mi bola!" (Or Luis: "¡tira tu bola!" Or Carmen: "¡tira mi bola!" or "¡tira tu bola!" Who speaks and which word are both drawn fresh.) He touches one of the three balls.
   - Right: the frog throws that ball. Canelo fetches it and shakes himself down by the water. Right signal.
   - Wrong: the frog throws another ball. Canelo does not run; he looks at the ball that was meant, then at the frog, and shakes himself where he sits. The frog is splashed. One drop fills. NARR: "No. Mi bola." (Or "No. Tu bola.", in the voice line of the one who asked: "Luis dice: No. Mi bola.") The sign; he does it; Canelo fetches.
10. **Decision B, Do (more or no more).** Canelo drops the small ball at the frog's feet and looks up. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Más!" (Or: "¡No más!" Drawn fresh.) Two doing bubbles: throwing, holding.
   - Right, "más": he throws; Canelo fetches and shakes himself by the water. Right signal.
   - Right, "no más": he holds the ball; Canelo goes down and drinks. Right signal.
   - Wrong (he holds it when Luis said "más"): Canelo waits, then shakes himself beside the frog. One drop fills. NARR: "No. ¡Más!" The sign; he does it.
   - Wrong (he throws when Luis said "no más"): Canelo fetches it all the same, comes back wetter and shakes himself beside the frog. One drop fills. NARR: "No. ¡No más!" The sign; he does it.
11. **Decision C, Do, last (three bubbles).** Canelo drops the ball again. One of three calls, drawn fresh, but never the one Decision B had. NARR: "Luis dice: ¡Otra vez!" (Or: "¡No más!" Or: "¡Dame la bola!") Three doing bubbles: throwing, holding, giving.
   - Right, "otra vez": he throws; Canelo fetches. Right signal.
   - Right, "no más": he holds it; Canelo drinks. Right signal.
   - Right, "dame la bola": he walks over and gives it to Luis; Luis throws it for Canelo himself. Right signal.
   - Wrong: he does one of the others. Canelo shakes himself beside the frog. One drop fills. NARR: "No. ¡Otra vez!" (Or "No. ¡No más!" Or "No. ¡Dame la bola!") The sign; he does it.

**The ending** (the same lines in every ending)

12. The sun is low. Canelo lies down at last, the small ball between his paws, and shuts his eyes. By how wet the frog is:
   - Dry: the frog stands bright and dry between Luis and dripping Carmen. Stars and a heart.
   - A few drops, or dripping: he is as wet as that.
   - Soaked: he stands in a puddle, water running off him; Carmen, just as wet, laughs with him.
13. NARR: "Canelo no quiere más." Canelo opens one eye and pushes the ball at the frog with his nose. NARR: "Luis dice: ¿Otra vez?" COQUÍ: "¡No más!" They all laugh.

**Fewest hearings before a word can cost:** mi 4 (step 1 three times, step 2), tu 3 (steps 1, 2, 3), más 5 (steps 4, 5 three times, 6), otra vez 5 (steps 4 twice, 5, 7, 8), no más 2 as a call (step 6 twice), dame many (story 15).

**A miss is filed under:** A: mi or tu. B: más. C: the word that was called (otra vez, más, or dame).

- "Coge" is used as in Puerto Rico: catch it, take it.
- The balls are told apart by size and pattern, not colour; colours come at the pond.
- Story 17 has no thing to lose: the frog gets wet.
